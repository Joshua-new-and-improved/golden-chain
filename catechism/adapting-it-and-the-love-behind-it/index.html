Adapting it, and the love behind it

What does the Catholic Church teach about adapting it, and the love behind it? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 23–25

In one sentence: Local teaching adapts the Catechism to each culture, and everything in it exists for love.

What the Catechism says

The Catechism deliberately does not adapt its teaching for every age, culture or situation. That work belongs to local catechisms and to those who teach.

It ends its prologue with the key to the whole book. All teaching and all catechesis are directed to the love that never ends. Every point of doctrine is there to show God's love and to help us love him in return.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 23 The Catechism emphasises the exposition of doctrine. It aims to deepen understanding of faith and is oriented to the maturing of faith, its putting down roots in life and its shining forth in conduct.

§ 24 By design it does not adapt doctrine to different cultures, ages, spiritual maturity or social and ecclesial conditions. Those adaptations belong to local catechisms and, still more, to those who instruct the faithful.

§ 25 A pastoral principle concludes the Prologue: the whole concern of doctrine and its teaching must be directed to the love that never ends. Whether something is proposed for belief, hope or action, the love of our Lord must always be made accessible, so that every Christian virtue springs from love and tends to love (quoting the Roman Catechism).

Words to know

Inculturation
Expressing the same faith in the language and life of a particular culture.
Charity
The theological virtue of love: loving God for his own sake, and our neighbour for God's sake.

In the Bible

  • 1 Cor 13:8: charity never falleth away

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.23 a.8: Charity is the form of all the virtues: it directs every other virtue to God, our last end.
  • ST II-II q.27 a.6: We are to love God without measure, because he is himself the measure.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “"My vocation is love": she found her place in the Church's heart.” (St Thérèse of Lisieux (Story of a Soul))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Teaching doctrine and showing love are opposites.
    What the Church teaches: All doctrine is directed to love (§ 25); true charity includes the truth.
  • Not quite: A catechist should simply read the Catechism aloud.
    What the Church teaches: Adaptation to the people in front of you is the catechist's work (§ 24).

To think about

Think of a teaching of the Church you find hard. How might it be an expression of love?

For catechists

Aim: Catechists understand their own role: faithful to the doctrine, attentive to their group, and always leading to love.

  1. How would you explain the Trinity differently to a 10-year-old and to a university student?
  2. Can you think of a teaching that seems strict but is really about love?
  3. What does it mean that doctrine should "take root in life" (§ 23)?

Activity: Take one doctrine (e.g. the Real Presence) and plan how you'd present it to children, teens and adults.
