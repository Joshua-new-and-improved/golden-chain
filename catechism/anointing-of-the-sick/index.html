Anointing of the Sick

What does the Catholic Church teach about anointing of the Sick? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1499–1532

In one sentence: Christ comes close to the seriously ill and the elderly in this sacrament, giving strength, peace, union with his Passion, and forgiveness.

What the Catechism says

Illness confronts us with our limits and can lead to anguish or to God. Christ healed the sick as a sign of the Kingdom and sent his disciples to anoint and heal. James describes the presbyters praying over the sick and anointing them.

The sacrament is for those in danger of death from illness or old age, and it can be repeated. Only priests and bishops give it, anointing the forehead and hands with blessed oil. Its graces: strength, peace and courage; union with the Passion of Christ; grace for the Church; forgiveness of sins if one cannot confess; restoration of health if it helps salvation; and preparation for the final journey. Viaticum, Communion for the dying, is the last sacrament of the Christian.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1499 "By the sacred anointing of the sick and the prayer of the priests the whole Church commends those who are ill to the suffering and glorified Lord, that he may raise them up and save them."

§ 1500–1505 Illness and suffering have always been among the gravest problems in human life. Illness can lead to anguish, self-absorption, sometimes even despair and revolt against God; it can also make a person more mature, helping him discern what is not essential and turn towards God. In the Old Testament, illness is mysteriously linked to sin and evil, and faithfulness to God restores life. Christ, the physician, shows compassion towards the sick; his healings are signs of the coming of the Kingdom. By his Passion and death on the Cross he gave a new meaning to suffering: it can configure us to him and unite us with his redemptive Passion.

§ 1506–1513 "Heal the sick!" The Church has received this charge and strives to carry it out by caring for the sick and by intercessory prayer. James testifies to a rite for the sick: "Is any among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord." Tradition recognises this as one of the seven sacraments. Over time it came to be conferred more and more exclusively on the dying ("Extreme Unction"); the Church has restored its broader meaning.

§ 1514–1516 It is not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death. The proper time is as soon as anyone begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age. If a sick person recovers and then falls ill again, or the illness worsens, the sacrament can be received again. It is fitting before a serious operation, and for the elderly whose frailty becomes more pronounced. Only priests (bishops and presbyters) are ministers. Pastors should instruct the faithful on its benefits, and the faithful should encourage the sick to call for a priest.

§ 1517–1519 It is a liturgical and communal celebration, in the family, hospital or church, for one or several sick persons; fittingly within the Eucharist. If circumstances suggest, it can be preceded by Penance and followed by Communion. The essential rite: the priest lays hands on the sick, prays over them in the faith of the Church, and anoints the forehead and hands with oil blessed, if possible, by the bishop.

§ 1520–1523 Effects: a particular gift of the Spirit: strengthening, peace and courage to overcome the difficulties of serious illness or old age; renewal of trust in God against temptations to discouragement and anguish in the face of death; healing of the soul, and of the body if God wills; forgiveness of sins if the person could not obtain it through Penance. Union with the Passion of Christ: the sick person receives strength to unite himself more closely to Christ's Passion, so that suffering acquires new meaning. An ecclesial grace: the sick contribute to the good of the People of God. A preparation for the final journey.

§ 1524–1525 Viaticum: to those about to leave this life, the Church offers, besides the Anointing, the Eucharist as viaticum, "food for the journey": the seed of eternal life and the power of resurrection. Thus, just as Baptism, Confirmation and Eucharist form a unity, Penance, Anointing and Eucharist (as viaticum) form, at the end of life, "the sacraments that prepare for our heavenly homeland".

Words to know

Viaticum
"Food for the journey": Holy Communion given to the dying.

In the Bible

  • Jas 5:14-15: is any man sick among you?
  • Mark 6:12-13: anointed with oil many that were sick

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.65 a.1: Anointing corresponds to the healing that completes recovery: it removes the remnants of sin and strengthens the soul.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Put more heart into your hands.” (St Camillus de Lellis (to his brothers))
  • “In the Cross of Christ not only is the Redemption accomplished through suffering, but also human suffering itself has been redeemed.” (St John Paul II (Salvifici Doloris 19, 1984))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Anointing is the "last rites", only for the dying.
    What the Church teaches: It is for anyone in danger of death from illness or old age, can be repeated, and is fitting before serious surgery (§§ 1514–1515).
  • Not quite: If someone is anointed, it means the family has given up hope.
    What the Church teaches: It strengthens, brings peace and sometimes bodily healing; it is a sacrament of hope (§§ 1520–1523).

To think about

Is someone you know seriously ill? Encourage them, or their family, to ask for the Anointing.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the Christian meaning of illness, the Anointing of the Sick and Viaticum, and know when to call a priest.

  1. How can suffering bring someone closer to God, or push them away?
  2. When should you ask for the Anointing for a family member?
  3. What does "food for the journey" mean?

Activity: Visit (or write cards to) the sick or elderly of the parish. Discuss beforehand how to talk with a sick person about the sacrament.
