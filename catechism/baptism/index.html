Baptism

What does the Catholic Church teach about baptism? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1210–1284

In one sentence: Baptism makes us children of God: it takes away all sin, gives new life in the Spirit, and joins us to Christ and his Church.

What the Catechism says

The seven sacraments touch all the stages of life. Baptism, Confirmation and the Eucharist are the sacraments of Christian initiation. Baptism is the gateway: it is the bath of rebirth, prefigured by creation, the flood, the Red Sea crossing and the Jordan, and instituted by Christ.

Its essential rite is immersion in water, or pouring water on the head, while invoking the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Infants are baptised in the faith of the Church. In case of necessity anyone can baptise. Baptism is necessary for salvation for those who have heard the Gospel; God is not bound by the sacraments, however, and the Church trusts in his mercy for those who die without it. Its effects: forgiveness of all sin, new birth, membership of the Church, and an indelible character.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1210–1212 The seven sacraments touch all the stages and important moments of Christian life. Baptism, Confirmation and the Eucharist lay the foundations: they are the sacraments of Christian initiation, by which we are born anew, strengthened, and nourished with the bread of eternal life.

§ 1213–1216 Baptism is the basis of the whole Christian life, the gateway to life in the Spirit, and the door to the other sacraments. Through it we are freed from sin, reborn as children of God, become members of Christ, and are incorporated into the Church. It is called Baptism ("plunging" or "immersing"), the washing of regeneration, and enlightenment.

§ 1217–1228 Prefigurations in the Old Covenant: the waters at creation, Noah's ark, the crossing of the Red Sea, the crossing of the Jordan. All are fulfilled in Christ, who began his public life by being baptised by John, and from whose pierced side flowed blood and water, types of Baptism and the Eucharist. From Pentecost the Church has celebrated and administered Baptism: "Repent, and be baptised."

§ 1229–1245 How it is celebrated: in the early Church adult initiation (catechumenate) culminated in Baptism, Confirmation and Eucharist at the Easter Vigil; the RCIA has restored this. The rite: the sign of the cross; proclamation of the Word; exorcism and renunciation of Satan; profession of faith; blessing of the baptismal water; the essential rite, immersion three times or pouring of water three times on the head while saying "N., I baptise you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit"; anointing with chrism; the white garment; the candle lit from the Easter candle.

§ 1246–1255 Who can receive Baptism? Every person not yet baptised. Adults go through the catechumenate. Children are baptised because, born with a fallen nature, they too need the new birth; the Church baptised infants from early times. Faith is required: the faith of the Church, in which the child is baptised, and the role of parents and godparents, who must be firm believers able to help the newly baptised on the road of Christian life.

§ 1256 Who can baptise? The ordinary ministers are the bishop and priest, and in the Latin Church also the deacon. In case of necessity, anyone, even a non-baptised person, with the required intention, can baptise, using the Trinitarian formula; the intention required is to will to do what the Church does.

§ 1257–1261 The necessity of Baptism: the Lord himself affirms that Baptism is necessary for salvation for those to whom the Gospel has been proclaimed and who have had the possibility of asking for it. "God has bound salvation to the sacrament of Baptism, but he himself is not bound by his sacraments." Those who die for the faith (baptism of blood) and catechumens who die before Baptism (baptism of desire) are saved. Every person ignorant of the Gospel who seeks the truth and does God's will can be saved; they would have desired Baptism if they had known its necessity. Children who die without Baptism are entrusted to God's mercy; the Church allows us to hope for their salvation.

§ 1262–1274 The grace of Baptism: forgiveness of original sin, all personal sins and all punishment due to sin; new birth as a "new creature", an adopted child of God, a partaker of the divine nature, a member of Christ, a temple of the Spirit; sanctifying grace, the theological virtues and the gifts of the Spirit; incorporation into the Church; a sacramental bond of unity of Christians; and an indelible spiritual mark (character) consecrating the baptised for Christian worship.

Words to know

Indelible character
A permanent spiritual seal configuring us to Christ; Baptism is never repeated.
Baptism of desire
Salvation of those who, without Baptism, sincerely seek God or die desiring Baptism.

In the Bible

  • Matt 28:19: baptising them
  • Rom 6:3-4: buried together with him by baptism
  • Acts 2:38: be baptised for the remission of sins

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.66 a.3: Water is the fitting matter of Baptism because it washes, cools and is everywhere available.
  • ST III q.68 a.2: Someone who desires Baptism but dies before receiving it can be saved by that desire.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Baptism is the most beautiful and magnificent of God's gifts… We call it gift, grace, anointing, enlightenment, garment of immortality, bath of rebirth, seal.” (St Gregory of Nazianzus (Oration 40.3–4))
  • “You were asked: Do you believe in God the Father almighty? You said: I believe; and you were immersed, that is, you were buried.” (St Ambrose (On the Sacraments II.7.20, paraphrased))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Infant Baptism is meaningless because babies can't believe.
    What the Church teaches: Children are baptised in the faith of the Church, with parents and godparents pledging to raise them in it (§§ 1250–1255).
  • Not quite: Unbaptised babies who die are certainly lost.
    What the Church teaches: The Church entrusts them to God's mercy and allows us to hope for their salvation (§ 1261).
  • Not quite: Only a priest can ever baptise.
    What the Church teaches: In necessity anyone can baptise, with the right intention and the Trinitarian formula (§ 1256).

To think about

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand Baptism's prefigurations, rite, effects and necessity, and can explain infant Baptism and emergency Baptism.

  1. Which Old Testament prefiguration of Baptism helps you most?
  2. What does each symbol of the rite (water, oil, white garment, candle) mean?
  3. How would you respond to parents grieving a baby who died unbaptised?

Activity: Hold a baptismal "walk-through": with a bowl of water, oil, a white cloth and a candle, explain each step of the rite. Teach everyone how to baptise in an emergency.

Part of the courses: Becoming Catholic (OCIA / RCIA), Confirmation preparation, First Communion (for parents and catechists).
