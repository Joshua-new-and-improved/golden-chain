Born of the Virgin Mary

What does the Catholic Church teach about born of the Virgin Mary? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 484–511

In one sentence: Mary was prepared by God from her conception, conceived Jesus by the Holy Spirit, and remained a virgin: the Mother of God.

What the Catechism says

God chose Mary to be the mother of his Son. To be able to give a free assent of faith, she was enriched with grace: from the first instant of her conception she was preserved from original sin (the Immaculate Conception), by the merits of Christ. By grace she remained free of every personal sin.

Because her Son is God, Mary is truly the Mother of God (Theotokos). Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit alone, without a human father; the Church confesses Mary's perpetual virginity. Her "yes" makes her the new Eve, the mother of all the living in the order of grace.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 484–486 The Annunciation inaugurates "the fullness of time": the Son is conceived by the Holy Spirit in Mary's womb. The Father sends the Spirit to sanctify her womb and make it fruitful. The Son's anointing by the Spirit begins at his conception.

§ 487–489 What the Catholic faith believes about Mary is based on what it believes about Christ, and in turn sheds light on faith in Christ. Mary was prepared by God throughout the Old Testament, in women who carried the promise (Eve, Sarah, Hannah, Deborah, Ruth, Judith, Esther).

§ 490–493 To become the mother of the Saviour, Mary was "enriched by God with gifts appropriate to such a role": the angel greets her as "full of grace". Over the centuries the Church came to understand that she was redeemed from the moment of her conception: the Immaculate Conception, defined by Pius IX (1854), preserved her immune from all stain of original sin, by a singular grace in view of the merits of Jesus Christ. By grace she remained free of every personal sin her whole life long.

§ 494–495 At the Annunciation Mary gave the obedience of faith: "Let it be to me according to your word." She became the mother of Jesus, embracing God's saving will wholeheartedly. As St Irenaeus said, "being obedient she became the cause of salvation for herself and for the whole human race": the knot of Eve's disobedience was untied by Mary's obedience. Called "the mother of my Lord" by Elizabeth, she is truly Mother of God (Theotokos), because the one she conceived as man is the eternal Son of the Father.

§ 496–498 From the first formulations of faith the Church confessed that Jesus was conceived solely by the power of the Holy Spirit, without a human father. The Fathers saw in the virginal conception the sign that it truly was the Son of God who came in a humanity like ours. Some see it as myth; but faith in the virginal conception was met with mockery from the beginning, and is accessible only to faith that understands the connection of the mysteries.

§ 499–507 The deepening of faith in the virginal motherhood led the Church to confess Mary's real and perpetual virginity, even in the act of giving birth. The Church's liturgy celebrates her as "ever-virgin". The "brothers and sisters" of Jesus mentioned in the Gospels are close relations, according to an Old Testament usage. Mary's virginity manifests God's absolute initiative in the Incarnation; it is a sign of her faith "unadulterated by any doubt" and of her undivided gift of herself to God. Mary is both virgin and mother because she is the figure and most perfect realisation of the Church.

Words to know

Theotokos
"God-bearer": Mother of God, defined at Ephesus (431).
Immaculate Conception
Mary's preservation from original sin from her conception, by Christ's merits.

In the Bible

  • Luke 1:26-38: the Annunciation
  • Matt 1:18-25: conceived of the Holy Ghost

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.35 a.4: Mary is rightly called Mother of God, because a mother is mother of the person she bears, and the person is divine.
  • ST III q.28 a.3: Thomas defends Mary's virginity after the birth.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The knot of Eve's disobedience was untied by Mary's obedience: what the virgin Eve bound by her unbelief, the Virgin Mary loosed by her faith.” (St Irenaeus (Against Heresies III.22.4))
  • “She remained a virgin in conceiving her Son, a virgin in giving birth to him, a virgin in carrying him, a virgin in nursing him.” (St Augustine (Sermon 186))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Immaculate Conception means Jesus was conceived without a human father.
    What the Church teaches: That is the Virgin Birth; the Immaculate Conception concerns Mary's own conception, free from original sin (§ 491).
  • Not quite: Mary didn't need a Saviour.
    What the Church teaches: She was redeemed in an eminent way, in view of Christ's merits (§ 492).
  • Not quite: Jesus had biological brothers and sisters.
    What the Church teaches: The Gospels' "brothers and sisters" are close relatives, following Hebrew usage (§ 500).

For catechists

Aim: Participants can explain the Immaculate Conception, the virginal conception, Mary as Mother of God and her perpetual virginity, all in relation to Christ.

  1. Why does what we believe about Mary always depend on what we believe about Jesus (§ 487)?
  2. What does Irenaeus mean by Mary "untying the knot" of Eve?
  3. How would you answer someone who says calling Mary "Mother of God" makes her a goddess?

Activity: Read Luke 1:26–45 together. Mark each title or description given to Mary, and say what it means.
