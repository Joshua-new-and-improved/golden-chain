Chapter One in brief

What does the Catholic Church teach about chapter One in brief? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 44–49

In one sentence: The whole of Chapter One in five short points.

What the Catechism says

Chapter One comes down to five points:

  • We are religious by nature and by vocation: made for communion with God.
  • Reason can know God with certainty from his works.
  • We can name God from creatures' perfections, while he remains beyond all our words.
  • Our speech about God is limited, but it is still true.
  • Prayer is the place where this knowledge becomes relationship.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 44 The human person is by nature and vocation a religious being: coming from God and going towards God, we live a fully human life only if we freely live in our bond with God.

§ 45 We are made to live in communion with God, in whom we find happiness. (St Augustine: "When I am completely united to you, there will be no more sorrow or trials.")

§ 46 When we listen to the message of creation and to the voice of conscience, we can arrive at certainty about the existence of God, the cause and end of everything.

§ 47 The Church teaches that the one true God can be known with certainty from his works, by the natural light of reason.

§ 48 We really can name God, starting from the perfections of his creatures, which are likenesses of the infinitely perfect God, even if our limited language cannot exhaust the mystery.

§ 49 Without the Creator, the creature vanishes (Vatican II, Gaudium et Spes 36). That is why believers know that the love of Christ urges them to bring the light of the living God to those who do not know him or who reject him.

Words to know

Vocation
A call; here, the call of every person to communion with God.

In the Bible

  • Ps 41:2-3 (42): as the hart panteth after the fountains

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.12 a.12: By natural reason we know of God that he is, and that he is the cause of all things; to know more, we need grace and revelation.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “When I am completely united to you, there will be no more sorrow or trials; entirely full of you, my life will be complete.” (St Augustine (Confessions X.28))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Religion is an optional hobby for some people.
    What the Church teaches: We are religious by nature and vocation; a fully human life includes our bond with God (§ 44).
