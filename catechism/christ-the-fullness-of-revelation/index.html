Christ, the fullness of revelation

What does the Catholic Church teach about christ, the fullness of revelation? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 65–73

In one sentence: Jesus is God's final and complete Word; nothing essential is left to be revealed.

What the Catechism says

In Christ, the Father has said everything. Christ is his one, perfect and unsurpassable Word, and there will be no further public revelation before his glorious return. St John of the Cross puts it this way: having given us his Son, God has nothing more to say.

Yet revelation is not yet fully explicit. Over the centuries the Church grows in understanding it. "Private" revelations, even ones the Church has recognised, do not add to or complete the deposit of faith; at best they help people live it more fully in a given time. The faithful, guided by the Church, can discern what in them is an authentic call of Christ.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 65 "In many and various ways God spoke of old to our fathers by the prophets; but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son" (Heb 1:1–2). Christ, the Son made man, is the Father's one, perfect and unsurpassable Word. In him the Father has said everything, and there will be no other word than this one. St John of the Cross: having given us his Son, his only Word, God has spoken everything at once, and has nothing more to say.

§ 66 The Christian economy, as the new and definitive covenant, will never pass away; no new public revelation is to be expected before the glorious manifestation of our Lord. Yet even though Revelation is already complete, it has not been made completely explicit; it remains for Christian faith gradually to grasp its full significance over the course of the centuries.

§ 67 Throughout the ages there have been "private" revelations, some recognised by the Church. They do not belong to the deposit of faith: their role is not to improve or complete Christ's definitive Revelation, but to help live it more fully in a certain period of history. Guided by the Magisterium, the sense of the faithful discerns what in them is an authentic call of Christ. Christian faith cannot accept "revelations" that claim to surpass or correct the Revelation fulfilled in Christ.

§ 68–73 In Brief: by love God revealed himself and gave himself to us; he revealed himself gradually through deeds and words; beyond the witness of creation he revealed himself to our first parents, made a covenant with Noah, chose Abraham, formed Israel, and has revealed himself fully by sending his own Son, the definitive Word.

Words to know

Public revelation
What God revealed for all, completed in Christ and handed on by the apostles.
Private revelation
Messages such as Lourdes or Fatima; never binding as faith, at most helpful for living it.

In the Bible

  • Heb 1:1-2: in these days he has spoken by his Son
  • John 1:14-18: full of grace and truth

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.174 a.6 ad 3: After Christ, prophecy did not cease, but it is given not to set out new doctrine, but to direct human actions.
  • ST II-II q.1 a.7 ad 4: The fullness of revelation came with Christ and the apostles.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “In giving us his Son, his only Word, God has spoken everything to us at once in this single Word, and he has nothing more to say.” (St John of the Cross (Ascent of Mount Carmel II.22))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Catholics must believe in Fatima, Lourdes or Medjugorje.
    What the Church teaches: No private revelation belongs to the deposit of faith; even approved ones are only aids (§ 67).
  • Not quite: New religions can add to or correct Christ.
    What the Church teaches: No new public revelation will come before Christ's return; claims to surpass him cannot be accepted (§§ 66–67).
