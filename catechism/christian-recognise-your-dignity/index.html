Christian, recognise your dignity

What does the Catholic Church teach about christian, recognise your dignity? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1691–1715

In one sentence: The moral life starts not with rules but with who we are: made in God's image, redeemed by Christ, called to beatitude.

What the Catechism says

"Christian, recognise your dignity" (St Leo the Great). Part Three begins with who we are. Incorporated into Christ by Baptism, Christians are called to live a life worthy of the Gospel, a life in the Spirit.

The human person is made in the image of God: endowed with a spiritual soul, intellect and free will, and directed from conception to eternal beatitude. Wounded by original sin, we remain inclined to evil and subject to error; but Christ, by his Passion, has freed us and given us new life in the Spirit.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1691–1698 "Christian, recognise your dignity" (St Leo the Great). Christians, made partakers of the divine nature, are called to lead a life "worthy of the gospel of Christ". The way of Christ "leads to life"; a contrary way "leads to destruction" (the "two ways" of the Didache). Catechesis of the "newness of life" in Christ should be: a catechesis of the Holy Spirit, of grace, of the beatitudes, of sin and forgiveness, of the human virtues, of the Christian virtues of faith, hope and charity, of the twofold commandment of charity, and an ecclesial catechesis. Its first and last point of reference is always Jesus Christ himself, "the way, the truth and the life".

§ 1699–1709 Man, the image of God: life in the Spirit fulfils the vocation of man. The dignity of the human person is rooted in creation in God's image and likeness; it is fulfilled in the vocation to divine beatitude. Endowed with a spiritual and immortal soul, intellect and free will, the human person is from conception ordered to God. Disfigured by original sin, the image is restored in Christ; the one who believes in Christ becomes a son of God, and this adoption transforms him, enabling him to follow Christ's example.

§ 1710–1715 Man is called to seek and love what is true and good. Conscience, the moral law, freedom and grace are the themes of what follows. "Christ… fully reveals man to himself and makes his supreme calling clear" (Gaudium et Spes 22). The interior life, a life of prayer, enables us to discern and choose.

In the Bible

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I-II prologue: The second part of the Summa studies the human person as God's image, the source of his own actions with free will and power over his works.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Christian, recognise your dignity, and now that you share in God's own nature, do not return to your former base condition.” (St Leo the Great (Sermon 21.3, Christmas))
  • “There are two ways, one of life and one of death, and there is a great difference between the two ways.” (Didache 1.1 (1st century))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Catholic morality is a list of rules.
    What the Church teaches: It begins with dignity and vocation: life in Christ, led by the Spirit, aiming at beatitude. The commandments serve that life (§§ 1691–1698).
  • Not quite: Being made in God's image means looking like him.
    What the Church teaches: It means having a spiritual soul with intellect and free will, made for communion with God (§§ 1703–1705).
