Confirmation

What does the Catholic Church teach about confirmation? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1285–1321

In one sentence: Confirmation completes Baptism, pouring out the Holy Spirit in fullness and strengthening us to witness to Christ.

What the Catechism says

Confirmation perfects baptismal grace. It roots us more deeply in our divine sonship, unites us more firmly to Christ, increases the gifts of the Spirit, binds us more perfectly to the Church, and gives special strength to spread and defend the faith.

Its essential rite is the anointing with chrism on the forehead, with the laying on of hands and the words "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit." In the Latin Church the ordinary minister is the bishop, which shows the link with the apostles; in the Eastern Churches the priest confirms immediately after Baptism. Like Baptism it gives a character and is received only once.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1285 Baptism, Eucharist and Confirmation together constitute the sacraments of Christian initiation. By Confirmation the baptised are more perfectly bound to the Church and enriched with a special strength of the Holy Spirit, obliged to spread and defend the faith by word and deed as true witnesses of Christ.

§ 1286–1292 In the Old Testament the prophets announced that the Spirit would rest on the Messiah. At Jesus' baptism the Spirit descended on him; Jesus promised the Spirit, and poured him out at Pentecost. From then on the apostles imparted the gift of the Spirit to the newly baptised by the laying on of hands. Very early, an anointing with perfumed oil (chrism) was added: "Christian" means "anointed". In the West, Confirmation came to be separated in time from Baptism so that the bishop could complete it; the East kept them together, with the priest confirming using chrism consecrated by the bishop.

§ 1293–1301 Signs and rite: anointing with oil is a sign of abundance and joy, cleansing, healing, and radiance. The person receives the "mark", the seal of the Spirit. The essential rite in the Latin Church is the anointing with sacred chrism on the forehead, done by the laying on of the hand, with the words "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit". The sign of peace that concludes the rite signifies and demonstrates ecclesial communion with the bishop and the faithful.

§ 1302–1305 Effects: the full outpouring of the Holy Spirit, as at Pentecost. It roots us more deeply in divine filiation ("Abba! Father!"); unites us more firmly to Christ; increases the gifts of the Spirit in us; renders our bond with the Church more perfect; and gives special strength to spread and defend the faith and never to be ashamed of the Cross. Like Baptism, it imprints a character, and is given only once.

§ 1306–1311 Every baptised person not yet confirmed can and should receive it. In the Latin Church the "age of discretion" is the reference point, but in danger of death children should be confirmed even if they have not reached it. Preparation should lead to a more intimate union with Christ and a livelier familiarity with the Spirit. The candidate should be in a state of grace (going to Penance first) and should choose a sponsor, ideally a baptismal godparent.

§ 1312–1314 The original minister is the bishop, which expresses the link with the apostles and the Church's unity. In the East the priest confirms, using chrism consecrated by the bishop. A Latin bishop may, for grave reasons, grant priests the faculty to confirm; in danger of death any priest should confirm.

Words to know

Chrism
Perfumed oil consecrated by the bishop, used in Baptism, Confirmation and Ordination.
Seal
The mark of the Spirit, showing we belong totally to Christ.

In the Bible

  • Acts 8:14-17: they laid their hands upon them
  • 2 Cor 1:21-22: he hath sealed us

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.72 a.1: Confirmation is to Baptism as growth is to birth: it gives the fullness of the Spirit for spiritual adulthood.
  • ST III q.72 a.5: It imprints a character, deputing us to profess the faith publicly.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Recall that you have received the spiritual seal, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and strength… Guard what you have received.” (St Ambrose (On the Mysteries 7.42))
  • “Having been made partakers of Christ, you are rightly called "christs", anointed ones.” (St Cyril of Jerusalem (Mystagogical Catecheses 3.1))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Confirmation is "graduation" from the Church.
    What the Church teaches: It is a beginning: the strength of the Spirit to witness for life (§ 1303).
  • Not quite: Confirmation is a sacrament where I confirm my faith.
    What the Church teaches: It is first God's action, sealing us with the Spirit; our response follows (§§ 1293–1305).
