God reveals his plan of loving goodness

What does the Catholic Church teach about god reveals his plan of loving goodness? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 50–53

In one sentence: Out of love, God chose to show us who he is and to adopt us as his children.

What the Catechism says

Reason can know God; but there is a whole order of knowledge reason cannot reach on its own: God's inner life and his plan for us. Out of sheer goodness, God freely chose to reveal himself and to make us his adopted children in his Son, through the Holy Spirit.

He reveals himself through deeds and words that are closely bound together, and he does it step by step. St Irenaeus describes God and humanity "getting used to" each other, as he prepared us for the coming of his Son.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 50 Reason can know God with certainty from his works. But there is another order of knowledge that we cannot reach by our own powers at all: divine Revelation. By a free decision God reveals himself and gives himself to us, revealing his own mystery: his plan of loving goodness, formed from all eternity in Christ.

§ 51 It pleased God, in his goodness and wisdom, to reveal himself and make known the mystery of his will: that through Christ, the Word made flesh, we have access to the Father in the Holy Spirit and come to share in the divine nature (Dei Verbum 2).

§ 52 God, who "dwells in unapproachable light", wants to communicate his own divine life to the people he freely created, to adopt them as his sons and daughters in his only Son. By revealing himself, God wishes to make us capable of responding to him, knowing him and loving him far beyond our own natural capacity.

§ 53 The divine plan of revelation is realised simultaneously "by deeds and words which are intrinsically bound up with each other". It involves a specific divine pedagogy: God communicates himself gradually, preparing us in stages to welcome the supernatural revelation that culminates in Christ. St Irenaeus speaks of God and humanity becoming accustomed to each other.

Words to know

Revelation
God's free self-communication: making himself and his plan known.
Divine pedagogy
God's gradual way of teaching humanity, stage by stage.

In the Bible

  • Eph 1:9-10: the mystery of his will
  • John 15:15: I have called you friends

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.1 a.1: It is fitting that God reveal and give himself, because goodness by its nature communicates itself (bonum est diffusivum sui).
  • ST II-II q.1 a.1: The object of faith is God himself, the First Truth, and whatever he reveals in relation to him.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The Word of God dwelt in man and became the Son of man, to accustom man to perceive God and to accustom God to dwell in man.” (St Irenaeus (Against Heresies III.20.2))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Revelation is mainly a set of rules handed down from above.
    What the Church teaches: God reveals himself and his loving plan, to make us his children (§§ 51–52).
  • Not quite: God revealed everything at once.
    What the Church teaches: He revealed himself gradually, by deeds and words, as a patient teacher (§ 53).

To think about

For catechists

Aim: Participants see revelation as God's personal self-gift, given step by step, aimed at friendship and adoption.

  1. Why would God reveal himself gradually rather than all at once?
  2. Why do deeds and words belong together in revelation (and in our own witness)?
  3. What is the difference between knowing facts about someone and being invited into their family?

Activity: Make a timeline of how you got to know a close friend: first impressions, deeper conversations, trust. Compare it with God's "pedagogy" (§ 53).
