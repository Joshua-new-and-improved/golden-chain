God the Creator, providence and evil

What does the Catholic Church teach about god the Creator, providence and evil? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 279–324

In one sentence: God freely created everything from nothing, out of love, keeps it in being, and guides it by his providence; evil does not have the last word.

What the Catechism says

Creation is the foundation of all God's plans. God created freely, by his wisdom and love, from nothing, not from himself or from pre-existing matter. The world is ordered and good, and it depends entirely on God: he upholds it in being at every moment. God's providence guides all things towards their goal, often through his creatures' free cooperation.

Why, then, is there evil? No single quick answer suffices: the whole of Christian faith is the answer. God permits physical evil in a world still "on the way", and moral evil because he respects freedom. He is in no way the cause of moral evil, yet he can bring good out of it, as he did supremely from the Cross.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 279–281 "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." Creation is the foundation of all God's saving plans, the beginning of the history of salvation that culminates in Christ. The Easter Vigil begins with the account of creation.

§ 282–289 Catechesis on creation is of major importance: it concerns the very foundations of human and Christian life, answering the basic questions: Where do we come from? Where are we going? What is our origin and end? Scientific studies of the age of the cosmos and the development of life are welcome and invite greater admiration for the Creator; but the question of origins goes beyond science. Human reason can find an answer: God's existence can be known through his works. Revelation adds certainty and depth. Genesis 1–3 occupies a unique place, expressing in solemn language the truths of creation: its origin and end in God, its order and goodness, the vocation of the human person, the drama of sin and the hope of salvation.

§ 290–292 "In the beginning God created": God alone created the universe freely, directly, without any help. The Son and the Spirit share in creation: the Father made all things through the Word, and in the Spirit, the Creator Spirit. Creation is the common work of the Holy Trinity.

§ 293–294 The world was made for the glory of God: not to increase his glory, but to show it forth and communicate it. God created out of goodness, to share his beatitude. "The glory of God is man fully alive" (Irenaeus).

§ 295–301 God created the world according to his wisdom, not by necessity or chance. He needs nothing pre-existent: he creates "out of nothing" (2 Macc 7:28). He creates an ordered and good world, and he transcends it, while being present to it more intimately than it is to itself. Creation is not abandoned: God upholds and sustains it in being at every moment; without him it would fall back into nothingness.

§ 302–308 Creation has its own goodness, but it was created "in a state of journeying" towards an ultimate perfection. Divine providence is the dispositions by which God guides creation towards that perfection. Scripture is unanimous: God's care is concrete and immediate. Jesus asks for childlike trust. God is sovereign master of his plan, but to carry it out he also uses his creatures' cooperation: he grants them the dignity of acting on their own, of being causes, and the power of freely sharing in his providence by work and prayer.

§ 309–314 If God is good and almighty, why does evil exist? No quick answer suffices; the whole of Christian faith is the answer: the goodness of creation, the drama of sin, the patient love of God, the covenants, the Incarnation, the gift of the Spirit, the Church, the sacraments, the call to blessedness. God's world is "in a state of journeying": with physical good there is also physical evil, as long as creation has not reached perfection. Angels and people, as free creatures, can go astray, and moral evil entered the world. God is in no way the cause of moral evil; he permits it, respecting freedom, and knows how to draw good from it. From the greatest moral evil, the Son's death, he brought the greatest good, our redemption. We will fully know God's ways only when we see him face to face.

Words to know

Creation ex nihilo
Creation "out of nothing": not from God's substance or from prior matter.
Providence
God's wise and loving guidance of all creatures to their end.

In the Bible

  • Gen 1:1-31: in the beginning
  • 2 Macc 7:28: God made them out of nothing
  • Rom 8:28: all things work together unto good

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.45 a.1: Creation is the bringing of all being into existence from nothing.
  • ST I q.2 a.3 ad 1: God allows evil only because he is so good and powerful that he can bring good even out of evil (from Augustine).

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Almighty God, because he is supremely good, would never allow any evil in his works unless he were so powerful and good as to bring good even out of evil.” (St Augustine (Enchiridion 11))
  • “Everything comes from love; all is ordained for the salvation of man; God does nothing without this goal in mind.” (St Catherine of Siena (Dialogue 138))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Church rejects science, especially evolution and the Big Bang.
    What the Church teaches: Scientific discoveries about the age and development of the cosmos invite greater admiration for the Creator (§ 283); the question of ultimate origins goes beyond science (§ 284).
  • Not quite: God made the world and then left it to run on its own (deism).
    What the Church teaches: God upholds everything in being at every moment and guides it by providence (§§ 301–303).
  • Not quite: God causes evil.
    What the Church teaches: He permits it, respects freedom, and can bring good out of it (§§ 311–312).

To think about

Think of a past evil that God brought some good out of. Thank him for it.

For catechists

Aim: Participants grasp creation from nothing, providence, and the Christian response to the problem of evil.

  1. What is the difference between "how" the universe developed (science) and "why" there is anything at all (faith)?
  2. Where have you seen providence in your life, perhaps only in hindsight?
  3. How would you comfort, rather than only argue with, someone who has lost faith because of suffering?

Activity: Read Genesis 50:20 (Joseph) and Romans 8:28. Ask each person to share, if willing, a time God brought good out of something painful.
