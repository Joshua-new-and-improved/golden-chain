Handing on the faith: catechesis

What does the Catholic Church teach about handing on the faith: catechesis? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 4–10

In one sentence: Catechesis is the Church helping people become, and grow as, disciples of Jesus.

What the Catechism says

Catechesis is the Church's whole effort to make disciples: helping people believe that Jesus is the Son of God, so that by believing they have life in his name, and building them up in that life. It is linked with, but distinct from, the first proclamation of the Gospel, worship, witness and service.

The Catechism notes that the Church's great periods of renewal have always been periods of catechesis, with saints and Fathers who were also teachers. It places itself in the line of the Second Vatican Council and of the Synod of Bishops (1985) that asked for a catechism of the whole faith.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 4 Catechesis is the whole of the Church's efforts to make disciples: to help people believe that Jesus is the Son of God, so that believing they may have life in his name, and to educate and build them up in that life.

§ 5 It is an education in the faith of children, young people and adults, especially in the teaching of Christian doctrine, given in an organic and systematic way, to initiate people into the fullness of Christian life.

§ 6 Catechesis is linked with, but distinct from, other elements of the Church's pastoral mission: the first proclamation of the Gospel, the search for reasons for belief, the experience of Christian living, celebration of the sacraments, integration into the Church community, and apostolic and missionary witness.

§ 7–8 Catechesis is bound up with the whole life of the Church: her growth depends on it. Periods of renewal in the Church have also been intense periods of catechesis; the great bishops and Fathers (Cyril of Jerusalem, Augustine, John Chrysostom, Ambrose and others) were great catechists.

§ 9–10 The Council of Trent led to the Roman Catechism (1566); Vatican II inspired a renewal of catechesis; and the 1985 Synod asked for a catechism of the whole Catholic doctrine, which John Paul II published in 1992.

Words to know

Initiation
Being brought into the Christian life through instruction, the sacraments and the community.
Organic
Taught as a living whole, each truth connected to the others, not as a list.

In the Bible

  • Luke 1:1-4: so that you may know the certainty
  • 1 Pet 3:15: give a reason for your hope

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.188 a.6: The Dominican ideal: contemplari et contemplata aliis tradere, to contemplate and to hand on to others what has been contemplated. Teaching flows from prayer.
  • ST II-II q.2 a.6: Not everyone is bound to know every truth explicitly, but teachers must know more, because they have to instruct others.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “His catechetical lectures in the 4th century prepared adults for Baptism at Easter, and are still read today.” (St Cyril of Jerusalem (Catecheses))
  • “Augustine advised catechists above all to teach with joy (hilaritas), because God loves a cheerful giver.” (St Augustine (On Catechising the Uninstructed))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Catechesis is just classes for children before First Communion or Confirmation.
    What the Church teaches: It is lifelong: for children, young people and adults, aimed at the fullness of Christian life (§ 5).
  • Not quite: Catechesis is only information.
    What the Church teaches: It aims at faith and life in Christ, not only knowledge (§ 4).

To think about

Who handed the faith on to you? What did they do that made it believable?

For catechists

Aim: Participants see catechesis as making disciples, not only passing on facts, and see themselves in the long line of those who hand on the faith.

  1. What is the difference between knowing about Jesus and being his disciple?
  2. Why do you think renewal in the Church has always come with renewed catechesis (§ 8)?
  3. Which elements of § 6 have shaped your own faith most?

Activity: Draw a "faith family tree": the people who handed the faith to you, and those you might hand it on to.
