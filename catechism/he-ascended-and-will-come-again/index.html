He ascended and will come again

What does the Catholic Church teach about he ascended and will come again? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 659–682

In one sentence: Christ ascended into heaven, intercedes for us, and will come in glory to judge the living and the dead.

What the Catechism says

After forty days, the risen Christ entered heaven with his humanity: where the Head has gone, the members hope to follow. Seated at the Father's right hand, he reigns and intercedes for us as our high priest, and pours out the Holy Spirit.

His Kingdom is already present in the Church, but not yet fulfilled. Before his return, the Church will pass through a final trial. On the last day he will come in glory to judge the living and the dead; then each person's relation to God and neighbour will be laid bare. Christ is the Judge because he is the Redeemer: those who reject his grace judge themselves.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 659–664 "So then the Lord Jesus, after he had spoken to them, was taken up into heaven, and sat down at the right hand of God." Christ's body was glorified from the moment of his Resurrection; but for forty days he ate and drank familiarly with his disciples. His final appearance ends with the irreversible entry of his humanity into divine glory, symbolised by the cloud and by heaven. Left to its own natural powers, humanity does not have access to the Father's house; only Christ can open it, so that we, his members, may confidently hope to go where he, our Head, has preceded us. As high priest of the New Covenant he intercedes constantly for us. Being seated at the Father's right hand means the inauguration of the Messiah's kingdom.

§ 668–672 Christ already reigns through the Church; the Kingdom is mysteriously present in her. But it is not yet fulfilled "with power and great glory" by the King's return to earth. It is still under attack by evil powers. Until then the Church on earth suffers the birth pangs of the new creation; that is why Christians pray, above all in the Eucharist, "Marana tha", "Come, Lord Jesus!"

§ 673–677 Since the Ascension, Christ's coming in glory has been imminent, though "it is not for you to know the times or seasons". His glorious coming will be accomplished after the recognition of the Messiah by "all Israel". Before it, the Church must pass through a final trial that will shake many believers' faith: a religious deception offering an apparent solution to problems at the price of apostasy from the truth, the "Antichrist". The Church will enter the glory of the Kingdom only through this final Passover, following her Lord in his death and Resurrection.

§ 678–682 Following the prophets and John the Baptist, Jesus announced the judgement of the Last Day. Then the conduct of each one and the secrets of hearts will be brought to light; attitude to one's neighbour will reveal acceptance or refusal of grace and divine love: "As you did it to one of the least of these my brethren, you did it to me." Christ is Lord of eternal life; full right to judge definitively belongs to him as Redeemer of the world. But the Son did not come to judge, but to save; by rejecting grace in this life, one already judges oneself.

Words to know

Parousia
Christ's glorious return at the end of time.

In the Bible

  • Acts 1:9-11: this Jesus shall so come
  • Heb 7:25: always living to make intercession
  • Matt 25:31-46: the last judgement

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.57 a.6: Christ's Ascension causes our salvation: it raises our hope, prepares our place, and he intercedes for us.
  • ST III q.59 a.2: Judgement belongs to Christ also as man, because of his humanity's union with God.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Christ is now exalted above the heavens, but he still suffers on earth all the pains that we, his members, endure.” (St Augustine (Sermon on the Ascension, 98))
  • “In the evening of life, we will be judged on love alone.” (St John of the Cross (Dichos 64))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Ascension means Jesus left and is now absent.
    What the Church teaches: He reigns, intercedes for us, and is present in the Church, the sacraments and the Spirit (§§ 662–669).
  • Not quite: Christians should calculate the date of the end of the world.
    What the Church teaches: "It is not for you to know the times or seasons" (§ 673).

To think about

If Christ came today, what would you want him to find you doing?
