He descended to the dead and rose again

What does the Catholic Church teach about he descended to the dead and rose again? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 631–658

In one sentence: Christ went down to the realm of the dead to free the just, and rose bodily on the third day: the crowning truth of our faith.

What the Catechism says

"He descended into hell" means that Jesus truly died and went to the realm of the dead (Sheol, Hades) to open heaven's gates for the just who had gone before him. He did not go to free the damned.

The Resurrection is a real event, historically attested (the empty tomb, the appearances), yet it also transcends history. The risen body is the same body that was crucified, but now glorious, no longer bound by space and time. The Resurrection confirms all Christ did and taught, fulfils the promises, shows his divinity, and is the source of our own justification and future resurrection.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 631–635 "He descended into hell": the apostolic preaching affirms that Jesus, like all people, experienced death and went to the realm of the dead. But he went there as Saviour, proclaiming the Good News to the spirits imprisoned. "Hell" (Sheol, Hades) here means the abode of the dead, deprived of the vision of God, where the just awaited their Redeemer. Jesus did not descend to deliver the damned, nor to destroy the hell of damnation, but to free the just who had gone before him. The descent is the last phase of his messianic mission: the spread of his redemptive work to all people of all times and places.

§ 638–644 "We bring you the good news that what God promised to the fathers, this he has fulfilled to us their children by raising Jesus." The Resurrection of Jesus is the crowning truth of our faith. It is a real event, with historically verifiable manifestations: the empty tomb, the first sign; and the appearances: to Mary Magdalene and the holy women, the first messengers; to Peter; to the Twelve; and to more than five hundred at once. The disciples' faith was not invented: they were discouraged and frightened, and did not believe at first. Their faith was born of their experience of the risen Lord.

§ 645–647 By touching and eating with them, Jesus showed that he was not a ghost: his risen body is the same body that was tortured and crucified, bearing the marks of his Passion. Yet this authentic, real body possesses the new properties of a glorious body, not limited by space and time. Christ's Resurrection was not a return to earthly life like that of Lazarus; it is an essentially different reality. No one witnessed the Resurrection itself; it remains at the very heart of the mystery of faith, as something that transcends history.

§ 648–650 The Resurrection is the work of the Holy Trinity: the Father raised Christ; the Son rose by his own power; the Spirit gave life to his body. The Fathers contemplate it from the divine Person of Christ, who remained united to both his soul and body even when separated by death.

§ 651–655 The Resurrection confirms all that Christ did and taught; fulfils the promises of the Old Testament and of Jesus himself; confirms his divinity ("When you have lifted up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he"); and is the principle of our justification: it opens the way to new life and makes us brothers and sisters of Christ. Finally, it is the source of our own future resurrection: "Christ has been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep."

Words to know

Hades / Sheol
The realm of the dead, where the just awaited the Redeemer.
Glorified body
The risen body: real and the same, but transformed by the Spirit.

In the Bible

  • 1 Pet 3:18-19: he preached to those spirits in prison
  • 1 Cor 15:3-8, 14: if Christ be not risen
  • Luke 24:36-43: handle and see

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.52 a.5: Christ's descent freed the holy fathers from the "limbo" of the just.
  • ST III q.54 a.1: Christ rose with a true body, the same body.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Something strange is happening: there is a great silence on earth… The King is asleep. God has died in the flesh and hell trembles with fear… "Awake, O sleeper, and rise from the dead."” (Ancient homily for Holy Saturday (Office of Readings))
  • “We are an Easter people, and Alleluia is our song.” (St John Paul II (Adelaide, 1986), echoing St Augustine)

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Resurrection is a symbol for the disciples' renewed hope.
    What the Church teaches: It is a real event with historically attested signs, though it also transcends history (§§ 639, 647).
  • Not quite: Jesus came back to the same earthly life, like Lazarus.
    What the Church teaches: His risen body is real but glorious, an essentially different reality (§ 646).
  • Not quite: The descent into hell means Jesus suffered among the damned.
    What the Church teaches: He went to the realm of the dead as Saviour, to free the just (§§ 632–633).

To think about

"If Christ be not risen, your faith is vain" (1 Cor 15:17). What difference does the Resurrection make to how you face death?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the descent to the dead and the Resurrection as real, transformative events, and can present the evidence and its meaning.

  1. Why is the empty tomb not enough on its own?
  2. What do the disciples' initial doubts tell us about the Resurrection's credibility?
  3. What difference does the Resurrection make to how you face death and suffering?

Activity: Read 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 and 12–20. Make two lists: the evidence Paul gives, and what follows if Christ is not raised.
