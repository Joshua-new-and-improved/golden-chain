He died, crucified, and was buried

What does the Catholic Church teach about he died, crucified, and was buried? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 595–630

In one sentence: Jesus freely offered his life on the Cross as the one perfect sacrifice that reconciles us with God.

What the Catechism says

Jesus' death was not a chance event but part of God's plan, foretold in the Scriptures: he "died for our sins". God took the initiative of redeeming love. Jesus freely embraced the Father's will, and at the Last Supper he anticipated his free offering of his life, giving it to the apostles in the Eucharist.

His death is the unique and definitive sacrifice. By his obedience unto death he made satisfaction for our sins, replacing our disobedience with love. He calls us to take up our cross and follow him. He was truly buried, and remained united to his divine Person even in the tomb.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 595–598 Among the religious authorities some did not oppose Jesus (Nicodemus, Joseph of Arimathea). The historical complexity of Jesus' trial is shown in the Gospels: personal sin of those involved is known to God alone. Jesus' death cannot be attributed to all the Jews then living, nor to Jews today; the Church rejects this. All sinners were the authors of Christ's Passion; Christians bear the graver responsibility, because "we profess to know him".

§ 599–605 Jesus' violent death was not the result of chance; it belongs to the mystery of God's plan, as Peter explained at Pentecost: "delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God." This does not mean those who handed him over were passive actors in a scenario: God permitted their acts, which flowed from their blindness. Christ died "for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures": the suffering Servant (Isaiah 53). God took the initiative of universal redeeming love: "he loved us and sent his Son to be the expiation for our sins." Christ's love excludes no one: there is not, never has been and never will be a single human being for whom Christ did not suffer.

§ 606–612 The Son of God came to do the Father's will; his whole life was a sacrifice for the sins of the world. Jesus embraced the Father's love freely: "No one takes my life from me, but I lay it down of my own accord." At the Last Supper he expressed the free offering of himself: "This is my body which is given for you." He made the Last Supper the memorial of his voluntary offering, and instituted his apostles as priests of the New Covenant. In the agony at Gethsemane, his human will accepted the Father's will: "not as I will, but as you will."

§ 613–618 Christ's death is the Paschal sacrifice that accomplishes the definitive redemption of humanity, through "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world", and the sacrifice of the New Covenant, which restores communion between God and humanity. It is unique, completing and surpassing all other sacrifices. By his obedience unto death, Jesus substituted himself for us, making satisfaction for our sins: it is love "to the end" that gives his sacrifice its value. The Council of Trent: "by his most holy Passion on the wood of the cross he merited justification for us." The cross is the unique sacrifice of Christ, but he calls his disciples to take up their cross and follow him; Mary was associated most intimately with it.

§ 624–630 "By the grace of God he tasted death for everyone." God's saving plan included that his Son not only die but "taste death": experience the separation of his soul and body during the time between his death and Resurrection. Christ's body remained united to his divine Person during the time in the tomb; it did not see corruption. Baptism, by immersion, signifies the descent into the tomb by which the Christian dies to sin with Christ.

Words to know

Redemption
Christ's buying us back from sin and death by his death and Resurrection.
Satisfaction
Making amends for sin through an offering of love greater than the offence.

In the Bible

  • Isa 53:4-12: he was wounded for our iniquities
  • John 10:17-18: I lay down my life
  • 1 Pet 2:24: by whose stripes you were healed

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.48 a.2: Christ's Passion was a superabundant satisfaction, because of the greatness of his love, the dignity of his life and the extent of his suffering.
  • ST III q.46 a.3: The Cross was the most fitting way to save us: it shows God's love and calls forth ours.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Apart from the cross there is no other ladder by which we may get to heaven.” (St Rose of Lima)
  • “No example of virtue is absent from the Cross: if you seek charity, patience, humility, obedience, or contempt for earthly things, you will find them there.” (St Thomas Aquinas (Collations on the Creed, art. 4))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: God the Father was angry and took it out on his innocent Son.
    What the Church teaches: God took the initiative of redeeming love; Jesus freely embraced the Father's will out of love (§§ 604, 609).
  • Not quite: Christ died only for some people.
    What the Church teaches: There is no one for whom Christ did not suffer (§ 605).
  • Not quite: Each Mass repeats Christ's sacrifice.
    What the Church teaches: His sacrifice is unique and once for all; the Mass makes it present (§§ 614, 1366).

To think about

Look at a crucifix for two minutes. What does it say to you about love?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand Christ's death as God's loving plan, freely accepted, a unique sacrifice for all, and see their call to share it.

  1. Why is it important that Jesus freely laid down his life?
  2. How does the Last Supper interpret the Cross?
  3. What does it mean for you to "take up your cross" this week?

Activity: Pray the Stations of the Cross together, pausing at the 11th station (Jesus nailed to the cross) to read John 10:17–18.
