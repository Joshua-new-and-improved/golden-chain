Heaven and earth: angels and the visible world

What does the Catholic Church teach about heaven and earth: angels and the visible world? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 325–354

In one sentence: God made all things visible and invisible: the angels, and a world whose every creature has its own goodness.

What the Catechism says

The existence of angels, spiritual non-bodily beings, is a truth of faith. They are personal and immortal creatures, servants and messengers of God, who surround Christ and accompany the Church. From infancy to death human life is surrounded by their care; each believer has an angel as protector and shepherd.

In the visible world each creature has its own goodness and reflects something of God's wisdom; they are interdependent and ordered in a hierarchy, with the human person as the summit. Respect for creation flows from this. The Sabbath shows that creation is for worship.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 325–327 The Creed professes God as creator of heaven and earth, "of all that is, seen and unseen". "Heaven and earth" means all that exists; "heaven" can also mean the firmament, God's own "place", and the place of the spiritual creatures, the angels. The Fourth Lateran Council (1215) taught that God from the beginning made both orders of creatures, the spiritual and the corporeal.

§ 328–330 The existence of spiritual, non-bodily beings, called angels, is a truth of faith. St Augustine: "Angel" is the name of their office, not their nature; by nature they are spirits, by office messengers. With their whole beings they are servants and messengers of God. As purely spiritual creatures, they have intelligence and will: they are personal and immortal, surpassing in perfection all visible creatures.

§ 331–333 Christ is the centre of the angelic world: they are his angels, created through and for him. They have been present since creation and throughout the history of salvation, announcing it from afar or near and serving its accomplishment: closing the gates of paradise, protecting Lot, saving Hagar, staying Abraham's hand, leading God's people, announcing births and callings. Gabriel announced the births of John and of Jesus. Angels surround the whole life of the Incarnate Word.

§ 334–336 The whole life of the Church benefits from the angels' help. In the liturgy the Church joins them in adoring God (the "Holy, holy, holy"). From infancy to death human life is surrounded by their watchful care and intercession. Beside each believer stands an angel as protector and shepherd, leading him to life (St Basil).

§ 337–344 God himself created the visible world in all its richness, diversity and order. Scripture presents it symbolically as six days of divine work concluded by the Sabbath rest. Each creature has its own goodness and perfection, reflecting a ray of God's infinite wisdom; so we must respect the particular goodness of every creature. God wills the interdependence of creatures: no creature is self-sufficient. The beauty of the universe reflects the Creator's beauty. There is a hierarchy of creatures, with the human person as the summit of the Creator's work; and there is solidarity among all creatures, since all have the same Creator.

§ 345–349 The Sabbath, the end of the work of the six days, shows that creation is ordered to worship: "Nothing is to be preferred to the work of God" (St Benedict). For us a new day has dawned: the day of Christ's Resurrection, the eighth day, on which the first creation finds its meaning and fulfilment in the new creation.

Words to know

Angel
A purely spiritual, personal and immortal creature; the name means "messenger".

In the Bible

  • Ps 90:11-12 (91): he hath given his angels charge over thee
  • Matt 18:10: their angels in heaven

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.50 a.1: The perfection of the universe required intellectual creatures without bodies.
  • ST I q.113 a.2: Each human being has a guardian angel.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “"Angel" is the name of their office, not of their nature. If you seek the name of their nature, it is "spirit"; if you seek the name of their office, it is "angel".” (St Augustine (Enarrationes in Psalmos 103))
  • “Beside each believer stands an angel as protector and shepherd, leading him to life.” (St Basil (Against Eunomius III.1))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Angels are dead people who "get their wings".
    What the Church teaches: Angels are a distinct kind of creature, purely spiritual; the saints remain human (§ 330).
  • Not quite: Animals and nature have no value except their usefulness to us.
    What the Church teaches: Each creature has its own goodness; we must respect creation (§ 339).

To think about

Pray the Angel of God prayer today, and thank your guardian angel.

For catechists

  2. What does the interdependence of creatures (§ 340) say about how we treat the environment?
  3. How does the Sabbath show what creation is for?
