Holy Orders

What does the Catholic Church teach about holy Orders? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1533–1600

In one sentence: Through Holy Orders, Christ's mission to the apostles continues in bishops, priests and deacons, who serve the Church in his name.

What the Catechism says

Holy Orders and Matrimony are directed to the salvation of others. Holy Orders is the sacrament of apostolic ministry, in three degrees: bishop (the fullness of the sacrament), priest and deacon. The ministerial priesthood is essentially different from the common priesthood of the faithful, and it is at its service.

The essential rite is the bishop's laying on of hands with the prayer of consecration. Only a validly ordained bishop can ordain. Only a baptised man validly receives the sacrament; the Church considers itself bound by Christ's choice of the Twelve. In the Latin Church priests are normally chosen from men who will remain celibate. Orders confers an indelible character: the ordained acts in persona Christi.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1533–1538 Baptism, Confirmation and the Eucharist are the sacraments of initiation. Two further sacraments, Holy Orders and Matrimony, are directed towards the salvation of others; they confer a particular mission in the Church and serve to build up the People of God. Holy Orders is the sacrament through which the mission entrusted by Christ to his apostles continues to be exercised in the Church until the end of time: the sacrament of apostolic ministry. It includes three degrees: episcopate, presbyterate and diaconate. "Order" in Roman antiquity meant an established civil body; ordination means integration into an order.

§ 1539–1553 In the Old Covenant the chosen people was constituted a "kingdom of priests", and the tribe of Levi was set apart for liturgical service. These prefigure the priesthood of the New Covenant. Everything the priesthood of the Old Covenant prefigured finds its fulfilment in Christ Jesus, the "one mediator between God and men". There are two participations in Christ's one priesthood: the common priesthood of all the faithful (through Baptism and Confirmation), and the ministerial or hierarchical priesthood of bishops and priests. They differ essentially, not only in degree; the ministerial priesthood is at the service of the common priesthood. In the ordained minister it is Christ himself who is present to his Church as Head, acting in persona Christi Capitis. This does not mean the minister is preserved from all weakness, sin or error, but Christ's action is guaranteed in the sacraments.

§ 1554–1571 The three degrees: Episcopal ordination confers the fullness of the sacrament: the high priesthood, the summit of the sacred ministry. Bishops are successors of the apostles, in a college with the Pope; each bishop has, as vicar of Christ, the pastoral office of the particular Church entrusted to him. Priests are co-workers of the bishops, consecrated to preach the Gospel, shepherd the faithful and celebrate divine worship; they exercise their ministry above all in the Eucharist, in a presbyterium around their bishop. Deacons are ordained "not unto the priesthood, but unto the ministry": to assist the bishop and priests, especially in liturgy, preaching and charity. Since Vatican II the Latin Church has restored the permanent diaconate, which married men can receive.

§ 1572–1580 The essential rite of the sacrament of Holy Orders for all three degrees consists in the bishop's imposition of hands on the head of the ordinand and the specific consecratory prayer. Only validly ordained bishops, in apostolic succession, can validly confer the three degrees. Only a baptised man validly receives sacred ordination: the Lord Jesus chose men to form the college of the Twelve, and the apostles did the same; the Church recognises herself bound by this choice. No one has a right to receive the sacrament; it is a call from God. In the Latin Church, priests are normally chosen from among men of faith who live a celibate life and intend to remain celibate "for the sake of the kingdom of heaven"; in the Eastern Churches married men can be ordained priests and deacons, but bishops are chosen from celibates.

§ 1581–1589 Effects: the sacrament configures the recipient to Christ by a special grace of the Holy Spirit, so that he may serve as Christ's instrument. It confers an indelible spiritual character and cannot be repeated or conferred temporarily. The grace of the Spirit proper to it is configuration to Christ as Priest, Teacher and Pastor. St John Vianney: "The priest continues the work of redemption on earth… If we really understood the priest on earth, we would die not of fright but of love."

Words to know

In persona Christi
"In the person of Christ": the priest acts as Christ the head in the sacraments.
Apostolic succession
The unbroken line of bishops from the apostles.

In the Bible

  • Heb 5:1-6: every high priest taken from among men
  • 2 Tim 1:6: the grace which is in thee by the imposition of my hands

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.63 a.3: The character of Orders configures the priest to Christ's priesthood.
  • ST Supplement q.34 a.1: Orders exists so that some may hand on the sacraments to others.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The priesthood is the love of the heart of Jesus.” (St John Vianney (Curé of Ars))
  • “Let no one do anything concerning the Church without the bishop.” (St Ignatius of Antioch (Letter to the Smyrnaeans 8))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The priest has more dignity than the laity.
    What the Church teaches: All have equal dignity; the ministerial priesthood is at the service of the common priesthood (§§ 872, 1547).
  • Not quite: If a priest is sinful, his Masses and absolutions are invalid.
    What the Church teaches: Christ's action is guaranteed in the sacraments even if the minister is unworthy (§ 1550).
  • Not quite: Deacons are just priests-in-training.
    What the Church teaches: The permanent diaconate is a distinct degree of Orders, open to married men (§ 1571).

To think about

Pray today for your parish priest, by name, and for vocations from your community.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the one priesthood of Christ shared in two ways, the three degrees of Orders, and the meaning of in persona Christi.

  1. What is the difference between the common priesthood and the ministerial priesthood?
  2. What do bishops, priests and deacons each do in your diocese?
  3. How can we support our priests and encourage vocations?

Activity: Invite a priest or deacon to speak briefly about his vocation and his day, or write thank-you cards to your clergy.
