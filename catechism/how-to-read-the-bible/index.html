How to read the Bible

How to read the Bible? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 109–119

In one sentence: Read Scripture as one whole, within the living Tradition and the analogy of faith, attentive to both its literal and its spiritual senses.

What the Catechism says

To understand Scripture we must look for what the human authors really meant and what God wanted to reveal through their words. That means attending to literary forms, times and cultures. And because the same Spirit inspired it, it must be read in the Spirit in which it was written, by three criteria: the content and unity of the whole of Scripture; the living Tradition of the whole Church; and the analogy of faith (how the truths of faith hang together).

Tradition distinguishes the literal sense (what the words mean, the basis of the rest) from the spiritual senses: allegorical (how events point to Christ), moral (how we should act) and anagogical (how they lead us to eternity).

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 109–110 In Scripture God speaks to us in a human way. To interpret it correctly, the reader must be attentive to what the human authors truly wanted to affirm and to what God wanted to reveal through their words. To discover the authors' intention, one must take into account the conditions of their time and culture, the literary genres in use, and the modes of feeling, speaking and narrating then current.

§ 111 But since Scripture is inspired, there is another and no less important principle: it must be read and interpreted in the light of the same Spirit by whom it was written. Vatican II gives three criteria.

§ 112–114 1. Be attentive to the content and unity of the whole Scripture: however different its books, it is a unity by reason of God's plan, of which Christ is the centre. 2. Read Scripture within the living Tradition of the whole Church: Scripture is written principally in the Church's heart rather than on parchment. 3. Be attentive to the analogy of faith: the coherence of the truths of faith among themselves and within the whole plan of Revelation.

§ 115–118 According to an ancient tradition, there are two senses of Scripture: the literal and the spiritual, the latter subdivided into the allegorical, moral and anagogical senses. The literal sense is the meaning conveyed by the words, discovered by sound exegesis; all other senses are based on it. The allegorical sense sees events in relation to Christ (the Red Sea as a sign of Baptism); the moral sense leads us to act justly; the anagogical sense views realities in terms of their eternal significance (the Church on earth as a sign of the heavenly Jerusalem). A medieval couplet sums it up: the letter speaks of deeds; allegory, what to believe; the moral, what to do; anagogy, where we are heading.

§ 119 It is the task of exegetes to work towards a better understanding of Scripture, so that the Church's judgement may mature; but all that concerns the interpretation of Scripture is ultimately subject to the judgement of the Church.

Words to know

Literal sense
What the words mean as intended by the author; all other senses rest on it.
Analogy of faith
The coherence of the truths of faith among themselves and within God's plan.

In the Bible

  • Luke 24:25-27: beginning at Moses and all the prophets
  • 1 Cor 10:1-11: these things were done in a figure

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.1 a.10: Thomas's classic account of the four senses: literal, allegorical, moral and anagogical, with the literal as foundation.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The letter teaches what happened; allegory what to believe; the moral sense what to do; anagogy where to go.” (Augustine of Dacia (13th century))
  • “All the senses are founded on the literal; nothing necessary to faith is contained in the spiritual sense that is not taught clearly elsewhere in the literal sense.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST I q.1 a.10))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Catholics can't read the Bible for themselves.
    What the Church teaches: The Church strongly encourages personal reading, interpreted within the Church's living faith (§§ 113, 133).
  • Not quite: The spiritual sense can ignore what the text actually says.
    What the Church teaches: All other senses are based on the literal sense (§ 116).

To think about

Pick one Gospel scene. What is its literal meaning? What does it say to you morally? How does it point to heaven?

For catechists

Aim: Participants learn the three criteria and the four senses, and practise them on a passage.

  1. Why does knowing the literary genre (poetry, history, parable) matter?
  2. Why is it dangerous to read one verse on its own?
  3. What changes when you read Scripture as part of the Church rather than alone?

Activity: Read Exodus 14:21–31 (the Red Sea). Find its literal, allegorical (Baptism), moral (trust in God) and anagogical (passing to the promised land of heaven) senses together.
