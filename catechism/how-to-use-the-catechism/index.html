How to use the Catechism

How to use the Catechism? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 18–22

In one sentence: Read it as a whole, follow the numbers in the margin, and use the "In Brief" summaries.

What the Catechism says

The Catechism is meant to be read as a whole. Numbers in the margin point to other paragraphs on the same theme, so you can follow a single truth across the book; an index of themes helps too.

Each section ends with an "In Brief" summary, written so it can be remembered. Small print gives historical background, explanations and quotations from the Fathers, saints and liturgy. The footnotes show the sources: Scripture first, then councils, popes and saints.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 18 The Catechism is conceived as an organic presentation of the whole faith; it should be read as a unity. Numbers in the margin (cross-references) refer to other paragraphs on the same theme, and an analytical index lets one follow each theme across the whole book.

§ 19 Scripture texts are often not quoted word for word but referenced ("cf."); for deeper understanding the texts themselves should be consulted.

§ 20 Passages in small print give historical or apologetic background, or supplementary explanations.

§ 21 Small-print quotations from patristic, liturgical, magisterial or hagiographical sources enrich the presentation; they have often been chosen for direct catechetical use.

§ 22 At the end of each thematic unit, "In Brief" texts summarise the essentials in condensed formulas, meant to be easy to memorise.

Words to know

Cross-reference
A marginal number that sends you to a related paragraph.
In Brief
Short summary paragraphs at the end of each section.

In the Bible

  • Acts 8:30-31: how can I understand unless someone guides me?

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.1 a.8: Sacred doctrine argues from authorities, above all Scripture, but uses reason to clarify and defend them: the same order as the Catechism's footnotes.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Hold fast to what has been believed everywhere, always and by all.” (St Vincent of Lérins (Commonitorium, 434))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: You can understand a doctrine from one paragraph.
    What the Church teaches: The Catechism is meant to be read as a whole, following its cross-references (§ 18).
  • Not quite: The small print is unimportant.
    What the Church teaches: It contains rich quotations from the Fathers, saints and liturgy, often chosen for catechesis (§ 21).
