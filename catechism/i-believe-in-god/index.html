I believe in God

What does the Catholic Church teach about i believe in God? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 198–231

In one sentence: There is only one God, who revealed his name, I AM, and who is Truth and Love.

What the Catechism says

The whole Creed rests on its first line. God revealed himself to Israel as the only God: "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God is one Lord." To Moses he revealed his name, I AM WHO AM: the one who simply is, who is always faithful, and who is near. Out of reverence Israel did not pronounce it.

God is Truth: his words cannot deceive. God is Love: his love for Israel is like a father's, a mother's, a bridegroom's. Believing in one God changes everything: we recognise his greatness, live in thanksgiving, see the dignity of every person, use created things rightly, and trust him in every circumstance.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 198–202 Our profession begins with God, the First and the Last, the beginning and end of everything. "I believe in God" is the most fundamental affirmation. God revealed himself to Israel as the only One: "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God is one Lord." Jesus himself confirms that God is the one Lord. To confess that Jesus is Lord does not divide this oneness.

§ 203–209 God revealed himself to Israel by making his name known. To Moses, from the bush that burns without being consumed, he says "I AM WHO AM". This name is both a revelation and a kind of refusal: God is mysterious, he is not one we can master. Yet he is the God who is near, faithful from age to age, and ready to save. Out of reverence Israel did not pronounce the name YHWH, replacing it with "Lord" (Adonai, in Greek Kyrios), the title later given to Jesus.

§ 210–213 After Israel's sin with the golden calf, God reveals himself as "merciful and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness". The name "I Am" expresses God's faithfulness: despite our unfaithfulness he keeps his steadfast love. Over the centuries Israel's faith deepened its understanding: God alone IS, without origin and without end; all creatures receive their existence from him.

§ 214–217 God, HE WHO IS, revealed himself as abounding in steadfast love (hesed) and faithfulness (emet). God is Truth: his words cannot deceive, so we can abandon ourselves to him. His truth is his wisdom, which orders the whole creation. When he reveals himself, the teaching from his mouth is "true instruction"; sending his Son, he came to bear witness to the truth.

§ 218–221 Israel discovered that God had only one reason for choosing them: his gratuitous love. His love is compared to a father's love for his son, stronger than a mother's for her children, and a bridegroom's for his bride. It is everlasting. St John goes further: "God is love." God's very being is love, an eternal exchange of love among Father, Son and Spirit, which he destines us to share.

§ 222–231 Believing in one God has consequences for our whole life: knowing his greatness and majesty; living in thanksgiving; knowing the unity and true dignity of all people; making good use of created things; and trusting God in every circumstance, even adversity. St Teresa's prayer sums it up: "Let nothing trouble you… God alone suffices."

Words to know

YHWH
The divine name revealed to Moses: "I AM WHO AM" (Exod 3:14).

In the Bible

  • Deut 6:4-5: Hear, O Israel
  • Exod 3:13-15: I AM WHO AM
  • 1 John 4:8: God is charity

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.13 a.11: "He who is" is the most proper name of God, because God's essence is his very existence.
  • ST I q.11 a.3: God is one, because he is infinite and simple.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Let nothing trouble you, let nothing frighten you. All things pass; God does not change. Patience obtains all. Whoever has God lacks nothing. God alone suffices.” (St Teresa of Avila (her bookmark))
  • “You were more inward to me than my most inward part, and higher than my highest.” (St Augustine (Confessions III.6))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Christians believe in three gods.
    What the Church teaches: We confess one God; belief in the Trinity does not divide God's oneness (§§ 202, 253).
  • Not quite: The God of the Old Testament is harsh and the God of the New is loving.
    What the Church teaches: Already in Exodus God reveals himself as merciful, gracious and abounding in steadfast love (§ 210).

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the divine name, God as Truth and Love, and the practical consequences of believing in one God.

  1. What does it say about God that his name is "I AM"?
  2. Why can we trust what God says (§ 215)?
  3. Which consequence of believing in one God (§§ 223–227) most needs to change your week?

Activity: Pray St Teresa of Avila's bookmark prayer ("Let nothing trouble you…") together; ask each person to name what "troubles" them and offer it to God.
