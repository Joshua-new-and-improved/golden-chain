I believe in the Holy Spirit

What does the Catholic Church teach about i believe in the Holy Spirit? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 683–747

In one sentence: The Holy Spirit, the Lord and giver of life, makes Christ present and builds up the Church.

What the Catechism says

No one can say "Jesus is Lord" except in the Holy Spirit. The Spirit is the first to awaken faith in us, though he is the last revealed. The Son and the Spirit are sent together: the Spirit reveals the Son, and the Son glorifies the Father.

The Spirit has many names (Paraclete, Spirit of truth) and symbols: water, anointing, fire, cloud and light, the seal, the hand, the finger, the dove. He spoke through the prophets, overshadowed Mary, and anointed Jesus. At Pentecost he was poured out on the Church, and now he builds, animates and sanctifies her, distributing gifts and charisms for the good of all.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 683–686 "No one can say 'Jesus is Lord' except by the Holy Spirit." To be in touch with Christ, we must first be touched by the Spirit. He goes before us and awakens faith in us. Through Baptism he gives us new birth in the Father's Son. The Spirit is the first to be revealed in action in our lives, though the last Person of the Trinity to be fully revealed; he is at work with the Father and the Son from the beginning to the completion of God's plan.

§ 687–690 "No one comprehends the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God." The Spirit who reveals God makes known to us Christ, his Word, but does not speak of himself. We know him only in the movement by which he reveals the Word to us and disposes us to welcome him. The Church is the place where we know the Spirit: in Scripture, Tradition, the Magisterium, the sacraments, prayer, charisms and ministries, the apostolic life and the witness of the saints. The mission of the Son and the Spirit is joint and inseparable: whenever the Father sends his Word, he always sends his Breath.

§ 691–701 "Holy Spirit" is the proper name of the third Person. Jesus also calls him the Paraclete ("he who is called to one's side", advocate, consoler) and the Spirit of truth. St Paul speaks of the Spirit of the promise, of adoption, of Christ, of the Lord, of God. The symbols: water (new birth in Baptism); anointing (the Christ, the "anointed"; chrism); fire (transforming energy, Elijah, Pentecost); cloud and light (Sinai, the Transfiguration); the seal (the indelible character); the hand (laying on of hands); the finger ("by the finger of God I cast out demons"); the dove (at the Flood and at Jesus' baptism).

§ 702–716 From the beginning until the fullness of time, the Spirit prepared for the Messiah. He was at work in creation ("the Spirit of God was moving over the waters"); in the promise to Abraham; in the theophanies and the Law; in the kingdom and the exile; and in the prophets, who announced a Messiah on whom the Spirit would rest (Isaiah 11 and 61) and a new heart and new spirit for God's people (Ezekiel 36).

§ 717–730 In the fullness of time, John the Baptist is "filled with the Holy Spirit" from his mother's womb: Elijah who is to come. Mary is the masterwork of the Spirit's mission in the fullness of time: prepared by him, made fruitful by him, the burning bush of definitive theophany, the new Eve. Christ Jesus is anointed with the Spirit; he reveals the Spirit gradually, and on the day of his Resurrection breathes on the apostles: "Receive the Holy Spirit."

§ 731–741 On the day of Pentecost Christ's Passover is fulfilled in the outpouring of the Spirit, manifested, given and communicated as a divine Person. The Spirit is God's gift of love, the first gift, which contains all others; the love "poured into our hearts". By his power the children of God bear fruit: "love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control". The Spirit builds, animates and sanctifies the Church, and makes her the sacrament of communion; he helps us in our weakness, interceding with sighs too deep for words.

Words to know

Paraclete
"One called to the side": advocate, helper, consoler.
Charism
A special grace of the Spirit given to build up the Church.

In the Bible

  • 1 Cor 12:3: no man can say the Lord Jesus but by the Holy Ghost
  • John 16:13-14: the Spirit of truth
  • Acts 2:1-4: Pentecost

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.37 a.1: "Love" is a proper name of the Holy Spirit: he proceeds as the love of the Father and the Son.
  • ST I q.38 a.2: "Gift" is likewise a proper name of the Spirit.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Through the Holy Spirit we are restored to paradise, led back to the Kingdom of heaven, and adopted as children.” (St Basil the Great (On the Holy Spirit 15.36))
  • “The true aim of the Christian life is the acquisition of the Holy Spirit of God.” (St Seraphim of Sarov (conversation with Motovilov))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Holy Spirit is a force or energy, not a Person.
    What the Church teaches: He is a divine Person, the third Person of the Trinity, given at Pentecost as a Person (§§ 689, 731).
  • Not quite: The Spirit only appears at Pentecost.
    What the Church teaches: He was at work from creation, through the prophets, and in Christ's whole life (§§ 702–730).

To think about

Which symbol of the Spirit (water, fire, wind, dove, seal) speaks to you most? Why?

For catechists

Aim: Participants know who the Holy Spirit is, his names and symbols, and how he acts in the Church and in each Christian.

  1. Why do you think the Spirit is the "hidden" Person of the Trinity?
  2. Which symbol of the Spirit speaks to you most?
  3. Where have you seen the fruit of the Spirit (Gal 5:22–23) in someone's life?

Activity: Set out seven symbols (water, oil, a candle, a cloud picture, a seal or stamp, an open hand, a paper dove). In groups, match each to a Bible passage and say what it teaches about the Spirit.
