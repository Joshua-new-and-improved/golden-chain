Prologue

What the Catechism is and how to use it · §§ 1–25

  • The life of man: to know and love God §§ 1–3 · God made you to share his own happiness, and he never stops calling you to it.
  • Handing on the faith: catechesis §§ 4–10 · Catechesis is the Church helping people become, and grow as, disciples of Jesus.
  • What the Catechism is for, and who it is for §§ 11–12 · The Catechism is the Church's reference summary of the whole faith: for bishops first, then for everyone.
  • The four pillars §§ 13–17 · The faith is believed (the Creed), celebrated (the sacraments), lived (the commandments) and prayed (the Our Father).
  • How to use the Catechism §§ 18–22 · Read it as a whole, follow the numbers in the margin, and use the "In Brief" summaries.
  • Adapting it, and the love behind it §§ 23–25 · Local teaching adapts the Catechism to each culture, and everything in it exists for love.

Part One: The Profession of Faith

What we believe: revelation, faith and the Creed · §§ 26–1065

  • The desire for God §§ 26–30 · We are made for God, so every human heart is, in some way, searching for him.
  • Ways of coming to know God §§ 31–35 · Both the world and the human person point to God, with real and reasonable certainty.
  • What the Church teaches about knowing God §§ 36–38 · Reason can know God, but we need revelation to know him easily, surely and without error.
  • How can we speak about God? §§ 39–43 · Our words about God are true, but God is always greater than anything they can say.
  • Chapter One in brief §§ 44–49 · The whole of Chapter One in five short points.
  • God reveals his plan of loving goodness §§ 50–53 · Out of love, God chose to show us who he is and to adopt us as his children.
  • The stages of revelation §§ 54–64 · God revealed himself step by step: to Adam and Eve, Noah, Abraham, Moses and the prophets.
  • Christ, the fullness of revelation §§ 65–73 · Jesus is God's final and complete Word; nothing essential is left to be revealed.
  • Tradition and Scripture §§ 74–83 · God's revelation reaches us through Scripture and Tradition, which flow from one source and are handed on together.
  • Who interprets the faith? §§ 84–100 · The whole Church holds the faith, but the bishops in communion with the Pope are its authentic interpreters, as servants of the Word.
  • Scripture: God's word in human words §§ 101–108 · God is the author of Scripture, and he wrote it through human authors who were true authors too; so it teaches without error the truth God wanted put in writing for our salvation.
  • How to read the Bible §§ 109–119 · Read Scripture as one whole, within the living Tradition and the analogy of faith, attentive to both its literal and its spiritual senses.
  • The canon, and the two Testaments §§ 120–141 · The Church discerned the canon: 46 books of the Old Testament and 27 of the New; both Testaments are God's word, and they explain each other.
  • The obedience of faith §§ 142–152 · Faith is our personal yes to God who reveals himself: trusting him with mind and will, as Abraham and Mary did.
  • What faith is like §§ 153–165 · Faith is a gift of grace and a truly human act: certain, seeking understanding, free, necessary for salvation, and a foretaste of heaven.
  • We believe: the faith of the Church §§ 166–184 · Faith is personal but never private: I believe because the Church believes, and I receive the faith from her as from a mother.
  • The creeds §§ 185–197 · The creeds are short summaries of the faith; the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds are the two most important.
  • I believe in God §§ 198–231 · There is only one God, who revealed his name, I AM, and who is Truth and Love.
  • The Father, and the Holy Trinity §§ 232–267 · The central mystery of the faith: one God in three Persons, Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
  • The Almighty §§ 268–278 · God's power is universal, loving and mysterious; he shows it most of all by forgiving.
  • God the Creator, providence and evil §§ 279–324 · God freely created everything from nothing, out of love, keeps it in being, and guides it by his providence; evil does not have the last word.
  • Heaven and earth: angels and the visible world §§ 325–354 · God made all things visible and invisible: the angels, and a world whose every creature has its own goodness.
  • The human person §§ 355–384 · Made in God's image, each human being is a unity of body and soul, male or female, called to friendship with God.
  • The Fall and original sin §§ 385–421 · Our first parents' sin wounded human nature; we inherit that wound, but Christ, the new Adam, is greater.
  • Jesus, Christ, Son of God, Lord §§ 422–455 · Each title says who Jesus is: Saviour, the Anointed One, God's only Son, and Lord.
  • The Word became flesh §§ 456–483 · The eternal Son became truly man without ceasing to be truly God: one Person in two natures.
  • Born of the Virgin Mary §§ 484–511 · Mary was prepared by God from her conception, conceived Jesus by the Holy Spirit, and remained a virgin: the Mother of God.
  • The mysteries of Christ's life §§ 512–570 · Everything in Jesus' life, from the manger to the entry into Jerusalem, reveals the Father and is for us.
  • Jesus and Israel §§ 571–594 · Jesus fulfilled the Law, honoured the Temple and revealed himself as God who forgives sins: the deepest reason for the conflict.
  • He died, crucified, and was buried §§ 595–630 · Jesus freely offered his life on the Cross as the one perfect sacrifice that reconciles us with God.
  • He descended to the dead and rose again §§ 631–658 · Christ went down to the realm of the dead to free the just, and rose bodily on the third day: the crowning truth of our faith.
  • He ascended and will come again §§ 659–682 · Christ ascended into heaven, intercedes for us, and will come in glory to judge the living and the dead.
  • I believe in the Holy Spirit §§ 683–747 · The Holy Spirit, the Lord and giver of life, makes Christ present and builds up the Church.
  • The Church in God's plan §§ 748–780 · The Church is God's plan from the beginning: prefigured, prepared in Israel, founded by Christ, revealed by the Spirit, and fulfilled in glory.
  • People of God, Body of Christ, Temple of the Spirit §§ 781–810 · The Church is God's people, Christ's body with him as head, and the Spirit's temple.
  • One, holy, catholic and apostolic §§ 811–870 · These four marks are gifts Christ gives the Church and tasks she must live out.
  • Hierarchy, laity and consecrated life §§ 871–945 · All the baptised share Christ's mission: the ordained serve as shepherds, the laity sanctify the world, and the consecrated witness to the Kingdom.
  • The communion of saints §§ 946–962 · The Church is a communion of holy things and holy people, in heaven, in purgatory and on earth.
  • Mary, Mother of the Church §§ 963–975 · Mary is the Church's model and mother in the order of grace; she was taken body and soul into glory.
  • The forgiveness of sins §§ 976–987 · Christ gave the Church power to forgive sins, first in Baptism and then in the sacrament of Penance.
  • The resurrection of the body §§ 988–1019 · Our bodies will rise again, transformed, as Christ's did; so we must honour the body and prepare for a holy death.
  • Judgement, heaven, purgatory and hell §§ 1020–1037 · At death each person is judged; heaven is perfect communion with God, purgatory a final purification, and hell self-exclusion from God.
  • The Last Judgement, the new creation, and Amen §§ 1038–1065 · At the end, Christ will judge all, and God will make a new heaven and a new earth; the Creed ends with Amen, "so be it".

Part Two: The Celebration of the Christian Mystery

How faith is celebrated: the liturgy and the seven sacraments · §§ 1066–1690

  • The liturgy: work of the Holy Trinity §§ 1066–1112 · In the liturgy the Father blesses us, Christ acts through signs, and the Holy Spirit makes it all present: it is the work of God and of his people.
  • What the sacraments are §§ 1113–1134 · The sacraments are effective signs of grace, instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church, by which divine life is given to us.
  • Celebrating: who, how, when, where §§ 1135–1209 · The whole Christ, head and members, celebrates, through signs, words and music, in the rhythm of the Church's year, in churches that are houses of prayer.
  • Baptism §§ 1210–1284 · Baptism makes us children of God: it takes away all sin, gives new life in the Spirit, and joins us to Christ and his Church.
  • Confirmation §§ 1285–1321 · Confirmation completes Baptism, pouring out the Holy Spirit in fullness and strengthening us to witness to Christ.
  • The Eucharist: source and summit §§ 1322–1344 · The Eucharist is the source and summit of the Christian life, instituted at the Last Supper and celebrated by the Church from the beginning.
  • The Mass: sacrifice, presence, communion §§ 1345–1419 · The Mass makes present Christ's one sacrifice; he is truly, really and substantially present; and in communion he unites us to himself and to each other.
  • Penance and Reconciliation §§ 1420–1470 · Christ gave the Church a sacrament to forgive sins after Baptism: contrition, confession and satisfaction, with the priest's absolution.
  • Indulgences, and how Confession is celebrated §§ 1471–1498 · An indulgence remits temporal punishment due to forgiven sin, drawn from the Church's treasury of grace.
  • Anointing of the Sick §§ 1499–1532 · Christ comes close to the seriously ill and the elderly in this sacrament, giving strength, peace, union with his Passion, and forgiveness.
  • Holy Orders §§ 1533–1600 · Through Holy Orders, Christ's mission to the apostles continues in bishops, priests and deacons, who serve the Church in his name.
  • Marriage §§ 1601–1666 · Marriage is a lifelong covenant between a man and a woman, ordered to their good and to children; between the baptised it is a sacrament.
  • Sacramentals and Christian funerals §§ 1667–1690 · Sacramentals (blessings, holy water, rosaries) prepare us for grace; Christian funerals commend the dead to God.

Part Three: Life in Christ

How we live: dignity, virtue, grace and the Ten Commandments · §§ 1691–2557

  • Christian, recognise your dignity §§ 1691–1715 · The moral life starts not with rules but with who we are: made in God's image, redeemed by Christ, called to beatitude.
  • The Beatitudes and our desire for happiness §§ 1716–1729 · God put a desire for happiness in every heart, and only he can satisfy it; the Beatitudes describe that happiness and the way to it.
  • Freedom §§ 1730–1748 · Freedom is the power to act deliberately; it reaches perfection when directed to God, and the more we do good, the freer we become.
  • The morality of human acts §§ 1749–1761 · An act is good when its object, intention and circumstances are all good; some acts are always wrong, whatever the intention.
  • The passions (emotions) §§ 1762–1775 · Emotions are part of being human; in themselves neither good nor bad, they become morally good when reason and will direct them to the good.
  • Conscience §§ 1776–1802 · Conscience is reason's judgement about the goodness of an act; it must be formed, followed, and corrected when it errs.
  • The virtues §§ 1803–1845 · Virtues are firm habits of doing good: four cardinal (human) virtues, three theological virtues, and the Spirit's gifts and fruits.
  • Sin §§ 1846–1876 · Sin is an offence against God and a failure in love; mortal sin destroys charity, venial sin wounds it, and God's mercy is greater than both.
  • The person and society §§ 1877–1896 · We are made for communion; society is needed, but the person is its origin and purpose, and conversion of heart comes before the reform of structures.
  • Authority, the common good and participation §§ 1897–1927 · Legitimate authority comes from God and serves the common good; every citizen is called to participate responsibly.
  • Social justice and solidarity §§ 1928–1948 · Social justice flows from the dignity of every person; differences are a call to share, and solidarity is a Christian virtue.
  • The moral law: natural, Old and New §§ 1949–1986 · God's law is a fatherly instruction: the natural law written in our hearts, the Law of Moses, and the New Law of grace.
  • Grace and justification §§ 1987–2029 · God justifies us by grace, freely, through faith and Baptism; our merit is itself his gift, and all are called to holiness.
  • The Church, mother and teacher; the precepts §§ 2030–2051 · The Church guides our moral life by her teaching, and the five precepts set the minimum of prayer and moral effort.
  • The Ten Commandments §§ 2052–2082 · The commandments are God's word to his people, a path of life, summed up by Jesus in love of God and neighbour.
  • The First Commandment §§ 2083–2141 · Worship God alone: by faith, hope and charity, adoration and prayer; avoid idolatry, superstition and irreligion.
  • The Second Commandment §§ 2142–2167 · Honour God's holy name: never use it to blaspheme, swear falsely, or curse.
  • The Third Commandment §§ 2168–2195 · Keep the Lord's Day holy: Christians celebrate Sunday, the day of the Resurrection, with Mass, rest and charity.
  • The Fourth Commandment §§ 2196–2257 · Honour your father and mother: the family is the original cell of society; it also covers our duties to authorities and theirs to us.
  • The Fifth Commandment: respect for life §§ 2258–2283 · Human life is sacred from conception to natural death; the deliberate killing of an innocent person is always gravely wrong.
  • The Fifth Commandment: dignity and peace §§ 2284–2330 · Respect for persons includes not leading others into sin, caring for health, respecting the body, and working for peace.
  • The Sixth Commandment: sexuality and chastity §§ 2331–2359 · Sexuality is a good gift, part of the whole person; chastity integrates it in love, and everyone is called to it in their state of life.
  • Marriage: fidelity and fruitfulness §§ 2360–2400 · The love of husband and wife is faithful and fruitful; adultery, divorce, contraception and cohabitation offend against it.
  • The Seventh Commandment §§ 2401–2463 · You shall not steal: respect others' goods, the universal destination of goods, just work and wages, care for creation and love for the poor.
  • The Eighth Commandment §§ 2464–2513 · Live in the truth: no lies, false witness, detraction or calumny; respect others' reputation, and use the media responsibly.
  • The Ninth Commandment §§ 2514–2533 · Purity of heart: guard your desires, thoughts and gaze, because the pure in heart shall see God.
  • The Tenth Commandment §§ 2534–2557 · Do not covet: avoid greed and envy, seek poverty of heart, and desire God above all.

Part Four: Christian Prayer

How we pray, taught through the Our Father · §§ 2558–2865

  • What prayer is, and how God taught it §§ 2558–2622 · Prayer is the raising of the heart to God: a gift, a covenant and a communion, revealed from Abraham and the Psalms to Jesus and Mary.
  • The forms of prayer §§ 2623–2649 · Blessing and adoration, petition, intercession, thanksgiving and praise: the five basic forms of Christian prayer.
  • Where prayer comes from, and who teaches it §§ 2650–2696 · We learn to pray from the Word, the liturgy and the virtues; we pray to the Father, through Jesus, in the Spirit, with Mary; and the family is the first school.
  • Vocal prayer, meditation and contemplation §§ 2697–2724 · Three expressions of prayer: vocal prayer with words, meditation that seeks with the mind, and contemplation, a gaze of faith fixed on Jesus.
  • The battle of prayer §§ 2725–2758 · Prayer is a battle against ourselves and the tempter: distractions, dryness and discouragement are overcome by humility, trust and perseverance.
  • The Our Father: "Our Father, who art in heaven" §§ 2759–2802 · The Lord's Prayer is the summary of the whole Gospel; we dare to call God "Abba, Father", together, and "heaven" means his majesty and our homeland.
  • The seven petitions, and Amen §§ 2803–2865 · Three petitions for God's glory, four for our needs: hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come, thy will be done; our daily bread, forgiveness, protection in temptation, deliverance from evil.
