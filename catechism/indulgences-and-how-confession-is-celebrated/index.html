Indulgences, and how Confession is celebrated

What does the Catholic Church teach about indulgences, and how Confession is celebrated? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1471–1498

In one sentence: An indulgence remits temporal punishment due to forgiven sin, drawn from the Church's treasury of grace.

What the Catechism says

Sin has a double consequence: grave sin deprives us of communion with God (eternal punishment), and every sin involves an unhealthy attachment to creatures needing purification (temporal punishment). Forgiveness removes the eternal punishment; temporal punishment remains to be purified, in this life or in purgatory.

An indulgence is a remission of temporal punishment for sins already forgiven, which the Church grants from the treasury of the merits of Christ and the saints. It can be partial or plenary, and can be applied to ourselves or the dead. Confession is ordinarily celebrated individually, or in a communal celebration with individual confession; general absolution is only for grave necessity.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1471–1473 An indulgence is "a remission before God of the temporal punishment due to sins whose guilt has already been forgiven, which the faithful Christian who is duly disposed gains under certain prescribed conditions through the action of the Church". Sin has a double consequence: grave sin deprives us of communion with God (eternal punishment); every sin, even venial, entails an unhealthy attachment to creatures, which must be purified here or after death (temporal punishment). These are not a vengeance inflicted from outside, but follow from the very nature of sin. Forgiveness of sin and restoration of communion entail remission of eternal punishment, but temporal punishment remains. The Christian should strive to accept this as a grace, through patient suffering, prayer and works of mercy, "putting off the old man".

§ 1474–1477 In the communion of saints: the Christian is not alone. The life of each of God's children is joined in Christ with the lives of all. The "treasury of the Church" is the infinite value of Christ's merits before God, together with the prayers and good works of Mary and all the saints, who attained their own salvation and cooperated in the salvation of their brothers in the unity of the Mystical Body.

§ 1478–1479 Through indulgences the Church, by the power of binding and loosing granted by Christ, intervenes for individual Christians and opens to them the treasury of merits. She does this not only to aid them, but to spur them to works of devotion, penance and charity. Since the faithful departed being purified are also members of the same communion of saints, we can help them by obtaining indulgences for them.

§ 1480–1484 The celebration of Penance: like all sacraments it is a liturgical action, with a greeting and blessing, the reading of God's word, an exhortation to repentance, confession, the imposing and acceptance of a penance, the priest's absolution, a prayer of thanksgiving and dismissal. The absolution formula: "God, the Father of mercies… I absolve you from your sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." Individual, integral confession and absolution remain the only ordinary way to be reconciled with God and the Church; general absolution is permitted only in cases of grave necessity, with the obligation to confess grave sins individually afterwards.

Words to know

Temporal punishment
The purification still needed after forgiveness, because of the unhealthy attachment that sin leaves.
Plenary indulgence
Remission of all temporal punishment; requires Confession, Communion, prayer for the Pope's intentions and freedom from attachment to sin.

In the Bible

  • 2 Macc 12:43-46: that they may be loosed from sins
  • Col 1:24: I fill up those things that are wanting

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • Supplement q.25 a.1 (from Thomas's Sentences commentary): Indulgences have real value, because of the unity of the Mystical Body and the superabundant merits of Christ and the saints.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The aim of indulgences is not only to help the faithful expiate the punishment of sin, but also to urge them to perform works of devotion, penance and charity.” (Pope St Paul VI (Indulgentiarum Doctrina 8, 1967, paraphrased))
  • “Indulgences have real value because of the unity of the Mystical Body, in which many have done more works of satisfaction than their own debts required.” (Supplement to the Summa, compiled from St Thomas's Sentences commentary (q.25 a.1))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Indulgences are a way to buy forgiveness of sins.
    What the Church teaches: They remit temporal punishment for sins already forgiven, and cannot be bought; selling them was an abuse the Church condemned (§ 1471).
  • Not quite: Temporal punishment means God is vindictive.
    What the Church teaches: It follows from the nature of sin, as purification from unhealthy attachments (§ 1472).
