Jesus and Israel

What does the Catholic Church teach about jesus and Israel? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 571–594

In one sentence: Jesus fulfilled the Law, honoured the Temple and revealed himself as God who forgives sins: the deepest reason for the conflict.

What the Catechism says

The Paschal mystery is at the centre of the Good News. Some of Israel's leaders opposed Jesus because of his attitude to the Law, the Temple and faith in one God. Jesus did not abolish the Law but fulfilled it perfectly, and he loved the Temple as his Father's house, even foretelling its destruction and identifying himself with it.

Above all, by forgiving sins he acted as God, which seemed blasphemous to those who did not believe. The Church does not hold the Jewish people collectively responsible for Jesus' death. All sinners were the authors of his Passion, and Christians bear the heavier responsibility.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 571–573 The Paschal mystery of Christ's cross and Resurrection is at the centre of the Good News. The Gospels give much space to his Passion, and faith tries to understand the circumstances of his death, faithfully handed on by the Gospels and illuminated by other historical sources.

§ 574–576 From the beginning of Jesus' public ministry, certain Pharisees and partisans of Herod, together with priests and scribes, agreed together to destroy him, accusing him of expelling demons by Beelzebul, forgiving sins, healing on the Sabbath and associating with sinners. In the eyes of many in Israel Jesus seemed to act against essential institutions: obedience to the Law, the centrality of the Temple, and faith in the one God.

§ 577–582 Jesus and the Law: he did not come to abolish the Law but to fulfil it perfectly, which only he, the divine Legislator, could do. The Law's perfect fulfilment would require someone born under the Law to keep it perfectly, which Jesus did. He gave the Law its definitive interpretation as one with divine authority ("But I say to you"), and he disavowed certain human traditions of the Pharisees that were "making void the word of God".

§ 583–586 Jesus and the Temple: he venerated the Temple, went up there for the feasts, and was zealous for it as his Father's house. He foretold its destruction, which some took as hostility, and presented himself as the definitive dwelling place of God among men: his body is the true Temple.

§ 587–591 Jesus and Israel's faith in the one God: the greatest scandal was his claim to act as God, especially by forgiving sins. His identification of himself with God was a real challenge: either he was blaspheming, or he was who he claimed to be. Faced with this, some leaders could only see blasphemy; others believed. Jesus asked for faith in his works; but the Sanhedrin judged him deserving of death for blasphemy, acting out of "ignorance" and the "hardness" of unbelief.

Words to know

Paschal mystery
Christ's Passion, death, Resurrection and Ascension: our "Passover".

In the Bible

  • Matt 5:17-19: not to destroy but to fulfil
  • Mark 2:5-12: who can forgive sins but God?

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.47 a.5: Thomas distinguishes degrees of guilt in Christ's death: the leaders acted with more knowledge, the crowd more in ignorance.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “We must regard as guilty all those who continue to relapse into sin. Our crimes made our Lord Jesus Christ suffer the torment of the cross.” (Roman Catechism I.5.11 (quoted in CCC 598))
  • “Nor did demons crucify him; it is you who have crucified him and crucify him still, when you delight in your vices and sins.” (St Francis of Assisi (Admonitions 5))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Jesus rejected the Jewish Law and the Temple.
    What the Church teaches: He fulfilled the Law, venerated the Temple, and revealed himself as its fulfilment (§§ 577, 583–586).
  • Not quite: "The Jews" killed Jesus.
    What the Church teaches: The Church rejects collective blame; all sinners are the authors of Christ's Passion (§§ 597–598).

To think about

Why might it matter that the Church says our sins, not "the Jews", crucified Christ?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand why Jesus came into conflict with some leaders, and reject antisemitic misreadings of the Passion.

  1. Why was Jesus' forgiving sins so shocking (Mark 2:7)?
  2. What does it mean that Jesus is the new Temple?
  3. Why does the Church insist that our sins, not "the Jews", crucified Christ?

Activity: Read Mark 2:1–12. Role-play the scribes' reaction, then discuss: what are the only two options if Jesus really forgives sins?
