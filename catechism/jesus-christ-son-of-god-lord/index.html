Jesus, Christ, Son of God, Lord

What does the Catholic Church teach about jesus, Christ, Son of God, Lord? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 422–455

In one sentence: Each title says who Jesus is: Saviour, the Anointed One, God's only Son, and Lord.

What the Catechism says

The heart of catechesis is a Person: Jesus of Nazareth, the eternal Son of God made man. His name means "God saves". He is the Christ (the Messiah, "anointed one"), who fulfils the Messianic hope of Israel in his triple office of priest, prophet and king.

He is the only Son of God, not by adoption but by nature, as Peter confessed. And he is Lord: the title used in the Greek Old Testament for God's own name. To call Jesus "Lord" is to confess his divinity; from the beginning Christians prayed to him, and his lordship extends over the world and history.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 422–429 "When the time had fully come, God sent forth his Son." This is the Gospel: the good news of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. At the heart of catechesis we find a Person: Jesus of Nazareth, the only Son from the Father, who suffered and died for us and now, after rising, is living with us forever. To catechise is to reveal in Christ the whole of God's plan, and to lead people into communion with him. Whoever is called to teach Christ must first seek the surpassing worth of knowing him.

§ 430–435 Jesus means in Hebrew "God saves". The name expresses both his identity and his mission: since God alone can forgive sins, it is God who, in Jesus his eternal Son made man, will save his people. In Jesus, God recapitulates all of his history of salvation. The name of Jesus is at the heart of Christian prayer; liturgical prayers conclude "through our Lord Jesus Christ".

§ 436–440 Christ comes from the Greek for the Hebrew Messiah, "anointed". In Israel those consecrated to God for a mission were anointed: kings, priests and sometimes prophets. Jesus fulfilled the divine mission of priest, prophet and king; the Spirit anointed him at his conception and baptism. Jesus accepted the title Messiah, but with reserve, because many understood it politically; he revealed its true meaning in the Son of Man who came to give his life as a ransom.

§ 441–445 In the Old Testament "son of God" was a title given to angels, the chosen people, and kings. But when Peter confessed Jesus as "the Christ, the Son of the living God", Jesus said this came from the Father: Peter recognised the transcendent character of his divine sonship. Jesus himself calls himself "the only Son of God", and the Father declares at the Baptism and Transfiguration: "This is my beloved Son." After the Resurrection his divine sonship is fully manifest.

§ 446–451 In the Greek Old Testament, the divine name YHWH is rendered Kyrios, "Lord". The New Testament uses "Lord" both for the Father and for Jesus, recognising him as God. Jesus attributes the title to himself; the disciples address him with it; and the first Christians confess that "Jesus is Lord", ascribing to him the power, honour and glory due to God. The lordship of Christ means that no earthly power can claim the absolute submission due to God alone. Christian prayer is marked by the title "Lord": "Marana tha", "Come, Lord!"

Words to know

Messiah / Christ
"Anointed one": the one consecrated by God as priest, prophet and king.
Kyrios
"Lord": the Greek word for God's name, applied to Jesus.

In the Bible

  • Matt 1:21: thou shalt call his name Jesus
  • Matt 16:16: thou art Christ, the Son of the living God
  • Phil 2:9-11: Jesus Christ is Lord

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.22 a.1: Christ is supremely a priest, because he unites God and humanity and offers himself.
  • ST III q.37 a.2: The name Jesus was fittingly given because it expresses the purpose of the Incarnation: salvation.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Jesus is honey in the mouth, music in the ear, a song of joy in the heart.” (St Bernard of Clairvaux (Sermon 15 on the Song of Songs))
  • “At the name of Jesus every knee should bow… and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.” (St Paul (Phil 2:10–11))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: "Christ" is Jesus' surname.
    What the Church teaches: It is a title: the Anointed One, the Messiah (§ 436).
  • Not quite: "Son of God" just means a very holy man.
    What the Church teaches: Jesus is the only Son of God in a unique, divine sense (§§ 441–445).

To think about

Which title of Jesus do you use most in prayer? Try praying with a different one this week.

For catechists

Aim: Participants can explain each title of Jesus and what it reveals about him.

  1. What does each title (Jesus, Christ, Son of God, Lord) add to our picture of him?
  2. Why did Jesus avoid the title Messiah at first?
  3. What would it mean for you to call Jesus "Lord" in practice?
