Judgement, heaven, purgatory and hell

What does the Catholic Church teach about judgement, heaven, purgatory and hell? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1020–1037

In one sentence: At death each person is judged; heaven is perfect communion with God, purgatory a final purification, and hell self-exclusion from God.

What the Catechism says

At death each person receives an eternal recompense in a particular judgement: entrance into heaven (directly or through purification) or eternal damnation.

Heaven is the ultimate end and fulfilment of our deepest longings: to see God face to face, with all the blessed. Purgatory is the final purification of those who die in God's grace but still imperfectly purified; the Church prays for them, especially in the Mass. Hell is the state of definitive self-exclusion from communion with God, chosen by dying in mortal sin without repenting. God predestines no one to hell; the warnings about it are a call to responsibility and conversion.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1020 The Christian who unites his own death to that of Jesus views it as a step towards him and an entrance into everlasting life. The Church speaks the words of commendation: "Go forth, Christian soul, from this world… May you return to your Creator."

§ 1021–1022 Death puts an end to life as the time open to accepting or rejecting divine grace. Each person receives eternal retribution in his immortal soul at the very moment of death, in a particular judgement that refers his life to Christ: either entrance into heaven, through a purification or immediately, or immediate and everlasting damnation. St John of the Cross: "At the evening of life, we shall be judged on our love."

§ 1023–1029 Those who die in God's grace and friendship and are perfectly purified live for ever with Christ; they see God "face to face". This perfect life with the Holy Trinity, this communion of life and love with the Trinity, the Virgin Mary, the angels and all the blessed is called heaven: the ultimate end and fulfilment of the deepest human longings, the state of supreme, definitive happiness. Because of his transcendence, God can be seen as he is only when he opens his mystery to our contemplation: the "beatific vision". Scripture speaks of it in images: life, light, peace, a wedding feast, wine of the Kingdom, the Father's house, the heavenly Jerusalem, paradise. "No eye has seen, nor ear heard… what God has prepared for those who love him."

§ 1030–1032 All who die in God's grace and friendship, but still imperfectly purified, are indeed assured of their eternal salvation; but after death they undergo purification, to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven. The Church calls this final purification of the elect purgatory; it is entirely different from the punishment of the damned. The Church formulated it especially at Florence and Trent, referring to Scripture (2 Macc 12:46; 1 Cor 3:15). From the beginning the Church has honoured the dead and offered prayers for them, above all the Eucharistic sacrifice, and also almsgiving, indulgences and works of penance.

§ 1033–1037 We cannot be united with God unless we freely choose to love him. To die in mortal sin without repenting and accepting God's merciful love means remaining separated from him for ever by our own free choice. This state of definitive self-exclusion from communion with God and the blessed is called hell. Jesus often speaks of it. Immediately after death the souls of those who die in a state of mortal sin descend into hell, where they suffer its punishments, "eternal fire". Its chief punishment is eternal separation from God, in whom alone we can have the life and happiness for which we were created. The affirmations about hell are a call to responsibility and to urgent conversion. God predestines no one to go to hell; for this, a wilful turning away from God (a mortal sin) is necessary, and persistence in it until the end. The Church prays that no one should be lost.

Words to know

Particular judgement
The judgement of each soul immediately after death.
Beatific vision
Seeing God face to face: the essence of heaven.

In the Bible

  • 1 Cor 13:12: then face to face
  • 1 Cor 3:13-15: saved, yet so as by fire
  • Matt 25:41-46: everlasting punishment, life everlasting

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I-II q.3 a.8: Final happiness is the vision of the divine essence.
  • ST I-II q.87 a.3: Mortal sin, unrepented, merits eternal punishment because it turns away from the eternal good.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “At the evening of life, we shall be judged on our love.” (St John of the Cross (Dichos 64))
  • “The souls in purgatory have a joy so great that only the joy of the saints in heaven can compare with it; for they are certain of seeing God.” (St Catherine of Genoa (Treatise on Purgatory))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Purgatory is a second chance after death, or a kind of temporary hell.
    What the Church teaches: It is the purification of those already saved, entirely different from the punishment of the damned (§§ 1030–1031).
  • Not quite: God sends people to hell against their will.
    What the Church teaches: Hell is definitive self-exclusion; God predestines no one there (§§ 1033, 1037).
  • Not quite: Heaven will be boring: clouds and harps.
    What the Church teaches: Heaven is perfect communion of life and love with the Trinity and all the blessed, beyond all we can imagine (§§ 1024–1027).

To think about

Pray for the souls in purgatory today, especially someone you knew.
