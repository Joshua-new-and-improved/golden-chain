Marriage

What does the Catholic Church teach about marriage? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1601–1666

In one sentence: Marriage is a lifelong covenant between a man and a woman, ordered to their good and to children; between the baptised it is a sacrament.

What the Catechism says

God himself is the author of marriage. Its vocation is written in human nature. Sin wounded it, the Law protected it, and Christ restored it to its original meaning, raising it to a sacrament: a sign of his love for the Church.

The spouses themselves are the ministers, exchanging consent freely before the Church. The essential properties of marriage are unity and indissolubility, and it is open to fertility. A valid, consummated sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved; a declaration of nullity (annulment) says that a true marriage never existed. The family is the "domestic church", the first school of Christian life.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1601 "The matrimonial covenant, by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of the whole of life, is by its nature ordered toward the good of the spouses and the procreation and education of offspring; this covenant between baptised persons has been raised by Christ the Lord to the dignity of a sacrament" (Canon Law).

§ 1602–1620 Marriage in God's plan: Scripture begins with the creation of man and woman in God's image and ends with "the wedding feast of the Lamb". The vocation to marriage is written in the very nature of man and woman as they came from the Creator. Marriage is not a purely human institution. Every person experiences evil, and marriage too is threatened by discord, domination, infidelity and jealousy: not from its nature but from sin. After the fall, marriage helps to overcome self-absorption. The Law, through the prophets, deepened understanding of its unity and indissolubility. Jesus begins his public life at the wedding at Cana; he teaches the original meaning of marriage: "What God has joined together, let no one put asunder." Christian marriage becomes an efficacious sign of the covenant of Christ and the Church. Virginity for the sake of the Kingdom is an unfolding of baptismal grace; both esteem of virginity and Christian marriage are inseparable and reinforce each other.

§ 1621–1632 In the Latin rite marriage normally takes place during Mass. The spouses are the ministers of Christ's grace, conferring the sacrament on each other by expressing their consent before the Church. Consent is the indispensable element that "makes the marriage". It must be free: without coercion, not impeded by any natural or ecclesiastical law. If freedom is lacking, the marriage is invalid. Consent must be expressed according to the Church's form (normally before a priest or deacon and two witnesses). Preparation for marriage is of prime importance.

§ 1633–1637 Mixed marriages (Catholic and baptised non-Catholic) need particular attention: they require permission from Church authority; the couple must not ignore the difficulties, and the Catholic party has the duty to preserve their own faith and to do all in their power to have the children baptised and raised Catholic. Marriage with a non-baptised person (disparity of cult) requires a dispensation for validity. The Church can, for grave reasons, declare a marriage null: that it never existed (an annulment).

§ 1638–1642 Effects: the marriage bond, established by God himself, so that a valid, consummated marriage between baptised persons can never be dissolved. The grace of the sacrament perfects the couple's love, strengthens their indissoluble unity, and helps them attain holiness in married life and in welcoming and educating children. Christ is the source of this grace: he dwells with them, gives them strength to take up their crosses, forgive one another, and bear one another's burdens.

§ 1643–1654 The goods and requirements of conjugal love: unity and indissolubility (polygamy is contrary to it; the spouses give themselves definitively and totally); fidelity (founded on God's fidelity to his covenant and Christ to his Church); and openness to fertility (children are the supreme gift of marriage and contribute greatly to the good of the parents). Couples to whom God has not granted children can have a conjugal life full of meaning, radiating fruitfulness of charity, hospitality and sacrifice. The divorced and civilly remarried remain members of the Church; they cannot receive Communion while the situation persists, but are to be accompanied, invited to Mass, prayer and works of charity.

§ 1655–1658 The domestic church: Christ chose to be born and grow up in the family of Joseph and Mary. The Church is God's family; from the beginning, whole households were converted. Today the family is a "domestic church", a community of grace and prayer, a school of human virtue and Christian charity: the first school of the Christian life. Single people too, especially those who have not chosen their state, must be welcomed into the "great family" of the Church.

Words to know

Indissolubility
A valid sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved by any human power.
Domestic church
The Christian family as the Church in miniature.

In the Bible

  • Gen 2:18-24: they shall be two in one flesh
  • Matt 19:3-9: what God hath joined
  • Eph 5:25-32: a great sacrament

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • SCG III.123: Marriage must be lifelong and exclusive because it is ordered to raising children and to the friendship of the spouses, the greatest friendship.
  • ST Supplement q.42 a.1: Marriage is a sacrament because it signifies the union of Christ and the Church.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “How can I ever express the happiness of the marriage joined by the Church, strengthened by an offering, sealed by a blessing, announced by angels and ratified by the Father?” (Tertullian (To His Wife II.8))
  • “I have taken you in my arms, and I love you, and I prefer you to my life itself… I place you before everything (advice to a young husband).” (St John Chrysostom (Homily on Ephesians 20))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: An annulment is a Catholic divorce.
    What the Church teaches: An annulment declares that a valid marriage never existed; divorce claims to dissolve one that did (§§ 1629, 2382–2385).
  • Not quite: The priest marries the couple.
    What the Church teaches: In the Latin Church the spouses are the ministers; the priest or deacon witnesses for the Church (§ 1623).
  • Not quite: Couples without children have a lesser marriage.
    What the Church teaches: Their marriage can be full of meaning and radiate fruitfulness in charity and service (§ 1654).

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand marriage as God's plan, a covenant and a sacrament, with its essential properties, and the family as domestic church.

  1. Why do you think Jesus began his public ministry at a wedding?
  2. What makes consent valid (§§ 1625–1628)?
  3. What could make your family (now or in future) more of a "domestic church"?

Activity: Interview a couple married for many years (in person or by video) about what has kept their marriage strong, then discuss how their answers relate to unity, fidelity and fruitfulness.
