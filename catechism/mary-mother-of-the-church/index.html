Mary, Mother of the Church

What does the Catholic Church teach about mary, Mother of the Church? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 963–975

In one sentence: Mary is the Church's model and mother in the order of grace; she was taken body and soul into glory.

What the Catechism says

Mary's role is inseparable from her Son's. From the Annunciation to the Cross she was united with him, and he gave her to the beloved disciple as mother. She is therefore mother of the Church, and the model of its faith and charity.

At the end of her earthly life she was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory, a singular share in her Son's Resurrection. Her motherhood continues: she intercedes for us. Devotion to her is intrinsic to Christian worship, but it differs essentially from the adoration given to God alone.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 963–965 After speaking of the Church, we turn to Mary, Mother of Christ and Mother of the Church. She is "clearly the mother of the members of Christ" because by her charity she joined in bringing about the birth of believers. Mary's role in the Church is inseparable from her union with Christ: from the virginal conception to his death, she stood by her Son, and at the Cross Jesus gave her to John: "Woman, behold your son." After the Ascension she prayed with the apostles for the gift of the Spirit.

§ 966 "Finally the Immaculate Virgin, preserved free from all stain of original sin, when the course of her earthly life was finished, was taken up body and soul into heavenly glory" (Pius XII, 1950). The Assumption is a singular participation in her Son's Resurrection and an anticipation of the resurrection of other Christians. The Byzantine liturgy: "In giving birth you kept your virginity; in your Dormition you did not leave the world, O Mother of God."

§ 967–970 By her complete adherence to the Father's will, to her Son's redemptive work, and to every prompting of the Spirit, Mary is the Church's model of faith and charity, the "exemplary realisation" of the Church. Her motherhood in the order of grace continues without interruption: taken up to heaven she continues by her intercession to bring us the gifts of salvation. So she is invoked as Advocate, Helper, Benefactress and Mediatrix. Her maternal role does not obscure or diminish Christ's unique mediation, but shows its power: all her saving influence flows from Christ's merits.

§ 971–975 "All generations will call me blessed." The Church's devotion to Mary is intrinsic to Christian worship. It differs essentially from the adoration given to the incarnate Word, the Father and the Spirit, and greatly fosters it; it is expressed in liturgical feasts and in Marian prayer such as the Rosary, "an epitome of the whole Gospel". In Mary we contemplate what the Church already is in her mystery and what she will be in the homeland: an eschatological icon of the Church.

Words to know

Assumption
Mary's being taken up body and soul into glory (defined 1950).
Hyperdulia
The special veneration given to Mary; different in kind from adoration (latria), given to God alone.

In the Bible

  • John 19:25-27: behold thy mother
  • Luke 1:48: all generations shall call me blessed
  • Rev 12:1: a woman clothed with the sun

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.25 a.5: Mary is given hyperdulia, a veneration above that of the saints but not the worship due to God.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The more one is consecrated to Mary, the more one is consecrated to Jesus Christ.” (St Louis de Montfort (True Devotion to Mary 120))
  • “Mary is the type of the Church in the order of faith, charity and perfect union with Christ (quoted in Lumen Gentium 63).” (St Ambrose (Exposition on Luke II.7))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Catholics believe Mary is a goddess or an equal mediator with Christ.
    What the Church teaches: Christ is the one Mediator; Mary's role flows entirely from his merits and leads to him (§ 970).
  • Not quite: The Assumption means Mary didn't die.
    What the Church teaches: The Church defines that she was taken up body and soul "when the course of her earthly life was finished"; it doesn't decide whether she died first (East: the Dormition) (§ 966).

To think about

Pray one Hail Mary slowly, for someone who needs a mother's care.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand Mary's role as Mother of the Church, the Assumption, and the difference between veneration and worship.

  1. How is Mary a model for the whole Church?
  2. How would you explain to a Protestant friend that Marian devotion leads to Christ?
  3. What does the Assumption tell us about our own future?

Activity: Pray one decade of the Rosary together, choosing the mystery of the Assumption. Before each Hail Mary, name someone who needs a mother's care.
