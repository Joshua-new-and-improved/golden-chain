One, holy, catholic and apostolic

What does the Catholic Church teach about one, holy, catholic and apostolic? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 811–870

In one sentence: These four marks are gifts Christ gives the Church and tasks she must live out.

What the Catechism says

The Church is one: one Lord, one faith, one baptism, held together by charity, the profession of faith, common worship and apostolic succession. The one Church of Christ subsists in the Catholic Church; yet many elements of truth and sanctification exist outside her visible bounds, and the desire for Christian unity is a gift and call of Christ.

She is holy, though containing sinners. She is catholic (universal), possessing the fullness of the means of salvation and sent to all. Outside the Church there is no salvation, which, rightly understood, means all salvation comes from Christ through his Church, while those who through no fault of their own don't know him can still be saved. She is apostolic, built on the apostles and still taught and governed by their successors.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 811–812 "This is the sole Church of Christ, which in the Creed we profess to be one, holy, catholic and apostolic." These four characteristics, inseparably linked, indicate essential features of the Church and her mission. The Church does not possess them of herself; it is Christ who, through the Spirit, makes her so, and calls her to realise each quality.

§ 813–822 The Church is one because of her source (the Trinity), her founder (Christ), and her soul (the Spirit). Bonds of unity: profession of one faith, common celebration of worship and the sacraments, and apostolic succession through Holy Orders. The sole Church of Christ subsists in the Catholic Church, governed by Peter's successor and the bishops in communion with him; yet many elements of sanctification and truth are found outside her visible confines: Scripture, grace, faith, hope, charity, the Spirit's gifts. Those born into communities separated from the Church cannot be charged with the sin of separation; they are rightly called Christians and accepted as brothers in the Lord. Christ always gives his Church the gift of unity, but she must pray and work for full unity (ecumenism): conversion of heart, prayer, mutual knowledge, dialogue and collaboration.

§ 823–829 The Church is holy: Christ loved her as his Bride and gave himself up to make her holy. She is "the holy People of God", and her members are called saints. Yet the Church, "clasping sinners to her bosom", is at once holy and always in need of purification. All members, ministers included, must acknowledge that they are sinners. By canonising saints, the Church recognises the power of the Spirit of holiness within her. Mary already embodies the Church's holiness.

§ 830–856 The Church is catholic, "universal": in her subsists the fullness of Christ's body, with the fullness of the means of salvation, and she has been sent out by Christ to the whole human race. Each particular Church (diocese) is catholic through communion with the Church of Rome. All people are called to belong to the People of God, in various ways: the Catholic faithful, other Christians, Jews ("the first to hear the Word of God"), Muslims (who "profess to hold the faith of Abraham"), and all who seek God. "Outside the Church there is no salvation" means, positively, that all salvation comes from Christ the Head through the Church; it does not apply to those who, through no fault of their own, do not know Christ but seek God sincerely and try to do his will. The Church still has the obligation and sacred right to evangelise all.

§ 857–870 The Church is apostolic: built on the foundation of the apostles; keeping and handing on their teaching with the Spirit's help; and taught, sanctified and guided by the apostles until Christ's return, through their successors, the bishops, assisted by priests, in union with the Pope. The whole Church is apostolic in that she is sent into the whole world; all members share in this mission, in various ways.

Words to know

Subsists in
The one Church of Christ is found in its fullness in the Catholic Church (Lumen Gentium 8).
Ecumenism
Work and prayer for the unity of all Christians.

In the Bible

  • John 17:20-23: that they all may be one
  • Eph 2:19-20: built on the apostles and prophets

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • Thomas, Commentary on the Apostles' Creed, art. 9: Thomas explains the Church's marks: one (in faith, hope and charity), holy (washed and anointed by Christ), catholic (in place, people and time) and firm, because it is built on Christ and the apostles.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “He cannot have God for his Father who does not have the Church for his mother.” (St Cyprian (On the Unity of the Church 6))
  • “Outside the Church one can have everything except salvation (said of those who knowingly separate themselves).” (St Augustine (Sermon to the People of Caesarea 6))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Catholic Church says only Catholics can be saved.
    What the Church teaches: All salvation comes from Christ through his Church, but those who through no fault of their own don't know Christ and seek God sincerely may be saved (§§ 846–848).
  • Not quite: Other Christians are not really Christians.
    What the Church teaches: They are rightly called Christians and accepted as brothers and sisters in the Lord (§ 818).
  • Not quite: The Church is holy, so its members are not sinners.
    What the Church teaches: The Church is holy and always in need of purification; all her members are sinners (§ 827).

To think about

Pray for Christian unity today, naming a Christian friend from another church.

For catechists

Aim: Participants can explain each mark of the Church, especially "subsists in", ecumenism, and the true meaning of "outside the Church no salvation".

  1. What unites you with a Christian friend from another church? What still divides?
  2. How would you explain "outside the Church there is no salvation" to an anxious friend?
  3. Why does the Church still need to evangelise if others can be saved?

Activity: Four corners: label the room's corners One, Holy, Catholic, Apostolic. Read statements (e.g. "the Pope succeeds Peter", "saints are canonised") and have people move to the mark it illustrates.
