People of God, Body of Christ, Temple of the Spirit

What does the Catholic Church teach about people of God, Body of Christ, Temple of the Spirit? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 781–810

In one sentence: The Church is God's people, Christ's body with him as head, and the Spirit's temple.

What the Catechism says

The Church is the People of God: one becomes a member not by birth but by faith and Baptism. This people shares Christ's offices as priest, prophet and king; its law is love, its mission to be salt and light, its destiny the Kingdom.

The Church is also the Body of Christ: he is its head, its members are united to him and to one another, especially through the Eucharist, with a diversity of gifts and a special place for the poor and suffering. And the Church is the Temple of the Holy Spirit, who is the soul of the Mystical Body and the source of its life and unity.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 781–786 God wills to make people holy and save them not as individuals without bond, but as a people. The People of God has distinct characteristics: it belongs to God; one becomes a member through faith and Baptism, "born of water and the Spirit"; its Head is Jesus Christ; its status is the dignity and freedom of the children of God; its law is the new commandment of love; its mission is to be the salt of the earth and light of the world; its destiny is the Kingdom of God. The whole people shares in Christ's three offices: priestly (offering themselves), prophetic (witnessing), and royal (serving, especially the poor).

§ 787–796 From the beginning Jesus associated his disciples with his own life; he proclaimed an intimate communion: "Abide in me, and I in you." By communicating his Spirit, Christ mystically constitutes as his body those brothers and sisters called from all nations. In this body there is a diversity of members and functions, all united to each other, especially to those who suffer and are persecuted. Christ is the Head of the body; the Church is also the Bride of Christ: "the two shall become one flesh. This is a great mystery".

§ 797–801 "What the soul is to the human body, the Holy Spirit is to the Body of Christ, the Church" (St Augustine). The Spirit is the principle of every vital and saving action in each part of the body. He makes the Church the "temple of the living God". He gives charisms, extraordinary or simple, ordered to building up the Church and the good of others; they must be discerned by the Church's pastors, and always ordered to charity, the true measure of all charisms.

§ 802–810 In Brief: Christ gave himself to make a people his own; the Church is the People of God, the Body of Christ, the Temple of the Spirit, and the Bride of Christ; the Church is a people gathered by the unity of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Words to know

Mystical Body
The Church as the body of which Christ is the head.
Common priesthood
The priesthood of all the baptised: offering their lives to God.

In the Bible

  • 1 Pet 2:9: a kingly priesthood
  • 1 Cor 12:12-27: you are the body of Christ
  • 1 Cor 3:16: you are the temple of God

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.8 a.1: Christ is the head of the Church by his dignity, his perfect grace, and his power to give grace to the members.
  • ST III q.8 a.1 ad 3: The Holy Spirit is compared to the heart, invisibly animating the Church.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “What the soul is to the human body, the Holy Spirit is to the Body of Christ, the Church.” (St Augustine (Sermon 267.4))
  • “Christ has no body now on earth but yours, no hands but yours, no feet but yours.” (St Teresa of Avila (attributed))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Being a Christian is just me and Jesus.
    What the Church teaches: God saves us as a people, a body; we belong to one another (§§ 781, 790–791).
  • Not quite: Charisms are only spectacular gifts like healing or tongues.
    What the Church teaches: Charisms can be extraordinary or simple and humble; all are for building up the Church in charity (§ 799).

To think about

"If one member suffer, all suffer" (1 Cor 12:26). Who in your parish or group is suffering right now?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the three great images (People, Body, Temple) and their own share in Christ's priestly, prophetic and royal mission.

  1. How do you exercise Christ's priestly, prophetic and royal offices as a lay person?
  2. "If one member suffers, all suffer" (1 Cor 12:26). Who is suffering in your community?
  3. What charism might God have given you?

Activity: Body-part exercise: each person picks a part of the body (eyes, hands, feet, ears) and names a role in the parish that it represents, then thanks someone who does it.

Part of the course: Becoming Catholic (OCIA / RCIA).
