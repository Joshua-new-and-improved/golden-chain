Scripture: God's word in human words

What does the Catholic Church teach about scripture: God's word in human words? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 101–108

In one sentence: God is the author of Scripture, and he wrote it through human authors who were true authors too; so it teaches without error the truth God wanted put in writing for our salvation.

What the Catechism says

In Scripture God speaks to us in a human way, just as the eternal Word took on our flesh. Through all its words, God speaks only one Word: his Son. That is why the Church venerates the Scriptures as she venerates the Lord's Body, and nourishes the faithful from both.

God inspired the human authors: he used them with their own abilities, so that they wrote everything and only what he wanted. The books therefore teach firmly, faithfully and without error the truth God wanted recorded for our salvation. Christianity is not a "religion of the book" but of the living Word, Christ.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 101 To reveal himself, God speaks to us in human words. God's words, expressed in human language, have become like human speech, just as the Word of the eternal Father took on human flesh.

§ 102–104 Through all the words of Scripture God speaks only one single Word, his Son. That is why the Church has always venerated the Scriptures as she venerates the Lord's Body, never ceasing to offer the faithful the bread of life from the one table of God's Word and Christ's Body. In Scripture the Church finds her nourishment and strength; in the sacred books the Father meets his children with great love and speaks with them.

§ 105 God is the author of Sacred Scripture. Holy Mother Church holds that the books of both Testaments, whole and entire, with all their parts, are sacred and canonical, because written under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, they have God as their author.

§ 106 God inspired the human authors. In composing the books he chose certain men who, all the while he employed them in this task, made full use of their own faculties and powers, so that, though he acted in them and by them, it was as true authors that they consigned to writing whatever he wanted written, and no more.

§ 107 The inspired books teach the truth: since all that the inspired authors affirm should be regarded as affirmed by the Holy Spirit, the books of Scripture firmly, faithfully and without error teach that truth which God, for the sake of our salvation, wished to see confided to the Sacred Scriptures.

§ 108 Still, the Christian faith is not a "religion of the book". It is the religion of the Word of God, not a written and mute word, but the Word incarnate and living. If Scripture is not to remain a dead letter, Christ must, through the Spirit, open our minds to understand it.

Words to know

Inspiration
The Holy Spirit's action on the human writers so that God is the author of what they wrote.
Inerrancy
Scripture teaches without error the truth God intended for our salvation.

In the Bible

  • 2 Tim 3:16-17: all scripture inspired of God
  • 2 Pet 1:20-21: holy men spoke, inspired by the Spirit

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.1 a.10: God is the author of Scripture; since he can express meaning not only by words but by things themselves, Scripture has a literal and a spiritual sense.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Christ.” (St Jerome (Commentary on Isaiah, prologue))
  • “We speak to God when we pray; we listen to him when we read the divine sayings.” (St Ambrose (On the Duties of Clergy I.20))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Inspiration means God dictated word for word.
    What the Church teaches: The human authors used their own faculties as true authors (§ 106).
  • Not quite: Inerrancy means Scripture is a science textbook.
    What the Church teaches: It teaches without error the truth God wanted recorded for our salvation (§ 107).
  • Not quite: Catholics don't really value the Bible.
    What the Church teaches: The Church venerates Scripture as she venerates the Lord's Body (§ 103).

To think about

How would you read the Bible differently if you remembered that God is speaking to you through it today?

For catechists

Aim: Participants can explain inspiration and inerrancy accurately, and approach Scripture as God speaking to them.

  1. How can a book be fully God's word and fully human words?
  2. What kind of truth does Genesis 1 teach? What kind does it not try to teach?
  3. How does the Mass feed us from "one table" of Word and Eucharist (§ 103)?

Activity: Compare Luke 1:1–4 (a human author describing his research) with 2 Tim 3:16 (inspiration). Discuss how both are true at once.
