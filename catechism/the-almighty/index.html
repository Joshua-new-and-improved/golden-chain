The Almighty

What does the Catholic Church teach about Almighty? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 268–278

In one sentence: God's power is universal, loving and mysterious; he shows it most of all by forgiving.

What the Catechism says

Of all God's attributes, only omnipotence is named in the Creed. His power is universal (he made everything and rules everything), loving (he is our Father), and mysterious: it can seem absent when evil happens. Yet faith sees it most in the weakness of the Cross and the Resurrection.

Nothing is impossible with God. The liturgy says he shows his almighty power above all by having mercy and forgiving. Only faith can embrace the ways of this power, which is "made perfect in infirmity".

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 268 Of all the divine attributes, only omnipotence is named in the Creed: confessing it has great bearing on our lives. God's power is universal, loving and mysterious; only faith can discern it when it is "made perfect in weakness".

§ 269 Scripture repeatedly confesses God's universal power: he is "the Mighty One of Jacob", "the Lord of hosts". Nothing is impossible with God; he disposes his works according to his will. He is master of history, governing hearts and events.

§ 270–271 God is the Father Almighty, whose fatherhood and power shed light on each other: he shows his almighty power by caring for our needs, by adopting us as children, and above all by his infinite mercy, freely forgiving sins. His power is in no way arbitrary: in God power, essence, will, intellect, wisdom and justice are all identical, so nothing can be in his power that cannot be in his just will or wise intellect.

§ 272–274 Faith in the Father Almighty can be tested by the experience of evil and suffering; God can seem absent and incapable of stopping evil. But God revealed his almighty power in the most mysterious way in his Son's voluntary humiliation and Resurrection, by which he conquered evil. Christ crucified is "the power of God and the wisdom of God". Nothing is better able to strengthen our faith and hope than holding fast to the conviction that nothing is impossible with God.

§ 275–278 With Job we confess that God can do all things; with Mary, that nothing is impossible with God. If we do not believe that God's love is almighty, how can we believe that the Father could create us, the Son redeem us and the Spirit sanctify us?

Words to know

Omnipotence
God's power to do all things that are possible.

In the Bible

  • Luke 1:37: no word shall be impossible with God
  • 2 Cor 12:9: power is made perfect in infirmity

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.25 a.3: God can do everything that does not involve a contradiction; whatever is truly possible.
  • ST I q.25 a.3 ad 3: God's omnipotence is shown chiefly in sparing and having mercy.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “O God, who manifest your almighty power above all by pardoning and showing mercy…” (Collect of the 26th Sunday in Ordinary Time)
  • “God's omnipotence is shown especially in sparing and having mercy, because by it he leads us to share in his infinite good.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST I q.25 a.3 ad 3))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: If God is almighty, he can do absurd things like make a square circle.
    What the Church teaches: Omnipotence means God can do everything possible; a contradiction is not a "thing" (§ 271; ST I q.25 a.3).
  • Not quite: Suffering proves that God is not all-powerful.
    What the Church teaches: God's power is most fully revealed in the weakness of the Cross and the victory of the Resurrection (§ 272).

To think about

Where do you find it hardest to believe God is almighty? Bring that place to him.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand God's power as fatherly and merciful, and can face the objection from suffering.

  1. How is God's power different from human power?
  2. Why does the liturgy say God shows his power most by mercy?
  3. Where have you seen strength in weakness (2 Cor 12:9)?

Activity: Read Luke 1:26–38 and Job 42:1–6. List what each says about God's power.
