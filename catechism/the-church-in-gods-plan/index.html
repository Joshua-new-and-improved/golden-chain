The Church in God's plan

What does the Catholic Church teach about Church in God's plan? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 748–780

In one sentence: The Church is God's plan from the beginning: prefigured, prepared in Israel, founded by Christ, revealed by the Spirit, and fulfilled in glory.

What the Catechism says

Christ is the light of the nations, and that light shines on the face of the Church. "Church" (ekklesia) means a convocation, the assembly of those God calls. She is described by many images: the sheepfold, God's field, God's building, our mother, the bride of Christ.

The Church was foreshadowed from the beginning, prepared in the history of Israel, instituted by Christ (above all in the Paschal mystery, when the Church was born from his pierced side), and revealed by the Spirit at Pentecost. She will be perfected in glory. She is at once visible and spiritual, human and divine, and she is the sacrament of salvation: the sign and instrument of communion with God and of unity among people.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 748–750 "Christ is the light of humanity" (Lumen Gentium 1); that light is reflected on the face of the Church. The article on the Church depends entirely on the article on Christ Jesus. The Church has no other light than Christ's. To believe that the Church is "holy" and "catholic" is inseparable from belief in God the Father, Son and Spirit: we believe the Church, not in the Church, to avoid confusing God with his works.

§ 751–752 The word "Church" (Latin ecclesia, from the Greek ek-kalein, "to call out of") means a convocation or assembly. In the Old Testament it was used for the chosen people gathered before God, especially at Sinai. The first Christians called themselves "Church". In Christian usage "church" means the liturgical assembly, the local community, and the whole universal community of believers. These three cannot be separated.

§ 753–757 Scripture gives many images of the Church: the sheepfold (Christ the gate and shepherd); the cultivated field and vineyard (Christ the true vine); the building of God, with Christ as cornerstone, God's household, the temple; our mother, the Jerusalem above; and the spotless bride of the spotless Lamb.

§ 758–769 The Church's origin, foundation and mission: the Church was in God's plan from the beginning, "prefigured from the world's beginning". It was prepared in the Old Covenant, in the calling of Abraham and Israel. It was instituted by Christ: he inaugurated it by preaching the Kingdom, choosing the Twelve with Peter as their head, and above all by his total self-giving on the Cross; the Church was born from his pierced side, "as Eve was formed from the sleeping Adam's side". It was revealed by the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, and will be perfected in glory at the end of time.

§ 770–776 The Church is in history but transcends it: only the eyes of faith see its spiritual reality. It is at once visible and spiritual, a hierarchical society and the Mystical Body of Christ, an earthly and a heavenly reality. The Church is the universal sacrament of salvation: the sign and instrument of intimate union with God and of the unity of the whole human race. In the Church this communion is already begun; it is the means and goal of God's plan.

Words to know

Ekklesia
"Those called out": the assembly God convokes.
Sacrament of salvation
The Church as sign and instrument of union with God and unity among all people.

In the Bible

  • Matt 16:18: upon this rock I will build my church
  • John 19:34: blood and water from his side
  • Eph 5:25-27: Christ loved the church

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.64 a.2 ad 3: The sacraments, from which the Church is built, flowed from Christ's side as he hung on the Cross.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The whole Christ, head and body: Christ and the Church.” (St Augustine (Sermon 341))
  • “About Jesus Christ and the Church, I simply know they're just one thing, and we shouldn't complicate the matter.” (St Joan of Arc (at her trial, 1431))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Jesus didn't intend to found a Church; it was a later human invention.
    What the Church teaches: Christ inaugurated the Church, chose the Twelve with Peter, and gave it life from the Cross; the Spirit revealed it at Pentecost (§§ 763–767).
  • Not quite: The Church is just its buildings, or just the clergy.
    What the Church teaches: "Church" means the whole people God calls together, in liturgy, locally and universally (§ 752).

  1. Which image of the Church (flock, vine, building, mother, bride) helps you most? Why?
  2. What does it mean that the Church was "born from Christ's side"?
  3. How can the Church be holy and full of sinners at the same time?
