The communion of saints

What does the Catholic Church teach about communion of saints? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 946–962

In one sentence: The Church is a communion of holy things and holy people, in heaven, in purgatory and on earth.

What the Catechism says

"Communion of saints" has two meanings: communion in holy things (the faith, the sacraments, charisms and charity) and communion among holy persons. The Church exists in three states: some are pilgrims on earth, some are being purified after death, and some are in glory.

These are united by charity. The saints in heaven intercede for us; we honour them and ask their prayers. We pray for the dead, so that they may be freed from their sins. And on earth, the least of our acts done in charity benefit all.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 946–948 After confessing the Church, the Creed adds "the communion of saints". In a sense this sums up the Church: "What is the Church if not the assembly of all the saints?" The communion of saints is the Church. The term has two closely linked meanings: communion in holy things (sancta) and among holy persons (sancti). "Holy things for the holy" (Byzantine liturgy).

§ 949–953 Communion in spiritual goods: communion in the faith received from the apostles; communion of the sacraments, especially Baptism and the Eucharist; communion of charisms, given for the common good; "they had everything in common"; and communion in charity: "none of us lives to himself"; the least of our acts done in charity redounds to the profit of all.

§ 954–959 The three states of the Church: some disciples are pilgrims on earth; others have died and are being purified; still others are in glory. All are united by charity. The saints in heaven intercede for us with the Father; we do not venerate them only for their example, but because union with them strengthens the Church's unity in the Spirit. The Church has from the beginning honoured the memory of the dead and offered prayers for them. Our prayer for them can help them and make their intercession for us effective.

§ 960–962 The Church is a communion of saints: an image of the Body of Christ, united in one family. We believe in the communion of all the faithful of Christ: pilgrims on earth, the dead being purified, and the blessed in heaven, all together forming one Church.

  • “Do not weep, for I shall be more useful to you after my death, and I shall help you then more effectively than during my life.” (St Dominic (on his deathbed))
  • “I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth.” (St Thérèse of Lisieux (Last Conversations, 17 July 1897))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Praying to saints takes away from God.
    What the Church teaches: The saints intercede with God for us; their prayers lead us to Christ, the one Mediator (§ 956).
  • Not quite: Once someone dies, we can do nothing for them.
    What the Church teaches: We can pray for the dead, and our prayer can help them (§ 958).

To think about

Choose a saint to be your friend this month. Ask for their prayers each day.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the communion of saints as one family across heaven, purgatory and earth, sharing spiritual goods.

  1. How can a small act of charity help the whole Church (§ 953)?
  2. Which saint do you feel closest to? Why?
  3. Who among your dead would you like to pray for?

Activity: Make a "family tree" of the communion of saints: your patron saint, a relative who has died, and someone on earth you pray for. Pray for each by name.
