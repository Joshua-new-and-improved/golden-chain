The creeds

What does the Catholic Church teach about creeds? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 185–197

In one sentence: The creeds are short summaries of the faith; the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds are the two most important.

What the Catechism says

From the beginning the Church expressed her faith in brief formulas, called creeds or "symbols" (from the Greek for a token of recognition). The Apostles' Creed is the ancient baptismal creed of the Church of Rome. The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed comes from the first two ecumenical councils (325 and 381) and is shared by the Churches of East and West.

The Catechism follows the Apostles' Creed, in three parts: God the Father and creation; Jesus Christ and redemption; the Holy Spirit and our sanctification. Reciting the Creed with faith is entering into communion with God and with the whole Church.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 185–188 Whoever says "I believe" says "I pledge myself to what we believe." From the beginning the Church expressed her faith in brief formulas for all. These are called professions of faith, "creeds" (from credo), or "symbols" of faith: the Greek symbolon was half of a broken object, matched with the other half as a sign of recognition.

§ 189–191 The first profession of faith is made at Baptism. Because Baptism is given "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit", the Creed has three parts: God the Father and creation; Jesus Christ and redemption; the Holy Spirit and our sanctification. Traditionally its statements are called "articles", like the joints of a body.

§ 192–195 Many creeds have arisen through the centuries, in response to the needs of different eras. None can be considered superseded. Two have a special place: the Apostles' Creed, the ancient baptismal symbol of the Church of Rome, a faithful summary of the apostles' faith; and the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed, from the first two ecumenical councils (325, 381), still common to the great Churches of East and West.

§ 196–197 The Catechism follows the Apostles' Creed, completed by references to the Nicene. As at Baptism, to say the Creed in faith is to enter into communion with the Father, Son and Spirit, and with the whole Church that hands on the faith. St Ambrose called it a spiritual seal and a treasure for the heart.

Words to know

Symbol of faith
Another name for a creed: a sign by which believers recognise one another.

In the Bible

  • 1 Cor 15:3-5: I delivered unto you first of all

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.1 a.9: It was necessary to gather the truths of faith into a creed, so that they could be more easily proposed to all.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The Creed is the spiritual seal, our heart's meditation and an ever-present guardian; it is, unquestionably, the treasure of our soul.” (St Ambrose (Explanation of the Creed 1))
  • “The Creed was not composed by human choice: the most important points were collected from all of Scripture to make one complete teaching of the faith.” (St Cyril of Jerusalem (Catecheses 5.12))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Creed is just a list of opinions.
    What the Church teaches: It is a pledge of the whole person to what the Church believes, received at Baptism (§§ 185, 197).
  • Not quite: Old creeds are outdated.
    What the Church teaches: None of the creeds can be considered superseded or irrelevant (§ 193).

To think about

Pray the Creed slowly today, pausing after each line.

For catechists

Aim: Participants know what a creed is, why it has three parts, and the place of the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds.

  1. Why might early Christians have needed a short summary of the faith?
  2. What is the link between the Creed and Baptism?
  3. Which line of the Creed do you find most moving? Most difficult?

Activity: Break a card in two; give each half to different people, and let them find their match. Explain symbolon: the Creed is how believers recognise one another.
