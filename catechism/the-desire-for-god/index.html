The desire for God

What does the Catholic Church teach about desire for God? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 26–30

In one sentence: We are made for God, so every human heart is, in some way, searching for him.

What the Catechism says

Faith is our response to God who reveals himself. Before speaking of revelation, the Catechism first looks at the person who receives it, who is made for God.

The desire for God is written in the human heart, because we were created by God and for God. Throughout history people have expressed this search in prayers, sacrifices, rituals and meditation: we are religious by nature.

That desire can be forgotten or even rejected, through suffering, scandal, distraction, sin, or the wish to hide from God. Yet God never stops calling, and seeking him takes the whole person: mind, will, an upright heart, and the witness of others.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 26 When we profess our faith we begin with "I believe" or "We believe". Before treating the faith itself, the Catechism considers the person who believes (Chapter One), God who reveals (Chapter Two), and the response of faith (Chapter Three).

§ 27 The desire for God is written in the human heart, because we are created by God and for God; he never ceases to draw us. Only in God will we find the truth and happiness we never stop seeking. Our dignity rests above all on this call to communion with God.

§ 28 Throughout history people have expressed their search for God in religious beliefs and behaviour: prayers, sacrifices, rituals, meditations. These expressions, despite their ambiguities, are so universal that we can call the human being a religious being.

§ 29 This intimate bond with God can be forgotten, overlooked or explicitly rejected, for many reasons: revolt against evil in the world, religious ignorance or indifference, worldly cares and riches, the bad example of believers, currents of thought hostile to religion, and the sinner's attitude of hiding from God.

§ 30 "Let the hearts of those who seek the Lord rejoice." Even if we forget or reject God, he never ceases to call every person to seek him and find life and happiness. This search demands every effort of intellect, a sound will, an upright heart, and the witness of others who teach us to seek God.

Words to know

Homo religiosus
"Religious man": the human person as naturally oriented to God.
Atheism
Denial of God; the Catechism lists many causes, from ignorance to revolt against evil to careless indifference (CCC 29).

In the Bible

  • Acts 17:26-28: in him we live and move
  • Ps 104:3-4 (105): let the hearts of those who seek the Lord rejoice

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.12 a.1: Every person naturally desires to know the cause of what they see; a mind that could never see God would be left unsatisfied.
  • Augustine, Confessions I.1: "You have made us for yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you": the line the Catechism quotes (CCC 30).

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Our heart is restless until it rests in you.” (St Augustine (Confessions I.1))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Religion is just a cultural invention.
    What the Church teaches: The universal search for God reflects how we are made: for God (§§ 27–28).
  • Not quite: Atheists are simply bad people.
    What the Church teaches: The Catechism lists many causes of unbelief, including the bad example of believers (§ 29).

To think about

Where in your own life have you felt that "restlessness" Augustine describes?

For catechists

Aim: Participants recognise their own longing for meaning as a longing for God, and understand why people turn away.

  1. Where do you see people searching for something more (music, sport, relationships, success)?
  2. Which reason for unbelief in § 29 do you encounter most?
  3. How can our own example help or hinder someone's search (§ 29)?

Activity: Play or read a popular song about longing. Discuss: what is the singer really looking for?
