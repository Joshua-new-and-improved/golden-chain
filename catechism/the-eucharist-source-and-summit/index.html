The Eucharist: source and summit

What does the Catholic Church teach about Eucharist: source and summit? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1322–1344

In one sentence: The Eucharist is the source and summit of the Christian life, instituted at the Last Supper and celebrated by the Church from the beginning.

What the Catechism says

The Eucharist completes Christian initiation. In it Christ makes present his one sacrifice and gives himself as food. It contains the whole spiritual good of the Church, Christ himself, our Pasch. The Church calls it by many names: Eucharist (thanksgiving), the Lord's Supper, the Breaking of Bread, the Holy Sacrifice, Holy Communion, Holy Mass.

Christ prepared it through the signs of bread and wine, the manna and the multiplication of loaves, and instituted it on the night he was betrayed: "Do this in memory of me." From the first community of Jerusalem, Christians gathered on the first day of the week to break bread.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1322–1323 The Holy Eucharist completes Christian initiation. At the Last Supper, on the night he was betrayed, our Saviour instituted the Eucharistic sacrifice of his Body and Blood, to perpetuate the sacrifice of the Cross throughout the ages until he comes, and to entrust to his Bride, the Church, a memorial of his death and Resurrection: a sacrament of love, a sign of unity, a bond of charity, a Paschal banquet.

§ 1324–1327 The Eucharist is "the source and summit of the Christian life". The other sacraments, ministries and works of the apostolate are bound up with it and oriented to it. In the Eucharist is contained the whole spiritual good of the Church, Christ himself, our Pasch. It is the efficacious sign and sublime cause of communion in the divine life and of the unity of the People of God. "Our way of thinking is attuned to the Eucharist, and the Eucharist in turn confirms our way of thinking" (St Irenaeus).

§ 1328–1332 Names: Eucharist (thanksgiving); the Lord's Supper; the Breaking of Bread; the Eucharistic assembly (synaxis); the memorial of the Lord's Passion and Resurrection; the Holy Sacrifice; the Holy and Divine Liturgy; Holy Communion; Holy Mass (from the dismissal, missio, sending the faithful out).

§ 1333–1336 Signs of bread and wine: at the heart of the celebration are bread and wine that become Christ's Body and Blood. Prefigured by Melchizedek's offering, the bread and wine of the Passover, and the manna; fulfilled in the miracles of the loaves and the wine at Cana. The first announcement of the Eucharist (John 6) divided the disciples: "This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?" The Eucharist and the Cross are stumbling blocks.

§ 1337–1344 The institution: Jesus chose the Passover to fulfil what he had announced at Capernaum. "This is my body which is given for you. Do this in memory of me… This cup is the new covenant in my blood." By celebrating the Last Supper with his apostles during the Passover meal, Jesus gave the Jewish Passover its definitive meaning. The command "Do this" is directed at the liturgical celebration of the memorial. From the beginning the Church has been faithful: "They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers." Christians gathered especially on "the first day of the week", Sunday.

Words to know

Eucharist
"Thanksgiving": the sacrament of Christ's Body and Blood.
Mass
From the dismissal (missa), sending the faithful to do God's will.

In the Bible

  • Luke 22:19-20: do this for a commemoration of me
  • John 6:52-59: my flesh is meat indeed
  • Acts 2:42: the breaking of bread

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.65 a.3: The Eucharist is the greatest sacrament, because it contains Christ himself, and all the other sacraments are ordered to it as their end.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Our way of thinking is attuned to the Eucharist, and the Eucharist in turn confirms our way of thinking.” (St Irenaeus (Against Heresies IV.18.5))
  • “O precious and wonderful banquet, that brings us salvation and contains all sweetness!” (St Thomas Aquinas (Office of Corpus Christi, reading))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Eucharist is one sacrament among others, no more important.
    What the Church teaches: It is the source and summit: all the other sacraments are ordered to it (§ 1324).
  • Not quite: "Do this in memory of me" means just remember Jesus.
    What the Church teaches: It commands the liturgical celebration of the memorial that makes his sacrifice present (§ 1341).
