The Fall and original sin

What does the Catholic Church teach about Fall and original sin? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 385–421

In one sentence: Our first parents' sin wounded human nature; we inherit that wound, but Christ, the new Adam, is greater.

What the Catechism says

The reality of sin can only be understood in relation to God. Scripture tells of a primeval event: our first parents, tempted by the devil, let their trust in God die and disobeyed. They lost original holiness and justice not only for themselves but for all.

Original sin is a sin "contracted", not "committed": a state, not an act. Human nature is not totally corrupted, but wounded: subject to ignorance, suffering, death and an inclination to sin (concupiscence). Baptism takes away original sin, though its consequences remain as a struggle. And the first gospel (Gen 3:15) already announces the victory of a new Adam and a new Eve.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 385–390 God is infinitely good and all his works are good. Yet no one escapes the experience of suffering, evil in nature, and moral evil. Sin is present in human history; trying to ignore it or give it other names is futile. Only the light of revelation clarifies it: sin is an abuse of the freedom God gives to persons so that they may love him and one another. The doctrine of original sin is the "reverse side" of the Good News that Jesus is Saviour of all. The account of the fall in Genesis 3 uses figurative language, but affirms a primeval event, a deed that took place at the beginning of human history.

§ 391–395 Behind our first parents' disobedient choice lurks a seductive voice opposed to God. Scripture and Tradition see in this being a fallen angel, Satan or the devil. The angels were created good by God, but became evil by their own free and irrevocable rejection of God. Their sin cannot be forgiven, not because of any defect in God's mercy, but because of the irrevocable character of their choice. The devil's power is not infinite; he is only a creature. Although he acts in the world out of hatred for God, he cannot prevent the building up of God's reign. It is a great mystery that providence permits his activity, but "we know that in everything God works for good with those who love him."

§ 396–401 God created man in his image and established him in his friendship; as a spiritual creature, man can live this friendship only in free submission to God. The prohibition against eating "of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil" symbolises the limits we must freely recognise. Tempted by the devil, man let his trust in his Creator die in his heart and, abusing his freedom, disobeyed. All subsequent sin would be disobedience towards God and lack of trust in his goodness. Man preferred himself to God. The consequences: the harmony of original justice was destroyed; the union of man and woman became subject to tensions and domination; harmony with creation was broken; and death entered human history.

§ 402–406 All people are implicated in Adam's sin (Rom 5:12–19). How? The whole human race is in Adam "as one body of one man". Adam and Eve received original holiness and justice not for themselves alone but for all human nature; by sinning they transmitted a human nature deprived of original holiness and justice. That is why original sin is called "sin" only in an analogical sense: it is a sin "contracted" and not "committed", a state and not an act. Human nature is not totally corrupted: it is wounded in its natural powers, subject to ignorance, suffering and death, and inclined to sin (concupiscence). Baptism, by imparting Christ's life of grace, erases original sin and turns a person back towards God, but the consequences for nature remain, calling us to spiritual battle.

§ 407–409 The doctrine of original sin gives lucid discernment of our situation and actions in the world. Ignoring that we have a wounded nature inclined to evil gives rise to serious errors in education, politics, social action and morals. Original sin and all personal sins put the world in a sinful condition: the whole of human history has been a hard battle against the powers of evil.

§ 410–412 After the fall, man was not abandoned by God. God calls him and announces in a mysterious way the coming victory over evil and his restoration from the fall: the "Protoevangelium" (Gen 3:15). Tradition sees here the New Adam, who by his obedience unto death makes amends superabundantly, and Mary, the New Eve. Why did God not prevent the first man from sinning? St Leo: Christ's inexpressible grace gave us blessings better than those the demon's envy took away. St Thomas: nothing prevents human nature being raised up to something greater even after sin. Hence the Exsultet: "O happy fault, which gained for us so great a Redeemer!"

Words to know

Original sin
The deprivation of original holiness and justice, transmitted to all by birth.
Concupiscence
The inclination to sin that remains after Baptism.

In the Bible

  • Gen 3:1-15: the Fall
  • Rom 5:12-19: by one man sin entered

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I-II q.82 a.3: Original sin is materially concupiscence, formally the lack of original justice.
  • ST I-II q.85 a.3: Sin leaves four wounds in nature: ignorance, malice, weakness and disordered desire.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “O truly necessary sin of Adam, destroyed completely by the death of Christ! O happy fault that earned so great, so glorious a Redeemer!” (Exsultet (Easter Vigil))
  • “Christ's inexpressible grace gave us blessings better than those the demon's envy had taken away.” (St Leo the Great (Sermon 73.4))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Original sin means a newborn baby is personally guilty of Adam's act.
    What the Church teaches: Original sin is "contracted", a state, not a personal act; it is sin only analogically (§ 404).
  • Not quite: Original sin means human nature is totally corrupt.
    What the Church teaches: Our nature is wounded, not totally corrupted (§ 405).
  • Not quite: The devil is God's evil equal.
    What the Church teaches: He is only a creature, powerful but not infinite, and cannot stop God's Kingdom (§ 395).

Psychology connection

Thomas's "four wounds" map onto modern findings on cognitive bias (ignorance), weak self-control (weakness) and strong impulses (disordered desire). Psychology describes the symptoms; the doctrine names the cause and the cure.

To think about

Which of Thomas's four wounds (ignorance, malice, weakness, disordered desire) do you notice most in yourself?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand original sin accurately (state, not act; wounded, not corrupted) and see it in the light of Christ's victory.

  1. Where do you see the effects of original sin in the world? In yourself?
  2. Why do false ideas about human nature lead to errors in politics and education (§ 407)?
  3. What does the Exsultet mean by "O happy fault"?

Activity: Read Genesis 3:1–15 and mark: the temptation (distrust), the act (disobedience), the consequences (shame, blame, broken relationships), and the promise (v. 15). Then read Romans 5:17–19.
