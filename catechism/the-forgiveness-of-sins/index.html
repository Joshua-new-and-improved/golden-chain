The forgiveness of sins

What does the Catholic Church teach about forgiveness of sins? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 976–987

In one sentence: Christ gave the Church power to forgive sins, first in Baptism and then in the sacrament of Penance.

What the Catechism says

The Creed links the forgiveness of sins with the Holy Spirit and the Church. Baptism is the first and chief sacrament of forgiveness: it unites us to Christ and takes away all sin. But since we remain weak, Christ gave his Church the power of the keys, to forgive the sins of the baptised in the sacrament of Penance.

There is no sin, however serious, that the Church cannot forgive. No one is so wicked that they cannot hope for forgiveness if their repentance is honest. Christ wills that the doors of forgiveness always be open to anyone who turns back.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 976 The Apostles' Creed links faith in the forgiveness of sins with faith in the Holy Spirit and in the Church. The risen Christ gave the apostles the Spirit and with him the power to forgive sins: "Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven."

§ 977–980 Our Lord tied the forgiveness of sins to faith and Baptism: "He who believes and is baptised will be saved." Baptism is the first and chief sacrament of forgiveness, uniting us to Christ who died and rose. In Baptism all sins are forgiven, original and personal, and all punishment for sin. But baptismal grace does not free human nature from weakness: we must still fight concupiscence. So the Church needed, beyond Baptism, the power to forgive the sins of the baptised: the sacrament of Penance, which the Fathers called "a laborious kind of baptism" and "the second plank after the shipwreck of the loss of grace".

§ 981–983 After his Resurrection Christ sent the apostles to preach repentance and the forgiveness of sins to all nations. The apostles and their successors carry out this ministry of reconciliation through Baptism and Penance. There is no offence, however serious, that the Church cannot forgive. Christ wills that the gates of forgiveness should always be open to anyone who turns away from sin. Catechesis must strive to awaken and nourish faith in this immeasurable greatness of the gift. St Augustine: "Were there no forgiveness of sins in the Church, there would be no hope of life to come."

§ 984–987 In Brief: the Creed links forgiveness of sins with the Spirit and the Church; Baptism is the first sacrament of forgiveness; by Christ's will the Church has power to forgive the sins of the baptised, exercised through bishops and priests in the sacrament of Penance; in forgiving sins, priests and sacraments are instruments that Christ, the only author of our salvation, uses to blot out our sins.

Words to know

Power of the keys
The authority Christ gave the apostles and their successors to bind and loose, including to forgive sins.

In the Bible

  • John 20:22-23: whose sins you shall forgive
  • Matt 16:19: whatsoever thou shalt loose

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.84 a.10: Penance can be repeated as often as we fall, because God's mercy has no limit.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Were there no forgiveness of sins in the Church, there would be no hope of life to come or of eternal liberation.” (St Augustine (Sermon 213.8))
  • “Penance is the second plank of salvation after the shipwreck which is the loss of grace.” (Tertullian (On Penance 4), quoted in CCC 1446)

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Only God can forgive sins, so priests can't.
    What the Church teaches: Christ himself gave the apostles the power to forgive in his name; the priest is his instrument (§§ 976, 987).
  • Not quite: Some sins are too great to be forgiven.
    What the Church teaches: There is no offence the Church cannot forgive to someone truly repentant (§ 982).

To think about

Is there a sin you secretly think is too big to be forgiven? Read CCC 982 and believe it.
