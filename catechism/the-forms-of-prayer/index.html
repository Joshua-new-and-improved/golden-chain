The forms of prayer

What does the Catholic Church teach about forms of prayer? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2623–2649

In one sentence: Blessing and adoration, petition, intercession, thanksgiving and praise: the five basic forms of Christian prayer.

What the Catechism says

The Holy Spirit teaches the Church the basic forms of prayer. Blessing and adoration: our response to God's gifts, and the homage of the creature before the Creator. Petition: asking, beginning with asking forgiveness, and above all for the Kingdom. Intercession: praying for others, even for enemies, as Jesus does.

Thanksgiving: every event and need can become an offering of thanks. Praise: giving glory to God simply because he is, for his own sake. The Eucharist contains and expresses all the forms of prayer.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2623–2625 On the day of Pentecost, the Spirit of the promise was poured out on the disciples, "gathered together in one place". The Spirit who teaches the Church and recalls all that Jesus said is also the teacher of Christian prayer. In the first community of Jerusalem, believers "devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers". The Church's prayer is founded on the apostolic faith, authenticated by charity, and nourished by the Eucharist.

§ 2626–2628 Blessing and adoration: blessing expresses the basic movement of Christian prayer: an encounter between God and man. God's gift and man's acceptance of it are united in dialogue. Because God blesses, the human heart can in return bless the One who is the source of every blessing. Adoration is the first attitude of man acknowledging that he is a creature before his Creator. It exalts the greatness of the Lord who made us and the almighty power of the Saviour who sets us free from evil. It is homage of the spirit to the "King of glory", respectful silence in the presence of the "ever greater" God.

§ 2629–2636 Petition: the vocabulary of supplication in the New Testament is rich in shades of meaning: ask, beseech, plead, invoke, entreat, cry out, even "struggle in prayer". By prayer of petition we express awareness of our relationship with God: as creatures, we are not our own beginning, nor master of adversity, nor our own last end. Asking forgiveness is the first movement of the prayer of petition (the tax collector: "God, be merciful to me a sinner!"). Christian petition is centred on the desire and search for the Kingdom to come. Every need can become the object of petition. Intercession is a prayer of petition which leads us to pray as Jesus did; it is characteristic of a heart attuned to God's mercy. Christian intercession participates in Christ's, and knows no boundaries: "for all men, for kings and all who are in high positions", for persecutors, for the salvation of those who reject the Gospel.

§ 2637–2649 Thanksgiving characterises the prayer of the Church which, in celebrating the Eucharist, reveals and becomes more fully what she is. Every event and need can become an offering of thanksgiving: "Give thanks in all circumstances." Praise is the form of prayer which recognises most immediately that God is God. It lauds God for his own sake and gives him glory, quite beyond what he does, but simply because he is. It shares in the blessed happiness of the pure of heart who love God in faith before seeing him in glory. The Eucharist contains and expresses all forms of prayer: it is "the pure offering" of the whole Body of Christ to the glory of God's name.

Words to know

Adoration
Acknowledging God as Creator and Lord; the first attitude of the creature.
Intercession
Asking on behalf of others.

  • 1 Thess 5:16-18: in all things give thanks
  • Ps 150:1-6: praise ye the Lord

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.83 a.17: Thomas's four parts of prayer from 1 Timothy: supplications, prayers, intercessions and thanksgivings.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Rejoice always, pray constantly, give thanks in all circumstances.” (St Paul (1 Thessalonians 5:16–18))
  • “He prays for us as our priest, prays in us as our head, and is prayed to by us as our God.” (St Augustine (Expositions on the Psalms 85.1))
  • “Take, Lord, and receive all my liberty, my memory, my understanding and my entire will.” (St Ignatius of Loyola (Suscipe))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Petition is a lower, selfish kind of prayer.
    What the Church teaches: Petition expresses our creaturely dependence; Jesus commanded us to ask, and intercession participates in his own prayer (§§ 2629–2636).
  • Not quite: Praise and thanksgiving are the same.
    What the Church teaches: Thanksgiving thanks God for what he has done; praise glorifies God simply because he is (§§ 2638–2639).

Psychology connection

Different forms of prayer may affect wellbeing differently. Studies of prayer type suggest that adoration and thanksgiving are associated with greater wellbeing than petition-focused prayer alone (e.g. Whittington & Scher 2010, International Journal for the Psychology of Religion), and gratitude interventions show small but reliable benefits (Davis et al. 2016). Correlational findings like these cannot show that prayer "works" psychologically; the Christian reason to pray is God, not mood.

For catechists

Aim: Participants can name and practise the five forms of prayer: blessing and adoration, petition, intercession, thanksgiving, praise.

  1. Which form of prayer do you use most? Least?
  2. Why does asking for forgiveness come first among petitions?
  3. How does the Eucharist contain all the forms of prayer?

Activity: Teach the ACTS pattern (Adoration, Contrition, Thanksgiving, Supplication). Pray it together for ten minutes, one form at a time, with brief silence between.
