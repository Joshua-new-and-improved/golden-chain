The four pillars

What does the Catholic Church teach about four pillars? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 13–17

In one sentence: The faith is believed (the Creed), celebrated (the sacraments), lived (the commandments) and prayed (the Our Father).

What the Catechism says

The Catechism follows the great catechisms of the past and is built on four pillars:

  1. The Creed: what we believe, God's gift of himself and our response of faith.
  2. The sacraments: how that faith is celebrated and how God's life reaches us.
  3. Life in Christ: how we live, under the Ten Commandments and the grace of the Spirit.
  4. Prayer: how we speak with God, taught through the Our Father.

Faith is believed, celebrated, lived and prayed.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 13 The Catechism follows the traditional order of the great catechisms, built on four pillars: the baptismal profession of faith (the Creed), the sacraments of faith, the life of faith (the Commandments), and the prayer of the believer (the Our Father).

§ 14 Part One: those who belong to Christ by faith and Baptism must confess their baptismal faith before others. It speaks first of Revelation and faith, then summarises the gifts of God in the Creed.

§ 15 Part Two explains how God's salvation, accomplished once for all through Christ and the Spirit, is made present in the liturgy, especially the seven sacraments.

§ 16 Part Three deals with our final end, created in God's image: beatitude, and the ways to reach it, by right conduct freely chosen with the help of God's law and grace, fulfilling the twofold commandment of love in the Ten Commandments.

§ 17 Part Four deals with the meaning and importance of prayer, ending with a commentary on the seven petitions of the Lord's Prayer: the sum of all the goods we should hope for.

In the Bible

  • Matt 22:37-40: the two great commandments

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • Thomas's Lenten sermons (Naples, 1273): Near the end of his life Thomas preached on the Creed, the Our Father, the Hail Mary and the Ten Commandments: almost exactly the Catechism's four pillars. The Roman Catechism of the Council of Trent (1566) took the same shape.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Thomas preached on the Creed, the Our Father, the Hail Mary and the Commandments: knowledge of what to believe, what to desire and what to do is all a person needs for salvation.” (St Thomas Aquinas (Lenten sermons, 1273))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Catechism is a list of rules.
    What the Church teaches: Only one of its four parts is about moral life, and even that begins with grace and beatitude (§ 16).
  • Not quite: Prayer is an optional extra at the end.
    What the Church teaches: Prayer is one of the four pillars; faith that isn't prayed is incomplete (§ 17).

To think about

For catechists

Aim: Participants can name the four parts of the Catechism and see how they hold together: believed, celebrated, lived, prayed.

  1. Which of the four pillars is strongest in your parish? Which is weakest?
  2. Why does the Catechism put the Creed before the Commandments?
  3. How does Sunday Mass include all four pillars?

Activity: Draw a house with four pillars and label them. Under each, write one thing you already do and one you'd like to grow in.
