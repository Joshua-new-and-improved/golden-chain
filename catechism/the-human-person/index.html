The human person

What does the Catholic Church teach about human person? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 355–384

In one sentence: Made in God's image, each human being is a unity of body and soul, male or female, called to friendship with God.

What the Catechism says

Of all visible creatures, only the human person is able to know and love the Creator, and is willed by God for his or her own sake. Being made in God's image gives every person dignity: not just something but someone, capable of self-knowledge, self-giving and communion.

The human person is one being, body and soul together. The soul is the spiritual principle, created immediately by God and immortal; it is the "form" of the body. Man and woman are created equal in dignity and for each other. Our first parents were created in friendship with God, in a state of holiness and justice.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 355–357 "God created man in his own image… male and female he created them." The human person occupies a unique place in creation: made in God's image; uniting the spiritual and material worlds in his own nature; created male and female; and established in God's friendship. Of all visible creatures only the human person is able to know and love the Creator, is willed for his own sake, and is called to share by knowledge and love in God's own life. Being in God's image, each person has the dignity of a person: not just something, but someone.

§ 358–361 God created everything for man, but man was created to serve and love God and to offer all creation back to him. Only in the mystery of the Word made flesh does the mystery of man become clear (Gaudium et Spes 22): Christ, the "new Adam", fully reveals man to himself. Because of its common origin the human race forms a unity; all peoples are one family, bound by a law of solidarity and charity.

§ 362–365 The human person is a being at once corporeal and spiritual. "Soul" in Scripture often means human life or the whole person, but also what is most intimate and valuable, that by which we are most especially in God's image: the spiritual principle in us. The human body shares in the dignity of the image of God: it is a human body precisely because it is animated by a spiritual soul. The unity of soul and body is so profound that the soul is the "form" of the body: spirit and matter are not two natures united, but form one single nature.

§ 366–368 Every spiritual soul is created immediately by God, not produced by the parents, and it is immortal: it does not perish when separated from the body at death, and will be reunited with the body at the final resurrection. The "heart" in Scripture means the depths of one's being, where the person decides for or against God.

§ 369–373 Man and woman have been created, which is to say, willed by God: in perfect equality as human persons, and in their respective beings as man and woman. Both reflect the Creator's wisdom and goodness. God is neither man nor woman; he is pure spirit. Man and woman were made "for each other": not that God left them half-made, but for a communion of persons, each a "helpmate" to the other. In marriage they are called to transmit life and to be stewards of creation, not in arbitrary domination but as God's representatives.

§ 374–379 The first man was created good and established in friendship with God and in harmony with himself and creation, in a state of "original holiness and justice". As long as he remained in intimacy with God, he would not have to suffer or die. The inner harmony of the person, between man and woman, and between the first couple and creation, made up original justice. The sign of this familiarity with God is the garden. All this harmony was lost by sin.

Words to know

Soul
The spiritual principle in us; it is the form of the body, and it is immortal.
Image of God
What makes every person a "someone" capable of knowing and loving God.

In the Bible

  • Gen 1:26-28: to our image and likeness
  • Gen 2:7: breathed into his face the breath of life

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.76 a.1: The intellectual soul is the substantial form of the body: we are not souls trapped in bodies, but one being.
  • ST I q.93 a.4: The image of God is found in the mind's capacity to know and love God.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “What made you establish man in so great a dignity? Certainly the incalculable love by which you have looked on your creature in yourself; you have fallen in love with it.” (St Catherine of Siena (Dialogue 13))
  • “Only in the mystery of the Word made flesh does the mystery of man take on light.” (Vatican II (Gaudium et Spes 22))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The soul is a ghost trapped inside a body.
    What the Church teaches: The soul is the form of the body; together they make one human nature (§ 365).
  • Not quite: Men are more in God's image than women.
    What the Church teaches: Man and woman are equal in dignity, both created in God's image (§ 369).
  • Not quite: God is male.
    What the Church teaches: God is neither man nor woman; he is pure spirit (§ 370).

Psychology connection

Psychology often studies "mind" and "brain" separately. The Catholic view of the person as one unified being (hylomorphism) fits well with research showing how deeply bodily states such as sleep, nutrition and stress shape thought and emotion, and vice versa.

Who do you find it hard to see as made in God's image? Pray for them today.
