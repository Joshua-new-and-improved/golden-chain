The Last Judgement, the new creation, and Amen

What does the Catholic Church teach about Last Judgement, the new creation, and Amen? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1038–1065

In one sentence: At the end, Christ will judge all, and God will make a new heaven and a new earth; the Creed ends with Amen, "so be it".

What the Catechism says

The Last Judgement will reveal the full meaning of creation and history, and that God's justice triumphs over all the injustices committed. It calls us to conversion while there is time.

At the end of time the Kingdom will come in its fullness: God will renew the universe, and humanity and the cosmos will be transformed. This hope does not weaken our concern for this world but strengthens it. The Creed ends with "Amen", a word of faith and trust: so be it. Christ himself is the "Amen", the faithful witness.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1038–1041 The resurrection of all the dead, "of both the just and the unjust", will precede the Last Judgement. Then Christ will come "in his glory, and all the angels with him", and all nations will be gathered before him. In his presence the truth of each person's relationship with God will be laid bare. The Last Judgement will come when Christ returns in glory; only the Father knows the day and the hour. Through his Son, he will pronounce the final word on all history; we will know the ultimate meaning of the whole work of creation and salvation, and the marvellous ways by which providence led everything to its final end. The Last Judgement will reveal that God's justice triumphs over all the injustices committed by his creatures, and that God's love is stronger than death. Its message calls us to conversion while God gives us "the acceptable time".

§ 1042–1047 At the end of time the Kingdom of God will come in its fullness. The righteous will reign for ever with Christ, glorified in body and soul, and the universe itself will be renewed. Scripture calls this "new heavens and a new earth". For humanity, this consummation will be the final realisation of the unity of the human race; the Church will be "the Bride, the wife of the Lamb", no longer wounded by sin. For the cosmos, the visible universe itself is destined to be transformed, "set free from its bondage to decay".

§ 1048–1050 We know neither the moment of the consummation of the earth nor the way the universe will be transformed. But far from diminishing our concern to develop this earth, the expectation of a new earth should spur us on, for it is here that the body of a new human family grows, foreshadowing the new age. Earthly progress must be distinguished from the growth of Christ's Kingdom; yet such progress is of vital concern to the Kingdom insofar as it contributes to better ordering human society. All the good fruits of our nature and enterprise, we will find again, cleansed and transfigured.

§ 1051–1060 In Brief: every person is judged at death; heaven, purgatory and hell are explained; the Church prays that no one is lost; at the end of time the Kingdom will come in fullness, and God will be "all in all".

§ 1061–1065 "Amen". The Creed, like the last book of Scripture, ends with the Hebrew word Amen. In Hebrew it comes from the same root as "believe", expressing solidity, trustworthiness, faithfulness. God is the "God of Amen", always faithful. Jesus Christ himself is the "Amen", the definitive Amen of the Father's love. Our Amen at the end of the Creed repeats and confirms its first words: "I believe."

Words to know

Amen
A Hebrew word expressing firmness and trust: "so be it", "truly".

In the Bible

  • Rev 21:1-5: behold I make all things new
  • Rom 8:19-23: creation groaneth
  • 2 Cor 1:20: in him Amen

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • SCG IV.97: After the judgement, the world will be renewed: creation itself shares in the glory of the redeemed.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “There we shall rest and see, we shall see and love, we shall love and praise. Behold what will be at the end without end.” (St Augustine (City of God XXII.30))
  • “Be what you see, and receive what you are. To "the Body of Christ" you reply "Amen".” (St Augustine (Sermon 272))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Hope for heaven means we can ignore the problems of this world.
    What the Church teaches: Expecting a new earth should spur us to work for justice now (§ 1049).
  • Not quite: The world will simply be destroyed.
    What the Church teaches: The universe will be transformed and renewed, "set free from its bondage to decay" (§ 1047).

To think about

Say the Amen at Mass this Sunday as a real "yes".

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the Last Judgement, the new creation, and the meaning of "Amen", and connect Christian hope with action in the world.

  1. What injustices do you long to see God put right?
  2. How does hope in a new creation shape care for the environment and for the poor?
  3. What are you saying when you say "Amen" at Communion?

Activity: Read Revelation 21:1–5. Each person writes one thing they hope God will "make new", and one thing they can do about it now.
