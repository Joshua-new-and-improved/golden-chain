The life of man: to know and love God

What does the Catholic Church teach about life of man: to know and love God? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1–3

In one sentence: God made you to share his own happiness, and he never stops calling you to it.

What the Catechism says

The Catechism opens not with rules but with a purpose. God, perfect and blessed in himself, freely made us to share his own blessed life. That is why he is never far from anyone: he calls every person to seek him, to know him and to love him.

History did not stop that call. When humanity was scattered by sin, God gathered people into his family, the Church, and in the fullness of time sent his Son. Christ, in turn, sent the apostles to hand on what they had received, so that people could believe, be baptised and live as God's children. Those who welcome this message pass it on, generation after generation.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1 God is perfect and blessed in himself; he created us freely, out of goodness, to share his blessed life. He is near to us at every moment, and after sin he gathered humanity into his family, the Church, through his Son and in the Spirit, so that we might become his adopted children.

§ 2 To make this call heard everywhere, Christ sent the apostles to proclaim the Gospel: "Go, teach all nations." Strengthened by this mission, they went out preaching, and the Lord confirmed their word with signs.

§ 3 Those who welcome the call are urged by Christ's love to proclaim it in turn. This treasure, received from the apostles, has been faithfully kept by their successors, and all Christians are called to hand it on from generation to generation, by professing it, living it in fraternal sharing and celebrating it in liturgy and prayer.

Words to know

Beatitude
Perfect happiness: sharing God's own life. Our final end.
Catechesis
Handing on the faith so that it can be understood, believed and lived.

In the Bible

  • John 17:3: this is eternal life
  • 1 Tim 2:4: God wills all to be saved
  • Matt 28:19-20: the apostles sent

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I-II q.3 a.8: Final happiness is nothing less than seeing God as he is; no created good can satisfy the mind's desire to know the cause of all.
  • ST I-II q.2 a.8: Why money, honour, pleasure and even health cannot be our last end.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The glory of God is a human being fully alive; and the life of man is the vision of God.” (St Irenaeus (Against Heresies IV.20.7))
  • “You have made us for yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you.” (St Augustine (Confessions I.1))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: God created us because he was lonely or needed something.
    What the Church teaches: God lacks nothing. He created out of sheer goodness, to share his own happiness (§ 1).
  • Not quite: Passing on the faith is the job of priests and catechists.
    What the Church teaches: Every believer who has welcomed Christ's call is sent to hand it on (§ 3).

To think about

If someone asked you what human life is for, what would you say? How does the Catechism's first answer compare?

For catechists

Aim: Participants grasp that the whole Catechism answers one question: what is human life for? God made us to share his blessed life.

  1. What do most people around you think life is for?
  2. What does it mean that God "lacks nothing", and why is that good news for us?
  3. Who first passed the faith on to you, and how?

Activity: Ask each person to write their own one-sentence answer to "Why am I here?" before reading § 1, then compare it with § 1 afterwards.
