The liturgy: work of the Holy Trinity

What does the Catholic Church teach about liturgy: work of the Holy Trinity? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1066–1112

In one sentence: In the liturgy the Father blesses us, Christ acts through signs, and the Holy Spirit makes it all present: it is the work of God and of his people.

What the Catechism says

"Liturgy" originally meant a public work, a service done for the people. In Christian tradition it means the people of God's participation in the work of God: Christ continues the work of our redemption in, with and through his Church. It is the summit towards which the Church's activity tends and the font from which all her power flows.

The Father is the source and goal of the liturgy: every blessing comes from him. Christ, now glorified, acts through the sacraments he instituted, and he is present in the assembly, in the word, in the minister and above all in the Eucharist. The Holy Spirit prepares us to meet Christ, recalls his mystery, makes it present, and unites us in communion.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1066–1068 In the Creed the Church confesses the mystery of the Trinity and God's plan; in her liturgy she celebrates it, so that the faithful may live it. Christ accomplished the work of redemption principally by his Paschal mystery; the Church celebrates this mystery in the liturgy, so that the faithful may live from it and bear witness to it. "Through the liturgy the work of our redemption is accomplished."

§ 1069–1075 "Liturgy" originally meant a public work or service done on behalf of the people. In Christian tradition it means the participation of God's people in the work of God: Christ continues the work of redemption in, with and through his Church. It includes proclamation and charity as well as worship. It is the summit towards which the Church's activity is directed and the font from which all her power flows. Liturgical catechesis (mystagogy) leads from the visible signs to the invisible mystery.

§ 1077–1083 The Father is the source and goal of the liturgy. "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing." From the beginning to the end of time God's whole work is a blessing. In the Church's liturgy the divine blessing is fully revealed and communicated: the Father is acknowledged and adored as the source of all blessings, and the Church, united with Christ, responds with blessing, thanksgiving and the offering of her own gifts.

§ 1084–1090 Christ's work in the liturgy: seated at the Father's right hand and pouring out the Spirit, Christ acts now through the sacraments he instituted to communicate his grace. His Paschal mystery, which happened once in history, does not pass away: it participates in the divine eternity and is made present in the liturgy. Christ is present in the sacrifice of the Mass (in the minister and above all under the Eucharistic species), in the sacraments, in his word, and when the Church prays and sings. The earthly liturgy is a foretaste of the heavenly liturgy.

§ 1091–1112 The Holy Spirit and the Church in the liturgy: the Spirit prepares the Church to encounter her Lord (as in the Old Testament, which the liturgy reads); recalls the mystery of Christ (especially in the Liturgy of the Word and the anamnesis, "remembering"); makes it present (the epiclesis, the invocation of the Spirit, at the heart of every sacrament, especially the Eucharist); and makes the Church a communion, bearing fruit in a life lived in him.

Words to know

Liturgy
The public worship of the Church; literally, "a work of (or for) the people".
Epiclesis
The prayer asking the Father to send the Spirit, so that the offerings become Christ's Body and Blood.

In the Bible

  • Eph 1:3-6: blessed us with every spiritual blessing
  • Matt 18:20: there am I in the midst of them

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.81 a.7: External acts of worship are needed because we are body and soul: through signs the mind is raised to God.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “What was visible in our Redeemer has passed over into his sacraments.” (St Leo the Great (Sermon 74.2))
  • “The liturgy is the summit towards which the activity of the Church is directed; it is also the font from which all her power flows.” (Vatican II (Sacrosanctum Concilium 10))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Liturgy is just the ceremonies and rules for Mass.
    What the Church teaches: It is the participation of God's people in Christ's work of redemption, the work of the whole Trinity (§§ 1069, 1077–1109).
  • Not quite: Each Mass is a new sacrifice.
    What the Church teaches: Christ's Paschal mystery happened once for all and is made present in the liturgy, because it participates in God's eternity (§ 1085).

To think about

Next time you go to Mass, notice when you're addressing the Father, the Son and the Spirit.
