The Mass: sacrifice, presence, communion

What does the Catholic Church teach about Mass: sacrifice, presence, communion? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1345–1419

In one sentence: The Mass makes present Christ's one sacrifice; he is truly, really and substantially present; and in communion he unites us to himself and to each other.

What the Catechism says

The Mass has kept the same structure since the second century: the Liturgy of the Word and the Liturgy of the Eucharist, together forming one act of worship. The Eucharist is thanksgiving and praise to the Father, the memorial that makes Christ's sacrifice present (not repeated, but made present), and a sacrifice in which the Church offers herself with Christ.

Christ is present in many ways, but in the Eucharist uniquely: truly, really and substantially, Body and Blood, soul and divinity. By consecration the whole substance of bread and wine becomes his Body and Blood: transubstantiation. Holy Communion deepens our union with Christ, separates us from sin, builds the Church, and commits us to the poor. To receive, one must be in a state of grace; anyone conscious of grave sin should go to Confession first.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1345–1355 The Mass of all ages: as early as the 2nd century, St Justin Martyr describes the basic structure still followed: readings from the apostles and prophets, the homily, prayers, the bringing of bread and wine, the Eucharistic prayer with the people's Amen, and Communion, with a collection for the needy. Two great parts form one act of worship: the Liturgy of the Word (readings, homily, Creed, prayers of the faithful) and the Liturgy of the Eucharist (presentation of gifts, the Eucharistic prayer with preface, epiclesis, institution narrative, anamnesis, intercessions, and Communion).

§ 1356–1361 The sacramental sacrifice is first thanksgiving and praise to the Father, for creation, redemption and sanctification. It is the sacrifice of praise by which the Church sings the glory of God in the name of all creation.

§ 1362–1372 It is the memorial of Christ's sacrifice. In Scripture, "memorial" is not merely recalling past events but proclaiming God's mighty works, which become present and real. When the Church celebrates the Eucharist, Christ's Passover is made present: the sacrifice he offered once for all on the Cross remains ever present. "The victim is one and the same: the same now offers through the ministry of priests, who then offered himself on the cross; only the manner of offering is different" (Trent). Because it is the memorial of Christ's Passover, it is also a sacrifice, the sacrifice of the Church, which offers herself with him; it is offered for the living and the dead.

§ 1373–1381 The presence of Christ by the power of his word and the Holy Spirit: Christ is present in many ways (in his word, in the Church's prayer, in the poor, in the sacraments, in the minister), but "most especially in the Eucharistic species". In the Eucharist "the body and blood, together with the soul and divinity, of our Lord Jesus Christ and, therefore, the whole Christ is truly, really, and substantially contained". This presence is "real" in the fullest sense: a substantial presence. By consecration the whole substance of bread becomes the substance of Christ's Body, and the whole substance of wine the substance of his Blood: transubstantiation. Christ is present whole and entire in each species and each of its parts, as long as the species subsist. Adoration of the Blessed Sacrament inside and outside Mass expresses this faith.

§ 1382–1401 The Paschal banquet: the Mass is at once sacrifice and sacred banquet of communion. Christ invites us to receive him: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man… you have no life in you." To respond we must prepare: one who is conscious of grave sin must receive Penance before Communion. The Eucharistic fast (one hour) and bodily demeanour express reverence. The Church obliges the faithful to receive Communion at least once a year, if possible during Easter, and warmly recommends frequent, even daily, reception. Fruits of Communion: it augments our union with Christ; separates us from sin, wiping away venial sins and preserving from future mortal sins; makes the Church ("because there is one bread, we who are many are one body"); and commits us to the poor. Non-Catholic Christians: with the Orthodox, communion in certain circumstances is possible; for Protestant communities lacking valid Orders, Communion is not generally possible.

§ 1402–1405 The pledge of glory to come: the Eucharist anticipates the heavenly banquet. "O sacred banquet, in which Christ is received… and a pledge of future glory is given to us." St Ignatius of Antioch called it "the medicine of immortality, the antidote for death".

Words to know

Transubstantiation
The change of the whole substance of bread and wine into Christ's Body and Blood, while the appearances remain.
Real presence
Christ's true, real and substantial presence in the Eucharist.

In the Bible

  • 1 Cor 11:23-29: discerning the body of the Lord
  • 1 Cor 10:16-17: the bread which we break

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.75 a.4: Thomas's explanation of transubstantiation: the substance changes, the accidents (appearances) remain.
  • Adoro te devote: Thomas's Eucharistic hymn: "sight, touch and taste in thee are each deceived; the ear alone most safely is believed".

From the Fathers and saints

  • “On the day called Sunday all who live in cities or in the country gather together… the memoirs of the apostles or the writings of the prophets are read… bread and wine and water are brought…” (St Justin Martyr (First Apology 67, c. 155))
  • “Breaking the one bread that provides the medicine of immortality, the antidote for death.” (St Ignatius of Antioch (Letter to the Ephesians 20))
  • “If we really understood the Mass, we would die of joy.” (St John Vianney (attributed))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Catholics believe Christ is re-crucified at each Mass.
    What the Church teaches: The one sacrifice of the Cross is made present, not repeated; only the manner of offering differs (§§ 1366–1367).
  • Not quite: The Real Presence is symbolic or spiritual only.
    What the Church teaches: Christ is truly, really and substantially present: transubstantiation (§§ 1374–1376).
  • Not quite: Christ is only present during Mass, and receiving under one kind is half a Communion.
    What the Church teaches: He remains present as long as the species subsist, and is whole and entire in each species (§ 1377).

To think about

At the consecration this Sunday, silently pray: "My Lord and my God."

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the structure of the Mass, the Eucharist as memorial-sacrifice, the Real Presence and transubstantiation, and how to receive Communion worthily.

  1. What surprises you about Justin Martyr's 2nd-century description of Mass (§ 1345)?
  2. How would you explain transubstantiation simply and accurately?
  3. What does Communion ask of us towards the poor (§ 1397)?

Activity: Hold a "teaching Mass" walk-through, or go through the parts of the Mass with a booklet. Then spend 15 minutes in silent adoration before the Blessed Sacrament.
