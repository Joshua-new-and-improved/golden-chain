The moral law: natural, Old and New

What does the Catholic Church teach about moral law: natural, Old and New? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1949–1986

In one sentence: God's law is a fatherly instruction: the natural law written in our hearts, the Law of Moses, and the New Law of grace.

What the Catechism says

Moral law is the work of divine Wisdom: fatherly instruction that prescribes the ways leading to happiness. The natural law is our participation in God's wisdom; it is universal and unchangeable, the foundation of moral and civil law. The Old Law, summed up in the Ten Commandments, is the first stage of revealed law; it prepares for the Gospel.

The New Law, or Law of the Gospel, is the perfection of the divine law: it is the grace of the Holy Spirit given through faith in Christ, working through charity. It is expressed above all in the Sermon on the Mount and is a law of love, grace and freedom. The evangelical counsels are added to the commandments for those called to them.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1949–1953 Called to beatitude but wounded by sin, man stands in need of salvation from God. Divine help comes to him in Christ through the law that guides him and the grace that sustains him. The moral law is the work of divine Wisdom: fatherly instruction, God's pedagogy. Law is "an ordinance of reason for the common good, promulgated by the one who is in charge of the community" (Aquinas). There are different expressions of the moral law, all interrelated: eternal law, the source of all law in God; natural law; revealed law (the Old Law and the New Law of the Gospel); and civil and ecclesiastical laws. The moral law finds its fullness and unity in Christ, "the end of the law".

§ 1954–1960 The natural moral law: man participates in the wisdom and goodness of the Creator, who gives him mastery over his acts. The natural law expresses the original moral sense which enables man to discern by reason the good and the evil, the truth and the lie. "The natural law is nothing other than the light of understanding placed in us by God; through it we know what we must do and what we must avoid" (Aquinas). Its chief precepts are expressed in the Decalogue. It is called "natural" not in reference to irrational beings but because reason, which decrees it, properly belongs to human nature. It is universal: present in the heart of each man and established by reason. Its application varies greatly and requires reflection adapted to different conditions. It is immutable and permanent throughout history; even when it is rejected in its very principles, it cannot be destroyed or removed from the heart of man. The precepts of natural law are not perceived by everyone clearly and immediately; in man's present situation, sinful man needs grace and revelation so moral and religious truths may be known "by everyone with facility, with firm certainty and with no admixture of error".

§ 1961–1964 The Old Law: God, our Creator and Redeemer, chose Israel for himself and revealed his Law to it. The Old Law is the first stage of revealed law; its moral prescriptions are summed up in the Ten Commandments. It is holy, spiritual and good, yet still imperfect: like a tutor, it shows what must be done, but does not of itself give the strength, the grace of the Spirit, to fulfil it. It is a preparation for the Gospel.

§ 1965–1974 The New Law or the Law of the Gospel is the perfection here on earth of the divine law, natural and revealed. It is the work of Christ and is expressed particularly in the Sermon on the Mount. It is also the work of the Holy Spirit: "the grace of the Holy Spirit given to the faithful through faith in Christ" (Aquinas). It works through charity; it uses the Sermon on the Mount to teach us what must be done, and the sacraments to communicate the grace to do it. It fulfils, refines, surpasses and leads the Old Law to its perfection. It is called a law of love (it makes us act out of love infused by the Spirit rather than fear), a law of grace (it confers the strength of grace), and a law of freedom (it sets us free from the ritual and juridical observances of the Old Law). Besides its precepts, the New Law includes the evangelical counsels (poverty, chastity, obedience), which aim to remove whatever might hinder the development of charity.

Words to know

Natural law
The light of reason by which we know what is good and evil; our share in God's eternal law.
New Law
The grace of the Holy Spirit, given through faith in Christ.

In the Bible

  • Rom 2:14-15: the law written in their hearts
  • Matt 5:17: not to destroy, but to fulfil
  • Rom 13:10: love is the fulfilling of the law

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I-II q.91 a.2: The natural law is nothing else than the rational creature's participation in the eternal law.
  • ST I-II q.106 a.1: The New Law is chiefly the grace of the Holy Spirit, given to those who believe in Christ.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Law is nothing other than an ordinance of reason for the common good, promulgated by the one who has care of the community.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST I-II q.90 a.4))
  • “The New Law is chiefly the grace itself of the Holy Spirit, given to those who believe in Christ.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST I-II q.106 a.1))
  • “Theft is punished by your law, Lord, a law written in the hearts of men which iniquity itself cannot efface.” (St Augustine (Confessions II.4.9))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Natural law means whatever happens in nature (animals do it, so it's natural).
    What the Church teaches: Natural law is the law known by human reason, which belongs to human nature (§ 1955).
  • Not quite: The Old Testament law is abolished and irrelevant.
    What the Church teaches: Its moral precepts (the Decalogue) remain; the New Law fulfils and perfects the Old (§§ 1962–1967).
  • Not quite: The New Law is just a stricter rulebook.
    What the Church teaches: It is principally the grace of the Holy Spirit given through faith in Christ: a law of love, grace and freedom (§§ 1966, 1972).

To think about

Thomas says the New Law is first of all grace, not a list of rules. How does that change how you see the commandments?

  3. How is the New Law "written on the heart" (Jer 31:33)?
