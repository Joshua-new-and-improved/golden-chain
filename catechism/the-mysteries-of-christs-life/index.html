The mysteries of Christ's life

What does the Catholic Church teach about mysteries of Christ's life? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 512–570

In one sentence: Everything in Jesus' life, from the manger to the entry into Jerusalem, reveals the Father and is for us.

What the Catechism says

The Creed only mentions Christ's birth and death, but his whole life is a revelation of the Father: his words and deeds, silences and sufferings. All of it is for us; he lived it as our model, and he lets us live in him what he lived.

The Catechism walks through the mysteries: his birth in poverty, the Epiphany, the Presentation, the hidden life at Nazareth, his baptism and temptations, his preaching of the Kingdom with signs and miracles, the choice of the Twelve and the keys given to Peter, the Transfiguration, and his ascent to Jerusalem. The Rosary is one way of entering these mysteries.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 512–513 The Creed speaks only of the Incarnation and of the Paschal mystery, but everything in Jesus' life should be seen as a sign of his mystery. The Catechism first considers what is common to all his mysteries, then the main mysteries of his hidden and public life.

§ 514–521 Many things about Jesus are not reported in the Gospels; what was written was set down "that you may believe". Christ's whole earthly life is a revelation of the Father: "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father." His whole life is a mystery of redemption, which comes to us above all through his blood, but is at work throughout his life. It is a mystery of recapitulation: all he did, said and suffered restores fallen humanity to its original vocation. All that Christ lived, he lived for us, and he enables us to live in him what he lived.

§ 522–534 God's preparations: the Old Covenant and John the Baptist. Christmas: Jesus born in a humble stable, to poor shepherds, the first witnesses; to become a child in relation to God is the condition for entering the Kingdom. The mysteries of Jesus' infancy: circumcision, the Epiphany (Jesus manifested as Messiah of Israel, Son of God and Saviour of the world), the Presentation, the flight into Egypt. The hidden life at Nazareth: an ordinary life of manual work and obedience, a model for family life and sanctifying everyday life. The finding in the Temple: "Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?"

§ 535–540 Jesus' public life begins with his baptism by John: he lets himself be numbered among sinners; the Spirit descends and the Father proclaims him his beloved Son. In Baptism the Christian is sacramentally assimilated to Jesus. After his baptism, Jesus is tempted in the desert: he is the new Adam who remains faithful where the first Adam gave in. His victory over the tempter in the desert anticipates his victory at the Passion.

§ 541–556 Jesus proclaims the Kingdom of God, inviting everyone, especially the poor and sinners, to enter by faith and conversion; parables reveal and veil it. Signs and miracles show that the Kingdom is present and that he is the promised Messiah, though they can also be occasions for "offence". He chooses the Twelve, and gives Peter the keys: authority to govern the house of God. At the Transfiguration, a foretaste of the Kingdom, the Father, Son and Spirit appear, and Jesus speaks of his "departure".

§ 557–560 Jesus resolutely goes up to Jerusalem, ready to die there. His messianic entry into Jerusalem, on a donkey, manifests the coming of the King who will accomplish his work by the Passover of his death and Resurrection. The Church celebrates it on Palm Sunday.

Words to know

Mystery (of Christ's life)
An event in Jesus' life that reveals God and has power to save us.
Kingdom of God
God's reign, begun in Jesus and his Church, fulfilled at the end of time.

In the Bible

  • Luke 2:51-52: he went down to Nazareth
  • Mark 1:14-15: the kingdom of God is at hand
  • Matt 17:1-9: the Transfiguration

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III qq.27–59: Thomas's "life of Christ" treatise studies each mystery in order: conception, birth, baptism, temptation, teaching, miracles, transfiguration, passion and exaltation.
  • ST III q.40 a.1: Christ chose to live among people, not as a hermit, to show the truth and bring us to God.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “We must continue to accomplish in ourselves the stages of Jesus' life and his mysteries, and often beg him to perfect and realise them in us and in his whole Church.” (St John Eudes (On the Kingdom of Jesus))
  • “The home of Nazareth is the school where we begin to understand the life of Jesus: the school of the Gospel.” (St Paul VI (Nazareth address, 5 January 1964))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Only Jesus' death matters for salvation; the rest of his life is just background.
    What the Church teaches: His whole life is a mystery of redemption, revelation and recapitulation (§§ 516–518).
  • Not quite: Miracles were just tricks to impress people.
    What the Church teaches: They are signs that the Kingdom is present and invite faith (§§ 547–548).

For catechists

Aim: Participants see Jesus' whole life as revealing the Father and as something we share in, and can connect the mysteries to the Rosary and the liturgical year.

  1. Which mystery of Jesus' life (birth, hidden life, baptism, temptation, miracles, Transfiguration) speaks most to your life now?
  2. What does the hidden life at Nazareth teach about ordinary work and family?
  3. How does the Rosary help us "live in him what he lived" (§ 521)?

Activity: Map the mysteries of the Rosary (Joyful, Luminous) onto the sections of §§ 522–560. Pray one decade on the mystery of the Baptism of the Lord.
