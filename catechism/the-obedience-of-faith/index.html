The obedience of faith

What does the Catholic Church teach about obedience of faith? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 142–152

In one sentence: Faith is our personal yes to God who reveals himself: trusting him with mind and will, as Abraham and Mary did.

What the Catechism says

By revelation the invisible God speaks to us as friends; the fitting response is faith. By faith we submit our intellect and will to God, freely. To obey (from the Latin ob-audire) is to "hear or listen to". Abraham is the model: he went out not knowing where. Mary is its most perfect embodiment: "be it done unto me according to thy word."

Faith is first a personal adherence to God, and at the same time a free assent to the whole truth he has revealed. It means believing in God, in his Son (we can believe in Jesus because he is God), and in the Holy Spirit, who reveals Jesus to us.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 142–143 By his Revelation the invisible God, from the fullness of his love, addresses us as friends, to invite us into his company. The adequate response to this invitation is faith. By faith we completely submit our intellect and our will to God, giving our assent to God who reveals.

§ 144 To obey (ob-audire, "to hear or listen to") in faith is to submit freely to the word that has been heard, because its truth is guaranteed by God, who is Truth itself.

§ 145–147 Abraham is the model: he went out, not knowing where he was to go; he lived as a stranger in the promised land; by faith Sarah conceived the son of the promise; and by faith he offered his only son in sacrifice. He fulfils the definition of faith in Hebrews 11:1 and is the "father of all who believe". The Old Testament is rich in witnesses to this faith (Hebrews 11).

§ 148–149 The Virgin Mary most perfectly embodies the obedience of faith. By faith she welcomed the tidings and promise of the angel, believing that "nothing will be impossible with God". Elizabeth called her blessed because she believed. Throughout her life and to the Cross, her faith never wavered; that is why the Church venerates in Mary the purest realisation of faith.

§ 150 Faith is first of all a personal adherence of the whole person to God. At the same time, and inseparably, it is a free assent to the whole truth that God has revealed. It is right and just to entrust oneself wholly to God and to believe absolutely what he says; it would be futile and false to place such faith in a creature.

§ 151–152 For a Christian, believing in God cannot be separated from believing in the One he sent, his beloved Son: we can believe in Jesus Christ because he is himself God, the Word made flesh. And one cannot believe in Jesus without sharing in his Spirit: it is the Holy Spirit who reveals to us who Jesus is. The Church never ceases to proclaim her faith in one only God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Words to know

Obedience of faith
Freely submitting mind and will to God who reveals; literally, "listening".

In the Bible

  • Rom 16:26: for the obedience of faith
  • Heb 11:8: Abraham went out, not knowing whither
  • Luke 1:38: be it done to me

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.2 a.2: To believe has three aspects: believing that God exists, believing God (trusting him who speaks), and believing into God (tending towards him).

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Mary conceived Christ in her heart by faith before she conceived him in her womb.” (St Augustine (Sermon 215))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Faith is a feeling.
    What the Church teaches: Faith involves the whole person, intellect and will, giving free assent to God who reveals (§ 143).
  • Not quite: Faith is only a set of beliefs, or only a relationship.
    What the Church teaches: It is both, inseparably: personal adherence to God and assent to what he reveals (§ 150).

To think about

Where in your life is God asking you, like Abraham, to go out "not knowing whither"?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand faith as personal trust and free assent, modelled by Abraham and Mary.

  1. Have you trusted someone enough to act without knowing the outcome?
  2. What does Mary's faith at the Cross teach us about faith in hard times (§ 149)?
  3. Why would it be wrong to put absolute faith in a creature (§ 150)?

Activity: Trust walk: one person, blindfolded, is guided by a partner's voice only. Debrief: what made it possible to trust? Connect with Abraham (Heb 11:8).
