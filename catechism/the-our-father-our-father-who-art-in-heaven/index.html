The Our Father: "Our Father, who art in heaven"

What does the Catholic Church teach about Our Father: "Our Father, who art in heaven"? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2759–2802

In one sentence: The Lord's Prayer is the summary of the whole Gospel; we dare to call God "Abba, Father", together, and "heaven" means his majesty and our homeland.

What the Catechism says

When the disciples asked "Lord, teach us to pray," Jesus gave them the Our Father. It is "the summary of the whole Gospel" (Tertullian) and the most perfect of prayers (St Thomas). It is at the centre of the Scriptures and of the liturgy, prayed at Baptism and in every Mass.

"We dare to say": only Jesus could teach us to call God "Abba". It expresses filial trust and humble boldness. "Our" expresses a new relationship and our communion with all believers, and it excludes individualism. "Who art in heaven" does not mean a place but God's majesty and his presence in the hearts of the just; heaven is the Father's house, our true homeland.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2759–2764 "He was praying at a certain place, and when he ceased, one of his disciples said to him, 'Lord, teach us to pray.'" In response, the Lord entrusts the fundamental Christian prayer, the Our Father. Luke gives five petitions; Matthew gives seven, the version the liturgical tradition of the Church has retained. The Lord's Prayer is "truly the summary of the whole gospel" (Tertullian); "the most perfect of prayers" (Aquinas). It is at the centre of the Scriptures: the Sermon on the Mount is teaching for life, the Our Father is prayer; in both the Spirit gives new form to our desires.

§ 2765–2776 "The Lord's Prayer": it comes to us from the Lord Jesus, the master and model of our prayer. He gives us both the words of his own filial prayer and the Spirit by whom these become "spirit and life" in us. The prayer of the Church: it is an integral part of the major hours of the Divine Office and of the sacraments of Christian initiation (Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist). In the Eucharistic liturgy it sums up the anaphora's intercessions and knocks at the door of the Banquet of the Kingdom. Its eschatological character: it is the prayer of the "last days", awaiting the Lord's return.

§ 2777–2785 "We dare to say": in the Roman liturgy the Eucharistic assembly is invited to pray with filial boldness (parrhesia): straightforward simplicity, filial trust, joyous assurance, humble boldness, the certainty of being loved. "Abba, Father": we can invoke God as "Father" because he is revealed to us by his Son become man and because his Spirit makes him known to us. Praying to our Father should develop in us two fundamental dispositions: the desire to become like him, and a humble and trusting heart. "When we pray to the Father, we are in communion with him and with his Son, Jesus Christ." The Fatherhood of God surpasses human fatherhood (and motherhood), though it is their origin and standard: "no one is father as God is Father". "We must remember… and know that when we call God 'our Father' we ought to behave as sons of God" (Cyprian).

§ 2786–2802 "Our" Father: "our" expresses not possession but an entirely new relationship with God: the New Covenant in Jesus Christ. It is the prayer of the whole Church; it leaves no room for individualism; it expresses the baptised's common prayer, and calls for the communion of all Christians. "Who art in heaven": this biblical expression does not mean a place ("space") but a way of being; it refers not to God's distance but to his majesty. "Heaven" is also where believers find their true homeland: "our commonwealth is in heaven."

Words to know

Abba
Aramaic for "Father", the intimate word Jesus used.

In the Bible

  • Matt 6:9-13: thus therefore shall you pray
  • Gal 4:6: crying Abba, Father
  • Rom 8:15: the spirit of adoption

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.83 a.9: The Lord's Prayer is the most perfect of prayers: it asks for everything we may rightly desire, in the order in which we should desire it.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The Lord's Prayer is truly the summary of the whole gospel.” (Tertullian (On Prayer 1))
  • “The Lord's Prayer is the most perfect of prayers… in it we ask not only for all the things we can rightly desire, but also in the sequence that they should be desired.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST II-II q.83 a.9))
  • “We must remember… that when we call God 'our Father' we ought to behave as sons of God.” (St Cyprian (On the Lord's Prayer 11))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Our Father is just a children's prayer.
    What the Church teaches: It is "the summary of the whole Gospel" and "the most perfect of prayers" (§§ 2761, 2763).
  • Not quite: "Heaven" in the Our Father means God is far away in the sky.
    What the Church teaches: It refers to God's majesty and way of being, not a distant place (§ 2794).
  • Not quite: Calling God "Father" means God is male.
    What the Church teaches: God transcends human fatherhood and motherhood; he is their origin and standard (§§ 239, 2779).

Psychology connection

Research on attachment to God suggests that people often relate to God in ways that mirror, or compensate for, their attachment to their parents (Granqvist & Kirkpatrick; Granqvist 2020, Attachment in Religion and Spirituality). For someone wounded by an earthly father, § 2779's call to purify our images of fatherhood is pastorally important, and therapy and spiritual direction can help to heal both.

To think about

Pray the Our Father very slowly, one phrase per breath.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the place of the Lord's Prayer in the Gospel and the liturgy, and the meaning of its address: "Our Father, who art in heaven".

  1. What does Tertullian mean by calling the Our Father "the summary of the whole gospel"?
  2. What changes when we pray "our" instead of "my"?
  3. How might someone with a difficult earthly father approach praying to God as Father (§ 2779)?

Activity: Pray the Our Father very slowly together, pausing after each phrase for 30 seconds of silence. Afterwards, share which phrase struck each person.
