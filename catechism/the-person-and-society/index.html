The person and society

What does the Catholic Church teach about person and society? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1877–1896

In one sentence: We are made for communion; society is needed, but the person is its origin and purpose, and conversion of heart comes before the reform of structures.

What the Catechism says

The human vocation is to show forth God's image and be transformed into the image of the Son. That vocation has a communal character: there is a resemblance between the union of the divine Persons and the fraternity people are to build. Society is necessary, and the person is and ought to be its principle, subject and end.

Subsidiarity means that a higher community should not take over what a smaller one can do, but should support it. Inner conversion of heart is needed for lasting changes in society, though changing unjust institutions is also required.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1877–1880 The vocation of humanity is to show forth the image of God and to be transformed into the image of the Father's only Son. This vocation takes a personal form but also concerns the human community as a whole. There is a certain resemblance between the union of the divine persons and the fraternity that men ought to establish among themselves. Love of neighbour is inseparable from love for God. The human person needs to live in society; through exchange with others, mutual service and dialogue he develops his potential. A society is a group of persons bound together organically by a principle of unity that goes beyond each one of them.

§ 1881–1885 Each community is defined by its purpose and obeys specific rules, but "the human person… is and ought to be the principle, the subject and the end of all social institutions". Certain societies, such as the family and the state, correspond more directly to human nature; they are necessary. Participation should be encouraged in voluntary associations and institutions. Socialisation also presents dangers: excessive intervention by the state can threaten personal freedom and initiative. The principle of subsidiarity: "a community of a higher order should not interfere in the internal life of a community of a lower order, depriving the latter of its functions, but rather should support it in case of need and help to coordinate its activity with the activities of the rest of society". God does not reserve to himself all exercise of power; he entrusts to every creature the functions it is capable of performing.

§ 1886–1889 Conversion and society: society is essential to the fulfilment of the human vocation. To attain this aim, respect must be accorded to the just hierarchy of values, which subordinates physical and instinctual dimensions to interior and spiritual ones. The inversion of means and ends leads to unjust structures. It is necessary to appeal to the spiritual and moral capacities of the person and to the permanent need for inner conversion, so as to bring about social changes that will really serve him. Charity is the greatest social commandment.

Words to know

Subsidiarity
Higher bodies should not absorb what lower ones (families, communities) can do, but should help them.

In the Bible

  • Gen 2:18: it is not good for man to be alone
  • John 17:21-22: that they may be one, as we also are one

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I-II q.90 a.2: Law is ordered to the common good, because the person is part of a community.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “It is an injustice… to assign to a greater and higher association what lesser and subordinate organisations can do.” (Pope Pius XI (Quadragesimo Anno 79, 1931))
  • “This is the rule of most perfect Christianity… to seek the common good.” (St John Chrysostom (Homily on 1 Corinthians 25.3))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Faith is private and has nothing to say about society.
    What the Church teaches: The human vocation is also communal; love of neighbour has a social form (§§ 1878–1879, 1889).
  • Not quite: Subsidiarity means the state should do nothing.
    What the Church teaches: It means higher bodies should not absorb what lower bodies can do, but should support and coordinate them (§ 1883).
