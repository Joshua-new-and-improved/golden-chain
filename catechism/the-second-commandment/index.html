The Second Commandment

What does the Catholic Church teach about Second Commandment? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2142–2167

In one sentence: Honour God's holy name: never use it to blaspheme, swear falsely, or curse.

What the Catechism says

"You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain." Among all the words of revelation, God's name is unique; it is entrusted only to those who trust him. We should use it only to bless, praise and glorify. Promises made to others in God's name engage his honour.

The commandment forbids blasphemy (speaking against God, the Church, the saints or holy things), false oaths and perjury, and using God's name to justify crimes. Christians are baptised in the name of the Trinity and receive their own name in Baptism: every name is sacred, and God calls each of us by name.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2142–2149 "You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain." The second commandment prescribes respect for the Lord's name. Like the first, it belongs to the virtue of religion and more particularly governs our use of speech in sacred matters. Among all the words of revelation, there is one which is unique: the revealed name of God. God confides his name to those who believe in him; he reveals himself to them in his personal mystery. The gift of a name belongs to the order of trust and intimacy. The sense of the sacred is part of the virtue of religion. Promises made to others in God's name engage the divine honour, fidelity, truthfulness and authority. Blasphemy is directly opposed to the second commandment: uttering against God, inwardly or outwardly, words of hatred, reproach or defiance; speaking ill of God; failing in respect toward him in speech; misusing God's name. It extends to language against the Church, the saints and sacred things. It is in itself a grave sin. Oaths which misuse God's name, though without the intention of blasphemy, show lack of respect for the Lord.

§ 2150–2155 Taking the name of God in vain: the second commandment forbids false oaths. Taking an oath or swearing is to take God as witness to what one affirms. Perjury is making a promise under oath with no intention of keeping it, or failing to keep a promise made under oath. Following St Paul, the Church has understood Jesus' words ("Do not swear at all") not to exclude oaths made for grave and right reasons, for example in court. Oaths may be refused when required by illegitimate civil authority or when contrary to human dignity.

§ 2156–2167 The Christian name: the sacrament of Baptism is conferred "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit". In Baptism, the Lord's name sanctifies man, and the Christian receives his name in the Church. This can be the name of a saint, a disciple who has lived a life of exemplary fidelity to the Lord; the patron saint provides a model of charity and the assurance of his prayer. The "baptismal name" can also express a Christian mystery or virtue. The Christian begins his day, prayers and activities with the Sign of the Cross. God calls each one by name. Everyone's name is sacred. The name is the icon of the person. In the Kingdom, the mysterious and unique character of each person marked with God's name will shine forth in splendour.

Words to know

Blasphemy
Words of hatred, reproach or defiance against God, the Church or holy things.
Perjury
Making a false promise under oath.

In the Bible

  • Exod 20:7: thou shalt not take the name of the Lord in vain
  • Matt 5:33-37: swear not at all
  • Isa 43:1: I have called thee by thy name

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.13 a.2: Blasphemy is always a mortal sin by its kind, because it is directly against God's goodness.
  • ST II-II q.89 a.3: An oath is lawful when made with truth, judgement and justice.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “"Hallowed be thy name" is asked not as if God's name were not holy, but that it may be held holy by men.” (St Augustine (Sermon on the Mount II.5.19))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The second commandment is only about swearing (bad language).
    What the Church teaches: Its heart is reverence for God's name; it forbids blasphemy, false oaths and perjury, and casual misuse of his name (§§ 2142–2155).
  • Not quite: Christians may never take an oath.
    What the Church teaches: Oaths for grave and just reasons, such as testimony in court, are permitted (§ 2154).

To think about

When you hear God's name misused this week, silently say: "Blessed be the name of the Lord."

For catechists

Aim: Participants grow in reverence for God's name, understand blasphemy, oaths and perjury, and value their own Christian name.

  1. Why is it significant that God revealed his name (Exod 3:14)?
  2. Why might "OMG" or using Jesus' name as an exclamation matter?
  3. What does your baptismal or Confirmation name mean, and who is your patron?

Activity: Learn and pray the Divine Praises together ("Blessed be God. Blessed be his holy Name…"), traditionally said in reparation for blasphemy.
