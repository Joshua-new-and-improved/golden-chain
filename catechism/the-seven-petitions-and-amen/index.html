The seven petitions, and Amen

What does the Catholic Church teach about seven petitions, and Amen? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2803–2865

In one sentence: Three petitions for God's glory, four for our needs: hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come, thy will be done; our daily bread, forgiveness, protection in temptation, deliverance from evil.

What the Catechism says

After placing ourselves in God's presence, the Spirit inspires seven petitions. The first three draw us towards the Father's glory: hallowed be thy name (that his holiness be recognised and lived in us), thy kingdom come (Christ's return and the Kingdom's growth now), thy will be done (that we unite our will to his).

The last four present our needs to his grace: give us this day our daily bread (material bread, the Word and the Eucharist, with solidarity for the hungry), forgive us our trespasses as we forgive (the outpouring of mercy can't reach us unless we forgive), lead us not into temptation (do not let us yield), and deliver us from evil, from the Evil One. The final Amen means: let it be so.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2803–2806 After placing ourselves in the presence of God our Father to adore, love and bless him, the Spirit of adoption stirs up in our hearts seven petitions. The first three, more theological, draw us toward the glory of the Father; the last four, as ways toward him, commend our wretchedness to his grace. "Deep calls to deep." The first series carries us toward him, for his own sake: thy name, thy kingdom, thy will. The second series offers him our expectations: they concern our life, to nourish it or heal it of sin, and our struggle for the victory of good over evil.

§ 2807–2827 "Hallowed be thy name": not that we make God holy, but that we recognise his name as holy and that it be hallowed in us and by us, in our lives and actions. "Thy kingdom come": the Kingdom of God has been coming since the Last Supper and in the Eucharist; we pray for its final coming with Christ's return, while working for justice and peace now. It does not divert the Church from her mission in the present world but commits her to it. "Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven": the Father's will is "that all men be saved and come to the knowledge of the truth"; we ask that our will be united to the Son's, as in Gethsemane: "not my will, but thine, be done."

§ 2828–2837 "Give us this day our daily bread": the trust of children who look to their Father for everything; and the bread of the Word and the Body of Christ. The petition carries a responsibility: the drama of hunger in the world calls Christians to effective solidarity. "Pray as if everything depended on God and work as if everything depended on you" (attributed to St Ignatius). "Daily" (Greek epiousios, found nowhere else) means "today", "what is necessary", and "super-essential": the Eucharist.

§ 2838–2845 "And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us": this petition is astonishing. The outpouring of mercy cannot penetrate our hearts as long as we have not forgiven those who have trespassed against us. It is not in our power not to feel or to forget an offence; but the heart that offers itself to the Holy Spirit turns injury into compassion and purifies the memory by transforming the hurt into intercession. Forgiveness is the high point of Christian prayer; it bears witness that, in our world, love is stronger than sin.

§ 2846–2865 "And lead us not into temptation": we ask God not to allow us to take the way that leads to sin, and to give us the Spirit of discernment and strength. God tempts no one; we ask not to give in to temptation. "But deliver us from evil": evil is not an abstraction but refers to a person, Satan, the Evil One, the angel who opposes God. Victory over the "prince of this world" was won once for all at the hour when Jesus freely gave himself up to death. In this final petition, the Church brings before the Father all the distress of the world. The final doxology ("for the kingdom, the power and the glory are yours") takes up the first three petitions. "Amen": "So be it", ratifying what is contained in the prayer God taught us.

Words to know

Petition
A request; the Our Father has seven.
Doxology
A prayer of praise: "For the kingdom, the power and the glory are yours…"

In the Bible

  • Matt 6:9-15: the Lord's Prayer and forgiveness
  • John 6:32-35: I am the bread of life
  • Jas 1:13-14: God tempteth no man

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.83 a.9: Thomas shows the order of the petitions: first the end (God's glory and our sharing in it), then the means (bread, forgiveness, freedom from temptation and evil).
  • Thomas, Commentary on the Lord's Prayer: Thomas's Lenten sermons explain each petition and pair them with the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Run through all the words of the holy petitions, and I do not think you will find anything in them that is not contained and included in the Lord's Prayer.” (St Augustine (Letter 130, to Proba, 12.22))
  • “God does not accept the sacrifice of a sower of disunion… our peace and brotherly concord is the greater sacrifice to God.” (St Cyprian (On the Lord's Prayer 23))
  • “The Lord who has taken away your sin and pardoned your faults also protects you and keeps you from the wiles of your adversary the devil.” (St Ambrose (On the Sacraments V.4.30))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: "Lead us not into temptation" implies God tempts us.
    What the Church teaches: God tempts no one; we ask not to enter into or give in to temptation (§ 2846). (Some translations now say "do not let us fall into temptation".)
  • Not quite: Forgiving means feeling fine about the hurt or forgetting it.
    What the Church teaches: It is not in our power not to feel or forget an offence; forgiveness turns the hurt into compassion and intercession (§ 2843).
  • Not quite: The doxology ("for thine is the kingdom") is a Protestant addition.
    What the Church teaches: It is an ancient liturgical ending (Didache 8.2) used in the Mass after the embolism (§ 2855).

Psychology connection

§ 2843 matches what forgiveness research distinguishes: "decisional" forgiveness (choosing to release revenge) and "emotional" forgiveness (replacing negative emotions over time) (Worthington 2006). Forgiveness interventions show moderate benefits for depression, anxiety and hope (Wade et al. 2014, meta-analysis). Forgiveness is not reconciliation, nor staying in an unsafe situation.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand each of the seven petitions and the doxology, and pray the Our Father more consciously.

  1. Which petition is hardest for you to pray honestly? Why?
  2. How can we forgive without pretending the hurt didn't happen (§ 2843)?
  3. What responsibility does "give us this day our daily bread" give us toward the hungry?

Activity: Petition by petition: assign each group one petition. They read its Catechism paragraphs, summarise its meaning in one sentence, and suggest one way to live it this week. Close by praying the Our Father together, slowly.
