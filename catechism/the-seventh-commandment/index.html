The Seventh Commandment

What does the Catholic Church teach about Seventh Commandment? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2401–2463

In one sentence: You shall not steal: respect others' goods, the universal destination of goods, just work and wages, care for creation and love for the poor.

What the Catechism says

The goods of creation are destined for the whole human race; the right to private property is legitimate but subordinate to that universal destination. The commandment forbids theft, keeping borrowed or lost things, fraud, unjust wages, tax evasion, forgery, waste and damage to public property. Stolen goods must be restored. It also requires care for animals and creation.

The Church's social doctrine judges economic and social matters: work is a duty and a right, wages must be just, and the economy exists for the person. Rich nations have duties of solidarity toward poor nations. Love for the poor, expressed in the works of mercy, is part of the Church's constant tradition.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2401–2406 "You shall not steal." The seventh commandment forbids unjustly taking or keeping the goods of one's neighbour and wronging him in any way with respect to his goods. It commands justice and charity in the care of earthly goods and the fruits of men's labour. The universal destination of goods: in the beginning God entrusted the earth and its resources to the common stewardship of mankind. The appropriation of property is legitimate for guaranteeing freedom and dignity, but the right to private property does not do away with the original gift of the earth to the whole of mankind. "Man should regard the external things that he legitimately owns not merely as exclusive to himself but common to others also". Political authority has the right and duty to regulate the legitimate exercise of the right to ownership for the sake of the common good.

§ 2407–2418 Respect for persons and their goods requires temperance (moderating attachment to this world's goods), justice (preserving our neighbour's rights) and solidarity. Theft is usurping another's property against the reasonable will of the owner; there is no theft if consent can be presumed or if refusal is contrary to reason (urgent and essential need). Also contrary: deliberately keeping goods lent or lost; business fraud; paying unjust wages; forcing up prices by taking advantage of ignorance or hardship; corruption; appropriation of public goods; work poorly done; tax evasion; forgery of cheques and invoices; excessive expenses and waste; wilfully damaging property. Promises and contracts must be kept. Commutative justice obliges strictly and requires reparation; every injustice demands reparation (restitution). Games of chance and bets are not in themselves contrary to justice, but become morally unacceptable when they deprive someone of what is necessary to meet his needs and those of others; the passion for gambling risks becoming an enslavement. Enslaving human beings, buying and selling them as merchandise, is a sin against their dignity. Respect for the integrity of creation: animals, plants and inanimate beings are destined for the common good of past, present and future humanity. Animals are God's creatures and should be treated with kindness; it is contrary to human dignity to cause them to suffer or die needlessly, though it is legitimate to use them for food, clothing and medical research within reasonable limits.

§ 2419–2449 The social doctrine of the Church: the Church makes a moral judgement about economic and social matters when the fundamental rights of the person or the salvation of souls requires it. The economy is for man; it must be ordered to the service of persons and of the whole human community. The Church has rejected the totalitarian and atheistic ideologies associated with communism and socialism; she has also refused to accept, in the practice of capitalism, individualism and the absolute primacy of the law of the marketplace over human labour. Human work proceeds directly from persons created in God's image; work honours the Creator's gifts, and can be a means of sanctification. Everyone should be able to draw from work the means of providing for his life and that of his family. Access to employment must be open to all without unjust discrimination. A just wage is the legitimate fruit of work; refusing or withholding it can be a grave injustice. Recourse to a strike is morally legitimate when it cannot be avoided and is aimed at a proportionate benefit. Justice and solidarity among nations: rich nations have a grave moral responsibility toward those unable to ensure their own development. Love for the poor: the Church's love for the poor is part of her constant tradition; it inspires the corporal and spiritual works of mercy. "Not to enable the poor to share in our goods is to steal from them" (Chrysostom). "When we attend to the needs of those in want, we give them what is theirs, not ours" (Gregory the Great).

Words to know

Universal destination of goods
Created goods are meant for all; private property serves this end.
Works of mercy
Corporal (feeding the hungry, sheltering the homeless…) and spiritual (counselling, consoling, forgiving…).

In the Bible

  • Exod 20:15: thou shalt not steal
  • Luke 19:8: if I have wronged any man, I restore him fourfold
  • Jas 5:1-6: the hire of the labourers

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.66 a.7: In extreme necessity all things are common: taking what one needs to survive is not theft.
  • ST II-II q.66 a.2: Private property is lawful, but as to use, we should hold goods as common, sharing with those in need.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Not to enable the poor to share in our goods is to steal from them and deprive them of life. The goods we possess are not ours, but theirs.” (St John Chrysostom (Homily on Lazarus 2.5))
  • “When we attend to the needs of those in want, we give them what is theirs, not ours. More than performing works of mercy, we are paying a debt of justice.” (St Gregory the Great (Pastoral Rule III.21))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The seventh commandment only forbids shoplifting and burglary.
    What the Church teaches: It also covers wage theft, fraud, corruption, tax evasion, poor work, waste, broken contracts, and care for creation (§§ 2409–2418).
  • Not quite: Catholic social teaching is socialist (or capitalist).
    What the Church teaches: The Church rejects totalitarian socialism and also the absolute primacy of the market; the economy must serve the person (§ 2425).
  • Not quite: Helping the poor is optional generosity.
    What the Church teaches: Love for the poor is a matter of justice as well as charity: we give them what is theirs (§ 2446).

Psychology connection

§ 2413's warning that gambling "risks becoming an enslavement" matches the clinical picture: gambling disorder is classified in DSM-5-TR alongside substance-related disorders because it activates similar reward pathways. CBT has the strongest evidence among treatments (Petry, Ginley & Rash 2017, Psychology of Addictive Behaviors, review). In South Africa, the National Responsible Gambling Programme offers free counselling (0800 006 008).
