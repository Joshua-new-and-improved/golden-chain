The Sixth Commandment: sexuality and chastity

What does the Catholic Church teach about Sixth Commandment: sexuality and chastity? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2331–2359

In one sentence: Sexuality is a good gift, part of the whole person; chastity integrates it in love, and everyone is called to it in their state of life.

What the Catechism says

God is love and created man and woman in his image, so sexuality affects every aspect of the person and our capacity to love. Each person should acknowledge and accept their sexual identity; man and woman are equal in dignity and complementary.

Chastity is the successful integration of sexuality within the person: an apprenticeship in self-mastery, which is a training in human freedom. It is a moral virtue, a gift of God and the fruit of spiritual effort, lived differently by married, single and consecrated people. Offences against chastity include lust, masturbation, fornication, pornography, prostitution and rape. Persons with homosexual tendencies must be accepted with respect, compassion and sensitivity; they too are called to chastity. Chastity blossoms in friendship.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2331–2336 "God is love and in himself he lives a mystery of personal loving communion. Creating the human race in his own image… God inscribed in the humanity of man and woman the vocation, and thus the capacity and responsibility, of love and communion." Sexuality affects all aspects of the human person in the unity of body and soul: especially the capacity to love and to procreate, and the aptitude for forming bonds of communion with others. Everyone, man and woman, should acknowledge and accept his sexual identity. Physical, moral and spiritual difference and complementarity are oriented toward the goods of marriage and the flourishing of family life. Man and woman are equal in dignity. Jesus came to restore creation to the purity of its origins; in the Sermon on the Mount he interprets God's plan strictly: "Everyone who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery with her in his heart."

§ 2337–2345 The vocation to chastity: chastity means the successful integration of sexuality within the person and thus the inner unity of man in his bodily and spiritual being. The chaste person maintains the integrity of the powers of life and love placed in him. Chastity includes an apprenticeship in self-mastery, which is a training in human freedom: "Either man governs his passions and finds peace, or he lets himself be dominated by them and becomes unhappy." It requires self-knowledge, ascesis adapted to the situations one faces, obedience to God's commandments, the moral virtues, and prayer. Self-mastery is a long and exacting work, never acquired once and for all; it has laws of growth which progress through stages marked by imperfection and too often by sin. Chastity is a moral virtue, a gift from God, a grace, a fruit of spiritual effort. It blossoms in friendship, and shows the disciple how to follow and imitate Christ.

§ 2346–2350 Chastity is lived in various forms: all the baptised are called to it, according to their state of life: virginity or consecrated celibacy, married chastity, and the chastity of those who are engaged or single, living continence. "There are three forms of the virtue of chastity: the first is that of spouses, the second that of widows, and the third that of virgins" (Ambrose). Those who are engaged to marry are called to live chastity in continence, reserving for marriage the expressions of affection that belong to married love.

§ 2351–2356 Offences against chastity: lust is disordered desire for or inordinate enjoyment of sexual pleasure, sought for itself, isolated from its procreative and unitive purposes. Masturbation is an intrinsically and gravely disordered action; to form an equitable judgement about subjects' moral responsibility, one must take into account affective immaturity, force of acquired habit, conditions of anxiety or other psychological or social factors that lessen or even extenuate moral culpability. Fornication is carnal union between an unmarried man and an unmarried woman, gravely contrary to the dignity of persons and of human sexuality. Pornography perverts the conjugal act, does grave injury to the dignity of its participants (actors, vendors, the public), and immerses all in the illusion of a fantasy world; it is a grave offence, and civil authorities should prevent its production and distribution. Prostitution does injury to the dignity of the person who engages in it; the imputability of the offence can be attenuated by destitution, blackmail or social pressure. Rape is the forcible violation of the sexual intimacy of another person; it is an intrinsically evil act, and gravely so when committed against children by parents or educators.

§ 2357–2359 Chastity and homosexuality: homosexuality refers to relations between men or between women who experience an exclusive or predominant sexual attraction toward persons of the same sex. Its psychological genesis remains largely unexplained. Tradition has always declared that homosexual acts are intrinsically disordered: they close the sexual act to the gift of life and do not proceed from genuine affective and sexual complementarity. The number of men and women who have deep-seated homosexual tendencies is not negligible; this inclination constitutes for most of them a trial. They must be accepted with respect, compassion and sensitivity. Every sign of unjust discrimination in their regard should be avoided. They are called to fulfil God's will in their lives and, if Christians, to unite to the sacrifice of the Lord's Cross the difficulties they may encounter. Homosexual persons are called to chastity. By the virtues of self-mastery, by the support of disinterested friendship, by prayer and sacramental grace, they can and should gradually and resolutely approach Christian perfection.

Words to know

Chastity
The virtue that integrates sexuality within the whole person, in love and self-mastery.

In the Bible

  • Gen 1:27: male and female he created them
  • Matt 5:27-28: whosoever shall look on a woman to lust after her
  • 1 Cor 6:18-20: glorify God in your body

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.151 a.1: Chastity is a virtue because it subjects the body's desires to reason.
  • ST II-II q.153 a.5: Lust's "daughters" include blindness of mind, rashness, inconstancy and self-love: it damages judgement itself.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Through chastity we are gathered together and led back to the unity from which we were fragmented into multiplicity.” (St Augustine (Confessions X.29.40))
  • “There are three forms of the virtue of chastity: that of spouses, that of widows, and that of virgins.” (St Ambrose (On Widows 23))
  • “Only the chaste man and the chaste woman are capable of true love.” (St John Paul II (Love and Responsibility, 1960, paraphrased))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Church thinks sex is bad or dirty.
    What the Church teaches: Sexuality is part of being made in God's image, ordered to love and communion; chastity integrates it, not suppresses it (§§ 2331–2337).
  • Not quite: Chastity is only for unmarried people.
    What the Church teaches: All the baptised are called to chastity according to their state, including married couples (§ 2348–2349).
  • Not quite: The Church hates gay people.
    What the Church teaches: Persons with same-sex attraction must be accepted with respect, compassion and sensitivity, and all unjust discrimination avoided; the teaching concerns acts, not persons' worth (§ 2358).
  • Not quite: Anyone who struggles with a sexual habit is fully culpable every time.
    What the Church teaches: Immaturity, habit, anxiety and other factors can lessen culpability (§ 2352).

Psychology connection

Self-regulation and habit research is directly relevant: compulsive behaviours are better changed by managing cues and environment, building replacement habits and addressing underlying stress, loneliness or anxiety than by shame. Shame is associated with more, not less, compulsive behaviour. Compulsive sexual behaviour disorder is recognised in ICD-11 (6C72). For someone stuck in a compulsive pattern, a therapist who respects his faith, a confessor and accountability can work together.

To think about

Chastity "blossoms in friendship" (CCC 2347). Which friendships help you live it?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand sexuality as part of the vocation to love, chastity as integration and freedom, its forms, and the offences against it, presented with truth and pastoral gentleness. Many participants carry wounds or struggles here; avoid shaming language.

  1. Why does the Catechism describe chastity as "integration" and as "freedom"?
  2. How does pornography harm the viewer, the people filmed, and relationships (§ 2354)?
  3. What would make our parish a place where people with same-sex attraction feel genuinely respected and welcomed (§ 2358)?

Activity: Practical chastity plan: discuss concrete helps (accountability friends, phone filters, avoiding near occasions, regular Confession, prayer, healthy friendships, exercise, sleep). Each person chooses one.
