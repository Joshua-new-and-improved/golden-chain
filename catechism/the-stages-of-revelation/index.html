The stages of revelation

What does the Catholic Church teach about stages of revelation? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 54–64

In one sentence: God revealed himself step by step: to Adam and Eve, Noah, Abraham, Moses and the prophets.

What the Catechism says

God made himself known from the beginning, to our first parents, and after the Fall he promised redemption. After the flood, the covenant with Noah embraces all nations and remains in force until the Gospel is proclaimed to all.

Then God chose Abraham, to make him the father of a multitude. He formed Israel as his people through Moses and the Law. Through the prophets he taught them to hope for salvation, a new and everlasting covenant written on hearts. The poor and humble of the Lord carried that hope, and Mary is its purest figure.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 54 God, who creates and conserves all things by his Word, gives us constant evidence of himself in created realities. From the beginning he also manifested himself to our first parents, inviting them to intimate communion and clothing them with grace and justice.

§ 55 This revelation was not broken off by our first parents' sin. After the fall, God buoyed them up with the hope of salvation by promising redemption (Gen 3:15), and he has never ceased to show his care for the human race.

§ 56–58 After sin shattered the unity of the human race, God at once sought to save humanity part by part. The covenant with Noah after the flood expresses the principle of the divine economy towards the "nations" (gathered in their lands, languages and families). This covenant remains in force during the times of the Gentiles, until the universal proclamation of the Gospel. Scripture honours great figures among the nations, such as Abel, Melchizedek, Noah, Daniel and Job.

§ 59–61 To gather scattered humanity, God calls Abram from his country and makes him Abraham, "the father of a multitude of nations". The people descended from Abraham would be the trustee of the promise made to the patriarchs, the chosen people, called to prepare for the gathering of all God's children. The patriarchs, prophets and other Old Testament figures have always been venerated as saints in the Church's liturgy.

§ 62–63 After the patriarchs, God formed Israel as his people by freeing them from slavery in Egypt. He established the covenant of Mount Sinai and gave them his law through Moses, so that they would recognise him as the one true God and wait for the promised Saviour. Israel is the priestly people of God, the "first to hear the word of God", our "elder brothers" in the faith of Abraham.

§ 64 Through the prophets, God forms his people in the hope of salvation, the expectation of a new and everlasting covenant, written on hearts. The poor and humble of the Lord carry this hope; holy women such as Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel, Miriam, Deborah, Hannah, Judith and Esther kept it alive, and Mary is its purest figure.

Words to know

Covenant
A sacred bond of mutual commitment made by God with his people.
Protoevangelium
"First gospel": Genesis 3:15, the first promise of a Redeemer.

In the Bible

  • Gen 3:15: I will put enmities
  • Gen 9:9-11: covenant with Noah
  • Gen 12:1-3: the call of Abram
  • Jer 31:31-33: a new covenant

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.1 a.7: The articles of faith grew in number over time, not in substance but in explicitness: what was contained implicitly in the faith of the fathers became explicit.
  • ST I-II q.98 a.2: The Old Law was good and from God, a preparation for Christ.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The New Testament lies hidden in the Old, and the Old is unveiled in the New.” (St Augustine (Questions on the Heptateuch 2.73))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Old Testament is replaced and no longer matters.
    What the Church teaches: It is a real stage of God's revelation; Israel remains the people to whom God first spoke, our "elder brothers" (§ 63).
  • Not quite: God only cares about Israel, or only about Christians.
    What the Church teaches: The covenant with Noah embraces all nations, and the call of Abraham is for the gathering of all (§§ 58–60).

For catechists

Aim: Participants can tell the story of salvation from Adam to the prophets and see Christ as its goal.

  1. What does each stage (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, the prophets) show us about God?
  2. Why does the Catechism call the Jewish people our "elder brothers"?
  3. Which Old Testament woman in § 64 would you like to learn more about?

Activity: Lay out cards with Adam and Eve, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, the prophets and Mary. Participants put them in order and say what God promised at each stage.
