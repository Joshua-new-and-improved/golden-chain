The Third Commandment

What does the Catholic Church teach about Third Commandment? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2168–2195

In one sentence: Keep the Lord's Day holy: Christians celebrate Sunday, the day of the Resurrection, with Mass, rest and charity.

What the Catechism says

"Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy." The Sabbath recalled creation and the liberation from Egypt; Jesus honoured it and is its Lord. For Christians, Sunday, the first day and the day of the Resurrection, fulfils the Sabbath.

The Sunday Eucharist is the foundation of Christian practice. The faithful are obliged to take part in Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation (or the evening before); deliberately failing to do so is a grave sin, unless excused for a serious reason such as illness or caring for infants. Sunday is also a day of rest, of family, of charity and of reflection. Christians should seek recognition of Sunday as a legal holiday, while respecting others' freedom.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2168–2173 "Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy." Scripture recalls creation: "in six days the Lord made heaven and earth… and rested the seventh day." It also recalls Israel's liberation: the sabbath is a memorial of deliverance from bondage in Egypt. God entrusted the sabbath to Israel as a sign of the irrevocable covenant. Rest is commanded so that humans, like God, can be refreshed. Jesus never failed to respect the holiness of this day; he gives it its authoritative interpretation: "The sabbath was made for man, not man for the sabbath." The Son of Man is "lord even of the sabbath".

§ 2174–2178 The Lord's Day: Jesus rose from the dead "on the first day of the week". Because it is the "first day", the day of Christ's Resurrection recalls the first creation; because it is the "eighth day" following the sabbath, it symbolises the new creation. For Christians it has become the first of all days, the first of all feasts: the Lord's Day (Dominica), Sunday. Sunday is distinguished from the sabbath, which it follows chronologically every week; for Christians its ceremonial observance replaces that of the sabbath. In Christ's Passover, Sunday fulfils the spiritual truth of the Jewish sabbath and announces man's eternal rest in God.

§ 2179–2188 The Sunday Eucharist: the Sunday celebration of the Lord's Day and his Eucharist is at the heart of the Church's life. "Sunday is the day on which the paschal mystery is celebrated in light of the apostolic tradition and is to be observed as the foremost holy day of obligation." The parish is the place where all the faithful are gathered for the Sunday Eucharist. The Sunday obligation: "On Sundays and other holy days of obligation the faithful are bound to participate in the Mass." The obligation is satisfied by attending Mass celebrated in a Catholic rite either on the holy day or on the evening of the preceding day. Those who deliberately fail in this obligation commit a grave sin, unless excused for a serious reason (illness, the care of infants) or dispensed by their own pastor. A day of grace and rest from work: Sunday is to be devoted to God, family, culture, the sick and the elderly, with rest from work that hinders worship, the joy proper to the Lord's Day, works of mercy, and appropriate relaxation of mind and body. Every Christian should avoid making unnecessary demands on others that would hinder them from observing the Lord's Day. Christians should seek recognition of Sundays and holy days as legal holidays.

Words to know

Lord's Day
Sunday, the day of Christ's Resurrection.

In the Bible

  • Exod 20:8-11: remember the sabbath
  • Mark 2:27-28: the sabbath was made for man
  • Acts 20:7: the first day of the week

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.122 a.4 ad 1: The commandment is moral and natural in requiring that some time be given to God; the particular day is ceremonial and fixed by law.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Those who lived according to the old order have come to a new hope, no longer keeping the sabbath, but the Lord's Day, on which our life was raised up through him.” (St Ignatius of Antioch (Letter to the Magnesians 9.1))
  • “Do not be afraid to give your time to Christ!” (St John Paul II (Dies Domini 7, 1998))
  • “Without the Lord's Day we cannot live.” (Martyrs of Abitina (304, Acts of the Martyrs))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Christians should keep Saturday, since that's the sabbath.
    What the Church teaches: Sunday, the day of the Resurrection, fulfils the sabbath and replaces its ceremonial observance for Christians (§§ 2175–2176).
  • Not quite: Missing Sunday Mass is a minor matter.
    What the Church teaches: Deliberately failing the obligation without serious reason is a grave sin (§ 2181).
  • Not quite: Rest on Sunday is optional; productivity matters more.
    What the Church teaches: Rest from work that hinders worship and family is part of the commandment (§§ 2184–2187).

Psychology connection

Regular rest and "detachment" from work are robustly linked to wellbeing: meta-analytic work on psychological detachment shows associations with lower exhaustion and better life satisfaction (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017, Frontiers in Psychology). In the Nurses' Health Study, frequent religious-service attendance was prospectively associated with lower depression (Li, Okereke et al. 2016, JAMA Psychiatry) and lower all-cause mortality (Li, Stampfer et al. 2016, JAMA Internal Medicine), though such studies are observational.

To think about

Plan this Sunday: Mass, rest, and one act of charity.

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand the sabbath, why Sunday is the Lord's Day, the Sunday obligation, and Sunday as a day of rest and joy.

  1. How does the double meaning of Sunday ("first day" and "eighth day") help you?
  2. What makes Sunday hard to keep holy in your life (sport, work, study)?
  3. What could make Sunday a day of real joy and rest in your home?

Activity: Sunday plan: design one Sunday this month with Mass, a family meal, a visit to someone lonely or sick, and genuine rest from work and screens. Report back next session.
