Tradition and Scripture

What does the Catholic Church teach about tradition and Scripture? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 74–83

In one sentence: God's revelation reaches us through Scripture and Tradition, which flow from one source and are handed on together.

What the Catechism says

God wants all people to come to the truth, so what he revealed in Christ had to be handed on whole to every generation. Christ commanded the apostles to preach the Gospel; they did so by their preaching, example and institutions (Tradition), and some of them, with others, wrote it down under the Holy Spirit (Scripture). To keep it intact, they left bishops as their successors.

Tradition and Scripture are not two sources but two ways of transmitting one deposit of faith, flowing from the same divine wellspring. Both are received with equal reverence. Apostolic Tradition (with a capital T) is distinct from the many local theological, liturgical or devotional traditions, which can change.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 74 God "desires all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth" (1 Tim 2:4), that is, of Christ. So Christ must be proclaimed to all peoples, and Revelation must be transmitted to the ends of the earth.

§ 75–76 Christ commanded the apostles to preach the Gospel. It was handed on in two ways: orally, by the apostles, who handed on by preaching, example and institutions what they had received from Christ's lips, way of life and works and from the Holy Spirit; and in writing, by the apostles and others associated with them who, inspired by the Holy Spirit, committed the message of salvation to writing.

§ 77 To preserve the Gospel full and alive in the Church, the apostles left bishops as their successors, handing over their own teaching role to them. This apostolic preaching, expressed especially in the inspired books, was to be preserved in a continuous line of succession until the end of time.

§ 78–79 This living transmission, accomplished in the Holy Spirit, is called Tradition. Through it the Church, in her doctrine, life and worship, perpetuates and transmits to every generation all that she herself is and all that she believes. The Father's self-communication through his Word in the Spirit remains present and active in the Church.

§ 80–82 Tradition and Scripture are bound closely together: flowing from the same divine wellspring, they form one thing and move towards the same goal. Scripture is the speech of God put down in writing under the Spirit's breath; Tradition transmits in its entirety the Word of God entrusted to the apostles. So the Church does not derive her certainty about revealed truths from Scripture alone; both must be accepted and honoured with equal devotion and reverence.

§ 83 The Tradition in question comes from the apostles and hands on what they received from Jesus and the Holy Spirit. It must be distinguished from the various theological, disciplinary, liturgical or devotional traditions born in local churches over time, which can be retained, modified or abandoned under the Magisterium's guidance.

Words to know

Apostolic Tradition
The living transmission of the Gospel from the apostles, through the Church, by the Holy Spirit.
Deposit of faith
Everything God revealed, entrusted by the apostles to the whole Church.

In the Bible

  • 2 Thess 2:14: hold the traditions, by word or by letter
  • 2 Tim 2:2: entrust it to faithful men

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.25 a.3 ad 4: Thomas notes that the apostles, prompted by the Spirit, handed on to the churches many things they did not leave in writing, kept in the Church's practice.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Irenaeus listed the bishops of Rome from Peter onwards as proof that the apostolic teaching had been faithfully handed on.” (St Irenaeus (Against Heresies III.3))
  • “Some things we hold from written teaching, others from apostolic tradition handed down to us in secret, and both have the same force.” (St Basil (On the Holy Spirit 27))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: "Tradition" means old customs.
    What the Church teaches: Apostolic Tradition is the living transmission of the Gospel; customs (small-t traditions) can change (§ 83).
  • Not quite: Catholics add Tradition to the Bible as a second source.
    What the Church teaches: Scripture and Tradition flow from one source and form one deposit (§ 80).

To think about

Which do you lean on more naturally: the Bible or the Church's teaching? How does this section change that picture?

For catechists

Aim: Participants can explain what Tradition is, how it relates to Scripture, and the difference between Tradition and traditions.

  1. The New Testament was written after the Church began. What does that tell us?
  2. Give an example of a small-t tradition that has changed (e.g. Friday abstinence rules, the language of the Mass).
  3. How would you respond to someone who says "only the Bible"?

Activity: Read 2 Thess 2:14 and 2 Tim 2:2 together. Map out the "chain" of handing on: Jesus → apostles → faithful men → others.
