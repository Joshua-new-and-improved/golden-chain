We believe: the faith of the Church

What does the Catholic Church teach about we believe: the faith of the Church? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 166–184

In one sentence: Faith is personal but never private: I believe because the Church believes, and I receive the faith from her as from a mother.

What the Catechism says

No one can believe alone, just as no one can live alone. We receive faith from others and hand it on. "I believe" is the faith of each believer; "We believe" is the faith of the Church, confessed by the bishops in council and by the assembly at Mass. The Church is the mother of our new birth, and we say with St Cyprian that no one can have God for Father who does not have the Church for Mother.

We do not believe in formulas but in the realities they express. Yet formulas matter: they let us express and hand on the faith. Across languages, cultures and centuries, the Church confesses one faith, received from one Lord.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 166 Faith is a personal act, the free response of the person to God's initiative. But it is not an isolated act: no one can believe alone, just as no one can live alone. We have received faith from others and should hand it on. Each believer is a link in the great chain of believers.

§ 167 "I believe" (Apostles' Creed) is the faith of the Church professed personally by each believer, especially at Baptism. "We believe" (Nicene Creed) is the faith of the Church confessed by the bishops in council or, more generally, by the liturgical assembly.

§ 168–169 It is the Church that believes first, and so bears, nourishes and sustains my faith. Everywhere it is the Church that first confesses the Lord. Salvation comes from God alone; but because we receive the life of faith through the Church, she is our mother: "We believe the Church as the mother of our new birth."

§ 170–171 We do not believe in formulas, but in the realities they express (St Thomas: the act of faith ends not in the statement but in the reality). Yet we approach these realities with the help of formulas, which permit us to express and hand on the faith. The Church, "pillar and bulwark of the truth", guards the faith once delivered to the saints, and teaches us the language of faith.

§ 172–175 Through the centuries, in so many languages, cultures and nations, the Church has confessed one faith, received from one Lord, transmitted by one Baptism. St Irenaeus: the Church, though scattered throughout the world, carefully guards this preaching and faith as if living in one house, with one soul and one heart. Languages differ, but the content of the Tradition is one and the same.

§ 176–184 In Brief: faith is a personal adherence of the whole person to God who reveals himself; we believe in no one but God; faith is a supernatural gift; believing is a human act; faith is certain, necessary for salvation, and a foretaste of the vision of God; it is the Church who believes first and hands on the faith.

Words to know

Creed
A summary of the faith; from the Latin credo, "I believe".

In the Bible

  • Eph 4:4-6: one Lord, one faith, one baptism

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.1 a.2 ad 2: The act of the believer does not terminate in the statement but in the reality: the formulas express the truth we reach through them.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “No one can have God for Father who does not have the Church for Mother.” (St Cyprian (On the Unity of the Church 6))
  • “The Church, though scattered through the whole world, carefully preserves the faith as if dwelling in one house, with one soul and one heart.” (St Irenaeus (Against Heresies I.10.2))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: My faith is a private matter between me and God.
    What the Church teaches: Faith is personal but never isolated: received from the Church and lived with her (§ 166).
  • Not quite: Creeds and formulas don't matter; only the heart does.
    What the Church teaches: We believe in realities, not formulas, but formulas let us express and hand on the faith truly (§ 170).

To think about

Who are the people through whom you received the faith? Thank God for one of them by name.

For catechists

Aim: Participants appreciate the communal nature of faith and the value of the Church's words of faith.

  1. Why can't anyone believe completely alone?
  2. What is the difference between "I believe" and "We believe"?
  3. How does it feel to say the same Creed as Christians across the world and through history?

Activity: Pray the Creed together slowly, each person reading one line, to show how the Church's faith is carried together.
