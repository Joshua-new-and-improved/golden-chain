What the Catechism is for, and who it is for

What the Catechism is for, and who it is for? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 11–12

In one sentence: The Catechism is the Church's reference summary of the whole faith: for bishops first, then for everyone.

What the Catechism says

The Catechism is an organic synthesis of the essentials of Catholic faith and morals, drawn from Scripture, Tradition, the Fathers, the liturgy and the Church's teaching. It is meant as a sure reference point for local catechisms, which adapt its teaching to different cultures and ages.

It is written first of all for bishops, as teachers of the faith, and through them for those who write catechisms, for priests and catechists, and for all the faithful.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 11 The Catechism aims to present an organic synthesis of the essential and fundamental contents of Catholic doctrine, on faith and morals, in the light of Vatican II and the whole Tradition. Its principal sources are Scripture, the Fathers, the liturgy and the Magisterium. It is meant to be a point of reference for national and diocesan catechisms.

§ 12 It is addressed above all to those responsible for catechesis: first the bishops, as teachers of the faith and pastors; through them, to those who compose catechisms, to priests and catechists; and it will also be useful reading for all the faithful.

Words to know

Synthesis
A bringing-together of many truths into one ordered whole.
Reference text
A standard by which other catechisms are checked, not a replacement for them.

In the Bible

  • Col 3:16: let the word of Christ dwell in you richly

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I prologue: Thomas wrote the Summa for beginners, to set out sacred doctrine briefly, clearly and in order, avoiding needless repetition: the same aim the Catechism sets itself.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “He called the Catechism "a sure norm for teaching the faith".” (St John Paul II (Fidei Depositum, 1992))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Catechism is a replacement for the Bible.
    What the Church teaches: Scripture is one of its principal sources; it presents the faith in a synthesis, constantly quoting Scripture (§ 11).
  • Not quite: Local catechisms are unnecessary now.
    What the Church teaches: The Catechism is a reference point for local catechisms, which adapt it to particular people (§§ 11, 24).
