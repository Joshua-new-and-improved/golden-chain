What the Church teaches about knowing God

What the Church teaches about knowing God? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 36–38

In one sentence: Reason can know God, but we need revelation to know him easily, surely and without error.

What the Catechism says

The Church holds, with the First Vatican Council, that God can be known with certainty from created things by the natural light of reason. Without this capacity we could not receive revelation at all.

In practice, though, it is hard. Our circumstances and the effects of sin get in the way, and people readily persuade themselves that what they would prefer not to be true is false. That is why we need revelation: not only for truths beyond reason, but also so that religious and moral truths reason could reach can be known by everyone, easily, with firm certainty and without error.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 36 The Church holds and teaches that God, the first principle and last end of all things, can be known with certainty from the created world by the natural light of human reason (First Vatican Council). Without this capacity we could not welcome God's revelation. We have it because we are made in his image.

§ 37 In the historical conditions in which we find ourselves, however, we experience many difficulties in coming to know God by reason alone: the senses and imagination get in the way, as do disordered appetites from original sin. People easily persuade themselves that what they would not like to be true is false or doubtful (Pius XII, Humani Generis).

§ 38 That is why we need to be enlightened by God's revelation, not only about truths that exceed our understanding, but also about religious and moral truths that are not in principle beyond reason, so that they can be known by everyone with ease, with firm certainty, and with no admixture of error.

Words to know

Natural light of reason
Our God-given ability to know truth by thinking, without revelation.
Moral certainty
Certainty strong enough to live by, though not mathematical.

In the Bible

  • Rom 1:20: so that they are without excuse

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST I q.1 a.1: Revelation was necessary even for truths reason can reach. Otherwise they would be known only by a few, after a long time, and with many errors mixed in. The Catechism makes Thomas's argument its own.
  • SCG I.4: Why God fittingly revealed truths that reason could, in principle, discover.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Without revelation, the truth about God would be known only by a few, after a long time, and with many errors mixed in.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST I q.1 a.1))
  • “Faith and reason are like two wings on which the human spirit rises to the contemplation of truth.” (St John Paul II (Fides et Ratio, 1998))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Church distrusts reason.
    What the Church teaches: The Church defends reason's power to know God with certainty (§ 36).
  • Not quite: Revelation is only needed for mysteries like the Trinity.
    What the Church teaches: It also helps us know truths reason could reach, easily, surely and without error (§ 38).

To think about

Can you think of a moral truth people "persuade themselves" is false because it would be inconvenient?

For catechists

Aim: Participants understand both the power and the limits of reason, and why revelation is a gift even for moral truths.

  1. Can you think of a moral truth whole societies have got wrong?
  2. Why do we sometimes "persuade ourselves" that what is inconvenient is false (§ 37)?
  3. How does revelation make the truth accessible to people without time for philosophy?

Activity: List three moral truths almost everyone agrees on and three that are disputed. Discuss how revelation helps with the second list.
