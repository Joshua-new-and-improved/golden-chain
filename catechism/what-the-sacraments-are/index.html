What the sacraments are

What the sacraments are? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 1113–1134

In one sentence: The sacraments are effective signs of grace, instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church, by which divine life is given to us.

What the Catechism says

The whole liturgical life of the Church revolves around the seven sacraments. They are "of Christ" (he instituted them), "of the Church" (by her and for her), "of faith" (they presuppose, nourish and express it), "of salvation" (they really confer the grace they signify), and "of eternal life" (they anticipate glory).

They act ex opere operato: by the very fact that the action is performed, because it is Christ who acts, not by the holiness of the minister. Yet the fruit depends also on the dispositions of the one who receives. Baptism, Confirmation and Holy Orders also confer a permanent "character", so they are never repeated.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 1113–1116 The whole liturgical life of the Church revolves around the sacrifice of the Eucharist and the sacraments. There are seven: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders and Matrimony. They are "of Christ": instituted by him. The mysteries of Christ's life are the foundations of what he now dispenses in the sacraments, through the ministers of his Church. Sacraments are "powers that come forth" from the Body of Christ.

§ 1117–1121 They are "of the Church": by the Spirit the Church has recognised this treasure and determined its "dispensation", discerning over the centuries that among her liturgical celebrations there are seven sacraments properly so called. They are "by her" (she is the sacrament of Christ's action) and "for her" (they make the Church). Baptism, Confirmation and Holy Orders confer a sacramental character, an indelible spiritual seal; they are never repeated.

§ 1122–1126 They are "sacraments of faith": they presuppose faith, and by words and objects they also nourish, strengthen and express it. The Church's faith precedes the faith of the believer. Lex orandi, lex credendi: the law of prayer is the law of faith. No sacramental rite may be changed or manipulated at will by the minister or the community.

§ 1127–1129 They are "sacraments of salvation": celebrated worthily in faith, they confer the grace they signify, because in them Christ himself is at work. They act ex opere operato, "by the very fact of the action being performed", by the power of Christ and the Spirit, not by the righteousness of the minister or recipient. Yet their fruits also depend on the disposition of the one who receives them. For believers the sacraments of the New Covenant are necessary for salvation.

§ 1130–1134 They are "sacraments of eternal life": the Church celebrates them as she awaits the Lord's return, "Maranatha!". They give a pledge of the glory to come.

Words to know

Sacrament
An effective sign of grace, instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church, by which divine life is given.
Ex opere operato
"By the work worked": the sacrament acts by Christ's power, not by the minister's worthiness.

In the Bible

  • Mark 16:15-16: he that believeth and is baptised
  • John 3:5: born again of water and the Holy Ghost

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST III q.60 a.2: A sacrament is a sign of a sacred thing insofar as it sanctifies us.
  • ST III q.65 a.1: Thomas shows why there are seven sacraments: they match the stages of natural life (birth, growth, nourishment, healing, social life).

From the Fathers and saints

  • “The word is added to the element, and it becomes a sacrament.” (St Augustine (Tractates on John 80.3))
  • “The sacraments of the New Law cause grace as instruments of Christ's divinity.” (St Thomas Aquinas (ST III q.62 a.1))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Sacraments are just symbols that help us remember.
    What the Church teaches: They are effective signs that really confer the grace they signify (§§ 1127–1131).
  • Not quite: If the priest is a sinner, the sacrament doesn't work.
    What the Church teaches: Sacraments act by Christ's power (ex opere operato), not by the minister's holiness (§ 1128).
  • Not quite: Sacraments work like magic, regardless of the recipient.
    What the Church teaches: Their fruit also depends on the recipient's dispositions (§ 1128).

To think about

Which sacrament have you received most recently? What did Christ do in you through it?

For catechists

Aim: Participants can define a sacrament, name the seven, and explain ex opere operato and the role of faith.

  1. Which of the seven sacraments have you received? What did each do?
  2. How is a sacrament different from a symbol like a flag?
  3. Why do Baptism, Confirmation and Orders happen only once?

Activity: Sacrament sorting: give each group cards with the seven sacraments; sort them into initiation, healing and service of communion, and name the matter and words of each.
