Where prayer comes from, and who teaches it

What does the Catholic Church teach about where prayer comes from, and who teaches it? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 2650–2696

In one sentence: We learn to pray from the Word, the liturgy and the virtues; we pray to the Father, through Jesus, in the Spirit, with Mary; and the family is the first school.

What the Catechism says

Prayer is not a spontaneous impulse only: we must learn it. The wellsprings of prayer are the Word of God, the liturgy, and the theological virtues; we also pray "today", in the present moment, where God meets us.

The way of prayer: we pray to the Father, through Jesus (the Jesus Prayer and devotion to the Sacred Heart), in the Holy Spirit ("Come, Holy Spirit"), in communion with Mary (the Hail Mary, the Rosary). Guides for prayer include the saints and the many spiritualities of the Church; the Christian family is the first place of education in prayer; ordained ministers, religious, catechists and prayer groups help too. Favourable places include a prayer corner at home, churches and monasteries.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 2650–2662 At the wellsprings of prayer: prayer cannot be reduced to the spontaneous outpouring of interior impulse; one must also learn how to pray. Through a living transmission (Tradition), the Holy Spirit teaches the children of God to pray. The Word of God: "prayer should accompany the reading of Sacred Scripture, so that a dialogue takes place between God and man" ("We speak to him when we pray; we listen to him when we read the divine oracles", Ambrose). Lectio divina: "seek in reading and you will find in meditating; knock in mental prayer and it will be opened to you by contemplation" (Guigo II). The liturgy of the Church. The theological virtues: entering into prayer is like entering into liturgy, through the narrow gate of faith. "Today": we learn to pray at certain moments by hearing the Word, but we pray in the events of each day.

§ 2663–2682 The way of prayer: prayer to the Father (through Jesus), to Jesus himself: the name of Jesus is at the heart of Christian prayer; the Jesus Prayer ("Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner") is a simple invocation that can be prayed constantly. Prayer to the Holy Spirit: "Come, Holy Spirit." In communion with the holy Mother of God: Christian prayer goes to the Father through Christ in the Spirit, and Mary is the one who shows us the way (Hodegetria). The Hail Mary has two movements: magnifying the Lord for what he did in Mary (the angel's and Elizabeth's greetings), and entrusting ourselves to her ("pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death"). Medieval piety in the West developed the Rosary as a popular substitute for the Liturgy of the Hours.

§ 2683–2696 Guides for prayer: the cloud of witnesses (the saints, who share in the living tradition of prayer by the example of their lives and their prayer for us). Servants of prayer: the Christian family is the first place of education in prayer, the "domestic church" where God's children learn to pray daily. Ordained ministers, religious, catechists, prayer groups and spiritual direction. Places favourable for prayer: the church (the proper place for liturgical prayer), a "prayer corner" at home with Scripture and icons, monasteries, and pilgrimages.

  • Rom 8:26: the Spirit helpeth our infirmity

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.83 a.4: We pray to God alone as the source of grace, and to the saints as intercessors.

  • “We speak to him when we pray; we listen to him when we read the divine oracles.” (St Ambrose (On the Duties of Ministers I.20.88))
  • “Seek in reading and you will find in meditating; knock in mental prayer and it will be opened to you by contemplation.” (Guigo II the Carthusian (The Ladder of Monks 2))
  • “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.” (The Way of a Pilgrim (19th century, Russian))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: Prayer to Mary and the saints takes away from prayer to God.
    What the Church teaches: Christian prayer goes to the Father through Christ in the Spirit; Mary and the saints pray with us and for us, and lead us to Christ (§§ 2673–2683).
  • Not quite: Real prayer must be spontaneous; set prayers are empty.
    What the Church teaches: Prayer must also be learned; the Church transmits it through Scripture, the liturgy and the saints (§§ 2650–2651).

To think about

Set up or tidy a small prayer corner at home this week.

For catechists

Aim: Participants learn the sources of prayer (Word, liturgy, virtues, daily life), its way (to the Father, Jesus and the Spirit, with Mary), and its guides and places.

  1. What is the difference between reading the Bible for information and praying with it?
  2. How can the name of Jesus be a prayer in itself?
  3. Where is the "prayer corner" in your home, or where could it be?

Activity: Practise lectio divina together with Luke 10:38–42 (Martha and Mary): read (lectio), reflect (meditatio), respond (oratio), rest (contemplatio). Close with one Hail Mary.
