Who interprets the faith?

Who interprets the faith? · Catechism of the Catholic Church §§ 84–100

In one sentence: The whole Church holds the faith, but the bishops in communion with the Pope are its authentic interpreters, as servants of the Word.

What the Catechism says

The deposit of faith is entrusted to the whole Church: the faithful hold fast to it in the apostles' teaching, communion, the breaking of bread and prayer. The task of giving an authentic interpretation belongs to the Magisterium, the bishops in communion with the successor of Peter. The Magisterium is not above the Word of God but serves it, teaching only what has been handed on.

Dogmas are lights on the path of faith; there is an organic connection between our spiritual life and them. The whole body of the faithful, anointed by the Spirit, cannot err in belief when it agrees in faith and morals (the sense of faith). Understanding of revelation grows through contemplation and study, the experience of spiritual realities, and the preaching of the bishops.

Paragraph by paragraph

§ 84 The apostles entrusted the "sacred deposit" of faith, contained in Scripture and Tradition, to the whole Church. By adhering to it, the entire holy people, united to its pastors, remains faithful to the apostles' teaching, to communion, to the breaking of bread and the prayers.

§ 85–87 The task of giving an authentic interpretation of the Word of God has been entrusted to the living teaching office of the Church alone, that is, to the bishops in communion with the successor of Peter. Yet this Magisterium is not superior to the Word of God, but its servant: it teaches only what has been handed on, listening devoutly, guarding it scrupulously and explaining it faithfully. Mindful of "He who hears you, hears me", the faithful receive its teachings with docility.

§ 88–90 The Magisterium exercises its authority to the fullest when it defines dogmas, truths contained in Revelation or having a necessary connection with them. There is an organic connection between our spiritual life and the dogmas: they are lights along the path of faith. Their mutual connection reveals the coherence of the whole; there is a "hierarchy" of truths, since they vary in their relation to the foundation of faith.

§ 91–93 All the faithful share in understanding and handing on revealed truth; they have received the anointing of the Spirit. The whole body of the faithful cannot err in matters of belief: this supernatural appreciation of faith (sensus fidei) is shown when, from the bishops to the last of the faithful, they manifest universal consent in matters of faith and morals.

§ 94–95 Thanks to the Spirit, understanding of the realities and words of the deposit of faith grows: through contemplation and study by believers, the intimate understanding of spiritual realities they experience, and the preaching of the bishops. Tradition, Scripture and the Magisterium are so connected that one cannot stand without the others; together, each in its own way, they contribute effectively to salvation.

§ 96–100 In Brief: what Christ entrusted to the apostles they handed on by preaching and writing; Tradition and Scripture form one deposit; the Church does not draw her certainty from Scripture alone; the whole people of God, by the sensus fidei, unfailingly adheres to the faith; the task of authentic interpretation belongs to the Magisterium.

Words to know

Magisterium
The Church's living teaching office: the bishops in communion with the Pope.
Sensus fidei
The supernatural sense of faith of the whole people of God.

In the Bible

  • Acts 2:42: the apostles' teaching and fellowship
  • Luke 10:16: he that heareth you heareth me

With St Thomas Aquinas

  • ST II-II q.1 a.10: It belongs to the Pope to publish a new edition of the creed, so that the faith may be held by all as one.
  • ST II-II q.5 a.3: Faith adheres to the First Truth as shown in Scripture and as taught by the Church.

From the Fathers and saints

  • “Doctrine progresses as the body grows: developing in years, but remaining the same in nature and meaning.” (St Vincent of Lérins (Commonitorium 23))
  • “To live is to change, and to be perfect is to have changed often: genuine development preserves the type and continuity of the idea.” (St John Henry Newman (Essay on Development, 1845))

Common misunderstandings

  • Not quite: The Magisterium can invent new doctrines.
    What the Church teaches: It is the servant of the Word and teaches only what has been handed on (§ 86).
  • Not quite: The sensus fidei means majority opinion or polls.
    What the Church teaches: It is the Spirit-given sense of the whole people, bishops included, in universal agreement (§ 92).
  • Not quite: All doctrines are equally central.
    What the Church teaches: There is a hierarchy of truths in relation to the foundation of faith (§ 90).

  2. How is the sense of the faithful different from a vote?
  3. Can doctrine develop without changing? (Think of an acorn and an oak.)
