leviticus 1|Burnt offerings|God tells Moses how to offer a whole animal on the altar: a bull, sheep, goat or bird without blemish, completely burned as a gift to God.|The whole burnt offering stands for giving oneself entirely to God. Christ's sacrifice fulfils it.
leviticus 2|Grain offerings|Rules for offerings of flour, oil and incense, baked or raw. Part is burned and the rest feeds the priests. No yeast, and always salt.|Ordinary food and daily work can be offered to God.
leviticus 3|Peace offerings|An offering in which part is burned, part goes to the priests, and the rest is eaten by the worshipper in a shared meal.|It is a meal of communion with God, a distant figure of the Eucharist.
leviticus 4|Offerings for sin|When a priest, the whole people, a leader or an ordinary person sins without meaning to, a set sacrifice is offered and the sin is forgiven.|Sin damages our relationship with God and has to be dealt with, even when it was not deliberate.
leviticus 5|Guilt offerings|More cases needing a sacrifice: failing to testify, touching what is unclean, rash oaths. The poor may bring birds or flour instead of a lamb.|God scales what he asks to what a person can afford.
leviticus 6|Restitution and the altar fire|Whoever cheats a neighbour must repay in full plus a fifth, then bring an offering. The priests must keep the altar fire burning always.|Being forgiven by God includes making things right with the person wronged.
leviticus 7|More rules on sacrifices|Details for guilt and peace offerings and the priests' share. Eating fat or blood is forbidden.|Worship is ordered by God, not improvised.
leviticus 8|Aaron and his sons ordained|Moses washes, clothes and anoints Aaron and his sons before the whole community and offers the sacrifices of ordination over seven days.|Priests do not appoint themselves; they are called and consecrated.
leviticus 9|The first sacrifices|Aaron offers sacrifice for himself and the people and blesses them. The glory of the Lord appears and fire consumes the offering.|God visibly accepts the worship he himself set up.
leviticus 10|Nadab and Abihu|Two of Aaron's sons offer incense in a way God had not commanded, and fire kills them. God says, "I will be shown holy among those who are near me."|Those who serve at the altar must treat holy things with reverence.
leviticus 11|Clean and unclean animals|Lists of animals Israel may and may not eat. "Be holy, for I am holy."|These rules trained Israel to be distinct. Christ later declared all foods clean.
leviticus 12|After childbirth|A mother observes a time of purification after giving birth and then brings an offering.|Mary and Joseph kept this law when they presented Jesus in the Temple.
leviticus 13|Skin diseases|Priests examine skin conditions and decide whether a person is clean or must live apart from the camp.|Leprosy becomes a picture of sin, which cuts a person off. Jesus touches and heals lepers.
leviticus 14|Cleansing the healed|A ritual restores the healed person to the community. Houses with mould are treated in a similar way.|Jesus tells the lepers he heals to show themselves to the priest, as this law required.
leviticus 15|Bodily discharges|Rules about ritual uncleanness from bodily discharges and how to be cleansed.|Ritual uncleanness was not sin; it taught reverence for approaching God.
leviticus 16|The Day of Atonement|Once a year the high priest enters the Holy of Holies with blood for the people's sins. A second goat, the scapegoat, is sent into the desert bearing their guilt.|The Letter to the Hebrews explains that Christ, our high priest, did this once for all with his own blood.
leviticus 17|The blood is the life|Sacrifices may be offered only at the tent of meeting. No one may eat blood, because the life is in the blood and it is given to make atonement.|This prepares for the meaning of Christ's blood poured out for us.
leviticus 18|Forbidden unions|God forbids incest, adultery, child sacrifice and other sexual sins practised in Egypt and Canaan.|Israel's family life is to be different from that of the nations around it.
leviticus 19|"Love your neighbour as yourself"|A wide set of commands: respect parents, leave part of the harvest for the poor, do not steal, lie or hold grudges, treat the foreigner as a native.|Jesus names verse 18 as the second great commandment.
leviticus 20|Penalties|Punishments are set for child sacrifice, consulting the dead, cursing parents and sexual crimes.|God sets his people apart to belong to him.
leviticus 21|Holiness of priests|Priests must keep stricter rules about mourning and marriage, because they offer the food of God.|Greater closeness to holy things brings greater responsibility.
leviticus 22|Holy offerings|Priests must be clean to eat the holy food, and animals offered must be without defect.|God deserves the best, not leftovers.
leviticus 23|The feasts of the Lord|The yearly calendar: Sabbath, Passover, Firstfruits, Weeks (Pentecost), Trumpets, the Day of Atonement and Tabernacles.|The Christian year grows from these roots. Easter and Pentecost fulfil Passover and Weeks.
leviticus 24|Lamp, bread and a blasphemer|The lamp must burn continually and twelve loaves be set before the Lord each Sabbath. A man who curses God's name is punished.|God's name is holy. The bread of the Presence points toward the Eucharist.
leviticus 25|Sabbath year and jubilee|Every seventh year the land rests. Every fiftieth year slaves go free and land returns to its original family.|The land belongs to God. Jesus announces his mission as a year of the Lord's favour.
leviticus 26|Blessings and warnings|Obedience will bring rain, harvest, peace and God's presence. Disobedience will bring disease, defeat and exile. Yet God will not forget his covenant.|Even God's punishments aim at bringing his people back.
leviticus 27|Vows and tithes|Rules for people, animals, houses and land dedicated to God, and for the tenth of all produce.|A promise made to God must be kept.
numbers 1|The first census|At Sinai Moses counts the men able to fight, tribe by tribe: over six hundred thousand. The Levites are counted separately for the tabernacle.|God knows his people by name and number.
numbers 2|The camp|Each tribe camps under its banner on one of the four sides, with the tabernacle in the middle.|God is at the centre of his people's life.
numbers 3|The Levites|The Levites are given to Aaron to serve the tabernacle, taking the place of Israel's firstborn sons.|Some are set apart for God's service on behalf of everyone.
numbers 4|Carrying the holy things|The three Levite clans receive their tasks for packing and carrying the tabernacle and its furnishings.|Every task in God's service has dignity.
numbers 5|Purity in the camp|The unclean are placed outside the camp, wrongs must be repaid, and a ritual is given for a husband who suspects his wife.|God lives in the camp, so its life must be clean and just.
numbers 6|The Nazirite vow and the blessing|Rules for those who vow special dedication to God: no wine, no cutting the hair. Then the priestly blessing: "The Lord bless you and keep you."|Samson and John the Baptist lived something like this vow. The blessing is still used today.
numbers 7|Gifts from the leaders|For twelve days the leader of each tribe brings identical offerings for the altar. Moses hears God's voice from above the ark.|Every tribe has an equal share in worship.
numbers 8|Lamps and Levites|Aaron sets up the seven lamps. The Levites are cleansed and presented to God for service.|Those who serve must first be purified.
numbers 9|The second Passover and the cloud|Those who were unclean or travelling may keep Passover a month later. The people move only when the cloud lifts from the tabernacle.|Israel learns to follow God's lead day by day.
numbers 10|Leaving Sinai|Two silver trumpets signal the camp. After nearly a year at Sinai, Israel sets out in order behind the ark.|The journey to the promised land resumes.
numbers 11|Complaints and quail|The people weep for the food of Egypt. Moses is worn out, and God shares his spirit with seventy elders. Quail arrive, followed by a plague.|Ingratitude makes slavery look attractive. Moses wishes all God's people were prophets.
numbers 12|Miriam and Aaron against Moses|They criticise Moses out of jealousy. God defends him as the one he speaks with face to face. Miriam is struck with leprosy, and Moses prays for her.|Moses is called the meekest of men. He prays for those who attack him.
numbers 13|The spies|Twelve men scout Canaan and return with huge grapes. Ten say the people there are giants and cannot be beaten. Caleb says, "We can do it."|Fear exaggerates the obstacles and forgets God.
numbers 14|Forty years|The people refuse to enter and want to go back to Egypt. Moses pleads, and God forgives, but that generation will die in the desert. Only Caleb and Joshua will enter.|Unbelief costs a whole generation the promise.
numbers 15|Offerings and tassels|Laws for offerings once in the land. A man who breaks the Sabbath is punished. The people wear tassels to remember the commandments.|We need visible reminders to keep God's word in mind.
numbers 16|Korah's rebellion|Korah and 250 leaders claim the priesthood for everyone. The earth swallows the rebels. When the people still complain, a plague begins and Aaron stops it with incense.|Sacred office is given by God, not seized.
numbers 17|Aaron's staff blossoms|Twelve staffs are placed before the ark. Aaron's alone buds, flowers and bears almonds.|God himself confirms whom he has chosen as priest.
numbers 18|Duties and support of priests|Priests and Levites are responsible for the sanctuary. They receive no land; the tithes are their share.|"I am your portion," God tells them. Those who serve the altar live from it.
numbers 19|The red heifer|The ashes of a red cow, mixed with water, cleanse anyone who has touched a dead body.|Hebrews says that if this purified the body, Christ's blood purifies the conscience far more.
numbers 20|Water from the rock; Aaron dies|Miriam dies. The people quarrel over water, and Moses strikes the rock twice in anger instead of speaking to it. He and Aaron will not enter the land. Aaron dies on Mount Hor.|Even Moses is held to account. Leaders must show God's holiness.
numbers 21|The bronze serpent|Poisonous snakes bite the complaining people. Moses lifts a bronze serpent on a pole, and whoever looks at it lives. Israel defeats two kings.|Jesus says the Son of Man must be lifted up in the same way.
numbers 22|Balaam and the donkey|King Balak hires the seer Balaam to curse Israel. On the road an angel blocks the way, and Balaam's donkey speaks to him.|No one can curse those God has blessed.
numbers 23|Balaam blesses Israel|Twice Balak sets up altars for a curse, and twice Balaam can only speak blessing.|"God is not man, that he should lie." His word stands.
numbers 24|A star from Jacob|Balaam blesses Israel a third time and foretells, "A star shall come out of Jacob, and a sceptre shall rise out of Israel."|Christians have long read this as pointing to the Messiah and linked it with the star of the Magi.
numbers 25|Sin at Peor|Israelite men are drawn by Moabite women into immorality and the worship of Baal. A plague kills thousands until Phinehas acts.|What enemies could not do from outside, temptation does from inside.
numbers 26|The second census|A new generation is counted on the plains of Moab. Of those counted at Sinai, only Caleb and Joshua remain.|God's word about the desert generation has come true.
numbers 27|Zelophehad's daughters; Joshua|Five sisters with no brother ask for their father's inheritance, and God grants it. Moses lays hands on Joshua as his successor.|God provides for the overlooked and for the future of his people.
numbers 28|Daily and festival offerings|The offerings for every morning and evening, each Sabbath, each new moon, Passover and Weeks.|Worship gives rhythm to time.
numbers 29|Feasts of the seventh month|Offerings for the feast of Trumpets, the Day of Atonement and the eight days of Tabernacles.|The year itself is offered back to God.
numbers 30|Vows|A man must keep his vow. Rules are given for vows made by women within a household.|Words spoken to God are binding.
numbers 31|War with Midian|Israel defeats Midian, which had led it into sin at Peor. The plunder is purified and divided.|A hard chapter. It shows how seriously the Old Testament treats what leads the people away from God.
numbers 32|Tribes east of the Jordan|Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh ask to settle east of the river. Moses agrees on condition that they first help their brothers conquer Canaan.|No one should rest while their brothers are still fighting.
numbers 33|The journey reviewed|A list of every camp from Egypt to the plains of Moab. God commands Israel to drive out the Canaanites and destroy their idols.|Looking back, Israel can see God's guidance at every stage.
numbers 34|Borders of the land|God sets the boundaries of Canaan and names a leader from each tribe to divide it.|The promise becomes concrete: real land with real borders.
numbers 35|Cities of refuge|The Levites receive forty-eight towns. Six are places where someone who kills by accident can be safe until trial.|Justice must distinguish accident from murder.
numbers 36|Keeping the inheritance|Daughters who inherit must marry within their own tribe so that each tribe keeps its land.|Each family's share in the promise is protected.
deuteronomy 1|Moses looks back|On the edge of the promised land, Moses begins his farewell speeches. He recalls leaving Sinai, the spies, and the people's refusal to enter.|Remembering past failure is meant to prevent repeating it.
deuteronomy 2|Years in the wilderness|Moses recalls the long wandering and how God told Israel to pass peacefully by Edom, Moab and Ammon, then gave victory over King Sihon.|"These forty years the Lord your God has been with you; you have lacked nothing."
deuteronomy 3|Victory over Og|Israel defeats Og of Bashan, and the land east of the Jordan is shared out. Moses begs to enter the land, but God lets him only see it.|Moses accepts God's no and prepares Joshua to finish the work.
deuteronomy 4|Listen and obey|Moses urges Israel to keep the law without adding or taking away, and never to make an idol. No other nation has a god so near.|Israel's wisdom is its closeness to God.
deuteronomy 5|The Ten Commandments again|Moses repeats the commandments given at Sinai to the new generation.|Each generation must receive the covenant for itself.
deuteronomy 6|Hear, O Israel|"The Lord our God is one Lord. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul and strength." Teach these words to your children and keep them always before you.|Jesus calls this the greatest commandment. Faith is handed on at home.
deuteronomy 7|A people set apart|Israel must not adopt the gods of Canaan. God chose them not because they were numerous but because he loved them.|God's choice is pure gift.
deuteronomy 8|Do not forget|God let Israel hunger and fed them with manna to teach that man does not live by bread alone. When they are prosperous they must not say, "My own power got me this."|Jesus quotes this chapter against the devil. Comfort is a greater test than hardship.
deuteronomy 9|Not because you are good|Israel is not given the land for its own righteousness. Moses recalls the golden calf and his forty days of pleading.|Grace is not earned. Moses stands in the gap for a stubborn people.
deuteronomy 10|What God asks|New tablets are made. What does the Lord require? To fear him, walk in his ways, love and serve him with all your heart, and love the stranger.|God looks for love from the heart, not only outward observance.
deuteronomy 11|Blessing or curse|Love God and keep his commands, and the land will have rain and harvest. Moses sets before the people a blessing and a curse.|Our choices have real consequences.
deuteronomy 12|One place of worship|Israel must destroy the pagan shrines and offer sacrifice only at the place God will choose.|Worship is given by God, not designed to suit ourselves.
deuteronomy 13|Guard against idolatry|If a prophet, a relative or a whole town urges the worship of other gods, Israel must not listen, even if signs come true.|A miracle does not prove a message. Loyalty to God comes first.
deuteronomy 14|Food laws and tithes|Clean and unclean animals are listed again. Every third year the tithe goes to the Levite, the foreigner, the orphan and the widow.|Generosity to the poor is built into the law.
deuteronomy 15|Cancelling debts|Every seventh year debts are released and Hebrew servants freed. "Open your hand wide to your brother, to the poor."|God wants no permanent underclass among his people.
deuteronomy 16|Three feasts and fair judges|Keep Passover, Weeks and Tabernacles at the sanctuary. Appoint judges who take no bribes.|"Justice, and only justice, you shall follow."
deuteronomy 17|Courts and kings|Hard cases go to the priests and the judge. A future king must not pile up horses, wives or gold, and must read God's law all his life.|The ruler is under God's law, not above it.
deuteronomy 18|A prophet like Moses|Israel must not practise divination or consult the dead. God will raise up a prophet like Moses and put his words in his mouth.|The New Testament sees this promise fulfilled in Jesus.
deuteronomy 19|Refuge and witnesses|Cities of refuge protect those who kill by accident. No one may be convicted on the word of a single witness.|Careful evidence protects the innocent.
deuteronomy 20|Rules for war|Before battle the priest encourages the army, and those who are newly married or afraid are sent home. Peace must be offered first.|Even war is placed under moral limits.
deuteronomy 21|Assorted laws|Rules for an unsolved murder, captive women, the rights of the firstborn, and a rebellious son. A hanged man's body must be buried the same day.|Paul applies the last law to Christ, who bore the curse for us on the cross.
deuteronomy 22|Care and purity|Return lost property, help a fallen animal, build a rail around your roof. Laws protect marriage and punish sexual violence.|Love of neighbour is spelled out in practical detail.
deuteronomy 23|The assembly and other laws|Who may enter the assembly, cleanliness in camp, shelter for escaped slaves, no interest on loans to a brother, keeping vows.|Holiness touches every corner of life.
deuteronomy 24|Protecting the weak|Rules on divorce, pledges and wages. Pay the labourer before sunset, and leave the gleanings for the foreigner, the orphan and the widow.|Jesus says the divorce rule was allowed because of hard hearts. God defends workers and the poor.
deuteronomy 25|Fairness|Punishment must be limited. A brother should raise children for a dead brother's name. Use honest weights and measures.|Honesty in business is a religious duty.
deuteronomy 26|Firstfruits|The Israelite brings the first of his harvest and recites the story: "A wandering Aramean was my father... the Lord brought us out of Egypt."|Giving thanks means remembering what God has done.
deuteronomy 27|The altar on Mount Ebal|After crossing the Jordan the people are to write the law on stones. The Levites pronounce twelve curses on secret sins, and the people answer "Amen".|The people publicly accept responsibility for the covenant.
deuteronomy 28|Blessings and curses|Obedience will bring blessing in city and field. Disobedience will bring disease, defeat, siege and exile, described at length.|The later history of Israel, including the exile, is read in the light of this chapter.
deuteronomy 29|The covenant in Moab|Moses gathers everyone, from leaders to water carriers, and renews the covenant, warning of what will happen if they turn away.|The covenant includes those "not here today": future generations.
deuteronomy 30|Choose life|If Israel is scattered and returns to God, he will gather them. The command is not too hard or far off. "I set before you life and death; choose life."|God respects our freedom and urges us to choose well.
deuteronomy 31|Joshua takes over|Moses, aged 120, hands leadership to Joshua: "Be strong and courageous." The law is to be read aloud to all the people every seven years.|God's work goes on after any one leader.
deuteronomy 32|The song of Moses|Moses sings of God the Rock, faithful and just, and of a people who grew fat and forgot him. God will judge and then have compassion.|The song is a witness for the future: God stays faithful when his people do not.
deuteronomy 33|Moses blesses the tribes|Before dying, Moses gives a blessing to each tribe. "The eternal God is your dwelling place, and underneath are the everlasting arms."|He ends with blessing, not reproach.
deuteronomy 34|The death of Moses|Moses climbs Mount Nebo, and God shows him the whole land. He dies there, and Israel mourns thirty days. No prophet like him has arisen since.|Moses sees the promise from afar. Joshua, whose name is the same as "Jesus", leads the people in.
