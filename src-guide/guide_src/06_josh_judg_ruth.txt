joshua 1|Be strong and courageous|After Moses' death God tells Joshua to lead the people across the Jordan and promises, "I will be with you." Joshua is to meditate on the law day and night. The people pledge to obey him.|Courage comes from God's presence, not from Joshua's own strength.
joshua 2|Rahab and the spies|Two spies enter Jericho and are hidden by Rahab, a prostitute, who confesses that Israel's God is God of heaven and earth. She is promised safety and hangs a scarlet cord from her window.|A pagan woman with a bad past is saved by faith. She appears in the family tree of Jesus.
joshua 3|Crossing the Jordan|The priests carry the ark into the flooded river. The water stops, and all Israel crosses on dry ground.|God repeats for a new generation what he did at the Red Sea.
joshua 4|Twelve stones|One man from each tribe carries a stone from the riverbed to build a memorial at Gilgal.|"When your children ask, What do these stones mean?" Faith is passed on by telling the story.
joshua 5|Circumcision and Passover|The men born in the desert are circumcised. Israel keeps Passover, eats the produce of the land, and the manna stops. Joshua meets the commander of the Lord's army.|The desert years are over. Joshua learns that the battle is God's.
joshua 6|The fall of Jericho|Israel marches around Jericho for seven days with the ark and trumpets. At the shout the walls collapse. Rahab and her family are spared.|The city falls to obedience and worship, not to siege engines.
joshua 7|Achan's sin|Israel is beaten at the small town of Ai. The cause is found: Achan secretly kept plunder devoted to God. He confesses and is punished.|One person's hidden sin harms the whole community.
joshua 8|Ai taken; the law read|This time Israel takes Ai by an ambush. Joshua builds an altar on Mount Ebal and reads the whole law aloud to everyone, including women, children and foreigners.|Victory is followed by renewing the covenant.
joshua 9|Tricked by the Gibeonites|The people of Gibeon pretend to come from far away, wearing worn-out clothes, and win a treaty. Israel did not ask the Lord. The oath is kept, and they become servants.|Decisions made without prayer bring trouble. A promise still has to be kept.
joshua 10|The sun stands still|Five kings attack Gibeon. Joshua marches all night to help, God sends hailstones, and the day is lengthened at Joshua's prayer. The south is conquered.|"The Lord fought for Israel."
joshua 11|The north conquered|A coalition of northern kings is defeated at the waters of Merom, and Hazor is burned.|Joshua left nothing undone of all that the Lord commanded Moses.
joshua 12|The defeated kings|A list of the kings conquered east and west of the Jordan, thirty-one in Canaan.|A record of promises kept.
joshua 13|Land still to be taken|Joshua is old and much remains unconquered. The land east of the Jordan is described. The tribe of Levi receives no territory.|The Lord himself is Levi's inheritance.
joshua 14|Caleb's reward|At eighty-five Caleb asks for the hill country he scouted forty-five years earlier, giants and all, and receives Hebron.|Caleb "wholly followed the Lord", and his faith has not aged.
joshua 15|Judah's territory|The borders and towns of the tribe of Judah. Caleb gives his daughter springs of water.|Judah, the tribe of David and of Christ, receives its place.
joshua 16|Ephraim's territory|The land given to Joseph's son Ephraim. They fail to drive out the Canaanites of Gezer.|Half-finished obedience will cause problems later.
joshua 17|Manasseh's territory|The land of Manasseh, including the share of Zelophehad's daughters. The tribe complains that it needs more room, and Joshua tells them to clear the forest.|God's gifts still require effort.
joshua 18|The tent at Shiloh|The tabernacle is set up at Shiloh. Seven tribes still have no land, so Joshua has it surveyed and casts lots. Benjamin receives its share.|"How long will you put off taking possession?"
joshua 19|The remaining tribes|Land is allotted to Simeon, Zebulun, Issachar, Asher, Naphtali and Dan. Joshua receives his own town last.|The leader takes his share after everyone else.
joshua 20|Cities of refuge|Six towns are named where a person who kills by accident can find safety.|Mercy and justice are built into the settlement of the land.
joshua 21|Towns for the Levites|The Levites receive forty-eight towns spread among all the tribes.|"Not one of all the good promises the Lord had made failed; all came to pass."
joshua 22|The altar by the Jordan|The eastern tribes go home and build a large altar. The others prepare for war, thinking it is rebellion, until they learn it is a memorial of shared faith.|Ask before you assume the worst of your brothers.
joshua 23|Joshua's farewell|Old Joshua reminds the leaders that God fought for them and warns them not to mix with the nations' gods.|"Not one word has failed." God's faithfulness calls for ours.
joshua 24|Choose whom you will serve|At Shechem Joshua retells Israel's history and calls for a decision. "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." The people renew the covenant. Joshua dies.|Faith is a choice each generation has to make.
judges 1|An unfinished conquest|After Joshua's death the tribes take some territory but leave many Canaanites in place.|Small compromises open the door to bigger ones.
judges 2|The cycle begins|A new generation grows up that does not know the Lord. They serve the Baals, fall to enemies, cry out, and God raises judges to save them. Then they fall again.|This pattern of sin, suffering, cry and rescue repeats through the whole book.
judges 3|Othniel, Ehud and Shamgar|Othniel defeats a king from Mesopotamia. Left-handed Ehud kills Eglon, the fat king of Moab, with a hidden dagger.|God uses unlikely rescuers.
judges 4|Deborah and Barak|The prophetess Deborah judges Israel and sends Barak against Sisera's iron chariots. Sisera flees and is killed by Jael with a tent peg.|Two women carry the day when the men hesitate.
judges 5|The song of Deborah|A victory song praising God, the tribes who came to fight, and Jael, and rebuking those who stayed home.|One of the oldest poems in the Bible. Victory is turned into praise.
judges 6|Gideon called|Midianite raiders ruin Israel. An angel calls Gideon, the least in his family, a "mighty warrior". He tears down his father's altar to Baal and asks for a sign with a fleece.|God sees what a person can become with his help.
judges 7|Three hundred men|God cuts Gideon's army from thirty-two thousand to three hundred, so that Israel cannot boast. With trumpets, jars and torches they throw Midian into panic.|God saves through weakness so that the credit is his.
judges 8|Gideon's later years|Gideon pursues the kings and refuses to be made king: "The Lord will rule over you." But he makes a golden ephod that becomes a snare.|A good leader can end badly.
judges 9|Abimelech|Gideon's son Abimelech murders his seventy brothers and makes himself king. Jotham tells a fable of trees choosing a bramble as king. Abimelech dies when a woman drops a millstone on him.|Power seized by violence destroys itself.
judges 10|Israel cries out|After two minor judges, Israel serves many gods and is crushed by Ammon. They cry out, and God says, "Go and cry to the gods you have chosen." They put the idols away.|Real repentance gets rid of the idols.
judges 11|Jephthah's vow|Jephthah, an outcast, defeats Ammon. He had rashly vowed to offer whatever first came out of his house, and it is his only daughter.|A terrible warning against rash vows. God had forbidden human sacrifice.
judges 12|Shibboleth|Ephraim quarrels with Jephthah, and civil war follows. Fugitives are identified by how they pronounce "Shibboleth". Three minor judges follow.|Israel is now turning on itself.
judges 13|Samson's birth announced|An angel tells a barren woman she will bear a son who must be a Nazirite from birth. Samson is born.|God prepares a deliverer even when the people have not asked.
judges 14|Samson's riddle|Samson wants a Philistine wife. He kills a lion with bare hands, later finds honey in its carcass, and poses a riddle at his wedding. His bride betrays the answer.|Samson has great strength and little self-control.
judges 15|Foxes and a jawbone|Samson burns the Philistines' fields with torches tied to foxes and kills a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone.|God uses even this flawed man against Israel's oppressors.
judges 16|Samson and Delilah|Delilah coaxes out the secret of Samson's strength. His hair is cut, he is blinded and enslaved. At a pagan feast he prays, pushes down the pillars, and dies with his enemies.|Samson's fall comes from playing with temptation. God hears his last prayer.
judges 17|Micah's idol|A man named Micah makes a silver idol, sets up a private shrine and hires a wandering Levite as his priest.|"Everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Religion made to order is idolatry.
judges 18|The tribe of Dan|The Danites, looking for land, steal Micah's idol and priest and set up their own shrine in the north.|False worship spreads from one house to a whole tribe.
judges 19|The crime at Gibeah|A Levite's concubine is abused all night by men of Gibeah in Benjamin and dies. He sends pieces of her body throughout Israel.|One of the darkest chapters in Scripture. Israel has become like Sodom.
judges 20|War against Benjamin|The tribes demand justice. Benjamin refuses to hand over the guilty, and in the civil war the tribe is nearly wiped out.|Sin left unchecked ends in the people destroying one another.
judges 21|Wives for Benjamin|The tribes grieve that one tribe is almost gone and arrange wives for the survivors by violent means.|The book ends: "In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes."
ruth 1|Ruth stays with Naomi|In Moab, Naomi loses her husband and both sons. Returning to Bethlehem, she urges her daughters-in-law to stay behind. Ruth refuses: "Your people shall be my people, and your God my God."|A foreign woman chooses Israel's God out of faithful love.
ruth 2|Gleaning in Boaz's field|Ruth gathers leftover grain in a field belonging to Boaz, a relative of Naomi. He notices her loyalty and protects and provides for her.|God's care arrives through the kindness of a good man and the law of gleaning.
ruth 3|At the threshing floor|On Naomi's advice Ruth goes to Boaz at night and asks him to act as her family redeemer. He praises her and promises to settle the matter.|Boaz acts with honour and restraint.
ruth 4|Boaz marries Ruth|At the town gate a closer relative gives up his right. Boaz marries Ruth, and their son Obed becomes the grandfather of King David.|A Moabite widow becomes an ancestor of David and of Jesus. Boaz the redeemer is a figure of Christ.
