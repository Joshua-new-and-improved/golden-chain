1-samuel 1|Hannah's prayer|Hannah is childless and mocked. She weeps and prays at Shiloh, promising to give her son to God. Samuel is born, and she brings him to the priest Eli.|God hears the prayer of the humiliated. Hannah gives back what she most wanted.
1-samuel 2|Hannah's song; Eli's sons|Hannah sings that God brings down the proud and lifts the lowly. Eli's sons abuse their priesthood, and a prophet announces judgement on his house.|Mary's Magnificat echoes Hannah's song. Sacred office does not excuse sin.
1-samuel 3|The call of Samuel|God calls the boy Samuel by name in the night. Three times he runs to Eli. Then he answers, "Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening."|Samuel's answer is a model prayer for anyone seeking God's will.
1-samuel 4|The ark captured|Israel carries the ark into battle like a lucky charm. The Philistines capture it and kill Eli's sons. Eli falls and dies at the news.|God cannot be used as a tool.
1-samuel 5|The ark among the Philistines|The ark is placed in the temple of Dagon, whose statue falls on its face before it. Plague strikes each city that holds the ark.|The Lord needs no army to defend his honour.
1-samuel 6|The ark returns|The Philistines send the ark home on a cart pulled by two cows, with gold offerings. It arrives at Beth-shemesh.|Even Israel's enemies learn to fear the Lord.
1-samuel 7|Samuel leads Israel|Israel puts away its idols and repents at Mizpah. God thunders against the Philistines. Samuel sets up a stone called Ebenezer, "stone of help".|"Thus far the Lord has helped us."
1-samuel 8|"Give us a king"|The elders ask for a king like other nations. God tells Samuel, "They have not rejected you; they have rejected me." Samuel warns what a king will take from them.|Wanting to be like everyone else can mean rejecting God's rule.
1-samuel 9|Saul meets Samuel|Saul, a tall young man from Benjamin, goes looking for lost donkeys and ends up at Samuel's door. God has told Samuel he is the one.|God guides through ordinary errands.
1-samuel 10|Saul anointed|Samuel anoints Saul privately, and God gives him a new heart. When he is chosen publicly by lot, he is found hiding among the baggage.|Saul begins with humility. The test will be whether it lasts.
1-samuel 11|Saul's first victory|The Ammonites threaten Jabesh. The Spirit comes on Saul, he rallies Israel and rescues the town. He refuses to punish those who doubted him.|At his best Saul gives God the credit.
1-samuel 12|Samuel's farewell|Samuel defends his honesty, reviews God's faithfulness and warns king and people to serve the Lord. He promises to keep praying for them.|"Far be it from me that I should sin against the Lord by ceasing to pray for you."
1-samuel 13|Saul's impatience|With his army melting away, Saul will not wait for Samuel and offers the sacrifice himself. Samuel tells him his kingdom will not last.|Fear leads Saul to disobey. God seeks a man after his own heart.
1-samuel 14|Jonathan's courage|Jonathan and his armour-bearer attack a Philistine post, trusting that God can save by many or by few. Saul's rash oath nearly costs Jonathan his life.|Jonathan's faith contrasts with his father's anxious control.
1-samuel 15|Saul rejected|Told to destroy Amalek completely, Saul keeps the best animals and spares the king, then blames the people. Samuel says, "To obey is better than sacrifice."|Partial obedience with a religious excuse is still disobedience.
1-samuel 16|David anointed|God sends Samuel to Jesse's sons in Bethlehem. Seven are passed over; the youngest, David, a shepherd, is anointed. He enters Saul's service as a harpist.|"Man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart."
1-samuel 17|David and Goliath|The giant Goliath taunts Israel for forty days. David, armed with a sling and five stones, says, "I come in the name of the Lord," and kills him.|Trust in God makes a shepherd boy braver than an army.
1-samuel 18|Saul's jealousy|Jonathan loves David as his own soul. The women sing that David has killed more than Saul, and Saul's envy grows. He twice throws a spear at him.|Envy poisons Saul. Jonathan's friendship is free of it.
1-samuel 19|David escapes|Saul orders David killed. Jonathan pleads for him, and David's wife Michal lowers him from a window.|God protects David through people who love him.
1-samuel 20|David and Jonathan's covenant|Jonathan tests his father's mood and warns David with a signal of arrows. The two friends weep and part.|True friendship puts the other's good first. Jonathan gives up a throne.
1-samuel 21|David on the run|At Nob the priest gives hungry David the holy bread and Goliath's sword. In Gath David pretends to be mad to escape.|Jesus cites the holy bread to show that mercy outranks ritual rules.
1-samuel 22|The priests of Nob|David gathers four hundred men in distress at the cave of Adullam. Saul has eighty-five priests killed for helping him.|Saul's fear has turned into tyranny.
1-samuel 23|Hunted in the wilderness|David rescues the town of Keilah and keeps asking God what to do. Saul hunts him, and Jonathan comes to strengthen him.|David seeks guidance at every step.
1-samuel 24|David spares Saul|Saul enters the very cave where David is hiding. David cuts off a corner of his cloak but will not harm "the Lord's anointed".|David refuses to seize by violence what God has promised him.
1-samuel 25|Abigail|Samuel dies. Rich, foolish Nabal insults David, who sets out for revenge. Nabal's wife Abigail hurries to meet him with gifts and wise words.|A wise word at the right time prevents bloodshed.
1-samuel 26|David spares Saul again|David slips into Saul's camp at night and takes his spear and water jug, leaving him unharmed.|Mercy toward an enemy, twice over.
1-samuel 27|Among the Philistines|Worn out, David takes refuge with the Philistine king of Gath and is given the town of Ziklag.|Even David has seasons of discouragement and compromise.
1-samuel 28|Saul and the medium|God no longer answers Saul. In disguise he consults a medium at Endor, which he himself had banned. Samuel appears and foretells his death.|Saul ends where disobedience leads: seeking in darkness what he refused in the light.
1-samuel 29|David sent back|The Philistine commanders do not trust David and send him away before the battle against Israel.|God spares David from fighting his own people.
1-samuel 30|Ziklag burned|Raiders burn Ziklag and carry off the families. David strengthens himself in the Lord, pursues, and recovers everything. He shares the plunder with those who stayed behind.|In crisis David turns to God first.
1-samuel 31|The death of Saul|The Philistines defeat Israel on Mount Gilboa. Jonathan is killed, and wounded Saul falls on his own sword.|A reign that began with promise ends in tragedy.
2-samuel 1|David's lament|David hears of the deaths of Saul and Jonathan and mourns them in a song: "How the mighty have fallen!"|David grieves for the man who tried to kill him.
2-samuel 2|King of Judah|David is anointed king over Judah at Hebron. Saul's general Abner sets up Saul's son Ish-bosheth over the other tribes, and war begins.|God's promise unfolds slowly.
2-samuel 3|Abner changes sides|Abner quarrels with Ish-bosheth and offers to bring Israel over to David. Joab murders him in revenge, and David publicly mourns.|David refuses to gain the throne through murder.
2-samuel 4|Ish-bosheth murdered|Two officers kill Ish-bosheth and bring his head to David, expecting a reward. David has them executed.|Evil done on his behalf wins no approval from David.
2-samuel 5|King of all Israel|All the tribes anoint David king. He captures Jerusalem and makes it his capital, and defeats the Philistines.|Jerusalem, the city of David, becomes the centre of God's people.
2-samuel 6|The ark comes to Jerusalem|David brings the ark with music. Uzzah touches it and dies. Later David dances before the Lord with all his might, and his wife Michal despises him.|David's joy in God is unashamed. Holy things call for reverence.
2-samuel 7|God's promise to David|David wants to build God a house. Through Nathan God answers that he will build David a house: a dynasty whose throne will last for ever.|This promise is fulfilled in Jesus, Son of David, whose kingdom has no end.
2-samuel 8|David's victories|David defeats the Philistines, Moab, Syria and Edom, and rules with justice.|The Lord gave victory to David wherever he went.
2-samuel 9|Kindness to Mephibosheth|For Jonathan's sake David seeks out his lame son Mephibosheth and gives him a place at the royal table.|David keeps a promise to a dead friend.
2-samuel 10|War with Ammon|David's envoys are humiliated by the Ammonite king. Joab and David defeat Ammon and their Syrian allies.|An insult to kindness leads to war.
2-samuel 11|David and Bathsheba|David stays home from war, sees Bathsheba bathing and sleeps with her. When she is pregnant he arranges for her husband Uriah to die in battle.|The greatest king falls through idleness, lust and a cover-up. Sin grows when hidden.
2-samuel 12|"You are the man"|The prophet Nathan tells of a rich man who stole a poor man's lamb. David is furious, and Nathan says, "You are the man." David repents. The child dies. Solomon is born.|David's repentance is real, and the consequences remain. Psalm 50 (51) is his prayer.
2-samuel 13|Amnon and Tamar|David's son Amnon rapes his half-sister Tamar. David is angry but does nothing. Two years later her brother Absalom has Amnon killed.|David's own sins are mirrored in his sons.
2-samuel 14|Absalom returns|Joab uses a wise woman to persuade David to bring Absalom back, but David refuses to see him for two years.|Half-hearted reconciliation heals nothing.
2-samuel 15|Absalom's revolt|Absalom wins the people's hearts and declares himself king. David leaves Jerusalem weeping, barefoot, over the Mount of Olives.|The scene foreshadows the Son of David on the same hill.
2-samuel 16|Cursed by Shimei|As David flees, Shimei throws stones and curses him. David forbids revenge: perhaps the Lord told him to curse.|David accepts humiliation as penance.
2-samuel 17|Two advisers|Ahithophel advises Absalom to strike at once. Hushai, loyal to David, argues for delay, and his advice wins.|God answers David's prayer through a faithful friend.
2-samuel 18|Absalom's death|David's army wins. Absalom is caught by his hair in a tree, and Joab kills him against orders. David cries, "My son Absalom! Would I had died instead of you!"|A father's love for a rebel son.
2-samuel 19|The king returns|Joab rebukes David for mourning. David crosses back over the Jordan, pardons Shimei and deals kindly with those who helped or failed him.|David restores peace by mercy.
2-samuel 20|Sheba's revolt|Sheba leads the northern tribes away. Joab murders his rival Amasa and besieges Sheba's town until a wise woman ends it.|The kingdom's unity is fragile.
2-samuel 21|Famine and giants|A famine is traced to Saul's breaking of the oath with Gibeon. Rizpah keeps watch over her dead sons. David's men kill four Philistine giants.|Broken promises have long consequences.
2-samuel 22|David's song|David praises God as his rock, fortress and deliverer who drew him out of deep waters.|This song is also Psalm 17 (18).
2-samuel 23|David's last words; his mighty men|David's final oracle about a just ruler, and the feats of his warriors. He pours out water they risked their lives to bring.|David will not enjoy what cost others so dear.
2-samuel 24|The census|David counts his fighting men out of pride. A plague follows. He buys a threshing floor in Jerusalem, builds an altar, and the plague stops.|That site becomes the place of the Temple. "I will not offer what costs me nothing."
1-kings 1|Solomon made king|David is old. His son Adonijah tries to take the throne. Nathan and Bathsheba remind David of his promise, and Solomon is anointed.|God's choice prevails over ambition.
1-kings 2|David's last instructions|David tells Solomon to be strong and keep God's law, and dies. Solomon secures the kingdom against his rivals.|The kingdom depends on faithfulness to God.
1-kings 3|Solomon asks for wisdom|God offers Solomon anything. He asks for an understanding heart to govern well. He proves it when two women claim the same baby.|Solomon asks for what helps him serve, and receives the rest besides.
1-kings 4|Solomon's kingdom|A list of his officials. Judah and Israel live in peace, each under his vine and fig tree. Solomon's wisdom is famous.|A glimpse of the peace God intends.
1-kings 5|Preparing the Temple|Solomon arranges with King Hiram of Tyre for cedar wood and gathers workers.|A foreign king helps build God's house.
1-kings 6|The Temple built|Solomon builds the Temple in seven years, with an inner sanctuary overlaid with gold for the ark.|God promises to dwell there if Solomon walks in his ways.
1-kings 7|Palace and furnishings|Solomon builds his own palace and has the bronze pillars, the great basin and the gold vessels made for the Temple.|Beauty and craftsmanship are offered to God.
1-kings 8|The Temple dedicated|The ark is brought in and the cloud of God's glory fills the house. Solomon prays that God will hear every prayer made toward this place, even a foreigner's.|"Heaven itself cannot contain you." The Temple is a house of prayer.
1-kings 9|God's warning|God appears again: if Solomon is faithful, his throne will stand; if Israel turns to other gods, the Temple will become ruins.|God's gifts come with responsibility.
1-kings 10|The queen of Sheba|The queen comes with hard questions and is overwhelmed by Solomon's wisdom and wealth.|Jesus says that "something greater than Solomon is here".
1-kings 11|Solomon's fall|Solomon marries many foreign wives and builds shrines for their gods. God announces that the kingdom will be torn in two. Solomon dies.|The wisest man is undone by a divided heart.
1-kings 12|The kingdom splits|Rehoboam answers the people harshly, and ten tribes break away under Jeroboam, who sets up golden calves at Bethel and Dan.|Arrogance divides the nation, and convenience creates false worship.
1-kings 13|The man of God from Judah|A prophet condemns the altar at Bethel. He is then deceived into disobeying God's command and is killed by a lion.|God's word must be obeyed even against a persuasive voice.
1-kings 14|Judgement on two kings|The prophet Ahijah foretells the end of Jeroboam's house. In Judah, Rehoboam's people turn to idols, and Egypt plunders the Temple.|Both kingdoms begin to decline.
1-kings 15|Good King Asa|In Judah, Abijam is followed by Asa, who removes idols. In Israel, kings are murdered and replaced.|Each king is judged by one thing: whether he was faithful to God.
1-kings 16|Omri and Ahab|Coups follow one another in Israel. Omri builds Samaria. His son Ahab marries Jezebel and builds a temple of Baal.|Ahab does more evil than all before him.
1-kings 17|Elijah|Elijah announces a drought. Ravens feed him by a brook. Then a poor widow shares her last flour and oil, which never run out, and Elijah raises her son.|God provides day by day for those who trust him.
1-kings 18|Fire on Mount Carmel|Elijah challenges 450 prophets of Baal. They cry out all day with no answer. Elijah prays, and fire falls on his drenched sacrifice. Rain returns.|"How long will you limp between two opinions?"
1-kings 19|The still small voice|Threatened by Jezebel, Elijah flees and wants to die. An angel feeds him, and at Mount Horeb God comes not in wind, earthquake or fire but in a gentle whisper. Elijah calls Elisha.|God meets his exhausted prophet with food, rest and quiet.
1-kings 20|Ahab's wars|God twice gives Ahab victory over the king of Syria. Ahab then spares him, and a prophet rebukes him.|God helps even Ahab, who still does not listen.
1-kings 21|Naboth's vineyard|Naboth will not sell his family land. Jezebel has him falsely accused and stoned, and Ahab takes the vineyard. Elijah confronts him.|God defends the ordinary man against the powerful.
1-kings 22|Micaiah and Ahab's death|Four hundred prophets promise victory. Micaiah alone tells the truth. Ahab goes into battle disguised and is killed by a random arrow.|The truth is not decided by majority.
2-kings 1|Elijah and King Ahaziah|Injured Ahaziah consults a pagan god. Elijah announces he will die, and fire falls on soldiers sent to arrest the prophet.|"Is there no God in Israel?"
2-kings 2|Elijah taken to heaven|Elijah parts the Jordan and is carried up in a whirlwind with a chariot of fire. Elisha receives a double share of his spirit and takes up his cloak.|The mission passes from master to disciple.
2-kings 3|War with Moab|Three kings march against Moab and run out of water. Elisha promises water and victory.|God helps for the sake of the faithful king of Judah.
2-kings 4|Elisha's miracles|A widow's oil multiplies to pay her debts. A childless woman is given a son, and Elisha raises him from death. Twenty loaves feed a hundred men.|These miracles anticipate those of Jesus.
2-kings 5|Naaman the leper|The Syrian general Naaman is told to wash seven times in the Jordan. He is insulted, then obeys and is healed.|Jesus cites Naaman: God's mercy reaches foreigners. Healing comes through humble obedience.
2-kings 6|Chariots of fire|An axe head floats. An army surrounds Elisha, and he prays for his servant's eyes to be opened to see the hills full of horses and chariots of fire.|"Those who are with us are more than those who are with them."
2-kings 7|The siege lifted|Samaria is starving. Elisha foretells plenty by the next day. Four lepers find the enemy camp abandoned.|The lepers say, "This is a day of good news, and we are keeping silent."
2-kings 8|Elisha and Hazael|The Shunammite woman gets her land back. Elisha weeps as he foretells that Hazael will become king of Syria and ravage Israel.|A prophet takes no pleasure in announcing judgement.
2-kings 9|Jehu anointed|A prophet anoints Jehu king. He kills the kings of Israel and Judah, and Jezebel is thrown from a window.|The judgement Elijah announced on Ahab's house arrives.
2-kings 10|Jehu's purge|Jehu wipes out Ahab's family and the worshippers of Baal, but keeps the golden calves.|Zeal against others' sins is not the same as full obedience.
2-kings 11|The boy king Joash|Queen Athaliah murders the royal family. One baby, Joash, is hidden in the Temple for six years and then crowned.|David's line survives by a thread.
2-kings 12|Joash repairs the Temple|Joash collects money to restore the Temple and pays honest workmen.|He does right while his teacher, the priest Jehoiada, guides him.
2-kings 13|The death of Elisha|Israel is oppressed by Syria. The dying Elisha promises victories. Later a dead man revives on touching Elisha's bones.|Catholics see here support for honouring the relics of the saints.
2-kings 14|Amaziah and Jeroboam II|Amaziah of Judah is defeated after picking a fight with Israel. Jeroboam II restores Israel's borders.|God shows mercy to Israel despite its kings.
2-kings 15|Kings come and go|Azariah (Uzziah) reigns long in Judah. In Israel five kings rule in quick succession, most murdered, as Assyria closes in.|A kingdom without faithfulness has no stability.
2-kings 16|Ahaz of Judah|Ahaz sacrifices his own son, buys Assyria's help with Temple treasure, and copies a pagan altar.|A king of David's line sinks to pagan practice.
2-kings 17|The fall of Samaria|Assyria conquers the northern kingdom and deports its people. The writer explains why: they rejected God's covenant and his prophets.|The ten tribes are lost. Foreign settlers become the Samaritans.
2-kings 18|Hezekiah trusts God|Hezekiah removes idols and holds fast to the Lord. Sennacherib of Assyria invades, and his officer mocks Jerusalem's faith.|"Do not let Hezekiah deceive you" is the enemy's line in every age.
2-kings 19|Jerusalem delivered|Hezekiah spreads the threatening letter before the Lord and prays. Isaiah promises rescue, and that night the Assyrian army is struck down.|Prayer is Hezekiah's first response to a threat.
2-kings 20|Hezekiah's illness|Told he will die, Hezekiah weeps and prays, and God adds fifteen years. He then shows off his treasures to envoys from Babylon.|Pride after mercy prepares future loss.
2-kings 21|Manasseh|Manasseh rebuilds the pagan shrines, practises sorcery and sheds innocent blood for fifty-five years.|The worst king of Judah. Exile becomes certain.
2-kings 22|The book of the law found|During Temple repairs under King Josiah a scroll of the law is found. Josiah tears his clothes on hearing it.|Scripture rediscovered brings repentance.
2-kings 23|Josiah's reform|Josiah renews the covenant, destroys every idol and shrine, and keeps a great Passover. He dies in battle at Megiddo.|The best of kings cannot undo generations of sin by himself.
2-kings 24|The first deportation|Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon takes Jerusalem, carries off the king, the craftsmen and the Temple treasures, and installs Zedekiah.|The warnings of the prophets come true.
2-kings 25|The fall of Jerusalem|Zedekiah rebels. After a long siege Babylon burns the Temple and the city and takes the people into exile. Years later the captive king Jehoiachin is released.|The story ends in ruin, with one small sign of hope for David's line.
