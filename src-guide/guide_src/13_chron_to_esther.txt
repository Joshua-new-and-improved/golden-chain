1-chronicles 1|From Adam to Abraham|Lists of names from Adam through Noah's sons to Abraham, and the families of Ishmael and Esau.|Israel's story is placed inside the story of the whole human race.
1-chronicles 2|The tribe of Judah|The sons of Israel, then the family of Judah down to Jesse and David.|The writer goes first to the royal tribe.
1-chronicles 3|David's descendants|David's sons, the kings of Judah after Solomon, and the royal family after the exile.|David's line continued even when there was no throne.
1-chronicles 4|Judah and Simeon|More clans of Judah and of Simeon. Jabez prays that God will bless him and keep him from harm, and God grants it.|A short prayer hidden in a list shows that God hears individuals.
1-chronicles 5|Tribes east of the Jordan|Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh. They win battles when they cry to God, and are later exiled for their idolatry.|Trust brings help; unfaithfulness brings loss.
1-chronicles 6|The tribe of Levi|The line of the high priests, the Temple singers appointed by David, and the Levites' towns.|Worship has an ordered ministry handed down through generations.
1-chronicles 7|Northern tribes|Family lists of Issachar, Benjamin, Naphtali, Manasseh, Ephraim and Asher.|Every tribe is remembered as part of Israel.
1-chronicles 8|Benjamin and Saul's family|The clans of Benjamin, ending with the family of King Saul and Jonathan.|The first king's family is recorded before his story is told.
1-chronicles 9|Those who returned|The people, priests, Levites and gatekeepers who settled again in Jerusalem after the exile.|The lists show the returned exiles that they belong to the same people.
1-chronicles 10|Saul's death|Saul and his sons die fighting the Philistines on Mount Gilboa.|The writer's verdict: Saul died for his unfaithfulness and for consulting a medium.
1-chronicles 11|David becomes king|All Israel anoints David at Hebron. He takes Jerusalem. His mighty men and their exploits are listed.|David's kingdom rests on God's word and on loyal friends.
1-chronicles 12|Those who joined David|Warriors from every tribe come over to David, even while he is still a fugitive.|Unity gathers around the king God has chosen.
1-chronicles 13|The ark and Uzzah|David sets out to bring the ark to Jerusalem on a cart. Uzzah steadies it with his hand and dies. The ark stays three months with Obed-edom.|Good intentions do not replace doing things God's way.
1-chronicles 14|David established|Hiram sends cedar for David's house. David asks God before each battle and twice defeats the Philistines.|David succeeds because he asks first.
1-chronicles 15|The ark carried properly|This time Levites carry the ark on their shoulders as the law requires, with singers and instruments. David dances.|Joy and reverence go together.
1-chronicles 16|A psalm of thanks|The ark is placed in its tent. David appoints Levites to praise God continually and gives them a song: "Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good."|Daily praise becomes a permanent part of Israel's worship.
1-chronicles 17|God's promise to David|David wishes to build a temple. God promises instead to build David a lasting house and throne. David prays in wonder.|The promise looks ahead to the Messiah.
1-chronicles 18|David's victories|David defeats the surrounding nations and dedicates the plunder to the Lord.|He rules with justice for all his people.
1-chronicles 19|War with Ammon|David's envoys are shamed, and Joab defeats the Ammonites and their Syrian allies.|"Be strong, and let the Lord do what seems good to him."
1-chronicles 20|Rabbah taken; giants killed|The Ammonite capital falls, and David's men kill Philistine giants.|The Chronicler passes over David's sin with Bathsheba; his interest is the Temple.
1-chronicles 21|The census and the altar|David counts Israel out of pride. A plague follows. He buys Ornan's threshing floor at full price and offers sacrifice, and fire falls from heaven.|This is the site where the Temple will stand.
1-chronicles 22|Preparing for the Temple|David gathers stone, iron, cedar and gold, and charges Solomon to build. He himself has shed too much blood to do it.|David prepares a work he will not live to see finished.
1-chronicles 23|The Levites organised|David counts the Levites and assigns them to assist the priests and to thank and praise God morning and evening.|Worship needs many kinds of service.
1-chronicles 24|Divisions of priests|The priests are arranged by lot into twenty-four groups to serve in turn.|Zechariah, father of John the Baptist, belonged to one of these divisions.
1-chronicles 25|The musicians|Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun and their sons are set apart to make music in the Lord's house.|Sacred music is a real ministry.
1-chronicles 26|Gatekeepers and treasurers|Levites are assigned to guard the gates and look after the treasuries.|Humble tasks keep God's house in order.
1-chronicles 27|Army and officials|Twelve army divisions serve a month each. Tribal leaders and stewards of the king's property are named.|Good government is orderly.
1-chronicles 28|David's charge to Solomon|Before the assembly David gives Solomon the plans for the Temple and tells him to serve God with a whole heart.|"Be strong and do it. The Lord God is with you."
1-chronicles 29|Gifts for the Temple|David and the leaders give generously. David prays, "All things come from you, and of your own have we given you." Solomon is made king, and David dies.|Everything we offer God was his gift first.
2-chronicles 1|Solomon asks for wisdom|At Gibeon God offers Solomon whatever he wants. He asks for wisdom to lead the people and receives wealth and honour too.|The right request pleases God.
2-chronicles 2|Preparing to build|Solomon asks Hiram of Tyre for cedar and a master craftsman, and organises the workforce.|"Who is able to build him a house, since heaven cannot contain him?"
2-chronicles 3|The Temple begun|Solomon starts building on Mount Moriah, where Abraham had offered Isaac, and overlays the inner room with gold.|The Temple stands on ground already marked by sacrifice.
2-chronicles 4|The furnishings|The bronze altar, the great basin, lampstands, tables and vessels are made.|Everything in God's house is made with care.
2-chronicles 5|The ark brought in|The priests set the ark in the Holy of Holies. As the singers praise God, a cloud fills the Temple.|God's presence answers his people's praise.
2-chronicles 6|Solomon's prayer|Solomon kneels and asks God to hear every prayer made toward this house: for forgiveness, rain, victory, and for the foreigner.|The Temple is meant as a place of mercy for all.
2-chronicles 7|Fire from heaven|Fire consumes the sacrifices and glory fills the house. God promises, "If my people humble themselves, pray, seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, I will forgive."|That promise of forgiveness is the heart of the book.
2-chronicles 8|Solomon's other works|Solomon builds cities, organises labour and keeps the appointed sacrifices.|The worship David planned is carried out.
2-chronicles 9|The queen of Sheba|The queen tests Solomon and praises God for him. His wealth is described. Solomon dies.|The nations are drawn by the wisdom God gives.
2-chronicles 10|The kingdom divides|Rehoboam rejects the elders' advice and answers harshly. Ten tribes leave.|Pride splits what David and Solomon built.
2-chronicles 11|Rehoboam strengthens Judah|God forbids civil war. Priests and faithful Israelites from the north move to Judah.|People leave home to be able to worship rightly.
2-chronicles 12|Egypt invades|Rehoboam abandons the law, and Shishak of Egypt attacks. The king and leaders humble themselves, and God limits the damage.|Humility turns away the worst.
2-chronicles 13|Abijah's stand|Outnumbered two to one, King Abijah declares that Judah has kept the true priesthood. Judah cries to the Lord and wins.|"They relied on the Lord, the God of their fathers."
2-chronicles 14|Asa trusts God|Asa removes foreign altars. When a huge army attacks, he prays, "Lord, there is none like you to help the weak," and wins.|Numbers do not decide the battle.
2-chronicles 15|Asa's reform|A prophet tells Asa, "The Lord is with you while you are with him." Asa clears out idols and the people renew the covenant with joy.|Seeking God wholeheartedly brings peace.
2-chronicles 16|Asa's later failure|Threatened by Israel, Asa buys help from Syria instead of trusting God. Rebuked, he jails the prophet. In illness he looks only to physicians.|A good start is not enough. "The eyes of the Lord search the whole earth to strengthen those whose heart is his."
2-chronicles 17|Jehoshaphat teaches the law|Jehoshaphat sends officials and Levites through Judah to teach the people from the book of the law.|Reform needs teaching.
2-chronicles 18|Micaiah and Ahab|Jehoshaphat allies with Ahab. Micaiah alone foretells defeat. Ahab dies in battle, and Jehoshaphat barely escapes.|A bad alliance puts a good man in danger.
2-chronicles 19|Honest judges|A prophet rebukes the alliance. Jehoshaphat appoints judges and tells them, "You judge not for man but for the Lord."|Justice is service to God.
2-chronicles 20|The singers lead the army|A vast enemy comes. Jehoshaphat prays, "We do not know what to do, but our eyes are on you." Singers go out in front, and the enemies destroy each other.|"The battle is not yours but God's."
2-chronicles 21|Jehoram|Jehoram murders his brothers and follows Ahab's ways. He dies of a painful disease, and no one regrets him.|Marrying into Ahab's family brought his sins into Judah.
2-chronicles 22|Ahaziah and Athaliah|Ahaziah reigns one year and is killed. His mother Athaliah seizes the throne and kills the royal children, but baby Joash is hidden.|God preserves David's line.
2-chronicles 23|Joash crowned|The priest Jehoiada crowns the seven-year-old Joash in the Temple. Athaliah is put to death and Baal's temple torn down.|A faithful priest saves the kingdom.
2-chronicles 24|Joash, good then bad|Joash repairs the Temple while Jehoiada lives. Afterwards he turns to idols and has Jehoiada's son Zechariah stoned.|Borrowed faith fails when the mentor is gone.
2-chronicles 25|Amaziah|Amaziah obeys a prophet and wins a battle, then worships the gods of the people he defeated and is beaten by Israel.|A half-hearted king.
2-chronicles 26|Uzziah's pride|Uzziah prospers as long as he seeks God. Grown powerful, he enters the Temple to burn incense, which only priests may do, and is struck with leprosy.|Success leads to pride, and pride to a fall.
2-chronicles 27|Jotham|Jotham does right, builds, and grows strong.|"He ordered his ways before the Lord his God."
2-chronicles 28|Ahaz|Ahaz worships idols, sacrifices his sons, is defeated on every side and shuts the Temple doors.|Trouble makes him worse instead of turning him back.
2-chronicles 29|Hezekiah reopens the Temple|In his first month Hezekiah opens and cleanses the Temple and restores sacrifice and song.|Renewal starts with worship.
2-chronicles 30|A great Passover|Hezekiah invites all Israel, north and south, to keep Passover. Many mock, some come. God pardons those not ritually ready.|God looks at the heart that seeks him.
2-chronicles 31|Tithes restored|The people destroy idols and bring so many offerings that they are piled in heaps.|Renewed faith shows in generosity.
2-chronicles 32|Sennacherib defeated|Assyria invades. Hezekiah encourages the people: "With him is an arm of flesh; with us is the Lord." An angel destroys the invaders.|Hezekiah later struggles with pride and humbles himself.
2-chronicles 33|Manasseh repents|Manasseh does great evil. Taken captive in chains, he humbles himself and prays, and God brings him back. He then removes the idols.|No one is beyond God's mercy.
2-chronicles 34|Josiah and the book|Josiah begins seeking God at sixteen. The book of the law is found during repairs, and he leads the people in renewing the covenant.|A young person can lead a nation back to God.
2-chronicles 35|Josiah's Passover|Josiah keeps the greatest Passover since Samuel. He later dies fighting Pharaoh Neco.|Jeremiah laments him.
2-chronicles 36|Exile and return|The last four kings fail. God sends messengers again and again until there is no remedy. Jerusalem is destroyed. Seventy years later Cyrus of Persia invites the exiles home.|The book ends with an open door.
ezra 1|Cyrus sends the exiles home|King Cyrus of Persia decrees that the Jews may return and rebuild the Temple, and gives back the sacred vessels.|God moves a pagan king to fulfil his word through Jeremiah.
ezra 2|The list of those who returned|The families, priests and Levites who came back, about fifty thousand people.|Each family counted.
ezra 3|Altar and foundations|The returned exiles rebuild the altar and lay the Temple's foundation. The young shout for joy; the old, who remember the first Temple, weep.|Starting again brings both joy and grief.
ezra 4|Opposition|Neighbours first offer to help, then intimidate the builders and write to the king. The work stops for years.|Doing God's work attracts resistance.
ezra 5|Work resumes|The prophets Haggai and Zechariah stir the people to build again. The governor reports to King Darius.|God's word restarts what fear had stopped.
ezra 6|The Temple finished|Darius finds Cyrus's decree and orders the work paid for. The Temple is completed and dedicated, and Passover is kept.|God turns the heart of kings.
ezra 7|Ezra arrives|Ezra, a priest and scribe, comes from Babylon with the king's authority to teach the law.|He had set his heart to study, practise and teach.
ezra 8|The journey|Ezra gathers his company, fasts and prays for safety instead of asking for soldiers, and arrives safely with the gold and silver.|He would not undercut his own testimony about God's protection.
ezra 9|Ezra's prayer|Ezra learns that many, including leaders, have married pagan wives. He tears his clothes and confesses the people's sin as his own.|A leader prays "we", not "they".
ezra 10|The people repent|The people gather in the rain, admit their guilt and agree to end these marriages.|The concern was idolatry returning, the very thing that caused the exile.
nehemiah 1|Nehemiah's prayer|Nehemiah, cupbearer to the Persian king, hears that Jerusalem's walls are in ruins. He weeps, fasts and prays.|Concern for God's people begins in prayer.
nehemiah 2|Sent to rebuild|The king notices his sadness. Nehemiah prays quickly and asks leave to rebuild. He inspects the walls by night.|A short silent prayer before a hard conversation.
nehemiah 3|The builders|A list of who repaired each section of the wall: priests, goldsmiths, merchants, and one man with his daughters.|Everyone builds the part in front of their own house.
nehemiah 4|Sword and trowel|Enemies mock and plot an attack. Nehemiah posts guards, and the builders work with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other.|"We prayed to our God and set a guard." Prayer and prudence together.
nehemiah 5|Justice for the poor|The poor complain that rich Jews are charging interest and taking their children as slaves. Nehemiah makes them give it all back, and refuses his own governor's allowance.|Rebuilding walls is pointless if the people inside exploit each other.
nehemiah 6|The wall finished|Enemies try to lure Nehemiah away and to frighten him. He replies, "I am doing a great work and cannot come down." The wall is finished in fifty-two days.|He stays focused.
nehemiah 7|The register|Nehemiah appoints guards and finds the record of the first returnees.|The city needs people as well as walls.
nehemiah 8|Ezra reads the law|Ezra reads the law aloud from dawn to noon while Levites explain it. The people weep, and are told, "The joy of the Lord is your strength."|Scripture read and explained to the assembly: the pattern of the Liturgy of the Word.
nehemiah 9|A prayer of confession|The people fast and the Levites pray through Israel's whole history: God's constant mercy and their constant rebellion.|"You are a God ready to forgive."
nehemiah 10|The covenant signed|Leaders put their names to a written promise to keep the law, the Sabbath and the support of the Temple.|Repentance becomes concrete commitments.
nehemiah 11|Repopulating Jerusalem|One family in ten is chosen by lot to live in the city, and others volunteer.|Some give up comfort for the common good.
nehemiah 12|The wall dedicated|After lists of priests, two great choirs walk the wall in opposite directions and meet at the Temple. The joy is heard far away.|The work ends in thanksgiving.
nehemiah 13|Nehemiah's last reforms|Returning after an absence, Nehemiah finds the Temple misused, the Sabbath broken and mixed marriages again. He puts things right.|Reform does not maintain itself.
tobit 1|Tobit in exile|Tobit, a faithful Israelite deported to Nineveh, gives alms and buries the dead at risk to his life.|Faithfulness is possible in a hostile place.
tobit 2|Tobit goes blind|After burying a man, Tobit sleeps outside and is blinded. He bears it patiently, though his wife reproaches him.|The just man is tested, like Job.
tobit 3|Two prayers|Tobit asks to die. Far away Sara, whose seven husbands were each killed by a demon, prays too. God hears both and sends the angel Raphael.|God answers two prayers with one plan.
tobit 4|A father's advice|Thinking he will die, Tobit instructs his son Tobias: honour your mother, give alms, and do to no one what you would hate. He tells him of money left in Media.|"Alms deliver from death."
tobit 5|The guide|Tobias finds a companion for the journey, the angel Raphael in disguise.|God sends help we do not recognise at the time.
tobit 6|The fish|A great fish attacks Tobias at the river. Raphael tells him to keep its heart, liver and gall, and to marry Sara.|The remedy for both troubles is found on the way.
tobit 7|Tobias and Sara betrothed|Sara's father Raguel welcomes them and gives her to Tobias in marriage.|Marriage is entered with family and blessing.
tobit 8|The wedding night|Tobias burns the fish's liver and the demon flees. The couple pray together before lying down.|Their prayer is read at Catholic weddings. Marriage is to be lived in prayer.
tobit 9|The money collected|Raphael fetches the money and brings Gabelus to the wedding feast.|The original errand is done almost in passing.
tobit 10|Anxious parents|Tobit and Anna fear their son is dead. Tobias takes leave of Sara's parents.|A blessing sends the young couple on their way.
tobit 11|Sight restored|Tobias returns, anoints his father's eyes with the fish's gall, and Tobit sees again.|Tobit blesses God for both chastising and healing him.
tobit 12|Raphael reveals himself|Offered wages, the guide says, "I am Raphael, one of the seven who stand before the Lord." He had carried their prayers to God.|Angels are real and present our prayers. Prayer with fasting and alms is good.
tobit 13|Tobit's song|Tobit praises God, who scourges and saves, and foresees Jerusalem rebuilt in splendour.|Suffering has become praise.
tobit 14|Tobit's last words|Tobit dies in peace, telling his family to leave Nineveh. Tobias lives to see its fall.|A faithful life ends in peace.
judith 1|Nebuchadnezzar's war|King Nebuchadnezzar defeats the king of the Medes and vows revenge on the nations that refused to help him.|The stage is set: a king who wants to be treated as a god.
judith 2|Holofernes sent|The king sends his general Holofernes west with a huge army.|No nation can stand against him.
judith 3|The nations submit|Peoples along the coast surrender. Holofernes destroys their shrines so that Nebuchadnezzar alone is worshipped.|The real issue is who is God.
judith 4|Israel prays|The Israelites fortify the mountain passes, fast in sackcloth and cry to God.|Preparation and prayer go together.
judith 5|Achior's warning|Achior the Ammonite tells Holofernes Israel's history: they cannot be beaten unless they sin against their God.|An outsider speaks the truth.
judith 6|Achior handed over|Holofernes, enraged, has Achior left at the town of Bethulia to share its fate. The people take him in.|"Who is God but Nebuchadnezzar?" The challenge is stated.
judith 7|The siege|Holofernes cuts off Bethulia's water. The people demand surrender, and the elders agree to give in if God does not help within five days.|They set God a deadline.
judith 8|Judith speaks|Judith, a devout and beautiful widow, rebukes the elders: who are you to test God? She says she will act.|Trust does not put conditions on God.
judith 9|Judith's prayer|In sackcloth and ashes Judith asks God to break the enemy's pride by a woman's hand.|"You are the God of the humble, the helper of the oppressed."
judith 10|Into the enemy camp|Judith dresses in her finest and walks out to the Assyrian camp with her maid.|She uses every gift God gave her.
judith 11|Before Holofernes|Judith tells Holofernes she will show him how to take the town. He is charmed.|He hears what his pride wants to hear.
judith 12|Three days in the camp|Judith eats her own food and goes out to pray each night. Holofernes invites her to a banquet and drinks himself senseless.|She keeps her faithfulness in the middle of the enemy.
judith 13|Holofernes beheaded|Alone with the drunken general, Judith prays for strength and cuts off his head with his own sword. She carries it back to Bethulia.|"Blessed are you among women." The Church applies this praise to Mary.
judith 14|Panic in the camp|Achior sees the head and believes in God. At dawn the Assyrians find their general dead.|A pagan comes to faith.
judith 15|Victory|The Assyrian army flees and Israel plunders the camp. The high priest praises Judith: "You are the glory of Jerusalem, the joy of Israel."|These words are used in the liturgy for feasts of Our Lady.
judith 16|Judith's song|Judith sings a hymn of thanks. She lives to a great age, honoured by all.|"The Lord Almighty has foiled them by the hand of a woman."
esther 1|Queen Vashti deposed|King Ahasuerus of Persia holds a lavish feast. Queen Vashti refuses to be displayed and loses her crown.|A proud court sets the scene.
esther 2|Esther becomes queen|Esther, a Jewish orphan raised by her cousin Mordecai, is chosen as queen and keeps her people secret. Mordecai uncovers a plot against the king.|God places people where they will be needed.
esther 3|Haman's plot|The official Haman is furious that Mordecai will not bow to him. He obtains a decree to destroy all Jews on a day chosen by lot.|Wounded pride turns into genocide.
esther 4|"For such a time as this"|Mordecai asks Esther to plead with the king. To approach uninvited means death. He says, "Who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"|Our position is given for a purpose.
esther 5|Esther goes to the king|The king receives her, and she invites him and Haman to a banquet. Haman builds a gallows for Mordecai.|Courage with patience.
esther 6|Mordecai honoured|The sleepless king reads of Mordecai's loyalty and has Haman lead him through the city in royal robes.|The reversal begins, by a sleepless night.
esther 7|Haman's fall|At the second banquet Esther reveals she is a Jew and names Haman. He is hanged on his own gallows.|The trap closes on its maker.
esther 8|A new decree|Mordecai is raised to honour, and the Jews are permitted to defend themselves.|Mourning turns to joy.
esther 9|The feast of Purim|The Jews overcome their enemies, and the feast of Purim is established to remember it.|Deliverance is remembered every year.
esther 10|Mordecai's dream explained|Mordecai is second to the king. He recalls a dream of two dragons and a small spring that became a river: the spring was Esther.|God had planned the rescue from the start. From here on the chapters come from the Greek text.
esther 11|The dream|Mordecai's dream is told: noise, two dragons ready to fight, and a little fountain that grew into a great river.|What looked small was God's instrument.
esther 12|The plot uncovered|A fuller account of how Mordecai exposed the plot against the king and how Haman came to hate him.|Background to the story.
esther 13|The decree and Mordecai's prayer|The text of Haman's decree. Mordecai prays, explaining that he refused to bow so as not to give a man God's honour.|He bows to God alone.
esther 14|Esther's prayer|Esther lays aside her royal robes and prays: "Help me, who am alone and have no helper but you."|The prayer of someone facing danger with no one but God.
esther 15|Esther before the king|Esther approaches the throne in fear and faints. God changes the king's spirit to gentleness.|God holds the hearts of rulers.
esther 16|The king's letter|The decree in favour of the Jews: Haman is condemned, and the Jews are declared just and children of the living God.|A pagan king ends by acknowledging God.
