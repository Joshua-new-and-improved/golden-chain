proverbs 1|The beginning of wisdom|The book's purpose: to give wisdom and discipline. "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge." A father warns his son against joining a gang, and Wisdom calls out in the streets.|Wisdom starts with reverence for God, and with choosing your friends.
proverbs 2|Search for wisdom like treasure|If you seek wisdom as you would silver, you will find the knowledge of God. It will protect you from evil men and from adultery.|Wisdom is a gift that has to be sought.
proverbs 3|Trust in the Lord|"Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding." Honour God with your wealth. The Lord corrects those he loves.|God's direction is better than our own cleverness.
proverbs 4|Guard your heart|A father passes on what his father taught him. "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life."|What we let into our heart shapes our whole life.
proverbs 5|Warning against adultery|The seductress's lips drip honey, but the end is bitter. "Rejoice in the wife of your youth."|Faithfulness in marriage is the wise path and the happy one.
proverbs 6|Debts, laziness and adultery|Do not guarantee another's debt. "Go to the ant, you sluggard." Seven things the Lord hates. Adultery is fire carried in the lap.|Practical wisdom about money, work and purity.
proverbs 7|The young man without sense|A scene: at dusk a married woman lures a foolish young man, who follows "like an ox to the slaughter".|Temptation is described so that it can be recognised and avoided.
proverbs 8|Wisdom speaks|Wisdom calls at the city gates. She was with God before the world began, his delight day by day.|The Church reads this of Christ, the Wisdom of God, and applies it to Our Lady in the liturgy.
proverbs 9|Two invitations|Lady Wisdom has built her house and set her table: "Come, eat of my bread." Lady Folly calls too: "Stolen water is sweet."|Everyone accepts one invitation or the other.
proverbs 10|Sayings of Solomon begin|Short two-line proverbs contrasting the wise and the foolish, the diligent and the lazy, careful and careless speech.|"Love covers all offences."
proverbs 11|Honest scales|A false balance is an abomination to the Lord. The generous person prospers; pride comes before disgrace.|Honesty and generosity pay; hoarding does not.
proverbs 12|Accepting correction|Whoever loves discipline loves knowledge. A good wife is her husband's crown. Truthful lips endure.|The wise listen to advice.
proverbs 13|Walk with the wise|"Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise." Hope deferred makes the heart sick. A father who loves his child disciplines him.|We become like the company we keep.
proverbs 14|The way that seems right|"There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death." Whoever oppresses the poor insults his Maker.|Feeling right is not the same as being right.
proverbs 15|A gentle answer|"A soft answer turns away wrath." Better a dinner of herbs where love is than a fattened ox with hatred.|How we speak decides whether conflict grows or dies.
proverbs 16|Man plans, God directs|"The heart of man plans his way, but the Lord establishes his steps." Pride goes before destruction.|Make plans, and hold them humbly.
proverbs 17|Friends and family|"A friend loves at all times." Even a fool who keeps silent is thought wise. A cheerful heart is good medicine.|Loyalty, restraint and joy.
proverbs 18|The power of the tongue|"Death and life are in the power of the tongue." To answer before listening is folly. The name of the Lord is a strong tower.|Listen first.
proverbs 19|Kindness to the poor|"Whoever is generous to the poor lends to the Lord." Many are the plans in a man's mind, but the Lord's purpose stands.|God counts kindness to the poor as a loan to himself.
proverbs 20|Wine and honesty|"Wine is a mocker." Diverse weights are an abomination. Even a child is known by his actions.|Self-control and fair dealing.
proverbs 21|The Lord weighs the heart|The king's heart is in the Lord's hand. Doing justice is more acceptable than sacrifice.|God looks at motives.
proverbs 22|A good name|"A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches." Train up a child in the way he should go. Thirty sayings of the wise begin.|Character outlasts wealth.
proverbs 23|Appetite and drink|Do not crave a ruler's delicacies or wear yourself out to get rich. A vivid picture of the drunkard. "Buy truth, and do not sell it."|Moderation at table and with money.
proverbs 24|Do not envy the wicked|A just man falls seven times and rises again. The field of the sluggard is overgrown with thorns.|Falling is not final; staying down is.
proverbs 25|More proverbs of Solomon|"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold." If your enemy is hungry, give him bread.|Paul quotes the saying about feeding your enemy.
proverbs 26|The fool and the sluggard|Portraits of the fool, the lazy man who says "There is a lion in the street", and the gossip. Without wood a fire goes out.|Quarrels die when gossip stops.
proverbs 27|Do not boast about tomorrow|"Let another praise you, and not your own mouth." Faithful are the wounds of a friend. Iron sharpens iron.|Real friends tell us the truth.
proverbs 28|Bold as a lion|"The wicked flee when no one pursues." Whoever conceals his sins will not prosper; whoever confesses and forsakes them obtains mercy.|A plain statement of why confession matters.
proverbs 29|Discipline and vision|One who is often reproved and stiffens his neck will be broken. Where there is no prophetic vision the people cast off restraint.|A society needs God's word to hold together.
proverbs 30|The words of Agur|"Give me neither poverty nor riches." Lists of four: things never satisfied, things too wonderful, small creatures that are wise.|A prayer for enough, and wonder at creation.
proverbs 31|The valiant woman|A queen mother's advice to her son, then a poem in praise of a capable wife: hard-working, generous to the poor, wise in speech.|"Charm is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the Lord is to be praised."
ecclesiastes 1|Vanity of vanities|The Preacher looks at life "under the sun": generations come and go, and nothing is new. Even wisdom brings sorrow.|The book asks what anything is worth if this life is all there is.
ecclesiastes 2|Pleasure and work tested|He tries pleasure, building, wealth and wisdom. All is vanity, because wise and fool both die. Enjoy your work as God's gift.|Nothing created can fill the heart.
ecclesiastes 3|A time for everything|"A time to be born and a time to die." God has made everything beautiful in its time and put eternity into man's heart.|We are made for more than time can give.
ecclesiastes 4|Two are better than one|He sees oppression, envy and lonely toil. "A threefold cord is not quickly broken."|Companionship is one of life's real goods.
ecclesiastes 5|Few words before God|Guard your steps in God's house, and keep your vows. Whoever loves money never has enough.|Reverence in worship; contentment with what God gives.
ecclesiastes 6|Wealth without enjoyment|A man may have everything and not be able to enjoy it.|The power to enjoy is itself a gift from God.
ecclesiastes 7|Wisdom in a crooked world|Better the house of mourning than the house of feasting. Do not ask why former days were better. God made man upright, but they have sought out many schemes.|Thinking about death makes us wise.
ecclesiastes 8|What we cannot understand|Obey the king. The wicked are sometimes treated as the just deserve. No one can find out all that God does.|Humility before mystery.
ecclesiastes 9|One fate for all|The same death comes to everyone. "Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might." The race is not to the swift.|Use the present well.
ecclesiastes 10|A little folly|Dead flies spoil perfume; a little folly outweighs wisdom. Sayings on rulers, laziness and careless speech.|Small faults can ruin much good.
ecclesiastes 11|Cast your bread on the waters|Be generous and take risks, for you do not know what will succeed. Young people should rejoice and remember that God will judge.|Act without waiting for certainty.
ecclesiastes 12|Remember your Creator|Remember God in your youth, before old age and death. The conclusion: "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man."|The answer to the book's question lies beyond "under the sun".
song-of-songs 1|The bride longs for her beloved|"Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth." The bride and bridegroom praise each other.|A love poem that Jewish and Christian tradition reads as the love between God and his people, Christ and the soul.
song-of-songs 2|Arise, my love|"I am the lily of the valleys." The beloved comes leaping over the hills: "Winter is past; arise and come away."|God calls the soul out of its winter.
song-of-songs 3|I sought him|The bride searches the city by night for him whom her soul loves, finds him and will not let go. Solomon's wedding procession follows.|The saints read this as the soul's search for God in prayer.
song-of-songs 4|You are all fair|The bridegroom praises the bride: "You are all fair, my love; there is no flaw in you. A garden enclosed is my sister, my bride."|The Church applies these words to Mary.
song-of-songs 5|I slept, but my heart was awake|The beloved knocks. She delays, and when she opens he is gone. She searches and describes him to the daughters of Jerusalem.|Slowness to answer God's call brings a time of dryness.
song-of-songs 6|My beloved is mine|The lovers are reunited. "Who is this that looks forth like the dawn, fair as the moon, bright as the sun?"|Also used in the liturgy of Our Lady.
song-of-songs 7|The beauty of the bride|The bridegroom praises her from feet to head. She answers, "I am my beloved's, and his desire is for me."|The joy of married love is good and holy.
song-of-songs 8|Love strong as death|"Set me as a seal upon your heart, for love is strong as death. Many waters cannot quench love."|Real love cannot be bought and does not end.
