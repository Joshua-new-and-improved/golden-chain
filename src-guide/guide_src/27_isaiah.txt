isaiah 1|A rebellious nation|God charges Judah: even an ox knows its owner, but my people do not know me. Their sacrifices mean nothing while their hands are full of blood. "Though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be white as snow."|God wants justice and repentance, not empty ritual, and he offers full forgiveness.
isaiah 2|The mountain of the Lord|In days to come all nations will stream to the Lord's house and beat swords into ploughshares. But first comes a day when all human pride is brought low.|Peace comes when the nations learn God's ways.
isaiah 3|Judgement on Jerusalem|God will remove Judah's leaders and leave it to incompetent rulers, because they crush the poor. The proud, wealthy women will lose their finery.|God holds leaders accountable for how they treat the poor.
isaiah 4|The Branch of the Lord|After judgement, a holy remnant will remain in Zion, cleansed, sheltered by God's cloud and fire.|Judgement purifies; it is not the end.
isaiah 5|The song of the vineyard|God planted a vineyard and cared for it, but it gave wild grapes: he looked for justice and found bloodshed. Six woes follow against greed, drunkenness and calling evil good.|Jesus builds his parable of the tenants on this song.
isaiah 6|Isaiah's call|Isaiah sees the Lord enthroned, with seraphim crying "Holy, holy, holy". He feels ruined, a man of unclean lips. A burning coal touches his mouth, and he answers, "Here am I; send me."|The Sanctus at Mass comes from here. Meeting God's holiness shows us our sin and then sends us.
isaiah 7|The sign of Immanuel|King Ahaz is terrified by two enemy kings. Isaiah tells him to trust and offers a sign: "The virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."|Matthew sees this fulfilled in the birth of Jesus from the Virgin Mary.
isaiah 8|Fear God, not conspiracies|Isaiah's son is given a name meaning swift plunder. Assyria will flood the land. The people are told to fear the Lord, not what everyone else fears, and not to consult mediums.|"To the teaching and to the testimony!"
isaiah 9|A child is born|The people walking in darkness have seen a great light. A child is born, called Wonderful Counsellor, Mighty God, Prince of Peace. The chapter then warns arrogant Israel.|Read at Christmas midnight Mass.
isaiah 10|Assyria, the rod of God's anger|Woe to those who write unjust laws. Assyria is God's tool for punishment but boasts as if the power were its own, so it too will be cut down. A remnant will return.|God uses the nations and still judges their pride.
isaiah 11|The shoot from Jesse|A new king will spring from David's family, filled with the Spirit of wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge and fear of the Lord. Wolf and lamb will live together.|The gifts of the Holy Spirit are drawn from this passage.
isaiah 12|A song of thanks|"God is my salvation; I will trust and not be afraid. With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation."|A short hymn sung by the saved.
isaiah 13|Against Babylon|The day of the Lord will come on Babylon; the proud city will be left to wild animals.|No empire is permanent.
isaiah 14|The fall of the tyrant|Israel will taunt the king of Babylon, who said, "I will ascend to heaven," and is brought down to the grave. Oracles on Assyria and Philistia follow.|Tradition has also read this as a picture of Satan's fall through pride.
isaiah 15|Against Moab|Moab's towns are ruined in a night, and its people weep.|The prophet himself grieves for Israel's enemy.
isaiah 16|Moab's pride|Moab seeks shelter in Zion. Its pride is its downfall.|A throne of mercy is promised in the tent of David.
isaiah 17|Against Damascus|Damascus and northern Israel will be ruined together, because they forgot God their rock.|A few gleanings will remain, and people will look to their Maker.
isaiah 18|Against Cush|Envoys come from a land beyond the rivers of Ethiopia. God watches quietly and will act at harvest time.|Distant nations will one day bring gifts to Zion.
isaiah 19|Egypt will know the Lord|Egypt will fall into civil war and drought. Then an altar to the Lord will stand in Egypt, and God will say, "Blessed be Egypt my people."|Israel's old enemies are included in God's blessing.
isaiah 20|A sign against Egypt|Isaiah walks barefoot and stripped for three years as a sign that Assyria will lead Egypt's captives away.|Do not put your hope in Egypt.
isaiah 21|Babylon has fallen|A watchman sees riders coming and cries, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon." Short oracles on Edom and Arabia follow.|Revelation echoes this cry.
isaiah 22|The key of the house of David|Jerusalem parties while disaster approaches. The proud steward Shebna is replaced by Eliakim, who receives the key of David's house: what he opens, none shall shut.|Jesus uses this image when he gives Peter the keys.
isaiah 23|Against Tyre|The rich trading city of Tyre will be humbled.|Wealth is no security.
isaiah 24|The earth laid waste|The whole earth is devastated because its people have broken the everlasting covenant. The Lord will reign on Mount Zion.|Judgement is worldwide, and so is God's kingship.
isaiah 25|The feast on the mountain|God will prepare a banquet for all peoples, swallow up death for ever and wipe away tears from every face.|Read at funerals. Death will be destroyed.
isaiah 26|A song of trust|"You keep him in perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you." Your dead shall live; their bodies shall rise.|An early Old Testament hope of resurrection.
isaiah 27|The vineyard restored|God will slay Leviathan and tend his vineyard, Israel, so that it fills the world with fruit.|The ruined vineyard of chapter 5 is made fruitful.
isaiah 28|The cornerstone|Woe to the drunken leaders of Ephraim and the scoffers of Jerusalem who trust in a pact with death. God lays in Zion a tested cornerstone.|The New Testament applies the cornerstone to Christ.
isaiah 29|Lips without heart|Jerusalem will be besieged and then delivered. "This people honours me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me." The deaf will hear and the blind see.|Jesus quotes this against hypocrisy.
isaiah 30|Do not run to Egypt|Judah seeks Egypt's help without asking God. "In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and trust shall be your strength." The Lord waits to be gracious.|Salvation comes from trusting, not from frantic schemes.
isaiah 31|Horses are flesh|Woe to those who rely on Egypt's horses and chariots. The Egyptians are men, not God.|God himself will protect Jerusalem.
isaiah 32|A just king|A king will reign in righteousness. The Spirit will be poured out from on high, and justice will bring peace.|Peace is the fruit of justice.
isaiah 33|The Lord is our king|The destroyer will be destroyed. Who can live with the consuming fire? The one who walks uprightly. "Your eyes will see the king in his beauty."|The pure of heart will see God.
isaiah 34|Judgement on the nations|God's sword falls on the nations, especially Edom, whose land becomes a wilderness.|A dark picture of the end of God's enemies.
isaiah 35|The desert blooms|The wilderness will blossom. The eyes of the blind will be opened and the lame leap like a deer. The redeemed will walk the Holy Way home with singing.|Jesus points to these signs when John the Baptist asks who he is.
isaiah 36|Sennacherib's threat|The Assyrian commander stands outside Jerusalem and mocks the people's trust in God.|The enemy's argument: your God cannot save you.
isaiah 37|Hezekiah prays|Hezekiah spreads the threatening letter before the Lord. Isaiah promises deliverance, and the Assyrian army is struck down.|Bring the threat straight to God.
isaiah 38|Hezekiah's illness|Told to prepare for death, Hezekiah prays and weeps, and God adds fifteen years. He writes a song of thanks.|"You have cast all my sins behind your back."
isaiah 39|Envoys from Babylon|Hezekiah shows his treasures to Babylonian visitors. Isaiah foretells that all of it will be carried to Babylon.|Vanity opens the door to future loss.
isaiah 40|Comfort my people|"Comfort, comfort my people." A voice cries, "Prepare the way of the Lord." All flesh is grass, but God's word stands for ever. Those who wait for the Lord will rise on wings like eagles.|The Gospels apply the voice in the wilderness to John the Baptist.
isaiah 41|Fear not|God calls a conqueror from the east and tells Israel, "Fear not, for I am with you; I will uphold you." The idols can say nothing.|God holds his people by the hand.
isaiah 42|The servant of the Lord|"Behold my servant, in whom my soul delights." He will not break a bruised reed. He will bring justice and be a light to the nations.|The first Servant Song, applied to Jesus at his baptism.
isaiah 43|I have called you by name|"Fear not, for I have redeemed you; you are mine. When you pass through the waters, I will be with you." God is doing a new thing and blots out sins for his own sake.|God's love for his people is personal.
isaiah 44|No other God|God will pour his Spirit on Israel's children. A craftsman burns half a log for warmth and makes a god from the rest. Cyrus is named as the one who will rebuild Jerusalem.|Idolatry is shown up as absurd.
isaiah 45|Cyrus, God's anointed|God calls the pagan king Cyrus his anointed and uses him to free Israel. "Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth. To me every knee shall bow."|God is Lord of history and of every nation.
isaiah 46|Gods that must be carried|Babylon's idols are loaded onto animals. Israel's God says, "I have carried you since birth; even to your old age I will carry you."|False gods are a burden; the true God carries us.
isaiah 47|Babylon humbled|Babylon, who said "I am, and there is no one besides me," will sit in the dust. Her sorcerers cannot save her.|Pride and cruelty bring empires down.
isaiah 48|Leave Babylon|God foretold these things so that Israel could not credit its idols. He has refined them in the furnace of affliction. "Go out from Babylon!"|"O that you had heeded my commandments! Your peace would have been like a river."
isaiah 49|A light to the nations|The servant, called from the womb, is to restore Israel and bring salvation to the ends of the earth. "Can a woman forget her nursing child? Even if she could, I will not forget you."|The second Servant Song. God's love is stronger than a mother's.
isaiah 50|The servant's obedience|"I gave my back to those who struck me; I did not hide my face from shame and spitting." The Lord God helps him.|The third Servant Song, read in Holy Week.
isaiah 51|Awake!|Look to Abraham, the rock from which you were cut. God's salvation lasts for ever. The cup of wrath is taken from Jerusalem's hand.|Remember what God has already done.
isaiah 52|Good news for Zion|"How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news." The fourth Servant Song begins: the servant will be lifted up, though his appearance was disfigured.|The messenger announces, "Your God reigns."
isaiah 53|The suffering servant|He was despised and rejected, a man of sorrows. He was pierced for our transgressions; by his wounds we are healed. Like a lamb led to slaughter he did not open his mouth.|The clearest Old Testament prophecy of Christ's Passion. Read on Good Friday.
isaiah 54|Sing, barren one|The abandoned wife will have many children. "With everlasting love I will have compassion on you. The mountains may depart, but my love shall not."|God's covenant of peace cannot be shaken.
isaiah 55|Come to the waters|"Come, buy wine and milk without money." Seek the Lord while he may be found. "My thoughts are not your thoughts." God's word does not return empty.|Grace is free. God's word always achieves its purpose.
isaiah 56|A house of prayer for all peoples|Foreigners and eunuchs who hold to the covenant will be welcomed. The chapter ends with a rebuke of lazy watchmen.|Jesus quotes this when he cleanses the Temple.
isaiah 57|Peace for the contrite|Idolatry is condemned. The High and Holy One dwells with the crushed and lowly in spirit, to revive them.|"There is no peace for the wicked."
isaiah 58|True fasting|The people fast and quarrel. The fast God chooses is to loose the bonds of injustice, share bread with the hungry and shelter the homeless.|Read at the start of Lent. Penance without charity is worthless.
isaiah 59|Sin separates|God's arm is not too short to save; your sins have hidden his face. Truth has stumbled in the streets. God himself puts on righteousness and comes as Redeemer.|When no one else can help, God acts.
isaiah 60|Arise, shine|"Arise, shine, for your light has come." Nations and kings come to Jerusalem's light bringing gold and frankincense.|Read on the Epiphany; the Magi fulfil it.
isaiah 61|The Spirit of the Lord is upon me|"He has anointed me to bring good news to the poor, to bind up the broken-hearted, to proclaim liberty to captives."|Jesus reads this in Nazareth and says, "Today this is fulfilled."
isaiah 62|A new name|Zion will no longer be called Forsaken. "As a bridegroom rejoices over his bride, so shall your God rejoice over you."|God delights in his people.
isaiah 63|The winepress and a prayer|God comes from Edom, his garments stained from treading the winepress alone. The people recall his mercies and pray, "You, O Lord, are our Father."|Judgement and mercy belong to the same God.
isaiah 64|Rend the heavens|"O that you would tear the heavens open and come down!" We are the clay, and you are the potter.|An Advent prayer, answered in the Incarnation.
isaiah 65|New heavens and a new earth|God held out his hands all day to a rebellious people. He will create new heavens and a new earth, where weeping is heard no more.|Revelation takes up this promise.
isaiah 66|The final gathering|Heaven is God's throne; he looks to the humble and contrite. Jerusalem will be comforted like a child by its mother. All nations will come to see God's glory.|The book ends with worship from every people and a warning of judgement.
