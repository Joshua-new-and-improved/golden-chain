jeremiah 1|The call of Jeremiah|"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you." Jeremiah protests that he is too young. God touches his mouth and appoints him over nations, to pluck up and to plant.|God calls before we are ready, and promises, "I am with you."
jeremiah 2|Broken cisterns|God remembers Israel's early love like a bride's. Now they have forsaken him, the fountain of living water, and dug leaking cisterns for themselves.|Sin swaps the real source of life for things that cannot hold water.
jeremiah 3|Return, faithless Israel|Israel and Judah have behaved like unfaithful wives. Still God calls, "Return, and I will not be angry for ever. I will give you shepherds after my own heart."|God's appeal is that of wounded love.
jeremiah 4|Disaster from the north|"Break up your fallow ground; circumcise your hearts." An invader is coming like a lion. Jeremiah writhes in anguish.|The prophet feels the pain he announces.
jeremiah 5|Not one just person|Search Jerusalem's streets for one who does justice. The people have eyes and do not see; prophets lie, and "my people love to have it so".|A society can prefer comforting lies.
jeremiah 6|The ancient paths|Jerusalem will be besieged. The leaders say "Peace, peace" when there is no peace. "Ask for the ancient paths, where the good way is, and walk in it."|Rest for the soul lies in the old, tested way.
jeremiah 7|The Temple sermon|At the Temple gate Jeremiah warns: do not chant "the temple of the Lord" while stealing, murdering and worshipping idols. You have made this house a den of robbers.|Jesus quotes this. Sacred places do not protect unrepentant people.
jeremiah 8|No balm in Gilead|Even storks know their seasons, but the people do not know God's ways. "The harvest is past, the summer is ended, and we are not saved."|Jeremiah's heart breaks for his people.
jeremiah 9|Boast in knowing God|"O that my head were a spring of water," to weep day and night. Let the wise not boast in wisdom, might or riches, but in knowing the Lord.|What matters is knowing God, who loves kindness and justice.
jeremiah 10|Scarecrows in a cucumber field|Idols are carved, decorated and nailed down; they cannot speak or walk. The Lord is the true and living God.|"It is not in man to direct his own steps."
jeremiah 11|The covenant broken|Judah has broken the covenant and will face its curses. Jeremiah's own townsmen plot to kill him.|The prophet is like a gentle lamb led to slaughter.
jeremiah 12|Why do the wicked prosper?|Jeremiah complains to God. God answers: if running with men tires you, how will you compete with horses?|Honest questions are allowed. Harder trials are coming.
jeremiah 13|The ruined loincloth|Jeremiah hides a linen cloth by the river and finds it rotten. So God will ruin Judah's pride. "Can the leopard change his spots?"|Israel was meant to cling to God as closely as a garment.
jeremiah 14|Drought and false prophets|In a terrible drought the people plead. God says the prophets promising peace are lying in his name.|Not everyone who claims to speak for God does.
jeremiah 15|Jeremiah's complaint|Jeremiah regrets being born to a life of conflict. "Your words were found, and I ate them, and they became my joy." God promises to make him a wall of bronze.|God's word sustains the lonely prophet.
jeremiah 16|No wife, no feast|Jeremiah is forbidden to marry or attend funerals and feasts, as a sign of what is coming. Yet God will one day bring Israel back.|The prophet's whole life becomes the message.
jeremiah 17|The heart is deceitful|Cursed is the one who trusts in man; blessed the one who trusts in the Lord, like a tree by water. The heart is deceitful above all things. Keep the Sabbath.|Only God fully knows the human heart.
jeremiah 18|The potter|Jeremiah watches a potter rework a spoiled pot. Israel is clay in God's hand: he can change his plan if a nation repents.|It is not too late while the clay is soft.
jeremiah 19|The broken jar|Jeremiah smashes a clay jar before the elders: so will God break this city.|Once hardened, the pot cannot be remade.
jeremiah 20|Fire in my bones|The priest Pashhur beats Jeremiah and puts him in the stocks. Jeremiah cries, "You deceived me, Lord." Yet if he tries to keep silent, the word is like fire shut up in his bones.|Faithfulness can feel like this.
jeremiah 21|Life or death|King Zedekiah asks whether God will rescue them. Jeremiah answers that the city will fall; those who surrender will live.|An unwelcome truth is still the truth.
jeremiah 22|Against the kings|Jeremiah addresses Judah's kings: do justice. He condemns Jehoiakim for building a palace with unpaid labour.|"Your father did justice; is not this to know me?"
jeremiah 23|The righteous Branch|Woe to shepherds who scatter the flock. God will raise up for David a righteous Branch, called "The Lord is our righteousness". False prophets speak visions of their own minds.|The good Shepherd-King is promised.
jeremiah 24|Two baskets of figs|Good figs stand for the exiles, whom God will bring back and give a heart to know him. Rotten figs stand for those left with Zedekiah.|The exile is, surprisingly, where hope lies.
jeremiah 25|Seventy years|Because Judah has not listened for twenty-three years, it will serve Babylon seventy years. All nations must drink the cup of God's wrath.|Judgement has a limit set by God.
jeremiah 26|Jeremiah on trial|After the Temple sermon priests and prophets demand his death. Officials recall the prophet Micah and spare him.|Speaking God's word can put your life at risk.
jeremiah 27|The yoke|Jeremiah wears a wooden yoke and tells the kings to submit to Babylon.|Accepting God's discipline is the way to survive it.
jeremiah 28|Hananiah|The prophet Hananiah breaks the yoke and promises freedom within two years. Jeremiah says he has made the people trust a lie.|The pleasant prophecy was the false one.
jeremiah 29|A letter to the exiles|Build houses, plant gardens, and pray for the city where you live. After seventy years God will bring you back. "I know the plans I have for you: plans for welfare, to give you a future and a hope."|God's good plans include a long wait.
jeremiah 30|Restoration promised|A time of distress is coming for Jacob, but he will be saved from it. "I will restore health to you and heal your wounds."|God wounds in order to heal.
jeremiah 31|The new covenant|"I have loved you with an everlasting love." Rachel weeps for her children. God will make a new covenant, write his law on hearts, and remember sins no more.|Jesus names this covenant at the Last Supper.
jeremiah 32|Buying a field|With the city under siege and himself in prison, Jeremiah buys a field at God's command.|An act of hope: fields will again be bought in this land.
jeremiah 33|Promises renewed|"Call to me and I will answer you." The city will again hear the voices of joy, and David will never lack an heir.|God's covenant is as fixed as day and night.
jeremiah 34|Freedom taken back|During the siege the people free their Hebrew slaves, then force them back when danger eases.|Repentance that lasts only as long as the emergency.
jeremiah 35|The Rechabites|A clan keeps its ancestor's command never to drink wine. Judah will not keep God's.|Human loyalty shames Israel's disloyalty.
jeremiah 36|The burned scroll|Baruch writes Jeremiah's words and reads them out. King Jehoiakim cuts the scroll up column by column and burns it. Jeremiah dictates it again.|God's word cannot be destroyed.
jeremiah 37|Jeremiah imprisoned|Accused of deserting, Jeremiah is beaten and jailed. Zedekiah asks him secretly, "Is there any word from the Lord?"|The king wants a word from God and lacks the courage to obey it.
jeremiah 38|In the cistern|Officials lower Jeremiah into a muddy cistern to die. Ebed-melech, an Ethiopian court official, pulls him out with ropes.|A foreigner shows the courage Judah's leaders lack.
jeremiah 39|Jerusalem falls|The Babylonians break in. Zedekiah is captured and blinded. Jeremiah is freed.|Everything Jeremiah said has come true.
jeremiah 40|Gedaliah governor|Jeremiah chooses to stay with the poor left in the land under the governor Gedaliah.|A small new beginning.
jeremiah 41|Gedaliah murdered|Ishmael assassinates the governor and many others.|Violence wrecks the fragile peace.
jeremiah 42|Do not go to Egypt|The survivors ask Jeremiah to pray for guidance and promise to obey. The answer is to stay in the land.|They ask for God's will with their minds made up.
jeremiah 43|Taken to Egypt|The leaders call Jeremiah a liar and take everyone to Egypt, including him.|Asking God and then ignoring the answer.
jeremiah 44|The queen of heaven|In Egypt the people insist on offering to a pagan goddess, claiming things went better when they did.|The exile has taught them nothing.
jeremiah 45|A word for Baruch|Jeremiah's weary secretary is told, "Do you seek great things for yourself? Seek them not." His life will be spared.|God notices the discouraged helper.
jeremiah 46|Against Egypt|Egypt is defeated by Babylon at Carchemish and will be invaded.|Israel is told not to fear.
jeremiah 47|Against the Philistines|Waters rise from the north to overwhelm the Philistine cities.|God's sword does not rest until its work is done.
jeremiah 48|Against Moab|Moab, long at ease like undisturbed wine, will be poured out for its pride.|Even here God promises to restore Moab in the end.
jeremiah 49|Against other nations|Oracles on Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar and Elam.|Every nation answers to God.
jeremiah 50|Against Babylon|The hammer of the whole earth will be broken. Israel and Judah will come home weeping, seeking the Lord.|The instrument of judgement is judged in turn.
jeremiah 51|Babylon's doom|Babylon will sink and never rise. Jeremiah has the scroll tied to a stone and thrown into the Euphrates.|"Flee from Babylon; let each save his life."
jeremiah 52|The fall of Jerusalem|A historical appendix: the siege, the burning of the Temple, the deportations, and the later release of King Jehoiachin.|The record proves the prophet right and closes with a small hope.
lamentations 1|The lonely city|Jerusalem sits like a widow, weeping in the night, with no one to comfort her. "Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by?"|The Church uses these words for Christ's Passion.
lamentations 2|The Lord's anger|God himself has acted like an enemy: Temple, walls and festivals are gone, and children faint in the streets.|Grief is poured out to the same God who struck.
lamentations 3|New every morning|A man who has seen affliction remembers: "The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases; his mercies are new every morning."|Hope appears at the centre of the darkest book.
lamentations 4|The horrors of the siege|Gold has grown dim. Children beg for bread, and those raised in luxury lie on ash heaps.|Sin's consequences are described without softening.
lamentations 5|A prayer for mercy|The people tell God their disgrace and ask, "Restore us to yourself, O Lord, that we may be restored."|Even return to God is his gift.
baruch 1|A confession from exile|Baruch reads his book to the exiles in Babylon. They weep, send money to Jerusalem and confess, "We have sinned."|Exile leads to honest confession.
baruch 2|God was right|The confession continues: all that Moses warned has happened. They ask for mercy for God's own sake.|The prayer recalls the promise of return and a new heart.
baruch 3|The source of wisdom|A cry from the depths, then a poem: Israel is in exile because it left the fountain of wisdom. No one has found wisdom's path but God.|"Afterwards he was seen on earth and conversed with men": the Fathers read this of the Incarnation.
baruch 4|Take courage, my children|Wisdom is the book of God's commandments. Jerusalem, like a mother, mourns her children and tells them to cry to God.|The one who brought the disaster will bring joy.
baruch 5|Put on the robe of glory|"Take off the garment of sorrow, Jerusalem." God will level mountains and lead Israel home with joy.|Read in Advent.
baruch 6|The letter of Jeremiah|A letter to those going into exile: Babylon's gods are wood covered in gold, cannot wipe the dust from their own faces, and should not be feared.|Do not be impressed by impressive idols.
