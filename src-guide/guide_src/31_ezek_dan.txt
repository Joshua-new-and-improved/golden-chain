ezekiel 1|The vision of God's glory|In exile by the river Chebar, the priest Ezekiel sees a storm cloud, four living creatures with four faces, wheels within wheels full of eyes, and above them a throne with a figure like a man.|God's glory is not tied to Jerusalem; it comes to the exiles.
ezekiel 2|Sent to a rebellious people|A voice tells Ezekiel to stand and sends him to Israel. He is to speak whether they listen or not. A scroll full of lament is held out to him.|The prophet's task is faithfulness, not success.
ezekiel 3|The watchman|Ezekiel eats the scroll, and it is sweet as honey. God makes him a watchman: if he fails to warn the wicked, he will answer for their blood.|Those who know the truth are responsible for passing it on.
ezekiel 4|The siege acted out|Ezekiel draws Jerusalem on a brick, lays siege to it, lies on his side for many days and eats rationed bread.|A message acted out for people who have stopped listening.
ezekiel 5|The hair divided|Ezekiel shaves his head and burns, strikes and scatters the hair in thirds.|A picture of what will happen to Jerusalem's people.
ezekiel 6|Against the mountains|The hilltop shrines will be destroyed. A remnant in exile will remember God.|"They shall know that I am the Lord," the book's refrain.
ezekiel 7|The end has come|Disaster is at the door. Silver and gold will be thrown in the streets, unable to save.|Wealth is useless on the day of judgement.
ezekiel 8|Abominations in the Temple|In a vision Ezekiel is carried to Jerusalem and shown idols and pagan rites inside the Temple itself.|The worst idolatry is inside God's own house.
ezekiel 9|The mark on the forehead|A man with a writing case marks the foreheads of those who grieve over the city's sins. The rest are struck down.|The mark, the letter tau, has been seen as a figure of the cross.
ezekiel 10|The glory moves|Burning coals are scattered over the city. The glory of the Lord rises from the Temple to its threshold.|God is leaving the house his people have defiled.
ezekiel 11|A new heart promised|Corrupt leaders are judged. To the exiles God promises, "I will remove the heart of stone and give them a heart of flesh." The glory leaves the city.|God himself will be a sanctuary for the scattered.
ezekiel 12|Packing for exile|Ezekiel digs through a wall and carries out his baggage in the dark as a sign.|The people say the vision is for far-off days; God says it is now.
ezekiel 13|Whitewashed walls|False prophets cry "Peace" and paint over a cracked wall. It will fall.|Comforting lies do real harm.
ezekiel 14|Idols in the heart|Elders come to consult Ezekiel with idols set up in their hearts. Even Noah, Daniel and Job could save only themselves.|No one can lean on another's holiness.
ezekiel 15|The useless vine|Vine wood is good for nothing but burning. So is unfaithful Jerusalem.|A vine exists to bear fruit.
ezekiel 16|The unfaithful bride|God found Jerusalem as an abandoned baby, raised her, married her and adorned her. She used his gifts for prostitution. Yet he will remember his covenant.|A shocking picture of ingratitude and of mercy greater still.
ezekiel 17|Two eagles and a vine|A riddle about the king of Judah breaking his oath to Babylon and turning to Egypt.|God will plant a tender shoot that becomes a great cedar.
ezekiel 18|Each answers for himself|The people blame their parents' sins. God answers: the soul that sins shall die; a wicked person who turns will live.|"I have no pleasure in the death of anyone. Turn, and live."
ezekiel 19|A lament for the princes|Judah is a lioness whose cubs were captured, and a vine torn up.|A funeral song for the royal house.
ezekiel 20|A history of rebellion|God reviews Israel's story from Egypt on: rebellion at every stage, and God sparing them for his name's sake.|He will bring them into the bond of the covenant.
ezekiel 21|The sword|God's sword is sharpened and drawn against Jerusalem. The king's crown is removed "until he comes whose right it is".|A hint of the Messiah in the middle of judgement.
ezekiel 22|No one in the gap|Jerusalem's sins are listed: bloodshed, contempt for parents, oppression of the stranger. God looked for someone to stand in the breach and found none.|Christ becomes the one who stands in the gap.
ezekiel 23|Two sisters|Samaria and Jerusalem are pictured as two sisters who chased foreign lovers.|Alliances with idolatrous powers end in ruin.
ezekiel 24|The death of Ezekiel's wife|The siege begins. Ezekiel's wife, the delight of his eyes, dies, and he is told not to mourn, as a sign.|The prophet's own loss mirrors the loss of the Temple.
ezekiel 25|Against the neighbours|Ammon, Moab, Edom and Philistia are judged for gloating over Judah's fall.|God sees how we react to others' misfortune.
ezekiel 26|Against Tyre|Tyre rejoiced at Jerusalem's ruin and will be scraped bare like a rock.|Profit from another's disaster is condemned.
ezekiel 27|The ship of Tyre|Tyre is a splendid merchant ship, loaded with the world's goods, that sinks in the heart of the sea.|Commercial glory is fragile.
ezekiel 28|The prince of Tyre|The ruler who said "I am a god" will die like a man. He was in Eden, perfect, until pride was found in him.|Tradition has read this as an image of Satan's fall.
ezekiel 29|Against Egypt|Pharaoh, the great dragon of the Nile, will be hooked and thrown into the desert. Egypt was a staff of reed to Israel.|Do not lean on what cannot hold you.
ezekiel 30|Egypt's day|The day of the Lord comes for Egypt and its allies. Pharaoh's arm is broken.|Human power is in God's hand.
ezekiel 31|The great cedar|Assyria was a towering cedar, cut down for its pride. Pharaoh will be the same.|Greatness that forgets God falls.
ezekiel 32|Lament for Pharaoh|Pharaoh goes down to the pit to join the fallen armies of other nations.|Every empire ends in the same grave.
ezekiel 33|News of the fall|Ezekiel is again appointed watchman. A fugitive arrives: the city has fallen. The people enjoy hearing Ezekiel but do not act.|Hearing sermons like love songs changes nothing.
ezekiel 34|The good shepherd|Israel's shepherds fed themselves, not the flock. "I myself will search for my sheep. I will seek the lost and bind up the injured."|Jesus claims this role in John 10.
ezekiel 35|Against Mount Seir|Edom nursed an ancient hatred and wanted Israel's land.|Old grudges bring their own ruin.
ezekiel 36|Clean water and a new heart|"I will sprinkle clean water on you. I will give you a new heart and put my Spirit within you."|The Church reads this of baptism and the gift of the Spirit.
ezekiel 37|The valley of dry bones|Ezekiel prophesies over a valley of bones. They come together, take flesh, and breath enters them. Two sticks become one: Israel and Judah reunited under one shepherd.|God can raise what is dead. Read at the Easter Vigil.
ezekiel 38|Gog|In the latter days Gog of Magog leads a horde against peaceful Israel. God himself fights him.|The final assault of evil fails.
ezekiel 39|Gog defeated|Gog's army falls; its weapons burn for seven years. God will no longer hide his face.|Revelation uses Gog and Magog for the last rebellion.
ezekiel 40|The vision of the new Temple|Fourteen years after the fall, a man with a measuring rod shows Ezekiel a new Temple: gates and courts.|Hope is given a precise shape.
ezekiel 41|The sanctuary measured|The inner rooms, the Holy of Holies and the carved decoration are measured.|God's dwelling will be restored.
ezekiel 42|The priests' rooms|Chambers where priests eat the holy offerings and change garments.|The holy is kept distinct from the common.
ezekiel 43|The glory returns|The glory of God comes from the east and fills the Temple. "This is the place of my throne."|The departure in chapter 10 is reversed.
ezekiel 44|The closed gate|The east gate stays shut because the Lord has entered by it. Rules are given for priests.|The Fathers saw in the closed gate a figure of Mary's perpetual virginity.
ezekiel 45|Holy land and honest scales|Land is set apart for the sanctuary and the prince. Use just balances.|Worship and honesty belong together.
ezekiel 46|The prince and the people|Rules for Sabbath worship and the prince's offerings.|Ruler and people worship together.
ezekiel 47|The river from the Temple|Water trickles from the threshold and becomes a river too deep to cross, making the Dead Sea fresh. Trees on its banks bear fruit every month.|A figure of grace flowing from Christ. Revelation ends with the same river.
ezekiel 48|The Lord is there|The land is divided among the twelve tribes. The city's name will be "The Lord is there".|The goal of everything: God with his people.
daniel 1|Four young men in Babylon|Daniel and three friends are taken to serve at court. They refuse the king's food to keep God's law and thrive on vegetables and water.|Faithfulness in small things when far from home.
daniel 2|The statue|Nebuchadnezzar dreams of a statue of gold, silver, bronze and iron, shattered by a stone that becomes a mountain. Daniel explains: four kingdoms, then God's kingdom that lasts for ever.|Christ is the stone not cut by human hands.
daniel 3|The fiery furnace|Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refuse to worship a golden statue and are thrown into a furnace. A fourth figure walks with them, and they sing a hymn calling all creation to bless the Lord.|"Even if he does not save us, we will not serve your gods." The hymn is prayed in the Church's morning prayer.
daniel 4|The king humbled|Nebuchadnezzar dreams of a great tree cut down. He loses his reason and lives like an animal until he acknowledges that the Most High rules.|Pride is a kind of madness.
daniel 5|The writing on the wall|King Belshazzar drinks from the Temple vessels. A hand writes on the wall: weighed and found wanting. That night Babylon falls.|Sacrilege and arrogance are judged.
daniel 6|The lions' den|Jealous officials get prayer banned. Daniel keeps praying three times a day with his windows open and is thrown to the lions. An angel shuts their mouths.|Daniel changes nothing in his prayer under threat.
daniel 7|Four beasts and the Son of Man|Four beasts rise from the sea. The Ancient of Days takes his throne, and "one like a son of man" comes on the clouds and receives everlasting kingship.|Jesus takes his favourite title, Son of Man, from here.
daniel 8|The ram and the goat|A goat from the west breaks a two-horned ram; a small horn then attacks the sanctuary.|Persia, Greece, and a persecuting king, with an end set by God.
daniel 9|Seventy weeks|Daniel confesses the people's sins. Gabriel announces seventy weeks of years until an anointed one comes and is cut off.|Christians have read this of the coming and death of Christ.
daniel 10|A vision by the Tigris|After three weeks of fasting Daniel sees a dazzling man and is strengthened. The messenger had been delayed by a struggle with a heavenly "prince".|Prayer is heard from the first day, even when the answer is delayed.
daniel 11|Kings of north and south|A detailed forecast of wars between two dynasties, ending with a king who profanes the Temple and persecutes the faithful.|"The people who know their God shall stand firm."
daniel 12|Those who sleep shall awake|Michael will arise. Many who sleep in the dust will awake, some to everlasting life, some to shame. The wise will shine like stars.|One of the clearest Old Testament statements of the resurrection.
daniel 13|Susanna|Two elders try to force the virtuous Susanna and, refused, accuse her of adultery. Young Daniel questions them separately and exposes the lie.|Susanna chooses death over sin, and God defends the innocent.
daniel 14|Bel and the dragon|Daniel shows with scattered ashes that priests, not the idol Bel, eat its food. He kills a serpent the Babylonians worship and survives the lions' den again.|Idols are exposed with a little common sense.
