hosea 1|Hosea's marriage|God tells Hosea to marry an unfaithful woman, Gomer. Their children's names mean "Not pitied" and "Not my people".|Hosea's broken marriage is a living picture of God and Israel.
hosea 2|I will win her back|Israel chased other lovers who she thought gave her bread and wine. God will lead her into the desert and speak to her heart.|"I will betroth you to me for ever."
hosea 3|Bought back|Hosea buys his wife back and takes her home.|God redeems his people at a price.
hosea 4|No knowledge of God|There is no faithfulness or kindness in the land. "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge," and the priests are to blame.|Ignorance of God ruins a people.
hosea 5|Judgement on the leaders|Priests and kings have been a snare. God withdraws until they seek his face.|God's absence is meant to bring them back.
hosea 6|Mercy, not sacrifice|"Come, let us return to the Lord." But their love is like morning mist. "I desire mercy and not sacrifice."|Jesus quotes this twice.
hosea 7|A half-baked cake|Israel is like a cake not turned, a silly dove flitting between Egypt and Assyria.|They cry out, but not to God from the heart.
hosea 8|Sowing the wind|They made kings and idols without God. "They sow the wind and reap the whirlwind."|Choices made without God have a harvest.
hosea 9|Days of punishment|Israel will not stay in the Lord's land. The prophet is called a fool.|Rejecting the messenger does not cancel the message.
hosea 10|Break up your fallow ground|Israel is a luxuriant vine that multiplied altars. "Sow righteousness, reap mercy; it is time to seek the Lord."|It is not too late to plough again.
hosea 11|When Israel was a child|"I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son. I taught them to walk. How can I give you up?"|God's love is that of a parent who cannot let go.
hosea 12|Jacob's example|Like their ancestor Jacob, who wrestled and wept, Israel should return to God.|"Hold fast to love and justice, and wait for your God."
hosea 13|No saviour but me|They grew fat and forgot God. "I will ransom them from the grave. O death, where are your plagues?"|Paul quotes this in triumph over death.
hosea 14|Return, O Israel|"Take words with you and return to the Lord." God answers, "I will heal their faithlessness; I will love them freely."|The book ends with healing.
joel 1|The locusts|A plague of locusts strips the land. Joel calls priests and people to fast and cry to God.|Disaster is read as a call to turn back.
joel 2|Rend your hearts|An army darkens the day of the Lord. "Return to me with all your heart; rend your hearts and not your garments." God promises, "I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh."|Read on Ash Wednesday. Peter quotes the promise at Pentecost.
joel 3|The valley of decision|God gathers the nations for judgement. Zion will be safe, and the mountains will drip sweet wine.|God is a refuge for his people.
amos 1|For three sins and for four|Amos, a shepherd, announces judgement on Israel's neighbours for their war crimes.|God judges all nations by basic justice.
amos 2|Israel is no exception|After Moab and Judah, the list reaches Israel: they sell the poor for a pair of sandals.|The listeners nodding along find themselves accused.
amos 3|You only have I known|"You only have I known of all the families of the earth; therefore I will punish you."|Being chosen brings greater responsibility.
amos 4|Prepare to meet your God|The rich women of Samaria crush the poor. God sent famine, drought and plague, "yet you did not return to me".|God's warnings went unheeded five times.
amos 5|Let justice roll|"Seek me and live." God hates their festivals. "Let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."|Worship without justice is noise.
amos 6|At ease in Zion|Woe to those who lie on beds of ivory and are not grieved over the ruin of their people.|Comfort can deaden conscience.
amos 7|The plumb line|Amos sees locusts, fire and a plumb line. The priest Amaziah tells him to go home. Amos replies that God took him from following the flock.|A layman sent by God outranks an official who silences him.
amos 8|A basket of summer fruit|Israel is ripe for the end. Merchants cheat with false scales. A famine is coming, of hearing the word of the Lord.|The worst famine is losing God's word.
amos 9|David's fallen tent restored|No one can escape judgement. Yet God will raise up the fallen booth of David, and the land will overflow.|James quotes this at the Council of Jerusalem.
obadiah 1|Against Edom|Edom, descended from Esau, gloated and looted when Jerusalem fell. "As you have done, it shall be done to you."|Standing aloof while a brother suffers is itself a sin.
jonah 1|Running from God|God sends Jonah to Nineveh. He sails the other way. A storm rises, the sailors throw him overboard, and a great fish swallows him.|You cannot outrun God's call.
jonah 2|A prayer from the deep|Inside the fish Jonah prays, and after three days it spits him onto land.|Jesus calls this "the sign of Jonah", pointing to his three days in the tomb.
jonah 3|Nineveh repents|Jonah preaches, and the whole city, from king to cattle, fasts in sackcloth. God relents.|Pagans repent at once.
jonah 4|Jonah is angry|Jonah sulks because God was merciful. God gives him a shade plant, lets it wither, and asks why he pities a plant and not a great city.|God's mercy is wider than his prophet likes.
micah 1|Judgement on the capitals|The Lord comes down, and the mountains melt. Samaria and Jerusalem are the centres of the sin.|Corruption starts at the top.
micah 2|Land-grabbers|Woe to those who lie awake planning to seize fields and houses.|God will gather a remnant like sheep.
micah 3|Corrupt leaders|Rulers tear the skin off the people, prophets preach for pay, priests teach for a price. Zion will be ploughed like a field.|Religion for hire brings ruin.
micah 4|Swords into ploughshares|In days to come nations will stream to Zion, and each will sit under his own vine, unafraid.|The same hope as Isaiah 2.
micah 5|From Bethlehem|"You, Bethlehem, too little to be among the clans of Judah, from you shall come a ruler in Israel."|The scribes quote this to Herod when the Magi ask where the Christ is to be born.
micah 6|What does the Lord require?|God pleads his case: what have I done to you? Not thousands of rams, but "to do justice, to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God".|The Good Friday Reproaches echo this. One verse sums up true religion.
micah 7|Who is a God like you?|The godly have vanished, but "when I fall, I shall rise". God delights in mercy and will cast our sins into the depths of the sea.|Sins forgiven are gone for good.
nahum 1|The Lord is slow to anger and great in power|A hymn to God, a stronghold in the day of trouble. Nineveh's end is announced.|Good news for those Assyria oppressed.
nahum 2|Nineveh falls|The attackers come: scarlet shields, racing chariots. The city is plundered.|The lion's den is empty.
nahum 3|Woe to the bloody city|Nineveh, full of lies and plunder, falls and no one mourns.|Cruelty earns no sympathy at the end.
habakkuk 1|How long, O Lord?|Habakkuk asks why God tolerates violence. God answers that he is raising the Babylonians. The prophet objects: they are worse.|An honest argument with God.
habakkuk 2|The just shall live by faith|Habakkuk waits on his watchtower. The answer: the proud will fail, but "the righteous shall live by his faith". Five woes follow.|Paul builds his teaching on this line.
habakkuk 3|Yet I will rejoice|A prayer recalling God's mighty acts. "Though the fig tree does not blossom, yet I will rejoice in the Lord."|Faith that does not depend on circumstances.
zephaniah 1|The day of wrath|God will sweep away idolaters and the complacent who say he does nothing. The great day of the Lord is near.|The medieval hymn Dies Irae draws on this chapter.
zephaniah 2|Seek the Lord|"Seek the Lord, all you humble of the land." Judgement on Philistia, Moab, Cush and Assyria.|The humble may be sheltered.
zephaniah 3|Sing, daughter of Zion|Jerusalem's leaders are condemned, but a humble remnant will remain. "The Lord your God is in your midst; he will rejoice over you with singing."|God sings over his people.
haggai 1|Consider your ways|The returned exiles live in panelled houses while the Temple lies in ruins. They work and never have enough. They start building.|Put God first.
haggai 2|The glory of this house|The new Temple looks poor beside the old. God says, "Be strong; I am with you. The latter glory of this house shall be greater."|Christ himself would enter this Temple.
zechariah 1|Return to me|"Return to me, and I will return to you." Zechariah sees horsemen patrolling the earth and four horns that scattered Judah.|God is jealous for Jerusalem.
zechariah 2|A city without walls|A man goes to measure Jerusalem. It will overflow its walls, and God will be a wall of fire around it.|"Many nations shall join themselves to the Lord."
zechariah 3|Clean garments|The high priest Joshua stands in filthy clothes with Satan accusing him. God rebukes Satan and has Joshua clothed in clean robes.|God removes guilt and silences the accuser.
zechariah 4|Not by might|A golden lampstand and two olive trees. "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit."|God's work is done by God's Spirit.
zechariah 5|The flying scroll|A huge scroll flies out against thieves and liars. Wickedness is carried off in a basket to Babylon.|Sin is removed from the land.
zechariah 6|Four chariots and a crown|Chariots go out to the four winds. A crown is set on the high priest Joshua, a sign of "the Branch" who will build the Lord's Temple.|Priest and king united point to Christ.
zechariah 7|True fasting|Asked about fast days, God answers: show kindness and mercy, and do not oppress the widow or the poor.|The same answer as Isaiah 58.
zechariah 8|Jerusalem's future|Old people will sit in the streets and children play there. Ten men from the nations will take hold of one Jew, saying, "God is with you."|Fasts will become feasts.
zechariah 9|The king on a donkey|"Rejoice, daughter of Zion! Your king comes to you, humble and riding on a donkey."|Fulfilled on Palm Sunday.
zechariah 10|The flock restored|The people wander like sheep without a shepherd. God will bring them home.|He cares for his flock.
zechariah 11|Thirty pieces of silver|The prophet acts as shepherd of a doomed flock and is paid thirty pieces of silver, which he throws into the Temple.|Matthew sees this fulfilled in Judas.
zechariah 12|The one they pierced|God pours out a spirit of grace and prayer. "They shall look on him whom they have pierced, and mourn."|John quotes this at the crucifixion.
zechariah 13|A fountain opened|A fountain is opened to cleanse from sin. "Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered."|Jesus quotes this on the night of his arrest.
zechariah 14|The Lord will be king|The nations attack Jerusalem, the Lord fights for it, and living waters flow from the city.|"The Lord will be king over all the earth."
malachi 1|A pure offering|"I have loved you," says the Lord. The priests offer blind and lame animals. "From the rising of the sun to its setting, a pure offering is made to my name among the nations."|The Church sees in that verse a prophecy of the Mass, quoted in the Eucharistic Prayer.
malachi 2|Unfaithful priests and husbands|Priests have turned many from the way. Men are divorcing the wives of their youth.|God seeks faithful teaching and faithful marriage.
malachi 3|The messenger|"I send my messenger to prepare the way. The Lord will suddenly come to his Temple," like a refiner's fire. Bring the full tithe.|The messenger is John the Baptist.
malachi 4|The sun of justice|The day is coming, burning like an oven. For those who fear God's name the sun of justice will rise with healing in its wings. Elijah will come first.|The last words of the prophets point straight to the Gospel.
