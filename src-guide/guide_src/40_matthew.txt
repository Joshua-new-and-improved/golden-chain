matthew 1|The family tree and birth of Jesus|Matthew lists Jesus' ancestors from Abraham through King David down to Joseph. Then he tells how Mary conceived by the Holy Spirit, and how an angel told Joseph in a dream to take her home and name the child Jesus.|The list shows Jesus is the promised heir of Abraham and David. The name Emmanuel, "God with us", says who this child really is.
matthew 2|Wise men, a jealous king, and the flight to Egypt|Wise men from the East follow a star and worship the child with gold, frankincense and myrrh. King Herod, afraid of a rival, kills the baby boys of Bethlehem. Joseph, warned in a dream, takes Mary and Jesus to Egypt and later settles in Nazareth.|From the start, foreigners adore Jesus while the powerful try to destroy him. Matthew shows the old prophecies coming true step by step.
matthew 3|John the Baptist and the baptism of Jesus|John preaches in the desert: turn back to God, because the kingdom is near. He warns the religious leaders not to rely on their ancestry. Jesus comes to be baptised, the Spirit comes down like a dove, and the Father's voice calls him "my beloved Son".|Jesus has no sin, yet he stands with sinners. Father, Son and Spirit are all revealed here at once.
matthew 4|Tempted in the desert, then the first disciples|After forty days of fasting, the devil tempts Jesus three times. Jesus answers each time with Scripture and does not give in. He then begins preaching in Galilee and calls four fishermen, Peter, Andrew, James and John, who leave their nets at once.|Where Israel failed in the desert, Jesus stays faithful. His call to the fishermen is the same call he makes to us: follow me.
matthew 5|The Sermon on the Mount begins: the Beatitudes|Jesus goes up a mountain and teaches. He calls blessed the poor in spirit, the meek, the merciful and the pure of heart. He says he has come to fulfil the Law, not cancel it, and he goes to the root of each command: no anger, no lust, no revenge, love even for enemies.|This is the charter of the Christian life. God asks for a changed heart, not only correct outward behaviour.
matthew 6|Giving, praying and fasting in secret; the Our Father|Jesus says to give to the poor, pray and fast without showing off, because the Father sees what is hidden. He teaches the Our Father. He warns that no one can serve both God and money, and tells his listeners not to be anxious about food and clothes.|Religion done to impress people is empty. Trusting God as a Father sets us free from worry.
matthew 7|Do not judge; ask, seek, knock; the two houses|Jesus warns against judging others while ignoring our own faults. He promises that the Father gives good things to those who ask. He gives the Golden Rule, warns about the narrow gate and false prophets, and ends with two builders, one on rock and one on sand.|Hearing Jesus is not enough. The person who puts his words into practice is the one whose life stands firm.
matthew 8|A leper, a centurion and a storm|Jesus heals a leper by touching him, heals a Roman officer's servant from a distance, and cures Peter's mother-in-law. He calms a storm with a word and drives demons out of two men into a herd of pigs.|His actions back up his teaching. The centurion's words, "Lord, I am not worthy", are the ones we pray before Communion.
matthew 9|Sins forgiven and Matthew called|Jesus forgives a paralysed man's sins and then heals him to prove he has that authority. He calls Matthew the tax collector and eats with sinners. He raises a dead girl, heals a woman who touches his cloak, and gives sight to two blind men.|Jesus is a doctor for sick souls. Forgiving sins is something only God can do, and he does it.
matthew 10|The Twelve are sent out|Jesus names the twelve apostles and sends them to preach and heal. He warns them they will be hated and dragged before courts, but tells them not to be afraid. Whoever loves anyone more than him is not worthy of him.|Following Jesus has a cost, and he says so plainly. He also promises that the Father, who counts every hair on our head, will not forget us.
matthew 11|John's question and the gentle yoke|From prison John the Baptist sends to ask if Jesus is the one to come. Jesus points to his works: the blind see and the poor hear good news. He rebukes towns that saw miracles and did not change, and invites the weary to come to him for rest.|God shows himself to the humble and simple. "Come to me, all you who labour" is one of the kindest lines in the Gospel.
matthew 12|Lord of the Sabbath|The Pharisees attack Jesus for letting his disciples pick grain and for healing on the Sabbath. They say his power comes from the devil. Jesus answers that a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand, and says his true family are those who do the Father's will.|The leaders see the same miracles as everyone else and still refuse to believe. Unbelief here is a choice, not a lack of evidence.
matthew 13|Parables of the kingdom|Jesus teaches the crowds in stories: the sower, the weeds among the wheat, the mustard seed, the yeast, the hidden treasure, the pearl and the net. He explains some of them privately to his disciples. His home town of Nazareth rejects him.|The kingdom starts small and grows quietly. Good and evil are mixed together for now, and God will sort them at the end.
matthew 14|Five loaves and walking on water|Herod has John the Baptist beheaded. Jesus feeds more than five thousand people with five loaves and two fish. That night he walks across the lake to the disciples' boat. Peter walks toward him, grows afraid and starts to sink, and Jesus catches him.|The feeding points ahead to the Eucharist. Peter sinks when he looks at the waves and is safe when he looks at Christ.
matthew 15|What really makes a person unclean|Jesus tells the Pharisees that human traditions must not override God's commandments. What defiles a person comes from the heart, not from food. He heals the daughter of a foreign woman who will not give up asking, and feeds four thousand.|Sin begins inside us. The Canaanite woman shows the kind of stubborn, humble faith Jesus loves.
matthew 16|Peter the rock|Jesus asks who the disciples say he is. Peter answers, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God." Jesus calls him the rock on which he will build his Church and gives him the keys of the kingdom. Then he foretells his death, and rebukes Peter for objecting.|Catholics see here the foundation of the papacy. Right after it comes the cross: there is no following Christ without it.
matthew 17|The Transfiguration|On a mountain Jesus' face shines like the sun before Peter, James and John. Moses and Elijah appear, and the Father says, "Listen to him." Coming down, Jesus heals a boy the disciples could not cure and pays the temple tax with a coin from a fish's mouth.|The three apostles see his glory so that their faith will survive seeing him crucified.
matthew 18|Becoming like children, and forgiving|Jesus says the greatest in the kingdom is the one who becomes humble like a child. He warns against leading little ones into sin, tells of the lost sheep, and explains how to correct a brother. Peter asks how often to forgive. Jesus says seventy times seven and tells of the servant who would not forgive.|We have been forgiven a huge debt. Refusing to forgive others makes no sense after that.
matthew 19|Marriage, children and the rich young man|Jesus teaches that marriage joins man and woman for life, as God made it from the beginning. He blesses children. A rich young man keeps the commandments but walks away sad when asked to sell everything and follow Jesus.|Jesus restores marriage to God's original plan. Wealth is dangerous when we cling to it more than to him.
matthew 20|Workers in the vineyard|A landowner pays the same wage to workers hired at dawn and at the last hour. Jesus foretells his Passion a third time. The mother of James and John asks for top places for her sons, and Jesus says greatness means serving. He heals two blind men near Jericho.|God's generosity is not a payment we earn. The Son of Man came to serve and to give his life as a ransom.
matthew 21|Palm Sunday and the cleansing of the Temple|Jesus rides into Jerusalem on a donkey while crowds shout "Hosanna". He drives the traders out of the Temple and curses a fig tree that bears no fruit. He tells the parables of the two sons and the wicked tenants against the leaders.|The King arrives humbly, as the prophet said. The leaders who should have welcomed him are the ones rejecting him.
matthew 22|The wedding feast and trick questions|In a parable, invited guests refuse a king's wedding feast, so others are brought in. Opponents try to trap Jesus about taxes to Caesar, the resurrection and the greatest commandment. He answers: love God with everything, and your neighbour as yourself.|All of God's law hangs on those two commands. An invitation from God can be refused, and that has consequences.
matthew 23|Seven woes against the scribes and Pharisees|Jesus tells the people to follow what their teachers say but not what they do. He condemns their hypocrisy seven times: they load others with burdens, love titles, and clean the outside of the cup while the inside is filthy. He weeps over Jerusalem.|This is the strongest language Jesus ever uses, and it is aimed at religious people who fake holiness.
matthew 24|The end of the Temple and the end of the world|Jesus foretells that the Temple will be destroyed. He speaks of wars, persecution, false messiahs and finally his own return in glory. No one knows the day or the hour, so his servants must stay awake and ready.|The Temple did fall in AD 70. The lesson is not to work out dates but to live ready every day.
matthew 25|Bridesmaids, talents, sheep and goats|Three pictures of the judgement. Ten bridesmaids wait for the groom and five run out of oil. Servants are given money to invest. Finally the King separates people like sheep and goats according to how they treated the hungry, the stranger, the sick and the prisoner.|Whatever we do for the least person, we do for Christ. We will be judged on love shown in practice.
matthew 26|The Last Supper, Gethsemane and the arrest|A woman anoints Jesus, and Judas agrees to betray him for thirty silver coins. At the Passover meal Jesus gives the bread and cup as his Body and Blood. He prays in agony in the garden, is arrested, tried before the high priest, and denied three times by Peter.|Here the Eucharist is given. Jesus goes to his death freely, saying to the Father, "your will be done".
matthew 27|The trial before Pilate and the crucifixion|Judas despairs and hangs himself. Pilate finds no guilt in Jesus but hands him over, and the crowd chooses Barabbas. Jesus is scourged, mocked, crucified between two thieves, and dies. The Temple curtain tears, and a centurion says, "Truly this was the Son of God." He is buried in a guarded tomb.|This is the centre of the Gospel: the innocent one dies for the guilty.
matthew 28|He is risen|At dawn on Sunday an angel rolls back the stone and tells the women the tomb is empty. Jesus meets them on the road. The guards are bribed to say the body was stolen. In Galilee Jesus sends the eleven to make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father, Son and Holy Spirit.|The resurrection is the foundation of the faith. His last words are a promise: "I am with you always."
