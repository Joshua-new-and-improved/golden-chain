luke 1|Two births announced|The angel Gabriel tells the old priest Zechariah that his wife Elizabeth will bear John the Baptist. Then Gabriel tells Mary, a virgin in Nazareth, that she will conceive the Son of God. Mary says yes, visits Elizabeth, and sings the Magnificat. John is born.|Mary's "let it be done to me" opens the door to our salvation. Her song and Zechariah's are still prayed by the Church every day.
luke 2|The birth of Jesus|Because of a census, Joseph and Mary travel to Bethlehem, where Jesus is born and laid in a manger. Angels announce it to shepherds. In the Temple old Simeon and Anna recognise the child. At twelve Jesus is found among the teachers in the Temple.|God comes in poverty, and poor shepherds are the first to hear. Mary keeps all these things and ponders them in her heart.
luke 3|John the Baptist preaches|John calls people to repent and tells tax collectors and soldiers what honest living looks like. Jesus is baptised and the Father's voice is heard. Luke then traces Jesus' family line all the way back to Adam.|Repentance has to show in how we treat people. The family tree back to Adam shows Jesus came for the whole human race.
luke 4|Temptation, then rejection at Nazareth|Jesus resists three temptations in the desert. In the synagogue at Nazareth he reads Isaiah, "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me", and says it is fulfilled today. The town turns on him and tries to throw him off a cliff. He goes on healing in Capernaum.|Jesus announces his mission: good news to the poor and freedom for captives. His own neighbours are the first to refuse it.
luke 5|"Put out into the deep"|After a night of catching nothing, Peter lets down the nets at Jesus' word and the catch nearly sinks the boat. Peter falls to his knees. Jesus heals a leper and a paralysed man, and calls Levi the tax collector.|Peter feels unworthy, and Jesus calls him anyway. He calls sinners, not people who think they are fine.
luke 6|The Sermon on the Plain|Jesus chooses the Twelve after a night of prayer. He blesses the poor and hungry and warns the rich and comfortable. He commands love of enemies, mercy, and not judging, and ends with the house built on rock.|"Be merciful, as your Father is merciful." The standard for how we treat others is how God treats us.
luke 7|Faith and forgiveness|Jesus heals a centurion's servant and raises a widow's only son at Nain. He praises John the Baptist. At a Pharisee's dinner a sinful woman washes his feet with her tears, and he tells her that her sins are forgiven.|The person who knows how much she has been forgiven loves much. Pride blocks that love.
luke 8|The sower and the power of Jesus|Women disciples travel with Jesus and support him. He tells the parable of the sower, calms a storm, frees a possessed man, heals a woman with a haemorrhage and raises the daughter of Jairus.|God's word bears fruit in those who hear it and hold on to it with patience.
luke 9|The road turns toward Jerusalem|Jesus sends out the Twelve, feeds five thousand, hears Peter confess him as the Christ, and is transfigured. He foretells his death and says his followers must take up their cross daily. Then he sets his face to go to Jerusalem.|From here the Gospel is a journey to the cross. Discipleship is a daily choice, not a single moment.
luke 10|The Good Samaritan; Martha and Mary|Jesus sends out seventy-two disciples. A lawyer asks, "Who is my neighbour?" and Jesus tells of a Samaritan who helps a beaten man that a priest and a Levite had walked past. At Bethany Martha is busy serving while Mary sits listening.|A neighbour is anyone in need whom I can help. Listening to Jesus comes before working for him.
luke 11|Lord, teach us to pray|Jesus gives the Our Father and tells his disciples to keep asking, seeking and knocking. He is accused of working by the devil's power. He rebukes Pharisees and lawyers for caring about appearances while neglecting justice and the love of God.|The Father gives the Holy Spirit to those who ask. Persevering in prayer is part of trusting him.
luke 12|Do not be afraid; be ready|Jesus warns against hypocrisy and greed. He tells of a rich fool who builds bigger barns and dies that night. He says not to worry, because the Father knows what we need, and to be like servants waiting for their master.|"Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also." Life is not measured by possessions.
luke 13|Repent while there is time|Jesus says disasters are not proof that the victims were worse sinners; everyone needs to repent. He tells of a fig tree given one more year, heals a bent woman on the Sabbath, speaks of the narrow door and laments over Jerusalem.|God is patient, but the time to turn back is limited. Patience is not the same as indifference.
luke 14|The lowest place|At a dinner Jesus tells guests to take the lowest seat and hosts to invite the poor. He tells of a great banquet whose invited guests make excuses. He says anyone who follows him must count the cost and put him before family and possessions.|"Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled." The kingdom is a feast, and the excuses of the busy keep them out of it.
luke 15|Lost and found|Three parables answer those who complain that Jesus eats with sinners: a shepherd leaves ninety-nine sheep for one, a woman sweeps the house for a coin, and a father runs to welcome back a son who wasted everything, while the elder brother sulks outside.|This is the heart of Luke's Gospel. God does not wait coldly for sinners; he goes looking and rejoices when they come home.
luke 16|Money and the life to come|Jesus tells of a dishonest manager who plans cleverly for his future, and says no one can serve God and money. Then he tells of a rich man who ignored the beggar Lazarus at his gate and found their places reversed after death.|What we do with wealth here matters for ever. The rich man's sin was not cruelty but never noticing.
luke 17|Faith, duty and thanks|Jesus warns against causing others to sin and commands forgiveness. He says servants who have done their duty should not expect praise. Ten lepers are healed, and only one, a Samaritan, comes back to thank him. He speaks of the coming of the kingdom.|Gratitude is rare. The one who returns to give thanks receives more than healing.
luke 18|Pray and do not lose heart|A widow wears down an unjust judge by asking again and again. A Pharisee boasts in his prayer while a tax collector says only, "God, be merciful to me, a sinner." Jesus welcomes children, meets a rich ruler, and heals a blind beggar near Jericho.|The humble prayer is the one God hears. The tax collector's words are a perfect short prayer.
luke 19|Zacchaeus and the entry into Jerusalem|Zacchaeus, a rich tax collector, climbs a tree to see Jesus, welcomes him home and promises to repay those he cheated. Jesus tells the parable of the coins, rides into Jerusalem, weeps over the city and clears the Temple.|"The Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost." Real conversion shows in putting things right.
luke 20|Jesus questioned in the Temple|The leaders challenge his authority. He tells of the wicked tenants, answers the question about paying taxes to Caesar, and defends the resurrection against the Sadducees: God is the God of the living.|"Give to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's." We bear God's image, so we belong to him.
luke 21|The widow's coins and the days to come|A poor widow gives two small coins, more than all the rich. Jesus foretells the fall of Jerusalem, persecution, and signs before his return. He tells his disciples to stay alert and pray.|"By your endurance you will gain your lives." Trouble is not a sign that God has lost control.
luke 22|The Last Supper and the arrest|Judas agrees to betray Jesus. At the Passover Jesus gives his Body and Blood and says, "Do this in memory of me." He prays in agony on the Mount of Olives, is arrested, and Peter denies him three times. Jesus turns and looks at Peter, who weeps bitterly.|The Eucharist and the priesthood begin here. Peter's tears show that failure need not be the end.
luke 23|The cross|Jesus is tried before Pilate and Herod and condemned. On the cross he prays, "Father, forgive them." One of the criminals beside him asks to be remembered, and Jesus promises, "Today you will be with me in paradise." He dies and is buried.|Even while dying Jesus forgives and saves. The good thief shows it is never too late to turn to him.
luke 24|The road to Emmaus|Women find the tomb empty. Two disciples walk to Emmaus with a stranger who explains the Scriptures, and they recognise Jesus when he breaks the bread. He appears to the apostles, eats with them, opens their minds to the Scriptures, and ascends.|The risen Lord is known in the Scriptures and in the breaking of the bread. That is the shape of every Mass.
