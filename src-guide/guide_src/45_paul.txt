romans 1|The Gospel is God's power to save|Paul greets the Christians of Rome and says he is not ashamed of the Gospel. He then shows the Gentile world's problem: people could know God from what he has made, but they chose idols, and their lives fell apart as a result.|God can be known by reason from creation. Turning from him is the root of moral disorder.
romans 2|God judges fairly|Paul turns to those who condemn others while doing the same things. God will repay each person according to their works and has no favourites. Gentiles have the law written on their hearts in conscience; having the Law of Moses does not help unless it is kept.|Everyone has a conscience that points to right and wrong. Religious privilege is no shield against judgement.
romans 3|All have sinned|Jews and Gentiles alike are under sin; no one is righteous by their own record. But now God offers righteousness as a gift, through faith in Jesus Christ, whose blood makes up for sins.|Nobody earns salvation. It is a gift won by Christ's sacrifice, and it leaves no room for boasting.
romans 4|Abraham believed|Abraham was counted righteous because he trusted God's promise, before he was circumcised. So he is the father of everyone who believes, Jew or Gentile. He believed that God could give life to the dead.|Faith was always the way to God, even in the Old Testament. Abraham's trust is the pattern for ours.
romans 5|Peace with God; Adam and Christ|Made right with God through faith, we have peace and a hope that does not disappoint, because God's love has been poured into our hearts. Through one man, Adam, sin and death came into the world. Through one man, Christ, grace and life overflow.|This chapter is a key source for the teaching on original sin. Christ's gift is far greater than Adam's damage.
romans 6|Dead to sin, alive in Christ|Should we sin more so that grace increases? Never. In baptism we were buried with Christ and raised to a new life. We are no longer slaves of sin, and we should offer ourselves to God.|Baptism really changes us; it is not only a symbol. Grace is the power to stop sinning, not permission to carry on.
romans 7|The struggle within|Paul says the Law is good, but it shows up sin without giving the power to defeat it. He describes the experience of wanting to do good and doing the evil he hates. "Who will rescue me? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ."|Everyone knows this inner battle. Knowing what is right is not enough; we need grace.
romans 8|Life in the Spirit|There is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. The Spirit makes us God's children, who can call him "Abba, Father". Creation groans, waiting to be set free, and the Spirit helps us pray. Nothing can separate us from the love of God.|This is one of the greatest chapters in the Bible. God works all things for good for those who love him.
romans 9|Israel and God's free choice|Paul grieves that most of his own people have not accepted Christ. He explains that God's promise has not failed: God has always been free in choosing, as with Isaac and Jacob, and he shows mercy as he wills.|Salvation depends on God's mercy, not on ancestry or effort. Paul treats this as a mystery to adore, not a puzzle to solve.
romans 10|Faith comes from hearing|Israel was zealous for God but tried to set up its own righteousness. Salvation is near: confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him. But people cannot believe unless someone is sent to preach.|This is why the Church sends missionaries. Faith is a response to a message someone has to bring.
romans 11|The olive tree|God has not rejected Israel. Some natural branches were broken off and Gentiles, like wild shoots, were grafted in, so they must not be proud. In the end all Israel will be saved. Paul ends in praise of God's wisdom.|Christians owe respect and gratitude to the Jewish people. God's gifts and his call are never withdrawn.
romans 12|A living sacrifice|Because of God's mercy, offer your whole self to him and do not be shaped by the world. The Church is one body with different gifts. Love sincerely, bless those who persecute you, and overcome evil with good.|Here doctrine turns into daily life. Worship includes how we live from Monday to Saturday.
romans 13|Rulers, debts and love|Paul teaches respect for lawful authority and paying what is owed. The one lasting debt is to love one another, which fulfils the whole law. The night is nearly over, so put on the Lord Jesus Christ.|Christians are good citizens. It was verses 13 and 14 that Augustine read at the moment of his conversion.
romans 14|Do not judge your brother|Christians disagreed about food and special days. Paul says not to despise or judge one another over such matters, since each will answer to God. Do not let your freedom make a weaker believer stumble.|On things that are not essential, charity matters more than being right.
romans 15|Bear with one another|The strong should carry the weaknesses of the weak, following Christ, who did not please himself. Christ came for both Jews and Gentiles. Paul describes his mission and his plan to visit Rome on the way to Spain.|The Scriptures were written to give us hope. Unity in the Church is built by patience.
romans 16|Greetings to friends|Paul commends Phoebe, who carries the letter, and greets a long list of co-workers by name, many of them women. He warns against those who cause divisions and ends with praise to God.|The early Church was a network of real friendships. Paul remembers people one by one.
1-corinthians 1|Divisions, and the foolishness of the cross|The Corinthians have split into fan clubs: "I belong to Paul", "I belong to Apollos". Paul asks, "Is Christ divided?" He preaches Christ crucified, which looks like foolishness to the world but is God's power and wisdom.|God chooses what the world thinks weak. Factions in the Church contradict the cross.
1-corinthians 2|Wisdom from the Spirit|Paul came to Corinth without clever speeches, relying on God's power. There is a wisdom he does teach, hidden from the world's rulers and revealed by the Spirit, who knows the depths of God.|Faith rests on God's power, not on the skill of preachers. Some truths can only be received as a gift.
1-corinthians 3|God's field, God's building|The Corinthians are still acting like infants. Paul planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. Each builder's work will be tested by fire. "You are God's temple, and the Spirit of God dwells in you."|Ministers are servants, not celebrities. The passage about being saved "as through fire" is one the Church has read in connection with purgatory.
1-corinthians 4|Servants of Christ|Apostles are stewards of God's mysteries, and what is asked of a steward is faithfulness. Paul contrasts the Corinthians' pride with the apostles' hunger, insults and hard work. He writes as their father in Christ.|"What do you have that you did not receive?" Everything good in us is a gift.
1-corinthians 5|A scandal tolerated|A man is living with his father's wife, and the community is proud of its tolerance. Paul tells them to remove him from the community for the sake of his salvation. A little yeast affects the whole dough.|The Church corrects serious public sin out of love, hoping for repentance. Christ our Passover has been sacrificed, so we are to live in sincerity.
1-corinthians 6|Your body is a temple|Christians should not drag one another before pagan courts. Paul lists sins that keep people out of the kingdom, then adds: "such were some of you, but you were washed." He says to flee sexual immorality, because the body belongs to the Lord.|What we do with our bodies matters. "You were bought with a price; so glorify God in your body."
1-corinthians 7|Marriage and celibacy|Paul answers questions about marriage. Husband and wife belong to each other. Marriage is good and permanent, and remaining unmarried for the Lord is a gift that allows undivided attention to him. Each should live the calling God has given.|Both marriage and consecrated celibacy are real vocations. The Church honours each.
1-corinthians 8|Food offered to idols|Idols are nothing, so the meat is just meat. But not everyone knows that, and a believer with a weak conscience could be led into sin. Knowledge puffs up; love builds up.|Being right is not enough. Love limits its own freedom for the sake of others.
1-corinthians 9|Paul gives up his rights|As an apostle Paul has a right to be supported, but he does not use it, so that the Gospel is free. He becomes all things to all people to save some. Like an athlete he trains hard for a crown that lasts.|The Christian life takes discipline. Paul himself does not take his own salvation for granted.
1-corinthians 10|Warnings from the Exodus|Israel was fed and led by God in the desert, yet many fell. So let anyone who thinks he stands take care. God will not let you be tempted beyond your strength. The cup and the bread are a sharing in the Blood and Body of Christ.|No temptation is irresistible, because God always gives a way out. The Eucharist makes the many into one body.
1-corinthians 11|The Lord's Supper|After instructions on conduct at worship, Paul rebukes the rich for humiliating the poor at the community meal. He hands on what he received: the words of Jesus at the Last Supper. Whoever eats and drinks unworthily answers for the Body and Blood of the Lord.|This is the earliest written account of the Eucharist. Paul's warning is why Catholics examine their conscience before Communion.
1-corinthians 12|One body, many parts|There are many gifts but one Spirit who gives them for the common good. As a body has many parts, so does Christ. The eye cannot say to the hand, "I do not need you."|No one in the Church is useless, and no one is the whole. If one member suffers, all suffer together.
1-corinthians 13|The hymn to love|Without love, tongues, prophecy, knowledge and even martyrdom are worth nothing. Love is patient and kind; it does not envy or boast. Gifts will pass away. Faith, hope and love remain, and the greatest is love.|This love is charity: willing the good of the other for God's sake. It is the measure of everything else.
1-corinthians 14|Order in worship|Paul prefers prophecy, which builds up the whole assembly, to speaking in tongues that no one understands. Everything in the gathering should be intelligible and done for building up.|"God is not a God of confusion but of peace." Worship is for God and for the good of all present, not for self-display.
1-corinthians 15|The resurrection|Paul repeats the creed he received: Christ died, was buried, rose on the third day, and appeared to many witnesses. If Christ is not risen, our faith is useless. But he is risen, the first fruits, and our bodies too will be raised imperishable.|Christianity stands or falls on a real event. "Death, where is your sting?"
1-corinthians 16|Collection and farewell|Paul arranges a collection for the poor Christians of Jerusalem, to be set aside on the first day of each week. He shares travel plans and greetings.|"Let all that you do be done in love." Even money and logistics belong to the life of faith.
2-corinthians 1|The God of all comfort|Paul blesses God, who comforts us in every trouble so that we can comfort others. He describes a danger in Asia so severe that he despaired of life, and explains why he changed his plans to visit.|Suffering shared with Christ equips us to console others. God's promises all find their "Yes" in Jesus.
2-corinthians 2|Forgive the offender|Paul wrote an earlier letter in tears. Now he urges the Corinthians to forgive and comfort someone who has been punished enough. Christians are "the aroma of Christ" wherever they go.|Church discipline aims at restoring people. Once someone repents, mercy should be quick.
2-corinthians 3|The new covenant|The Corinthians themselves are Paul's letter of recommendation, written by the Spirit on hearts. The old covenant on stone had glory; the new covenant of the Spirit has far more. With unveiled faces we are being changed into the Lord's likeness.|"The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life." The Christian life is a gradual transformation.
2-corinthians 4|Treasure in clay jars|Paul does not lose heart. The light of the Gospel is carried in fragile human beings, so that the power is clearly God's. Afflicted but not crushed, he looks to what is unseen and eternal.|God works through weak people on purpose. Present troubles are light compared with the glory to come.
2-corinthians 5|A new creation|Our earthly body is a tent; God is preparing an eternal home. We walk by faith, not by sight, and all must appear before Christ's judgement seat. Anyone in Christ is a new creation. God has given the apostles the ministry of reconciliation.|"Be reconciled to God." The Church sees the ministry of reconciliation continued in the sacrament of confession.
2-corinthians 6|Now is the day of salvation|Paul lists the hardships that prove his ministry: beatings, sleepless nights, hunger, yet always rejoicing. He urges the Corinthians to open their hearts and not to be yoked together with unbelievers.|Do not put off responding to grace. Close partnerships shape who we become.
2-corinthians 7|Good grief|Titus brings news that the Corinthians took Paul's severe letter well. Paul rejoices: sorrow that leads to repentance brings salvation and no regret, while worldly sorrow only produces death.|There are two kinds of feeling bad about sin. The good kind moves us to change.
2-corinthians 8|Generous giving|The poor churches of Macedonia gave beyond their means for the Christians in Jerusalem. Paul encourages Corinth to finish its own collection, pointing to Christ, who was rich and became poor for our sake.|Christian giving imitates Jesus. The aim is that those with plenty supply those in need.
2-corinthians 9|God loves a cheerful giver|Whoever sows sparingly reaps sparingly. Each should give what he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under pressure. God is able to provide all that is needed for every good work.|Generosity is an act of trust in God, and it produces thanksgiving to him.
2-corinthians 10|Paul defends his authority|Critics say Paul is bold in letters but unimpressive in person. He answers that his weapons are spiritual, able to demolish arguments, and that he boasts only in the Lord.|The battle for souls is fought with truth, not worldly methods. We take every thought captive to obey Christ.
2-corinthians 11|Paul's "foolish" boasting|False apostles are impressing the Corinthians. Paul reluctantly lists what he has suffered: five floggings, three beatings with rods, a stoning, three shipwrecks, hunger, cold, and daily anxiety for the churches.|The marks of a true apostle are sufferings borne for Christ, not polish or success.
2-corinthians 12|Strength in weakness|Paul speaks of being caught up to paradise. To keep him humble he was given "a thorn in the flesh". Three times he begged the Lord to remove it and was told, "My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness."|God sometimes leaves a weakness in place and gives the grace to bear it. "When I am weak, then I am strong."
2-corinthians 13|Examine yourselves|Paul warns that on his third visit he will not spare those who keep sinning. He tells the Corinthians to test whether they are living in the faith, and closes with a blessing that names Christ, God and the Holy Spirit.|The final blessing is used as a greeting at Mass. Self-examination is a normal part of Christian life.
galatians 1|No other Gospel|Paul is astonished that the Galatians are turning to a different gospel. Even if an angel preached something contrary, it should be rejected. His own Gospel came by revelation from Christ, who called him when he was persecuting the Church.|The faith is received, not invented or edited. Paul's own conversion shows it came from God.
galatians 2|Paul confronts Peter|In Jerusalem the leading apostles recognised Paul's mission to the Gentiles. Later, at Antioch, Paul opposed Peter to his face for drawing back from eating with Gentile Christians. A person is made right with God by faith in Christ, not by works of the Law.|Peter's fault was in conduct, not in teaching. "It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me."
galatians 3|Children of Abraham by faith|"O foolish Galatians!" They received the Spirit by believing, not by keeping the Law. The Law was a guardian until Christ came. All who are baptised have put on Christ; there is neither Jew nor Greek, slave nor free, male nor female.|Baptism makes all believers equal as God's children and heirs of the promise.
galatians 4|Sons, not slaves|When the time was right, God sent his Son, born of a woman, so that we could be adopted as his children and cry "Abba, Father". Paul pleads with the Galatians not to go back to slavery, and uses Hagar and Sarah as a picture of two covenants.|A Christian relates to God as a child to a father, not as a slave to a master.
galatians 5|Freedom and the fruit of the Spirit|Christ set us free, so do not accept the yoke of slavery again. What counts is faith working through love. Freedom is not an excuse for self-indulgence. The works of the flesh are plain; the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control.|Real freedom is the ability to love and do good. The Spirit's fruit is the sign that he is at work in us.
galatians 6|Carry one another's burdens|Gently restore anyone caught in sin. Bear each other's burdens. People reap what they sow, so do not tire of doing good. Paul boasts in nothing but the cross of Christ.|Faith shows in practical care for others. What matters is being a new creation.
ephesians 1|Every spiritual blessing|Paul praises God, who chose us in Christ before the world was made, adopted us, redeemed us through his blood, and sealed us with the Holy Spirit. He prays that the readers may understand the hope they are called to. Christ is head over all things for the Church.|God's plan is to unite everything in Christ. Our lives are part of something planned from eternity.
ephesians 2|Saved by grace|We were dead in sin, but God, rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ. It is by grace, through faith, as a gift. Christ has broken down the wall between Jew and Gentile and made one new people, built on the apostles and prophets.|We are saved by grace, and created for good works. The Church is one household, with Christ as cornerstone.
ephesians 3|The mystery revealed|Paul explains the secret now made known: Gentiles are fellow heirs with Israel. He kneels before the Father and prays that the readers may grasp the breadth, length, height and depth of Christ's love.|Christ's love is beyond knowledge, yet we are meant to know it. God can do far more than we ask or imagine.
ephesians 4|One body, one Spirit|Live worthy of your calling, with humility and patience. There is one Lord, one faith, one baptism. Christ gives apostles, prophets, pastors and teachers to build up the body. Put off the old self; speak the truth, and do not let the sun go down on your anger.|Unity is a gift to be guarded. Growing up in Christ means leaving old habits behind.
ephesians 5|Walk in love; husbands and wives|Imitate God as beloved children. Avoid impurity and greed, and live as children of light. Husbands and wives are to give themselves to one another as Christ and the Church do; husbands must love their wives as Christ loved the Church and gave himself for her.|Marriage is a sign of Christ's love for his Church. The standard set for husbands is the cross.
ephesians 6|The armour of God|Children should obey their parents, and fathers should not provoke their children. Paul then describes the Christian's armour: the belt of truth, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, the word of God.|Our real fight is not against other people but against spiritual evil. Prayer is how we stand firm.
philippians 1|To live is Christ|Writing from prison, Paul thanks God for the Philippians and is sure that God, who began a good work in them, will finish it. His chains have helped spread the Gospel. He is torn between dying to be with Christ and staying to serve them.|Paul's joy does not depend on his circumstances. "To live is Christ, and to die is gain."
philippians 2|The humility of Christ|Paul asks for unity and humility, each looking to the interests of others. He quotes a hymn: Christ, though in the form of God, emptied himself, became a servant and was obedient to death on a cross. So God exalted him, and every knee shall bow.|This is one of the earliest statements that Jesus is God. His way down is the model for how Christians treat one another.
philippians 3|Everything counted as loss|Paul lists his impressive Jewish credentials and calls them rubbish compared with knowing Christ. He has not arrived yet; he presses on toward the goal. "Our citizenship is in heaven."|Knowing Jesus outweighs every achievement. The Christian life is a race still being run.
philippians 4|Rejoice in the Lord always|Paul urges two women to be reconciled. Do not be anxious; bring everything to God in prayer with thanksgiving, and his peace will guard your hearts. Think about whatever is true and honourable. Paul has learned to be content in any situation.|Paul writes this from prison. "I can do all things through him who strengthens me."
colossians 1|Christ above all|Paul thanks God for the Colossians' faith. Then he praises Christ: the image of the invisible God, through whom and for whom all things were made, head of the Church, the firstborn from the dead, who made peace by the blood of his cross.|Jesus is not one spiritual power among many. Everything holds together in him.
colossians 2|Fullness in Christ|Let no one mislead you with empty philosophy or rules about food, festivals and angels. In Christ the whole fullness of God dwells bodily, and you were buried and raised with him in baptism. He nailed the record of our debt to the cross.|We do not need secret extras beyond Christ. Baptism joins us to his death and resurrection.
colossians 3|Put on the new self|Since you have been raised with Christ, set your minds on things above. Put to death impurity, greed, anger and lying. Put on compassion, kindness, humility and patience, and above all love. Paul gives guidance for wives, husbands, children and servants.|"Whatever you do, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus." Holiness is lived at home and at work.
colossians 4|Prayer and wise speech|Persevere in prayer with thanksgiving. Behave wisely toward outsiders, and let your speech be gracious. Paul sends greetings from his companions, including Luke the physician and Mark.|Ordinary conversations are a chance to witness. The letter is to be read aloud and shared with other churches.
1-thessalonians 1|A model church|Paul thanks God for the Thessalonians' faith, love and hope. They received the word with joy in spite of suffering, turned from idols to the living God, and their example has spread everywhere.|This is probably the oldest writing in the New Testament. Conversion means turning from idols to serve the true God.
1-thessalonians 2|How Paul preached|Paul reminds them he did not flatter or seek money. He was gentle among them like a nursing mother and encouraged them like a father. They received his preaching as what it really is, the word of God.|The apostles' preaching carries God's own word. Good ministry shares life, not only information.
1-thessalonians 3|Timothy's good news|Unable to bear not knowing, Paul sent Timothy to see how they were coping with persecution. Timothy reports that they stand firm. Paul prays that their love may increase.|Paul's joy is tied to the faith of the people he taught. Christians are to strengthen one another under trial.
1-thessalonians 4|Holiness and hope for the dead|God's will is that you be holy: keep away from sexual immorality and love one another. About those who have died, do not grieve like people without hope. The Lord will come, the dead in Christ will rise, and we will be with the Lord always.|Christians do grieve, but with hope. Death does not break our union with Christ.
1-thessalonians 5|The day of the Lord|That day will come like a thief in the night, so stay awake and sober. Respect your leaders, be patient with everyone, rejoice always, pray constantly, give thanks in all circumstances, and test everything.|We do not know the date, so we live ready. "Pray without ceasing" describes a whole way of life.
2-thessalonians 1|Encouragement under persecution|Paul thanks God that their faith and love keep growing during persecution. God is just: he will give relief to the afflicted and repay those who afflict them when the Lord Jesus is revealed.|Suffering for the faith is not a sign that God has abandoned us. Justice will be done.
2-thessalonians 2|Do not be alarmed|Some claimed the day of the Lord had already arrived. Paul says a rebellion and "the man of lawlessness" must come first, and the Lord will destroy him. Meanwhile, stand firm and hold to the traditions taught by word of mouth or by letter.|Paul puts oral teaching beside written teaching. This is one basis for the Catholic regard for Tradition alongside Scripture.
2-thessalonians 3|Get back to work|Some believers had stopped working and were meddling in others' affairs. Paul points to his own example of labour and gives the rule: anyone unwilling to work should not eat. Do not grow tired of doing good.|Waiting for the Lord is no excuse for idleness. Ordinary work is part of a holy life.
1-timothy 1|Guard the true teaching|Paul tells Timothy to stop people teaching speculation and myths. The aim of instruction is love from a pure heart. Paul calls himself the foremost of sinners, shown mercy as an example of Christ's patience.|"Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners." Paul never forgot what he had been forgiven.
1-timothy 2|Prayer for everyone|Prayers should be offered for all people, including rulers. God wants everyone to be saved, and there is one mediator between God and humanity, the man Christ Jesus. Paul gives directions for conduct at worship.|God's saving will reaches every person. All Christian prayer goes to the Father through Christ.
1-timothy 3|Bishops and deacons|Paul lists the qualities needed in a bishop and in deacons: self-controlled, faithful, able to manage their own household, respected by outsiders. The Church is "the pillar and foundation of the truth".|Character matters for those in office. The Church is called the support of truth, not merely a gathering of like-minded people.
1-timothy 4|Train yourself in godliness|False teachers will forbid marriage and certain foods, though everything God created is good. Timothy is to train in godliness and not let anyone look down on his youth, but to set an example and not neglect the gift given by the laying on of hands.|Young people can lead by example. Ordination by the laying on of hands gives a real gift.
1-timothy 5|Care within the Church|Treat older men as fathers and younger women as sisters. The Church should support widows who are truly alone, while families care for their own. Elders who lead well deserve honour, and accusations against them need witnesses.|Faith includes practical duty to one's family. The Church organises its charity with care.
1-timothy 6|The love of money|Those who want to be rich fall into temptation. The love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Timothy must fight the good fight of faith. The rich should be generous and put their hope in God.|We brought nothing into the world and take nothing out. Contentment is great gain.
2-timothy 1|Rekindle the gift|From prison, near death, Paul writes to Timothy and recalls the faith of his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. He tells him to fan into flame the gift he received through the laying on of hands, and not to be ashamed of the Gospel.|Faith is often handed down in families. God gives a spirit of power, love and self-control, not of fear.
2-timothy 2|A good soldier of Christ|Timothy is to entrust the teaching to faithful people who can teach others. Like a soldier, an athlete and a farmer, he must endure hardship. He should avoid pointless quarrels and correct opponents gently.|This chain of teachers handing on the faith is apostolic tradition in action. "If we are faithless, he remains faithful."
2-timothy 3|Hard times and Scripture|In the last days people will love themselves, money and pleasure more than God. Timothy should continue in what he learned from childhood. All Scripture is inspired by God and useful for teaching, correcting and training in righteousness.|Scripture is God-breathed. It equips us for every good work.
2-timothy 4|"I have fought the good fight"|Paul charges Timothy to preach the word whether it is welcome or not. He knows his death is near: he has finished the race and kept the faith. Almost everyone has left him, but the Lord stood by him.|These are among Paul's last words. He faces death with confidence in the Lord, the just judge.
titus 1|Elders in Crete|Paul left Titus in Crete to appoint elders in every town. A bishop must be blameless, hospitable, self-controlled and able to teach sound doctrine and correct those who contradict it.|From the beginning the Church had ordained leaders responsible for guarding the faith.
titus 2|Sound teaching for everyone|Titus is to teach older men, older women, young people and servants how to live well. The grace of God has appeared, training us to give up ungodliness and to live upright lives while we wait for our blessed hope.|Grace teaches as well as forgives. Good lives make the Gospel attractive.
titus 3|Saved by mercy|Be obedient to rulers and gentle to all. We too were once foolish, but God saved us, not because of deeds we had done but by his mercy, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.|"The washing of rebirth" is baptism. Those who believe should be careful to devote themselves to good works.
philemon 1|A runaway slave welcomed as a brother|Paul writes to Philemon about Onesimus, a slave who ran away and became a Christian through Paul. Paul sends him back and asks Philemon to receive him "no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother", offering to pay anything owed.|Paul appeals out of love instead of giving orders. The Gospel changes relationships from the inside.
hebrews 1|God has spoken through his Son|Long ago God spoke through the prophets; now he has spoken through his Son, who is the radiance of his glory and upholds all things. The Son is far above the angels, as many Old Testament texts show.|Jesus is God's final and complete word. He is not an angel or a prophet but the eternal Son.
hebrews 2|Made like his brothers|We must pay close attention to what we have heard. Jesus, for a little while made lower than the angels, shared our flesh and blood, so that by dying he might destroy the power of death and help those who are tempted.|Because he suffered and was tempted, he can help us. He is not ashamed to call us brothers.
hebrews 3|Greater than Moses|Moses was faithful as a servant in God's house; Christ is faithful as the Son over it. The Israelites who left Egypt hardened their hearts and did not enter God's rest. "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts."|Starting well is not enough. We need to encourage one another every day so that no one is hardened by sin.
hebrews 4|The rest that remains|A sabbath rest still awaits God's people, so let us strive to enter it. The word of God is living and sharper than any sword. We have a great high priest who sympathises with our weakness, so we can approach the throne of grace with confidence.|Jesus was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin. We can come to him without fear.
hebrews 5|A high priest called by God|Every high priest is chosen from among men and can deal gently with the weak. Christ did not take this honour for himself; God appointed him, a priest for ever in the order of Melchizedek. He learned obedience through what he suffered.|Christ's priesthood is the source of all priesthood. The author scolds readers who still need milk when they should be on solid food.
hebrews 6|Press on to maturity|The author warns of the danger of falling away after receiving God's gifts, but is confident of better things for his readers. God's promise, backed by his oath, is a sure and steady anchor for the soul.|Hope is an anchor fixed where Jesus has already gone ahead of us.
hebrews 7|Melchizedek|Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, blessed Abraham. Christ is a priest like him: not by descent from Levi, but by the power of an indestructible life. He lives for ever to intercede for us.|Jesus' priesthood never ends, so he can save completely those who come to God through him.
hebrews 8|A better covenant|Our high priest serves in the true sanctuary in heaven. Jeremiah had foretold a new covenant, with God's law written on hearts and sins remembered no more. That covenant has come.|The Old Testament itself announced that something greater was coming.
hebrews 9|The blood of Christ|The old tent had a Holy of Holies that the high priest entered once a year with the blood of animals. Christ entered heaven itself once for all with his own blood, winning eternal redemption.|The sacrifices of the Temple were shadows. Christ's one sacrifice is the reality, and the Mass makes it present.
hebrews 10|Once for all|The blood of bulls and goats could never take away sins. Christ offered one sacrifice for sins for ever. So let us draw near with confidence, hold fast to our hope, and not neglect to meet together.|Do not stay away from the assembly. Christian faith is lived with others, especially at worship.
hebrews 11|Heroes of faith|Faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen. By faith Abel, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses, Rahab and many others obeyed, endured and looked forward to a homeland they did not yet see.|The Old Testament saints lived by trusting God's promise. We are part of the same story.
hebrews 12|Run the race|Surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us run with perseverance, looking to Jesus. God disciplines us as a father disciplines his children. We have come to Mount Zion, the city of the living God.|Hardship can be the Father's training. Keeping our eyes on Jesus is how we finish.
hebrews 13|Final instructions|Keep loving one another, welcome strangers, remember prisoners, honour marriage, and be content with what you have. Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and for ever. Obey your leaders, and offer God a sacrifice of praise.|Faith shows in hospitality, purity and generosity. Christ does not change, whatever else does.
